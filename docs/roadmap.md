Live roadmap in the parsimonious format (`docs/roadmap-template.md`). Rules: only cards in **Next** get built; **Now** holds at most three cards (the current batch); ideas enter **Later** as one line; **Done** is one line per shipped item. Full build briefs for open items live in `docs/specs/`; the original phased plan is preserved in `docs/roadmap-history.md`.

## Done

- 2026-09-22 — LIS clipboard bridge (user request, size M). The LIS turned out to be a Citrix published application: `ahk_class Transparent Windows Client`, `ahk_exe wfica32.exe`, **no ClassNN on any field**, and field advance on Ctrl+] rather than Tab. There is no control to read, so `ControlGetText`, UI Automation and every emulator API are ruled out from the client side for good; the clipboard is the entire interface, and it was measured working in both directions. One payload format serves both ways, defined in `src/lisbridge.js` and split again by `tools/lis-bridge.ahk`: a `<<<KEY>>>` marker on its own line above its text, plain enough to read in Notepad when a field arrives empty, which a control character would not be. Out: **Copy for LIS** emits every non-empty block in report order; on the LIS side one keypress loads the queue and one pastes the next block, with automatic field advance present but switched off, because a single wrong advance would put a diagnosis in the wrong field and the caret deciding means the bridge never needs to know the LIS field order. In: **Capture from LIS** fills Specimen, Clinical history and Macroscopic at once from a marked payload, or fills the next empty box in order from a plain single-field copy, re-running the intake strip and the macro parse exactly as typing does. `phiWarnings` now runs at capture, not only at Drive save, because this text came straight out of the clinical system. 1160 tests, plus three browser checks; writing the browser check found a pre-existing defect it was not looking for — the intake strip's template picker is pinned to 28 px at 13 px type so a row stays on one line, which on a phone is under the 44 px thumb target and under the 16 px at which iOS zooms the page on focus and stays zoomed. It went unseen because the strip only appears once a specimen has been entered, and no phone check had entered one. Spec: `docs/specs/lis-bridge.md`.

- 2026-09-21 — Reported defect: "Build parts" did nothing for a specimen with one part. "Antrum" is a gastric biopsy to any pathologist reading it and the app returned nothing at all. Two separate causes, and the second was only found because the first was measured rather than assumed. The parser cut parts at `(A)` markers alone, and **not one of the 4545 specimen texts in the 2024–2026 case export carries a letter** — the house LIS letters a list and never a single specimen, so every one-part case fell through a parser built from the six lettered samples on file. The samples were not wrong, they were unrepresentative, and six of them could not show what 4545 rows show. Unlettered text is now the last block of the paste, cut on newlines and on a comma with no space after it: that comma is the export's own separator, 899 rows use it, and the same comma with a space stays inside a part as "Site, detail", so "Breast, Right mastectomy" is one part and "Antrum,Angular,Body" is three. Fixing the parse exposed the second cause. Score is `2×procedure + 2×clinical − 4×not`, so a bare site word scores zero for every candidate, ties below two preselect the generic form, and even `(A) antrum` had been offering "Generic free text" — the defect was always there, hidden behind the parse. The first fix for it, a blanket "prefer a biopsy template when no procedure is named", regressed three parts of the ovarian debulking fixture into colonic polyps and was rejected: it is not true that silence implies a biopsy. Silence implies a *particular* template only where that template's rivals are always named, which is a fact about each organ and belongs in each schema. So `match.bare` is a per-template declaration worth +2, enough to clear both the confidence margin and the generic floor, and a bonus rather than a penalty on the rivals so that clinical history still outranks it — "Gallbladder" alone is a cholecystectomy, "Gallbladder" with "CA gallbladder" is not confident. Which templates earn it was decided by the export's test codes (`B*` biopsy, `S*` surgical, `Y*` cytology) over single-part rows naming no procedure, and the measurement overturned four of my six guesses. Flagged: gastric biopsy 157/179, endometrial biopsy 107/128, thyroid FNA 125/137 cytology, gallbladder 46/46, appendix 18/18. Not flagged, against my first instinct: colonic polyp and IBD biopsy, because a bare colon segment is as likely a resection segment as a polyp and the debulking fixture had already said so in a different voice; cervix, because a bare "Cervix" is a smear 239 times in 306; duodenum at 3 of 14; prostate at 9 of 29, where "prostate chips" is a TURP. Those stay a `choose` with candidates offered, which is the honest answer and not a failure. The remaining work was vocabulary the site lists never held, each measured before adding: "Angular" is the house word for the incisura, written alone on 20 gastric mapping requests against 57 writing "Incisura", and it now reaches the subsite pick-list rather than only the template; `caecal`/`cecal` on colonic polyp at 36 pieces; `sector` on prostate biopsy at 91 pieces, every one coded B3, a systematic core and not a TURP; `hemithyroid` on the benign thyroid, a lobectomy written without "-ectomy" that would otherwise have been read as an FNA. Two cosmetic reads of the same export: a leading "Others" is its null category and not a site, 959 of 6384 part-pieces, and 730 pieces repeat one phrase as both category and description, so "Placenta, Placenta" printed the word twice in the specimen name. Both are now dropped, narrowly — only a leading "Others", only an all-identical repeat. Measured over the 60 commonest specimen texts in the export, 73 parts: 59 confident and 14 generic against 52 and 21 before, and every one-part case that produced nothing now produces a part. "Sputum" at 143 pieces and "Vault" stay generic because no respiratory or vault cytology template exists, which is the roadmap's own Later card answering for itself. Golden fixtures and the intake fixture untouched; the one changed assertion is test 570, whose old contract was the defect. 1145 tests, 0 lint errors.
- 2026-09-20 — Sixth family plate, adult-type diffuse gliomas, and the first plate whose entities do not carry a grade in the name. This is the family CNS5 cut hardest: a dozen named adult diffuse gliomas reduced to three entities decided by one branch point. `cnspathway.js` already walks that branch, so the first job was establishing the plate is not a duplicate. It is not. Run against a fully worked case the pathway returns three stages of resolved checks; run against a case carrying only the family selection it returns a single stage of outstanding orders. It is a work-tracker, and it never lays the three entities side by side, which is precisely the view wanted before the molecular results land. The criteria are taken from the sources the pathway already cites, so the two agree by construction rather than by inspection. The fork is IDH rather than 1p/19q — forking on codeletion would draw astrocytoma and oligodendroglioma as siblings of equal standing when the algorithm settles IDH first, and would leave no rail for the wildtype entity that same question decides; a test asserts the arrangement. What is new against the five earlier plates is grade. Those families carry a fixed grade per entity or none at all, and the cross-check test tying plate grades to `proposedGrade` was binary to match: either the plate states a number and the generator must propose it, or the plate states none and the generator must propose null. Here only glioblastoma is fixed at 4. Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant runs 2, 3 or 4 and oligodendroglioma runs 2 or 3, both derived from the findings, so a node now states a span and the test reads it — the generator must open at the bottom with nothing recorded and reach the top on a grade-defining feature. No defect was found this time. An early probe suggested necrosis and CDKN2A/B were yielding grade 2 on the astrocytoma, which would have been serious; it used invented option strings. Re-probed against the real ones every path is correct: astrocytoma 2 bare, 3 on mitoses at the threshold of three, 4 on microvascular proliferation, on necrosis with or without pseudopalisading, on CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion and on PDGFRA amplification; oligodendroglioma 2 bare, 3 at six mitoses and on microvascular proliferation; glioblastoma 4. That is three plates running with no grade defect after three consecutive plates that each found one. The teaching point is a read-across trap of the kind the earlier plates were built to catch, and here the app was already right. CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion "defines a grade 4 assignment irrespective of the histological features" in IDH-mutant astrocytoma (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10216527/). The identical finding in oligodendroglioma does not: cIMPACT-NOW update 12 records that such deletions "are not encountered in CNS WHO grade 2 oligodendrogliomas" and that within grade 3 they mark poor prognosis, but that outcomes "do not justify a CNS WHO grade 4 designation", and it is not an independent grade 3 criterion either (https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/advance-article/doi/10.1093/neuonc/noag108/8687120). One molecular finding, opposite consequences in two entities on the same rail. The generator implements both correctly — verified, 4 against 2 — and a test now pins the pair together with the mitotic cut-offs, three for astrocytoma against update 12's pragmatic six for oligodendroglioma, so neither can drift onto the other. The bench rows carry the caution the fork demands: a negative IDH1 R132H stain is not an IDH-wildtype result, the antibody seeing only the canonical mutation while non-canonical IDH1 and every IDH2 mutation stay invisible to it, so sequencing is indicated where the stain is negative but ATRX is lost or p53 is mutant-pattern, particularly under 55 or where the histology is lower grade. Retained ATRX settles nothing, being retained in oligodendroglioma and in most IDH-wildtype glioblastomas alike. Glioblastoma keeps the update 11 qualifications on each molecular route — TERT not the sole defining factor with low-grade histology, EGFR amplification and +7/-10 not the sole defining factor under 40 — with the note that those two are the specific pair while TERT is the frequent one shared with oligodendroglioma and melanoma. Figures are deep links as always, to the one source found under an open licence carrying an entity-matched figure for each of the three: MDPI Brain Sciences, CC BY 4.0, licence checked on the article page, four figures verified to resolve. Screenshot QA passed first time on geometry — the longest grade chip, "CNS WHO grade 2, 3 or 4", sets on one line at 122px inside a 512px node, and the fork label fits the fixed 260px box — but caught a gate node reading "Grading is no longer graded across the family", both clumsy and overlapping the node beside it; it now states the naming consequence instead, that glioblastoma means IDH-wildtype and secondary glioblastoma and anaplastic astrocytoma are retired. Six plates of nineteen families. The cross-check now covers 25 entities. 1140 tests, 0 lint errors, fixtures unchanged.
- 2026-09-20 — Fourth and fifth family plates, the medulloblastoma pair, and the first plate to find no grade defect. The design question flagged when the plate contract was written — whether one family needs a plate spanning two fields — was answered by the schema rather than by new machinery: `dxFamily` carries two separate options, molecularly defined and histologically defined, feeding `dxMedMol` and `dxMedHist`, and `FAMILY_FIELD` already mapped both. So this is two plates, each naming its own `entityField`, and the contract shipped with the first plate was enough. The molecular plate forks on YAP1, with three entities on the expressed rail and one on the absent rail. That asymmetry is the point. β-catenin, YAP1 and GAB1 resolve three groups and no more — group 3 against group 4 is not separable by immunohistochemistry at all and needs profiling (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8029101/) — so the lone rail is where the stains stop, and both SHH entities stay on one rail because nothing on a slide splits them; only TP53 sequencing does. The trap is in the bench rows and it is a real one: GAB1 is often weak or absent in nodular regions and YAP1 is attenuated in those same regions, so in desmoplastic/nodular tumours and MBEN the two stains that call SHH fail together, in the histology most likely to be SHH (https://www.iccr-cancer.org/datasets/docs/iccr-cns-mole-medulloblastoma/). Filamin A is in the panel as a row explaining why it was dropped — its expression mirrored YAP1 across the three groups and it lacked specificity for any individual subgroup, so it adds a stain rather than a discriminator. p53 immunohistochemistry gets the same treatment: concordance with mutation status is incomplete and in one series positivity was distributed almost equally across all subgroups, so it is a surrogate and not a substitute for sequencing. The consequence that leaves the report is germline. Fifty-six per cent of children with SHH/TP53-mutant tumours carry a germline TP53 mutation, and germline mutations were not observed in the WNT tumours that also carry TP53 mutations (https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6x10w8zk); five-year overall survival is 41 ± 9 per cent against 81 ± 5 for the wild-type form. In one large cohort germline TP53 was present in eight per cent of paediatric SHH cases and twenty per cent of those aged five to sixteen, and was found only in SHH (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5984248/), so the detail panel says outright that a TP53 mutation here is a reason to raise Li-Fraumeni and genetic counselling, not only to name the entity. The histological plate forks on desmoplasia demonstrated on reticulin, which puts desmoplastic/nodular with MBEN and classic with large cell/anaplastic. Nodularity alone will not do it — classic tumours may show nodular zones that lack desmoplasia, so architecture without the stain does not settle the type, and both the nodularity and the desmoplasia must be present; the separation of MBEN from desmoplastic/nodular is then quantitative, how much of the tumour is nodule rather than a different finding, with a less conspicuous perinodular reticulin ring, so a weak stain in a strongly lobular tumour points towards MBEN rather than away from the nodular rail (https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/1186464, CC BY 3.0). Focal anaplasia does not reclassify a classic tumour unless the pattern predominates, but the foci are still reported — the judgement changes the type, not the description. On grade, all eight entities were already correct at grade 4 and nothing needed fixing, which is the first time in four plates. The reason it had to be checked carefully is a near-miss: WNT-activated, non-WNT/non-SHH and the aggregate histologically-defined row carry a dagger in the ICCR table, footnoted that these grades are described in the section or chapter but not in the definition. That is not the "n/a" that sent three embryonal entities into `UNGRADED` last batch — the grade exists and prints correctly, and the four histological types are covered by the aggregate row rather than appearing individually. The cross-check test tying plate grades to `proposedGrade` now covers 22 entities, up from 14. Screenshot QA earned its place twice: the histological plate's fork label set on two lines where the other four plates set on one, so it was shortened to fit the fixed 260px box with a comment recording the constraint; and the shot of that plate was silently duplicating the node shot because the dialog retained scroll position from the plate before it, which the harness now resets. Figures are deep links as always, the IntechOpen chapter verified CC BY 3.0 before its criteria were used; the Wiley review is link-only with no licence found. 1136 tests, 0 lint errors, fixtures unchanged.
