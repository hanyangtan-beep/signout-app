# Cervical cytology (Bethesda 2014): cell descriptions and reference gallery

Research notes for the `cervical_cytology` template, Phase 55. Compiled 7 October 2026.

**How to read this document**
- Each statement links to the page it was taken from. The Bethesda atlas itself (Nayar and Wilbur, 3rd edition) was not read; its criteria come from open summaries: the CytoJournal monograph chapters, PathologyOutlines, StatPearls and Eurocytology.
- Where two sources give different values, both are given.
- Nothing here prints in a released report. The released wording is in `docs/specs/cervical-cytology-descriptions.md`; the criteria sit in the schema help text and the gallery points.

## 1. Adequacy

- A transformation zone component needs at least 10 well-preserved endocervical or squamous metaplastic cells ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html); [CytoJournal, TBS terminology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168399/)).
- The absence of a transformation zone component does not by itself call for a repeat test ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)).
- The minimum squamous cellularity is 5000 cells, lowered to 2000 in atrophic, post-hysterectomy (vault) and post-therapy samples; any sample with abnormal cells is satisfactory by definition ([CytoJournal, TBS terminology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168399/)).

## 2. Organisms

- **Trichomonas vaginalis:** pear-shaped cyanophilic flagellates, 15 to 30 µm across, with a pale eccentric nucleus and eosinophilic cytoplasmic granules; Leptothrix suggests their presence ([Eurocytology, Trichomonas](https://win.eurocytology.eu/static/eurocytology/ENG/cervical/mod5img2a.html)). CytoJournal gives 8 to 30 µm, with giant forms up to 150 µm ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)).
- **Candida:** yeasts of 3 to 7 µm (2 to 8 µm for C. glabrata) ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)).
- **Shift in flora (bacterial vaginosis):** clue cells covered by tiny coccobacilli that obscure the cell margins, in a filmy or sandy background, ideally without lactobacilli ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)).
- **Actinomyces:** fuzzy dark blue "woolly balls", with thin filaments radiating from a central tangled mass ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)); a quarter of IUD-associated reactive changes may be associated with Actinomyces ([CytoJournal, TBS terminology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168399/)).
- **Herpes simplex virus:** multinucleation, moulding and margination of chromatin, ground-glass nuclei, and Cowdry type A inclusions in the third stage ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/); [PathologyOutlines, LSIL](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologyLSIL.html)).
- **Cytomegalovirus:** cellular and nuclear enlargement with a large nuclear inclusion surrounded by a halo ("owl's eye"), and occasionally smaller nuclear or cytoplasmic inclusions ([PathologyOutlines, CMV](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcmv.html)); mostly in endocervical glandular cells ([GLOWM, cytopathology](https://www.glowm.com/section-view/heading/Cytopathology/item/18)).

## 3. Reactive change, radiation, IUD, atrophy and endometrial cells

- **Reactive (inflammatory) change:** the nuclei stay under twice the size of an intermediate cell nucleus ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)).
- **Repair:** flat sheets in a "school of fish" arrangement or with "taffy pull" cytoplasm; vesicular, non-overlapping nuclei with smooth outlines, fine chromatin and prominent nucleoli ([CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/); [PathologyOutlines, AIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixais.html)).
- **Radiation:** cells enlarge three- to four-fold with an unchanged N:C ratio and nuclei enlarge 2 to 10 times; vacuolated or polychromatic cytoplasm and multinucleation; acute changes are rarely seen after six months ([CytoJournal, radiation](https://cytojournal.com/cervical-cytology-radiation-and-other-therapy-effects/)). Radiation change lacks mitoses, apoptosis and a markedly high N:C ratio ([CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/)).
- **IUD:** single endometrial-type cells with a high N:C ratio that may resemble HSIL, vacuolated "bubble gum" cells, and Actinomyces ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)).
- **Atrophy:** parabasal-sized cells, syncytial-like aggregates and bare nuclei ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)); benign nuclear membranes are smooth and uniform ([CytoJournal, ASC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168398/)).
- **Endometrial cells** are reported in women aged 45 or older ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)).

## 4. Squamous categories

- **ASC-US:** nuclei 2.5 to 3 times the area of a normal intermediate nucleus (about 35 µm²) or twice a metaplastic nucleus (about 50 µm²), with a slightly raised N:C ratio ([CytoJournal, ASC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168398/)); minimal hyperchromasia, mildly irregular outlines and poorly formed halos. The category includes atypical parakeratosis, atypical repair and atypia in atrophy ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)).
- **ASC-H:** nuclei 1.5 to 2.5 times a metaplastic nucleus; in liquid-based preparations the cells are small and mostly single, with nuclei 2 to 3 times a neutrophil, and groups of fewer than 10 cells may stream in mucus ([CytoJournal, ASC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168398/)). The patterns are atypical immature metaplasia, crowded sheets, markedly atypical repair, severe atrophy and post-radiation change ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)).
- **LSIL:** nuclei more than 3 times an intermediate nucleus; koilocytosis counts only with nuclear abnormality, and koilocytes have a broad, sharply delineated clear perinuclear zone with a dense peripheral rim ([PathologyOutlines, LSIL](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologyLSIL.html)); bi- or multinucleation and inconspicuous nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)). Inflammatory "trich halos" are narrower and poorly defined ([CytoJournal, inflammation and repair](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168397/)).
- **HSIL:** cells about the size of parabasal cells, single, in sheets or in syncytium-like aggregates, with a high N:C ratio, hyperchromasia, irregular outlines with grooves, fine or coarse evenly distributed chromatin and, as a rule, no nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines, HSIL](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixHSILcytology.html); [PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)). Flattening at the edge of a group and central whorling favour HSIL over a glandular lesion ([PathologyOutlines, HSIL](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixHSILcytology.html)); HSIL lacks the palisading and feathering of AIS ([CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/)).
- **HSIL with features suspicious for invasion:** highly pleomorphic HSIL cells without a diathesis, or a diathesis without overtly malignant cells ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)).
- **Squamous cell carcinoma:** single cells or aggregates with irregular nuclear membranes, dense opaque nuclei, coarsely granular chromatin and a tumour diathesis of necrotic debris and broken-down blood, with keratotic changes in the keratinising type ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)); prominent nucleoli and necrotic debris ([PathologyOutlines, HSIL](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixHSILcytology.html)).

## 5. Glandular categories

- **AGC, endocervical:** nuclei 3 to 5 times normal endocervical nuclei, with overlap, pseudostratification, hyperchromasia and rare nucleoli, beyond reactive change but short of AIS or adenocarcinoma ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html); [StatPearls, AGC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564318/)). CytoJournal states the same multiple against intermediate cell nuclei ([CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/)).
- **AGC, endometrial:** small groups, usually of 5 to 10 cells; slightly enlarged nuclei, mild hyperchromasia, occasional small nucleoli and scant cytoplasm with or without vacuoles ([StatPearls, AGC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564318/); [CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/)).
- **AGC, favour neoplastic:** sheets and strips with crowding, overlap or pseudostratification; rosettes, glands or feathering; enlarged, elongated hyperchromatic nuclei with coarse chromatin; occasional mitoses or apoptotic bodies ([StatPearls, AGC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564318/)).
- **AIS:** clusters, pseudostratified strips and rosettes with crowding, enlargement, hyperchromasia, coarse chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, apoptotic bodies and mitoses in a clean background ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)); feathering of oval to cigar-shaped nuclei at the edge of groups, better seen in conventional smears than in liquid-based preparations ([CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/)). AIS and early invasive adenocarcinoma cannot be separated with certainty ([Eurocytology, adenocarcinoma](https://www.eurocytology.eu/course/cytology-of-adenocarcinoma/)).
- **Endocervical adenocarcinoma:** the features of AIS with pleomorphism, macronucleoli, chromatin clearing and a tumour diathesis ([PathologyOutlines, Bethesda](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixcytologybethesda.html)); the diathesis is the best supporting feature ([CytoJournal, AGC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168395/)).
- **Endometrial adenocarcinoma:** small tight clusters or single round cells with scant vacuolated cytoplasm and intracytoplasmic neutrophils, and a "watery", finely granular diathesis that clings to the edges of clusters in liquid-based preparations ([PathologyOutlines, endometrial adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixendometrialadenocarcinomacyto.html)).
- **Extrauterine adenocarcinoma:** rare cells with the features of the primary, usually without a diathesis; psammoma bodies are commoner with ovarian and tubal carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines, endometrial adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixendometrialadenocarcinomacyto.html)).
