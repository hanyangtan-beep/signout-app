## 1. Handling and reporting a targeted liver-mass core biopsy (generic requirements)

### 1.1 Indications and request form
- Targeted biopsies are taken "for the diagnosis of suspected metastatic malignancy, especially in the context of malignancy of unknown origin" or "for the diagnosis of suspected primary liver neoplasm" ([RCPath G050, section 10](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- Caution for colorectal metastases: "hepatobiliary surgeons advise against needle biopsy to confirm a diagnosis of CRCLM where future surgical excision may be an option because of the risk of chest wall recurrence at the biopsy site, as a consequence of seeding" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- Request form: "should clearly indicate that the biopsy is from a focal lesion. It should include the size and intrahepatic site (segment) of the lesion targeted with other relevant clinical information such as a previous history of malignant disease and imaging findings and specify whether primary or metastatic disease is suspected" ([RCPath G050, 10.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- Adequacy: "Unlike medical liver biopsies, there is no minimum recommended specimen size ... A small biopsy containing diagnostic tumour tissue can still be regarded as adequate" ([RCPath G050, 10.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).

### 1.2 Sectioning and first-line work-up
- "Initially, one or two shallow levels stained with H&E should be examined; if two levels are cut, intervening unstained sections should be kept on slides suitable for immunohistochemistry." "If no tumour tissue is seen initially, deeper levels should be requested before reporting a biopsy as being negative for tumour." ([RCPath G050, 10.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf))
- Pitfall, unrecognised well-differentiated lesion: "The possibility that the biopsy is from a well-differentiated hepatocellular lesion (FNH, HCA, well-differentiated HCC or focal fatty change/sparing) should be considered if hepatocellular tissue is present without normal architectural landmarks (portal tracts and hepatic veins)." ([RCPath G050, 10.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf))
- Missed lesion: "If there is no lesional tissue present, the report should indicate that additional biopsies/investigations are required for diagnosis." ([RCPath G050, 10.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf))
- Strategy: "Histological work up of a potentially primary liver lesion has a different strategy from the differential diagnosis of metastatic disease; this possibility should be considered before requesting a panel of immunohistochemistry routinely used for diagnosing metastatic adenocarcinoma." "when there is a history of previous malignancy or radiological features of a primary tumour, a compatible morphology is often sufficient without immunohistochemistry, especially where previous histology is available for review." ([RCPath G050, 10.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf))
- Tissue conservation for molecular testing: "for a targeted tumour biopsy where cholangiocarcinoma is in the differential diagnosis, it is important to minimise immunohistochemistry for tumour diagnosis to conserve adequate tissue for future genetic tests"; "molecular testing for FGFR2 fusion is recommended for cholangiocarcinoma patients eligible for treatment" ([RCPath G050, section 6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- Hepatoid mimic: "pancreatic acinar cell carcinoma may mimic HCC histologically and be associated with raised AFP; immunohistochemistry for trypsin should be considered to investigate for this" ([RCPath G050, 10.3.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).

### 1.3 Special stains other than immunohistochemistry (RCPath G050 Appendix S, verbatim)

| Stain | RCPath comment |
|---|---|
| PAS | "Glycogen commonly present in hepatocellular neoplasms, rarely in adenocarcinoma" |
| PAS-diastase | "Presence of luminal PAS-D positive material and/or cytoplasmic mucin vacuoles favours a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. HCC may contain PAS-D positive intracytoplasmic globules (e.g. α-1-antitrypsin) or PAS-D positive luminal material in pseudoglandular/adenoid pattern" |
| Perls | "Bile retains green colour and may be more easily recognised than in an H&E-stained section. Presence of intracellular or canalicular bile pigment favours diagnosis of hepatocellular neoplasm" |
| Reticulin | "Normal reticulin fibre content retained in dysplastic nodules and benign hepatocellular lesions (e.g. HCA, FNH)"; "Reticulin fibres usually reduced or absent in HCC (but may be focally retained in some well differentiated HCCs.)"; "Reticulin fibres may be reduced in areas of steatosis, including steatotic HCA" |

Source: [RCPath G050, Appendix S](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf). A peer-reviewed review adds that "benign conditions, such as fatty liver disease, areas adjacent to necrosis, and congestive hepatopathy, may also demonstrate reticulin loss" ([Choi & Thung, J Yeungnam Med Sci 2025, PMC11812079](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)).

### 1.4 Required report content for a targeted biopsy (RCPath G050 section 10.4, verbatim list)
"The report should include the following:" ([RCPath G050, 10.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf))
1. "the clinical information received with the biopsy"
2. "a macroscopic description, including biopsy size"
3. "the presence or absence of tissue from the focal lesion, and of non-lesional liver tissue (hepatocytes, bile ducts) as histological confirmation that the specimen is indeed from the liver"
4. "a morphological description of the lesion"
5. "the results of any additional stains carried out, including immunohistochemistry"
6. "a comment on the non-lesional liver, if sufficient is included"
7. "a definite diagnosis of the focal lesion where possible, or a discussion of the differential diagnosis. This would include a discussion of tumours compatible with or excluded by immunohistochemistry"
8. "a concise, single-line summary to conclude the report"
9. "an appropriate SNOMED code"
10. "a record (including names) of any intra-departmental consultation, outside referral for second opinion and/or discussion with clinician that has contributed to the histopathology report."

For metastatic disease the RCPath liver dataset defers to the CUP dataset: "When the clinical suspicion and/or initial morphology is suggestive of metastatic disease, the RCPath Dataset for cancer of unknown primary and malignancy of unknown primary origin should be followed." ([RCPath G050, 10.3.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). The CUP dataset core items begin with "Site of sample", "Type of sample", "Morphology" and "Immunohistochemistry" ([RCPath G167, section 6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).

A dedicated CAP or ICCR **biopsy** template for adult primary liver carcinoma: **n.a.** (the CAP HCC protocol excludes biopsies, and the ICCR dataset is for resections, as quoted in section 0).

## 2. Background (non-lesional) liver on a mass biopsy

- RCPath (biopsy): the report needs "a comment on the non-lesional liver, if sufficient is included" ([RCPath G050, 10.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- RCPath (resection, HCC): "The presence of hepatitis (viral or autoimmune), haemochromatosis, alcohol related liver disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) should be recorded, as should both the stage of fibrosis and the nature and severity of inflammatory/metabolic disease. Because of its importance in prognosis, the fibrosis stage of underlying chronic liver disease is regarded as a core data item. The aetiology may not always be known to the pathologist and is thus considered to be a non-core item." ([RCPath G050, 5.3.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). RCPath does not name a fibrosis staging system in the passages fetched (named system: n.a. in RCPath).
- RCPath colorectal-metastasis proforma value list for "Background liver": "Normal", "Steatosis", "Chronic liver disease with fibrosis", "Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome" ([RCPath G050 proforma](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- ICCR (resection): options under coexistent pathology include "Steatosis", "Steatohepatitis", "Iron overload", "Chronic hepatitis, specify type if known", "Biliary disease, specify if known"; fibrosis "Not identified / Indeterminate / Present" with "ISHAK stage /6", "KLEINER stage", "METAVIR stage", "BATTS-LUDWIG stage" or "SAF system" ([ICCR liver 2nd ed.](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)). ICCR: "We recommend that the type of disease and degree of fibrosis are recorded separately; for the latter any one of the three main systems in widespread use for semi-quantitative assessment is suitable although it is recognised that the Kleiner and SAF systems were developed for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease while the METAVIR, Ishak and Batts-Ludwig systems were designed for those with chronic (viral) hepatitis." ICCR also advises assessing the background "as far away from the main tumour mass as possible to avoid the confounding factor of peritumoral effects" and notes "histological pointers such as iron overload, evidence of HBV infection (ground glass hepatocytes) or a1AT accumulation" ([ICCR liver 2nd ed.](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)).
- CAP (resection): "The scoring system described by Ishak is recommended by the AJCC Cancer Staging Manual, 8 edition, but other commonly used schemes (Batts-Ludwig, Metavir) can be used. The name of the staging scheme and its scale should be included." ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)). Also: "Low-grade dysplastic nodules are difficult or impossible to distinguish from large regenerative nodules, and their inclusion in the report is not necessary." ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true))
- **Disagreement:** CAP prefers Ishak (per AJCC 8th); ICCR accepts any of Ishak/Kleiner/METAVIR/Batts-Ludwig/SAF; RCPath requires a fibrosis stage without naming a system. A semi-quantitative steatosis threshold or iron grading scheme for the background liver on a mass biopsy: **n.a.** in these datasets.

## 3. Benign lesions

### 3.1 Cavernous haemangioma (incl. sclerosed/hyalinised haemangioma, HSVN/anastomosing haemangioma)

- **Core biopsy morphology.** "On hematoxylin-eosin staining microscopy, HH appear as dilated vascular channels lined by a single layer of endothelial cells. Complications of HH include necrosis, thrombin, sclerosis or calcification. No malignant transformation has been reported" ([Bajenaru et al., J Med Life 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4564031/)). Degenerative changes are listed as "thrombosis, hyalinization, progressive fibrosis and sclerosis becoming central scar" ([J Med Life 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4564031/)).
- **Sclerosed variant.** "Hemangioma degeneration can occur by increasing degree of fibrosis and thrombosis of its vascular channels, which is called sclerosing and/or hyalinizing hemangioma"; such lesions "can be indistinguishable from malignancy, primary, or metastatic, based on imaging characteristics" ([Radiol Case Rep 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4878953/)). The German-language Pathologie review notes that strongly sclerosed haemangiomas "machen im Vergleich zum konventionellen (kavernösen) Hämangiom oft eine Leberbiopsie nötig" (often need a liver biopsy, unlike conventional haemangioma) ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)).
- **Biopsy yield and risk.** "Due to its vascular nature, biopsy with histological sampling has a great risk of hemorrhage ... in a study with 36 patients, the diagnostic material was obtained in only 21 of them" ([J Med Life 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4564031/)).
- **IHC.** Classical haemangiomas show "typischem Immunprofil" (a typical immunoprofile) and are "in der Regel diagnostisch kein Problem" ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)). For HSVN, the lining "ist zytologisch blande und reagiert immunhistochemisch positiv gegenüber klassischen Endothelzellmarkern wie CD31, ERG und auch CD34" ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)). A dataset-endorsed haemangioma panel: **n.a.**
- **Grading/classification.** Not graded. Entities listed: "konventionelle (kavernöse) Hämangiome, kongenitale Hämangiome, infantile hepatische Hämangiome, Lymphangiome und die Gruppe der 'hepatic small vessel neoplasms' (HSVN)/anastomosierende Hämangiome" ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)).
- **Pitfalls.** HSVN/anastomosing haemangioma "sollten trotz der architekturalen Komplexität nicht als Angiosarkom fehlinterpretiert werden" (should not be misread as angiosarcoma despite architectural complexity); "Das HSVN weist häufig Mutationen im GNAQ-, GNA11- und GNA14-Gen auf" ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)). Conversely, well-differentiated angiosarcoma enters the differential, with discriminators "Hochgradige Kernatypien; hohe Mitoserate; atypische Mitosen" ([Pathologie 2025, Tab. 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)).
- **What the report states.** No RCPath/ICCR/CAP data item for benign vascular lesions: **n.a.**

### 3.2 Focal nodular hyperplasia (FNH)

- **Morphology.** "Histologically, the lesion displays nodular architecture composed of benign-appearing hepatocytes arranged in one to two cell-thick plates ... Dystrophic thick-walled arteries are present in the central scar. The ductular reaction can be highlighted by immunostaining for cytokeratin 7 (CK7) and cytokeratin 19 (CK19) at the interface between fibrous areas and nodules"; "true portal tracts are absent" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)). RCPath Appendix P: "Central scar with abnormal vessels, fibrovascular septa with ductular reaction and inflammation"; "Polyclonal" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **IHC.** GS "reveals wide interconnected bands of lesional hepatocytes creating a highly distinctive geographic or map-like pattern"; "CD34 usually exhibits patchy sinusoidal staining; however, it can show diffuse staining in a subset of cases" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)). FNH "shows only patchy staining for these markers [CRP and SAA] and is characterized by a map-like pattern of GS staining" ([Virchows Arch 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)). RCPath Appendix O also lists CD34 as "May also be positive in benign arterialised lesions such as FNH and HCA" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **Pitfalls.** "The diffuse GS staining observed in β-catenin–activated HCA (B-HCA) or HCC should not be misinterpreted as a map-like pattern, particularly in core needle biopsies"; "FNH-like lesions mimic FNH, but they lack the characteristic map-like GS staining pattern"; "HCCs are differentiated from FNH by cytological atypia, thick cell plates (>two cells thick), and reticulin loss" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)). IHCA can show "fibrous septa, nodularity, and ductular reactions"; "FNHs show map-like GS staining, whereas I-HCAs stain positively for SAA and CRP" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)).
- **Grading.** Not applicable. **Report content:** dataset item **n.a.**

### 3.3 Hepatocellular adenoma (HCA): subtype panel

RCPath subtypes: "HNF1A mutated (H-HCA), inflammatory (I-HCA), beta-catenin mutated exon 3 (Bex3-HCA), beta-catenin mutated exon 7/8 (Bex7,8-HCA), sonic hedgehog HCA (sh-HCA), and unclassified when none of the above can be demonstrated" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). WHO 6th: HCA "now includes the newly defined subtype sonic hedgehog HCA (SHH-HCA), in addition to other subtypes HNF1A-inactivated HCA (H-HCA), inflammatory HCA (IHCA), β-catenin activated HCA (B-HCA) and β-catenin inflammatory HCA (B-IHCA)" ([WHO 6th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.70116)). Panel: "LFABP, CRP, SAA, β-catenin, and GS" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)); UK minimum: "glutamine synthetase, amyloid A and beta catenin has been proposed to allow identification of the clinically important HCA types, inflammatory and beta-catenin activated HCA" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).

| Subtype | Frequency (source) | Key H&E | Defining IHC / molecular (verbatim) | Clinical significance |

### 3.4 Bile duct hamartoma (von Meyenburg complex) vs bile duct adenoma vs metastatic adenocarcinoma

- **Bile duct hamartoma (VMC).** "Discrete and well-defined nodules, often multiple and usually less than 0.5 cm ... classically related to portal tracts"; "rounded or irregularly shaped and dilated bile ducts, containing bile or eosinophilic proteinaceous debris, often with branching and U shapes, embedded in a dense fibrous stroma ... The lining cells are flattened or cuboidal, cytologically uniform, and lack mitoses"; immunophenotype "staining positive for cytokeratin (CK) 7, CK8, CK18, and CK19" ([Sarcognato et al., Pathologica 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8299320/)).
- **Bile duct adenoma (BDA).** "Defined as a benign epithelial lesion made of a proliferation of small, normal-looking bile ducts. Peribiliary gland hamartoma is still an acceptable synonym"; criteria: "i) a patternless and disordered proliferation of small, relatively uniformly shaped and spaced tubules and/or ductular structures with an intact basement membrane, in a connective tissue stroma, ii) a single layer of cuboidal to columnar cells with regular nuclei without atypia and mitosis, and iii) absence of infiltrative borders"; "usually solitary and subcapsular (nearly 90% of the cases), and more than 90% are less than 10 mm"; "BRAF V600E mutations have been described in high percentage of cases (> 50%)"; may express "D10, 1F6, MUC6, MUC5AC, and TFF2" ([Pathologica 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8299320/)).
- **IHC to separate from carcinoma.** "The keratin profile is not useful, since BDAs and pancreato-biliary cancers share the same immunophenotype ... Ki67, EZH2, p53, and p16 may help ... However ... a low proliferation index and a low EZH2 and p53 expression do not exclude malignancy" ([Pathologica 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8299320/)). In a UCSF series, "Most cholangiocarcinomas (23/26; 88.5%), but none of the benign lesions (0/46; 0%), had proliferative indices greater than 10%"; "Strong nuclear p53 ... only seen in cholangiocarcinomas (9/26; 34.6%)"; mean Ki-67: "biliary hamartoma, 1.2%; bile duct adenoma, 2%" ([Hum Pathol 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5250508/)).
- **Clues against metastasis.** "The presence of dilated glands containing bile is the major clue to exclude metastasis, while the complete absence of any cytological atypia is helpful to rule out CCA"; for BDA in frozen section, "the differential is based on tumor size, cytological and architectural patterns, the presence of pre-existing portal tracts within the lesion, and the presence of invasive features" ([Pathologica 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8299320/)). Multiple small hypodense lesions may include "biliary hamartomas" alongside metastasis in the imaging differential ([Radiol Case Rep 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4878953/)).
- **Report content.** No dataset item: **n.a.**

## 4. Borderline / uncertain hepatocellular lesions

### 4.1 Well-differentiated hepatocellular neoplasm not callable adenoma or carcinoma

- **Terms in use (no single WHO-endorsed term confirmed).** RCPath: "For such lesions the diagnostic terms 'atypical hepatocellular adenoma-like neoplasm', 'hepatocellular neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential' or ... 'atypical hepatocellular neoplasms'" can be used ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). Virchows 2026: "The terms 'borderline lesion' or 'atypical hepatocellular neoplasm' or 'hepatocellular neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential' have been used when an HCA presents atypical morphological features, such as some alterations or focal loss of the reticulin network or cyto-architectural modifications (small cell changes, pseudo-glands) that are worrisome but insufficient (focal and/or incomplete) to make a definite diagnosis of HCC"; "This situation is more frequent in case of B-HCA or B-IHCA" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)). Choi & Thung: "The term 'borderline HCA' is applied to B-HCAs that exhibit focal cytoarchitectural atypia and reticulin abnormalities that are insufficient for the diagnosis of HCC"; "atypical hepatocellular neoplasm" proposed "for all β-catenin–activated tumors, excluding those with exon 7/8 mutations"; HUMP proposed "because 'atypical hepatocellular neoplasm' may not adequately reflect the need for close follow-up" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)). CAP hepatoblastoma protocol (paediatric) uses "Hepatocellular neoplasm, not otherwise specified (HCN-NOS)" ([CAP hepatoblastoma biopsy 5.0.0.1](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-June-2025/Liver.Hepatoblastoma.Bx_5.0.0.1.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download)).
- **Features concerning for HCC on biopsy.** "Deficiency of reticulin (although reticulin may be lost in steatotic areas) or hepatocyte plates >2 cells thick, positivity for glypican 3, diffuse positivity for glutamine synthetase (also seen in beta-catenin activated HCA), or nodule-in- nodule appearance are features concerning for HCC"; "The morphological atypia in these lesions is focal and therefore may be absent from biopsies (reticulin loss, cytological atypia or pseudogland formation)"; additional risk markers: "genetic abnormalities (beta catenin gene (CTTNB1) activation) or an unusual clinical context (male, female age >50 or <15 years)" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **What the report states.** RCPath: "Well differentiated HCC can be very difficult to distinguish from HCA on biopsy"; "It may not be possible, with currently available investigations, to differentiate between HCA and well differentiated HCC" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). Required wording/data items: **n.a.**

### 4.2 High-grade dysplastic nodule (HGDN) vs early/well-differentiated HCC (cirrhotic liver)

- **Definitions (ICGHN).** "An H-DN is defined as having architectural and/or cytologic atypia, but the atypia is insufficient for a diagnosis of HCC"; early HCC features: "(1) increased cell density more than 2 times that of the surrounding tissue, with an increased nuclear/cytoplasm ratio and irregular thin-trabecular pattern; (2) varying numbers of portal tracts within the nodule (intratumoral portal tracts); (3) pseudoglandular pattern; (4) diffuse fatty change; and (5) varying numbers of unpaired arteries"; "Stromal invasion is defined as tumor cell invasion into the portal tracts or fibrous septa within vaguely nodular lesions"; "stromal invasion remains most helpful in differentiating early HCC from H-DNs" ([ICGHN, Hepatology 2009](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/dfa8b98f-c0f5-748b-e053-3a05fe0a3a96/HCC.pdf)).
- **Pseudoinvasion.** "If areas of questionable invasion are present, immunostaining for keratins 7 or 19 may be useful; if such staining demonstrates a ductular reaction, the focus is considered a pseudoinvasion and does not warrant a diagnosis of HCC" ([ICGHN 2009](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/dfa8b98f-c0f5-748b-e053-3a05fe0a3a96/HCC.pdf)).
- **Biopsy limitation.** "Minute biopsy specimens may not contain intratumoral portal tracts, thus precluding the detection of stromal invasion. Similarly, the detection of unpaired arteries, mitoses, and various immunohistochemical markers are prone to sampling error" ([ICGHN 2009](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/dfa8b98f-c0f5-748b-e053-3a05fe0a3a96/HCC.pdf)). RCPath: "a definitive diagnosis may not be possible from a biopsy" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **Three-marker panel (GPC3, HSP70, GS).** RCPath: "Immunohistochemistry useful in this situation includes glutamine synthetase, glypican 3 and HSP70. Positivity for at least two of these favours HCC" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). Di Tommaso: "The sensitivity and specificity of the individual markers for the detection of eHCC-G1 were 59% and 86% for GS, 69% and 91% for GPC3, and 78% and 95% for HSP70"; "when at least 2 of them, regardless which, were positive, the sensitivity and specificity ... were respectively 72% and 100%" ([Di Tommaso et al., Hepatology 2007](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.21531)). RCPath Appendix O patterns: GPC3 "Cytoplasmic, membranous, canalicular ... <10% HGDN"; HSP70 "Nucleus and cytoplasm ... <10% HGDN"; GS "Diffuse strong cytoplasmic ... 14% HGDN" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **CD34 and reticulin.** "Diffuse positivity of the sinusoidal endothelium for CD34 corresponds to 'capillarisation of sinusoids' ... and can provide useful confirmation that the lesion identified on imaging has been sampled; however, it does not distinguish between high grade dysplastic nodule and well differentiated HCC"; "Abnormality or loss of reticulin and an infiltrative growth pattern may enable a definite diagnosis of HCC to be made, although the latter is rarely seen in biopsies" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). RCPath Appendix O: "Normal reticulin fibre content retained in dysplastic nodules"; "Reticulin fibres usually reduced or absent in HCC"; "Reticulin fibres may be reduced in areas of steatosis" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **Report content.** CAP: "Low-grade dysplastic nodules are difficult or impossible to distinguish from large regenerative nodules, and their inclusion in the report is not necessary" ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)).

It is not a hepatocyte-specific marker"; "AFP 37"; "K19 stains a minority of HCC and is associated with a poorer prognosis"; metastasis-favouring: "K7, K20 10–34, 9", "CDX2 5", "K19 <10", "S100, HM45 0" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **Pitfalls.** Glypican-3: "14% of gastrointestinal and pancreatic metastatic liver carcinomas positive"; TTF1 "93% HCC cytoplasmic positive, 0% nuclear positive"; "Glutamine synthetase diffusely stains some HCC but also stains beta-catenin activated hepatocellular adenoma" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).

### 5.2 Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA): large-duct vs small-duct

- **Classification.** WHO 5th: "Two main subtypes: a large duct type, which resembles extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, and a small duct type, which shares aetiological, pathogenetic and imaging characteristics with hepatocellular carcinoma" ([WHO 5th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.13975)). WHO 6th: "has resulted in two separate entities: small duct (SD-ICCA) and large duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (LD-ICCA)" ([WHO 6th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.70116)).
- **Morphology.** Large duct: "usually has a periductal infiltrating growth pattern with or without a mass forming component. It is composed of columnar, often mucin secreting cells"; small duct: "composed of non-mucin producing cuboidal cells with ductular or cord-like pattern resembling cells of a ductular reaction. It is often densely sclerotic at the centre with a cellular periphery" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). Cholangiolocarcinoma: "no longer considered a subtype of combined hepatocellular–cholangiocarcinoma, but rather a subtype of small duct intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma" ([WHO 5th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.13975)).
- **IHC panel.** Marker table: "Large duct type iCCA|S100P, MUC5AC, MUC6, TFF1, AGR2, MMP7, pCEA, CA19-9, CLDN18, CLDN18.2, Smad4/DPC4, EVI1, PPP1R1B"; "Small duct type iCCA|CRP, N-cadherin, CD56, Nestin, OPN, TUBB3, FGB" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1653534/full)). "According to the 5th edition of the WHO Classification, immunohistochemical markers for small duct type iCCA include C-reactive protein (CRP), N-cadherin, and CD56"; CRP positive in "65.0% to 100.0%" of small duct vs "5.3% to 71.7%" of large duct; N-cadherin "55.0% and 95.5%" vs "5.6% and 49.1%"; N-cadherin "does not manifest strong background staining"; S100P large duct "68.5% to 100.0%" vs small duct "0.0% to 57.6%"; MUC5AC large duct "50.0% to 96.7%" vs small duct "1.8% to 25.0%" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1653534/full)). CK7/CK19 per-subtype frequencies verbatim: **n.a.** in fetched sources.
- **Grading.** ICCR semiquantitative scheme: "Well differentiated (more than 95% of tumour composed of glands)", "Moderately differentiated (50% to 95% of tumour composed of glands)", "Poorly differentiated (up to 49% of tumour composed of glands)" ([ICCR liver 2nd ed.](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)); "Definitive criteria for histological grading of cholangiocarcinomas have not been established" ([ICCR](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)).
- **Variants.** CAP (citing WHO 6th, 2026): "Adenosquamous, mucinous / colloid, mucoepidermoid and enteroblastic sub-types are now considered variants of large duct type iCCA, while cholangiolocellular, ductal plate malformation-like, lymphoepithlioma-like [sic] and solid-tubulocystic are considered variants of small duct type iCCA" ([CAP intrahepatic bile duct 4.4.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/BileDuctIH_4.4.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)). RCPath (WHO 2019): "Adenosquamous, squamous, mucinous, signet ring, clear cell, mucoepidermoid, lymphoepithelioma-like (Epstein–Barr virus associated) and sarcomatous intrahepatic CCs are regarded as variants of the large duct subtype" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **Molecular.** Small duct "harbors mutations of IDH1/2 (10–30%) as well as fusions of FGFR2 (10–25%) ... and BAP1 (9–25%)"; large duct "KRAS (15–30%) and TP53 (10–40%)" ([Curr Oncol 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9857472/)).
- **Metastasis vs iCCA (key pitfall).** "Distinction from metastatic adenocarcinoma particularly from stomach or pancreas is based on the single or dominant intrahepatic mass and absence of a known extra-hepatic primary tumour. Currently available immunohistochemistry is not reliable in making this distinction" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). Large-duct mucin markers "should be cautiously employed when differentiating large duct type iCCA from other liver-metastatic adenocarcinomas"; "albumin in situ hybridization can serve as a novel method for the histological subtype diagnosis and differential diagnosis of iCCA" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1653534/full)). CRP stromal staining pitfall: "it often appears as diffuse strong positive staining in tumor stroma in cases with pronounced inflammation" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1653534/full)).

### 5.3 Combined hepatocellular–cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-CCA)

- **Definition.** "Combined hepatocellular – cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-CCA) is defined as containing unequivocal, intimately mixed elements of both hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma ... Collision tumours are not considered as combined neoplasms. A minimum cut off amount of each component for the diagnosis of cHCC-CCA has not been established and the diagnosis should be made regardless of the percentage of each component. The diagnosis of cHCC-CCA is based on routine histo-morphologic features and may be supported by IHC stains"; intermediate cell carcinoma: tumours "composed entirely of cells with histological features that are intermediate between those of hepatocytes and cholangiocytes" ([ICCR liver 2nd ed.](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)).
- **Consensus (Brunt et al.).** "It is recommended that diagnosis is based on routine histopathology with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E); immunostains are supportive, but not essential for diagnosis"; "there should no longer be formal diagnostic subtypes based on the identification of stem/progenitor cells"; "IHC should not define the diagnosis, in and of itself" ([Brunt et al., Hepatology 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6340292/)).
- **Biopsy caveat.** "Biopsy-based tissue samples ... may not contain salient tumor components, and intratumoral heterogeneity of most cHCC-CCA can lead to incorrect diagnosis if a biopsy does not contain adequate tissue"; "there are no published consensus guidelines for minimum amounts of HCC or iCCA to qualify for the diagnosis, either in biopsy material or in resected/explant samples"; "Some investigators recommend reporting percent of each component present, even in biopsy samples" ([Brunt et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6340292/)).
- **Grading/staging.** A cHCC-CCA-specific grade: **n.a.** RCPath: "These tumours should be staged as for intrahepatic CC"; "The recognition of 'stem cell features' either by morphology or immunohistochemistry ... is no longer a diagnostic requirement"; "Sampling of macroscopically different areas in heterogeneous tumours is important to avoid underdiagnosis due to sampling error" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).

### 5.4 Fibrolamellar carcinoma (FLC)

- **Morphology.** "Large eosinophilic neoplastic cells with large vesicular nuclei and prominent nucleoli; usually numerous intracytoplasmic pale and/or hyaline bodies; neoplastic cells positive for K7 and CD68; dense lamellar intratumoural fibrosis; non-cirrhotic background liver" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). "Even with a biopsy, the detection of lamellar bands is often unresolved" ([Semin Liver Dis 2018 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6020845/)).
- **IHC.** "CK7 and CD68 are positive in nearly all fibrolamellar carcinomas but are not specific as a subset of classical HCC can be positive for these markers" ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)).
- **Molecular.** DNAJB1-PRKACA "can be detected by reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) or fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) break-apart probes. This alteration is seen in 80% to 100% of FLM cases and has not been reported in classical HCC. For cases showing borderline features of fibrolamellar carcinoma, the diagnosis can be confirmed by 1 of these molecular techniques" ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)). "Detection of DNAJB1-PRKACA is a very sensitive and specific finding in support of the diagnosis of fibrolamellar carcinoma"; FISH was tested against "6 scirrhous hepatocellular carcinomas, which can closely mimic fibrolamellar carcinoma" ([Graham et al., Mod Pathol 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol20154)). RCPath: "Although fibrolamellar carcinomas carry a characteristic fusion (DNAJB1-PRKACA), it is not specific and is not necessary to identify it for routine diagnosis" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)). WHO 5th summary: "Fibrolamellar carcinoma defined by DNAJB1–PRKACA translocation" ([WHO 5th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.13975)).
- **Mimics.** Scirrhous HCC (above); FNH-like central scar on imaging: "Like Focal nodular hyperplasia the mass in FLC frequently contains a central scar" ([Semin Liver Dis 2018 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6020845/)).

### 5.5 Hepatoblastoma (paediatric; CAP biopsy protocol)

- **Classification list.** "Fetal with low mitotic activity (well-differentiated fetal pattern) Fetal, mitotically active (crowded fetal) Embryonal Pleomorphic (poorly differentiated) Small-cell undifferentiated Epithelial mixed ... Cholangioblastic Epithelial macrotrabecular pattern Mixed epithelial and mesenchymal Without teratoid features With teratoid features"; plus "Malignant rhabdoid tumor of the liver (INI-1 expression lost)" and "Hepatocellular neoplasm, not otherwise specified (HCN-NOS)" ([CAP hepatoblastoma biopsy 5.0.0.1](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-June-2025/Liver.Hepatoblastoma.Bx_5.0.0.1.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download)).
- **Pattern criteria and IHC.** "By definition, a diagnosis of 'pure fetal hepatoblastoma' cannot be made on a biopsy specimen"; WDF: "the mitotic rate is low (2 or fewer mitoses per 10 high-power fields)"; WDF "typically show a 1-2+ fine stippled pericanalicular (cytoplasmic) staining pattern with glypican-3 (GPC3) and variable nuclear staining for beta-catenin. Glutamine synthetase (GS) is usually diffusely positive in tumor cells whereas background liver shows a pericentral zonal distribution. SALL4 is negative in WDF"; crowded fetal: "This is the most common pattern seen in biopsy specimens and resections. By definition, >2 mitoses per 10 high-power fields"; "GPC3 typically shows a course [sic] diffuse cytoplasmic staining pattern that is 2-3+. GS shows diffuse strong staining and SALL4 is negative"; embryonal: "GPC3 is typically strongly positive (3+ staining)"; "SALL4 is frequently strongly nuclear positive"; macrotrabecular: "trabeculae 5 cells thick and greater"; "if tumor cells demonstrate pleomorphic cytomorphology with macrotrabecular arrangement, then consideration should be given to hepatocellular neoplasm (HCN), NOS (HCN-NOS) or HCC"; SCU: "'pure small cell undifferentiated HB' ... are now known to represent malignant rhabdoid tumor with SMARCB1 alterations and loss of INI-1 expression" ([CAP hepatoblastoma biopsy 5.0.0.1](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-June-2025/Liver.Hepatoblastoma.Bx_5.0.0.1.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download)).
- **What the biopsy report states (CAP data items).** Serum AFP category ("Less than 100 ng / mL", "100 ng / mL - 1.2 million ng / mL", "Greater than 1.2 million ng / mL"); "Beta-catenin IHC" with "Positive (specify pattern)"; "+Glypican-3 IHC" with "+Pattern of Glypican-3 IHC Staining"; "INI-1 IHC" ("Expression retained"/"Expression lost") ([CAP hepatoblastoma biopsy 5.0.0.1](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-June-2025/Liver.Hepatoblastoma.Bx_5.0.0.1.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download)).

## 6. Metastatic tumours

### 6.1 Metastatic carcinoma: site-directed IHC (RCPath G167)

- **Principle.** "Adenocarcinoma with a specific IHC profile is now often managed (and thus classified) as originating from the relevant site e.g. CK20-positive CDX2-positive metastatic adenocarcinoma is generally treated as of colorectal origin"; panels "tailored to clinical scenario and morphology" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).
- **Site markers (verbatim).** "Specific IHC markers include NKX3.1 and PSA for prostate, TTF1 for lung tumours and CDX2 for gastrointestinal"; "PSA can be positive in other tumours, e.g. salivary gland and some breast carcinomas"; TTF1 "may show cross- reaction in other tumours, especially colorectal carcinoma ... TTF1 is also expressed in thyroid and in small cell carcinomas from any site"; "Napsin A can serve as an additional lung marker, although it is also often present in renal carcinoma (especially papillary), adrenocortical carcinoma and ovarian clear cell carcinomas"; "A minority of adenocarcinomas originating in lung may be CK7-positive but negative with TTF1; in this setting, consider staining with SMARCA4"; "ER is positive in many breast and gynaecological adenocarcinomas. GCDFP-15 is positive in many breast carcinomas but GATA3 may be a more sensitive marker for breast, with the caveat that GATA3 is expressed in a range of tissue and tumour types, including squamous and urothelial"; "PAX8 stains most primary ovarian serous, endometrioid and clear cell adenocarcinomas, as well as most primary renal carcinomas and most thyroid and thymic tumours"; "CK7 is positive in many adenocarcinomas; almost all breast, lung, ovary and pancreaticobiliary adenocarcinomas are CK7 positive. Cholangiocarcinoma (biliary carcinoma) shows a phenotype similar to pancreatic (and some upper gastrointestinal) adenocarcinomas"; site markers in NEN include "SATB2 for lower gastrointestinal" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).
- **Colorectal pitfall.** "Colorectal adenocarcinomas with deficiency of mismatch repair (dMMR) show a variable immunophenotype, with over 15% showing loss of staining with CK20 and/or CDX2" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).
- **HCC vs metastasis.** G167 table: "Solid carcinoma: liver Hep Par-1, canalicular CD10 or pCEA, glypican- 3"; "Hep Par-1: 55–99% sensitive; moderately specific (may stain some adenocarcinomas)" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)). Glypican-3 is positive in "14% of gastrointestinal and pancreatic metastatic liver carcinomas" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
- **iCCA vs metastatic pancreatobiliary/upper GI adenocarcinoma.** IHC cannot separate them ("Currently available immunohistochemistry is not reliable in making this distinction", [RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)); see 5.2.

### 6.2 Metastatic neuroendocrine neoplasm (NET/NEC)

- **WHO 5th grading table (GEP-NEN), verbatim** ([Nagtegaal et al., WHO 5th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.13975)):

| Terminology | Differentiation | Grade | Mitotic rate (mitoses/2 mm²) | Ki-67 index |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NET, G1 | Well differentiated | Low | <2 | <3% |
| NET, G2 | Well differentiated | Intermediate | 2–20 | 3–20% |
| NET, G3 | Well differentiated | High | >20 | >20% |
| NEC, small-cell type (SCNEC) | Poorly differentiated | High† | >20 | >20% |
| NEC, large-cell type (LCNEC) | Poorly differentiated | High† | >20 | >20% |

  Footnotes: "Mitotic rates are to be expressed as the number of mitoses/2 mm² as determined by counting in 50 fields of 0.2 mm² (i.e. in a total area of 10 mm²); the Ki-67 proliferation index value is determined by counting at least 500 cells in the regions of highest labelling (hot-spots), which are identified at scanning magnification; the final grade is based on whichever of the two proliferation indexes places the neoplasm in the higher-grade category"; "Poorly differentiated NECs are not formally graded, but are considered high-grade by definition" ([WHO 5th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.13975)).
- **WHO 6th changes.** "Expression of at least two neuroendocrine markers (preferably including one of chromogranin A, synaptophysin, and INSM1) is required to confirm neuroendocrine differentiation"; "CD56 is considered insufficiently specific to be used as a marker of neuroendocrine differentiation"; "If the mitotic count and Ki67 proliferative index potentially yield different grades, the higher grade (usually based on the Ki67 index) should be provided"; "it was decided to record the presence or absence of tumour necrosis alongside the grade in all NETs" ([WHO 6th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.70116)).
- **RCPath G167.** "Useful general IHC markers include synaptophysin and chromogranin; strong positivity with these markers is usually seen only in neuroendocrine neoplasms"; "Other neuroendocrine markers including CD56 and NSE are generally less specific and may stain other tumour types. Well-differentiated neuroendocrine neoplasms may also show staining with markers specific to their site of origin, e.g. TTF1 for lung, CDX2 for gastrointestinal and SATB2 for lower gastrointestinal" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).
- **INSM1.** "All of the primary gep-nens and 94% of the metastatic gep-nens expressed insm1"; "insm1 can be used as a single first-line marker for primary and metastatic gep-nen" ([Histopathology 2019, INSM1 study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/his.13899)).
- **Biopsy-specific Ki-67 adequacy in a liver core:** **n.a.** (no source gives a minimum for liver cores beyond the 500-cell rule).

### 6.3 Metastatic melanoma

- **First-line panel.** "Melanocytic markers e.g. S100, SOX10, Melan-A and HMB45" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).
- **Performance.** S100 "Sensitivity – 97–100%, specificity 75–87%"; HMB45 "Sensitivity – 69–93% (77–100% in primary melanomas, 56–83% in metastatic melanomas)", also positive in "PEComas (angiomyolipomas, lymphangiomyomatosis, pulmonary 'sugar' tumors)"; MART-1/Melan-A "Sensitivity – 75–92%, specificity – 95–100%" ([Ohsie et al., J Cutan Pathol 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0560.2007.00891.x)). SOX10 sensitivity/specificity: **n.a.** (not in the fetched sources).
- **Pitfall.** HMB45/Melan-A positivity is shared with hepatic PEComa (section 7.3); S100 and SOX10 are not discussed as PEComa discriminators in the fetched PEComa sources (**n.a.**).

### 6.4 Metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST)

- **IHC.** "Over 95% of GISTs show expression of KIT (CD117) – cytoplasmatic, membranous, or perinuclear dot-like staining – regardless of the mutational status"; "ANO1/DOG1 immunostain is a sensitive and specific marker that is positive in approximately half of KIT-negative GISTs" ([GEIS guidelines 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10467260/)).
- **Mitoses and biopsy caution.** "The mitotic count has prognostic value and should be expressed as the number of mitoses on a total area of 5 mm²"; "Due to intratumor heterogeneity, the assessment of risk stratification features such as mitotic count should be deferred to the surgical resection specimen, as a core biopsy can misguide its evaluation" ([GEIS guidelines 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10467260/)).

## 7. Cannot-miss entities

### 7.1 Epithelioid haemangioendothelioma (EHE)

- **Morphology.** "Strands and nests of epithelioid cells in a myxohyaline stroma, with the presence of intracytoplasmic vacuoles which may contain erythrocytes" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025, CAMTA1-immunonegative EHE](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1478036/full)).
- **Molecular/IHC.** WWTR1::CAMTA1 in ">90%" and "CAMTA1 immunohistochemistry (IHC) is a highly sensitive and specific tool"; but CAMTA1-immunonegative, CAMTA1-rearranged cases exist, so "diagnosis of EHE should be based on morphology, combined with CAMTA1 and TFE3 IHC" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1478036/full)). "EHEs harbor major CAMTA1 rearrangement and <5% TFE3 rearrangement"; the TFE3 subtype "is characterized by prominent vascular spaces, brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm, solid growth pattern, and a better prognosis" ([Frontiers Oncol 2025, TFE3 EHE case](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11799294/)). German review: "In den übrigen 10 % der Fälle liegt eine YAP1::TFE3-Translokation vor. Diese zumeist als Subtyp der EHE angesehenen Fälle zeigen einen etwas aggressiveren tumorbiologischen Verlauf" (in the remaining 10%, YAP1::TFE3; a somewhat more aggressive course) ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)). **Disagreement:** TFE3 frequency (<5% vs 10%) and prognosis (better vs more aggressive).
- **Biopsy pitfalls.** EHE "können ... in der Stanzbiopsie als reaktive/entzündliche Läsionen oder Granulationsgewebe fehlgedeutet werden" (may be misread on core biopsy as reactive/inflammatory lesions or granulation tissue); "Da EHE (wie auch Angiosarkome) Zytokeratine ... exprimieren können, sind insbesondere Adenokarzinome mit Stromadesmoplasie ... wichtige Differentialdiagnosen" (because EHE, like angiosarcoma, may express cytokeratins, desmoplastic adenocarcinoma is a key differential) ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)).
- **Grade:** not graded (**n.a.**).

### 7.2 Angiosarcoma

- **IHC.** In 24 hepatic angiosarcomas, "100% of the HAS samples were positive for ERG expression"; "CD31 was expressed in 79.2% (19/24) of samples, CD34 was expressed in 87.5% (21/24) of samples and FVIIIRAg was expressed in 41.7% (10/24)" ([World J Gastroenterol 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974537/)). German review: ERG and CD31 are "die aktuell besten immunhistochemischen Marker" (currently the best markers), "ERG in 87,5–100 %, CD31 in 79,2–100 %"; "Eine diagnostische Stütze kann eine p53-Immunhistochemie in mutierten Fällen sein" (p53 IHC can support the diagnosis in mutated cases) ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)).
- **Pitfalls.** "Als diagnostischer Pitfall seien Adenokarzinome der Prostata genannt, die ebenfalls oft ERG exprimieren ... (z. B. NKX3.1, PSA)" (prostatic adenocarcinoma often expresses ERG; resolve with NKX3.1, PSA); "Ein weiterer immunhistochemischer Fallstrick ist eine aberrante Zytokeratinexpression" (aberrant keratin expression is another pitfall); HSVN/anastomosing haemangioma should not be overcalled as angiosarcoma ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)). Grading: **n.a.**

### 7.3 Epithelioid angiomyolipoma / PEComa

- **IHC.** In 36 hepatic PEComas: "HMB-45(35/36,97.2%), Melan-A (34/35,97.1%), SMA (23/26,88.5%) and CD34(86.7%)"; "negative expressions include AFP, Hep-Par1, CD117, CK8/18, and TFE-3"; "It is recommended to detect the expression of TFE-3 protein, because TFE3-positive PEComas are associated with poor prognosis" ([Frontiers Oncol 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2024.1416254/full)). "TFE3 is a new marker that is strongly expressed in 15% of PEComas, indicating the presence of TFE gene rearrangement" ([J Clin Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8072725/)). Cathepsin K: in 23 PEComas (9 liver), "All displayed a high percentage of cells with moderately to strongly positive reactions for cathepsin K (mean 91%; range 80–100%). HMB45, Melan-A and smooth muscle actin (SMA) were expressed in 78, 87 and 87% of cases"; "Cathepsin K appears to be more powerful than other commonly used markers in diagnosing a wide spectrum of PEComas" ([Histopathology 2013, cathepsin K study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/his.12059)).
- **Malignancy criteria.** "Folpe et al (6) have suggested malignant PEComas are characterized by any two of the following criteria: tumor size>5cm, infiltrative growth pattern, high nuclear grade and cellularity, necrosis, vascular invasion, mitotic activity of more than 1/50 high power field(HPF), and aggressive clinical behavior" ([Frontiers Oncol 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2024.1416254/full)).
- **Biopsy pitfall.** "Preoperative diagnosis, even in core needle biopsy, is difficult" ([J Clin Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8072725/)); Hep Par-1/AFP negativity (above) and HMB45 positivity separate it from HCC; HMB45 is shared with melanoma (section 6.3).

### 7.4 Undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma (UESL) and mesenchymal hamartoma (MH)

- **UESL morphology/IHC.** "Hypercellular sheets of highly pleomorphic tumor cells, necrosis, high mitotic index, frequent atypical mitoses and apoptotic bodies"; "eosinophilic hyaline globules"; "the proliferative index of UESL by Ki67 immunohistochemistry is usually high (≥30%)"; "Most cases of UESL are positive for Vimentin, alpha-1 antytrypsin and CD68" ([Hepatic Oncology 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7338921/)).
- **UESL pitfalls.** "Cytokeratins AE1/AE3, CAM5.2 and OSCAR may show focal or dot-like expression in UESL, which could suggest the diagnosis of carcinoma"; "Glypican 3, which is often expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma and hepatoblastoma, stains close to half of the cases of UESL" ([Hepatic Oncology 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7338921/)).
- **MH.** "A lobular growth of myxomatous connective tissue containing scattered bland stellate-shaped mesenchymal cells"; "Branching bile ducts similar to ductal plate malformation are also present"; sporadic MH "frequently associated with a translocation involving MALAT1 gene at chromosome 11q13 and C19MC gene at 19q13"; "UESL may arise in the background of HMH" ([Hepatic Oncology 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7338921/)). UESL genomics (C19MC, TP53): [PLoS Genet 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7192511/). Grading: **n.a.**

### 7.5 Solitary fibrous tumour (SFT)

- "STAT6 has been identified as a highly sensitive, almost completely specific immunohistochemical marker"; "5–10% of SFTs are nonreactive to CD34, making the differential diagnosis of SFTs based on histopathology difficult" ([hepatic SFT report, PMC6482201](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6482201/)). Risk stratification for hepatic SFT on biopsy: **n.a.**

### 7.6 Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour (IMT) vs IgG4-related inflammatory pseudotumour

- **IMT.** ALK IHC positive in "approximately 50–60% of cases, exhibiting cytoplasmic, membranous, perinuclear, or dot-like staining"; "ROS1 and NTRK3 gene rearrangements are detected at similar frequencies in approximately 5–10% of ALK-negative IMTs"; "Epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcomas (EIMSs) typically harbor a RANBP2::ALK fusion" ([Cancers 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12026078/)).
- **Hepatic IPT types.** "The fibrohistiocytic type was defined as having abundant histiocytic infiltration involving foamy cells or multinucleated giant cells. The lymphoplasmacytic type was defined by an inflammatory process, mainly involving lymphoplasmacytic infiltration"; "IgG4-positive plasma cells were significantly more numerous in the lymphoplasmacytic than fibrohistiocytic type"; "The lymphoplasmacytic type is unique, and could belong to the so-called IgG4-related diseases"; but "two of the fibrohistiocytic tumors had relatively high IgG4-/IgG-positive plasma cell ratios (43.7 and 36.7%)" ([Zen et al., Mod Pathol 2007](https://www.nature.com/articles/3800836)).
- **IgG4-RD criteria.** "The critical histopathological features are a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, a storiform pattern of fibrosis, and obliterative phlebitis"; "a confident pathological diagnosis of IgG4-related disease requires the presence of two of the three major histological features"; "On biopsy specimens, the presence of >10 IgG4 + plasma cells has been proposed"; ratio ">40% as a comprehensive cutoff value in any organ"; "we do not accept an IgG4 +/IgG + plasma cell ratio of >40% in and of itself as sufficient pathological evidence" ([Deshpande et al., Mod Pathol 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201272)). Biliary guideline: "A cut-off of >10 IgG4-positive cells per HPF was adopted to make a biopsy-based diagnosis of IgG4-SC possible"; pitfall: in cholangiocarcinoma ">50 IgG4-positive cells per HPF were found in 9% of 54 resected specimens" ([IgG4-SC guideline, J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jhbp.596)). Liver-mass-specific IgG4 count threshold: **n.a.** (organ-specific cut-offs in Deshpande are in a figure, not text).

### 7.7 Hepatic lymphoma, including hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL)

- **HSTCL pattern and phenotype.** "HSTCL typically involves the liver, spleen, and bone marrow in a sinusoidal pattern"; "Three patterns of infiltration in the liver have been described: sinusoidal, marked periportal infiltration with spoilage into the sinusoids, and nodular parenchymal infiltration"; immunophenotype "CD2+, CD3+, CD4−, CD5−, CD7+, CD8−, γδ TCR+, TIA1+"; "Cytotoxic granule–associated markers TIA-1 and granzyme M are typically positive whereas perforin and granzyme B are negative"; "The most frequent chromosomal aberrations associated with HSTCL are isochromosome 7q and trisomy 8" ([Blood 2020 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7596851/)). WHO-HAEM5: "While most cases express TCRγδ (~75%) followed by TCRαβ (~25%), a small subset of cases, about 5%, are TCR-silent"; "only 49% of patients were younger than 60" ([WHO-HAEM5 lymphoid summary, Leukemia 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41375-022-01620-2)).
- **Other primary/secondary hepatic lymphomas (e.g. DLBCL) on liver-mass biopsy:** source-verified criteria **n.a.** in this brief; the first-line panel in a CUP-type biopsy includes "a lymphoid marker e.g. CD45 (LCA)" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).

### 7.8 Myeloid sarcoma and extramedullary haematopoiesis (EMH)

- **Myeloid sarcoma.** "The most sensitive immunohistochemical markers were CD43 and lysozyme present in all cases with MS (13/13, 100%)"; "An immunohistochemical panel including CD43, lysozyme, myeloperoxidase (MPO), CD68 (or CD163), CD117, CD3 and CD20 can successfully identify the vast majority of MS variants"; "MS is frequently mistaken for non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), small round cell tumor ... and undifferentiated carcinoma" ([Diagn Pathol 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1746-1596-2-42)).
- **EMH.** "Multiple megakaryocytes and erythroid cells with some myeloid precursors within sinusoids and portal tracts" ([EMH case report, PMC7380837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7380837/)). Trilineage mix and megakaryocytes favour EMH; a sheet of blasts favours myeloid sarcoma (inference from the two sources above; explicit discriminating criteria **n.a.**).

### 7.9 Abscesses and hydatid cyst

- **Pyogenic abscess.** "A liver abscess is a limited, often encysted, purulent inflammation with parenchymal necrosis"; common pathogens "Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Enterococcus faecalis, Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus haemolyticus"; "Klebsiella pneumoniae is often isolated in Southeast Asia in people with colorectal cancers" ([Clin Exp Hepatol 2021 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8977881/)). Abscesses "are also caused by parasites, malignancy, foreign bodies" ([StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538230/)). A histological checklist for a biopsy of abscess wall: **n.a.**
- **Amoebic abscess.** "Amebic liver abscesses are the most common manifestation of extraintestinal amebiasis"; "E. histolytica may be observed with ingested red blood cells (erythrophagocytosis)" ([CDC DPDx amebiasis](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/amebiasis/index.html)). Aspirate: "The high density and chocolate color of the obtained aspirate are characteristic"; direct microscopy "frequently fails to identify trophozoites in the aspirate" ([Clin Exp Hepatol 2021 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8977881/)).
- **Echinococcal (hydatid) cyst.** "Host tissue (A) encapsulates the hydatid cyst wall, which is composed of an acellular laminated layer (B) and a nucleate germinal layer (C) from which the brood capsule (D) arises. Inside the brood capsule are numerous protoscolices (E) with visible hooklets (F)"; "rupture of the cyst may also lead to cyst dissemination"; Echinococcus multilocularis "affects the liver as a slow growing, destructive tumor ... This may be misdiagnosed as liver cancer" ([CDC DPDx echinococcosis](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/echinococcosis/index.html)).

- **Abscess.** A histological checklist for a biopsy of abscess wall: **n.a.** ([StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538230/); [Clin Exp Hepatol 2021 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8977881/)).

### 9.2 Disagreements

1. **Number of HCC grade tiers.**
   - RCPath: "Recent authors, including those of the WHO classification, favour a three-point grading system (well, moderately or poorly differentiated)" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
   - ICCR: "Grade 1 and 2 HCC of Edmondson and Steiner are combined as well-differentiated HCC in the three-point grading system" ([ICCR liver 2nd ed.](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)).
   - CAP keeps the AJCC four-tier G1–G4. Its "Undifferentiated category is rarely used and is reserved for tumors that do not show obvious hepatocellular or other differentiation on morphology or immunohistochemistry" ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)).
2. **What a "WHO" grade is based on.**
   - The WHO 5th-edition table grades by global assessment. Grade 3, for example, is "Clearly malignant on H&E staining, but morphology is consistent with broad spectrum of poorly differentiated carcinomas" ([Hessisches Krebsregister 2025](https://hessisches-krebsregister.de/media/04_ag_tumordokumentation_maerz2025_dokumentation_von_lebertumoren.pdf)).
   - A review table also labelled "World Health Organization" grades by architecture, e.g. moderately differentiated "Trabecular (3 or more cells in thickness) and acinar" ([Gisder, Hepatoma Res 2022](https://www.oaepublish.com/articles/2394-5079.2021.106)).
3. **Macrotrabecular threshold.**
   - WHO 5th (via RCPath): ">50% of the tumour with macrotrabecular growth pattern; trabeculae ≥10 cell-thick" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
   - A 2026 review: "macrotrabeculae (≥ 6 cells thick)" with "a 50% tumour area threshold" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)).
   - For hepatoblastoma, a different entity, CAP uses "trabeculae 5 cells thick and greater" ([CAP hepatoblastoma biopsy 5.0.0.1](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-June-2025/Liver.Hepatoblastoma.Bx_5.0.0.1.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download)).
4. **Steatohepatitic HCC threshold.**
   - WHO 5th (via RCPath): ">50% tumour shows histological features of steatohepatitis" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
   - Versus "Currently, no consensus exists regarding the minimum threshold of steatohepatitic features required for a formal diagnosis" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)).
5. **Fibrosis staging system for the background liver.**
   - CAP: "The scoring system described by Ishak is recommended by the AJCC Cancer Staging Manual, 8 edition" ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)).
   - ICCR: "the Kleiner and SAF systems were developed for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease while the METAVIR, Ishak and Batts-Ludwig systems were designed for those with chronic (viral) hepatitis" ([ICCR liver 2nd ed.](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICCR-Liver-2nd-ed-v2.0-bookmark.pdf)).
   - RCPath requires a fibrosis stage but names no system in the passages fetched ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
6. **HCA subtype frequencies.**
   - sh-HCA: "5 to 10% of HCAs" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)) and "5–10%" ([WHO 6th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.70116)), versus 4% ([Nault 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/hep.29884)) and "∼4% of HCA" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
   - Unclassified: "less than 5% of HCAs" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)) versus 8% ([Nault 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/hep.29884)).
   - β-catenin exon 7/8: "10% of all cases" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)) versus bex7,8HCA 3% + bex7,8IHCA 4% ([Nault 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/hep.29884)).
7. **Malignant transformation risk (different denominators).**
   - RCPath on b-HCA: "Increased risk of malignant transformation (up to 40%)" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
   - Virchows 2026 for HCA overall: "malignant transformation (4–8%)" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)).
8. **sh-HCA surrogate marker.**
   - RCPath lists "Diffuse expression of argininosuccinate synthase 1 (ASS1)" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
   - Virchows 2026: "prostaglandin D2 synthase (PTGDS) expression is a specific but not sensitive marker, while overexpression of argininosuccinate synthase 1 (ASS1) is more sensitive but not specific" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)).
9. **Nuclear β-catenin in b-HCA on biopsy.**
   - "frequent nuclear β-catenin staining" ([Choi & Thung 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812079/)).
   - Versus "sensitivity is too low to be reliable in routine practice, especially on biopsies" ([Virchows 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-026-04685-x)).
10. **DNAJB1-PRKACA in fibrolamellar carcinoma.**
    - WHO 5th: "Fibrolamellar carcinoma defined by DNAJB1–PRKACA translocation" ([WHO 5th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.13975)).
    - "a very sensitive and specific finding in support of the diagnosis of fibrolamellar carcinoma" ([Graham et al., Mod Pathol 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol20154)).
    - CAP: "seen in 80% to 100% of FLM cases and has not been reported in classical HCC" ([CAP HCC 4.3.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/protocols/Liver.HCC_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)).
    - RCPath: "it is not specific and is not necessary to identify it for routine diagnosis" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
11. **Which iCCA subtype the lymphoepithelioma-like variant belongs to.**
    - RCPath (WHO 2019) puts "lymphoepithelioma-like (Epstein–Barr virus associated)" among the "variants of the large duct subtype" ([RCPath G050](https://www.rcpath.org/static/cbae24ce-220c-4d9f-95c5b83dd1f0ad2b/Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-liver-resection-specimens-and-liver-biopsies-for-primary-and-metastatic-carcinoma.pdf)).
    - CAP, citing WHO 6th, lists "lymphoepithlioma-like [sic]" among "variants of small duct type iCCA" ([CAP intrahepatic bile duct 4.4.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/BileDuctIH_4.4.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf?download=true)).
12. **CD56 (strength of wording, plus a pitfall).**
    - "CD56 is considered insufficiently specific to be used as a marker of neuroendocrine differentiation" ([WHO 6th summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/his.70116)).
    - Versus "CD56 and NSE are generally less specific and may stain other tumour types" ([RCPath G167](https://www.rcpath.org/static/555302b1-8b11-4d8a-a24431d0693d3287/G167-Dataset-for-histopathological-reporting-of-cancer-of-unknown-primary-and-malignancy-of-unknown-primary-origin.pdf)).
    - Pitfall: CD56 is listed as a small-duct iCCA marker ([Frontiers Oncol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1653534/full)) and is positive in gallery image 10.
13. **TFE3-rearranged EHE.**
    - "EHEs harbor major CAMTA1 rearrangement and <5% TFE3 rearrangement", and the TFE3 subtype has "a better prognosis" ([Front Oncol 2025, TFE3 EHE case](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11799294/)).
    - Versus the remaining 10% (YAP1::TFE3): "Diese zumeist als Subtyp der EHE angesehenen Fälle zeigen einen etwas aggressiveren tumorbiologischen Verlauf" ([Pathologie 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12552387/)).
14. **Hepatic PEComa markers.**
    - In a 36-case series, "HMB-45(35/36,97.2%)", with TFE-3 listed among the "negative expressions". The same series still advises testing because "TFE3-positive PEComas are associated with poor prognosis" ([Frontiers Oncol 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2024.1416254/full)).
    - TFE3 "is strongly expressed in 15% of PEComas" ([J Clin Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8072725/)).
    - In 23 PEComas, "HMB45, Melan-A and smooth muscle actin (SMA) were expressed in 78, 87 and 87% of cases", and "Cathepsin K appears to be more powerful than other commonly used markers" ([Histopathology 2013, cathepsin K study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/his.12059)).
15. **IgG4 thresholds (caveat rather than direct conflict).**
    - "On biopsy specimens, the presence of >10 IgG4 + plasma cells has been proposed", but "we do not accept an IgG4 +/IgG + plasma cell ratio of >40% in and of itself as sufficient pathological evidence" ([Deshpande et al., Mod Pathol 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201272)).
    - The biliary guideline adopted ">10 IgG4-positive cells per HPF" for biopsy diagnosis, while in cholangiocarcinoma ">50 IgG4-positive cells per HPF were found in 9% of 54 resected specimens" ([IgG4-SC guideline, J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jhbp.596)).
