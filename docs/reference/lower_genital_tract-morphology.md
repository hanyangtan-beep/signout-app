# Vulval and vaginal biopsies: morphology for the cervical biopsy form

Specimens: vulval and vaginal biopsies, punch biopsies and avulsions, reported in the cervical biopsy form from Phase 70 (12 October 2026). British spelling; quotations keep the original spelling.

## 1. Scope

- The form is now "Cervical, vaginal and vulval biopsy" (picker label "Cervix / vagina / vulva"). Its specimen types are cervical biopsy, endocervical curettings, cervical polyp, vaginal biopsy and vulval biopsy. Each vulval or vaginal part shows its own squamous, benign and stain fields; the cervical-only fields (transformation zone tissue, glandular lesions, gland involvement, cervical benign findings) are hidden.
- Out of scope: vulvectomy, vaginectomy, wide local excision, margins and lymph nodes. Those belong to the planned vulval and vaginal carcinoma resection cards.
- Terminology: two tiers, LSIL and HSIL, with the VaIN grade in brackets in the vagina. Vulval HSIL prints as usual-type VIN. Differentiated VIN is HPV-independent and is never called HSIL.

## 2. Intake

- A pasted line opens the form when it names the vulva (vulva, vulval, vulvar, labium, labia, fourchette, introitus, clitoris) or the vagina (vagina, vaginal). The one exception is a line that also names tissue passed per vaginam, products of gestation, decidua, endometrium, a cyst, abscess, lipoma, skin tag or Bartholin gland, a hysterectomy or the adnexa, an excision or resection, a margin, a stitch, a sentinel node or lymph node, the cervix, curettings or the nose. Those lines keep their current forms.
- The line fills the specimen type, the site (organ words, procedure words and clinical queries such as "?LS ?VIN" removed), the procedure (punch biopsy, avulsion) and granulation tissue when the line names it.
- A deliberate change: "Vulval skin biopsy" lines now open this form rather than the Dermatoses form. The vulval form carries lichen sclerosus, lichen simplex chronicus, lichen planus, spongiotic and psoriasiform dermatitis, PASD and p53, which are the stains needed to separate dVIN from a dermatosis.

## 3. Vulval squamous lesions

- HPV-associated HSIL: atypia through the full thickness with suprabasal mitoses, often with "extension in hair follicles and skin appendages"; LSIL has koilocytes in the upper layers and mitoses in the lower third; HSIL is p16 block positive and p53 wild-type ([PathologyOutlines: HPV-associated squamous intraepithelial lesions of the vulva (Choschzick)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvaVIN.html)).
- Differentiated VIN: "Acanthosis, parakeratosis, elongation and anastomosis of rete ridges", with basal and parabasal atypia, and "full thickness atypia is lacking"; "Enlarged squamous cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm, vesicular nuclei and prominent nucleoli". p53 shows "basal overexpression, diffuse overexpression, absent (null) or cytoplasmic" staining; p16 is "negative or patchy"; Ki-67 stays basal. It can be hard to separate from lichen simplex chronicus in a punch biopsy ([PathologyOutlines: HPV-independent VIN (Choschzick)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvadifferentiatedVIN.html)).
- Squamous cell carcinoma: block-type p16 is the surrogate for HPV-associated carcinoma, with a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 98.4% in [Kortekaas et al., Histopathology 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7383647/). The form records the HPV status as HPV-associated, HPV-independent or not determined.
- Pigmented lesions: HSIL has block p16 and wild-type p53, whereas dVIN has aberrant p53 and negative or patchy p16 ([PathologyOutlines: pigmented lesions of the vulva (Erem, Turashvili)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvapigmented.html)).

## 4. Vaginal squamous lesions

- VaIN 1 is atypia in the lower third, VaIN 2 in the lower two-thirds and VaIN 3 in more than two-thirds, reported in two tiers. p16 shows "Strong diffuse block positivity involving basal keratinocytes and extending beyond the lower third" "in nearly all high grade lesions"; p53 is wild-type ([PathologyOutlines: VaIN (Khan, Buza)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vaginavain.html)).

## 5. Cervical squamous gallery

- The cervical squamous field now has a gallery as well. LSIL is usually p16 negative or weak and patchy, although about a third overexpress it, so p16 helps only when it is negative or patchy ([PathologyOutlines: cervical LSIL (Al Dallal, Salih)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixLSIL.html)). In HSIL, block p16 shows "upward extension involving at least one" third of the epithelium; CIN II keeps maturation in the upper third, and thin HSIL can mimic atrophy ([PathologyOutlines: cervical HSIL (Alkhateeb, Salih)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cervixhsilciniii.html)).

## 6. p16, Ki-67 and p53

- p53 is read in six patterns: scattered and mid-epithelial with basal sparing are wild-type; basal, diffuse (basal to parabasal), null with a positive internal control, and cytoplasmic are mutant-type. Parabasal overexpression in more than 10% of the lesion makes a basal pattern diffuse ([Kortekaas et al., Histopathology 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7383647/)). The basal pattern in dVIN is also shown from [Dasgupta et al., Histopathology 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12703432/).
- The p16 and Ki-67 fields are shared by all five specimen types. Block positive supports HSIL; patchy or mosaic staining does not.

## 7. Paget disease

- Paget cells are "enlarged polygonal epithelial cells with abundant pale cytoplasm, large nuclei and small to prominent nucleoli"; "Intracytoplasmic mucin is frequently present". They are "Predominantly located in the basal and parabasal portions of the squamous epithelium" and extend upwards singly and in small clusters. Invasion is by "single cells or small groups" "infiltrating the dermis with an associated desmoplastic reaction" ([PathologyOutlines: Paget disease of the vulva (Salari, Ma)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvapagetsdisease.html)).
- Stains: primary disease is CK7, GATA3, CEA and GCDFP-15 positive and CK20 and p63 negative. "Secondary Paget disease, anorectal primary: CK20, CDX2, SATB2"; "Secondary Paget disease, urothelial primary: CK7, CK20, GATA3, uroplakin III, p63" ([PathologyOutlines: Paget disease of the vulva (Salari, Ma)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvapagetsdisease.html)). SOX10 is offered to record exclusion of melanoma in situ.
- The form flags primary Paget disease with CK20 positivity, a CK20 recorded as both positive and negative, and Paget disease with no stains recorded.

## 8. Benign vulval findings

- Lichen sclerosus: vacuolar interface change with sclerosis between the infiltrate and the epithelium; lichen planus has a band directly under the epithelium without sclerosis; lichen simplex chronicus has epidermal hyperplasia and hypergranulosis without basal damage or a sclerotic band; lichen sclerosus is p53 wild-type ([PathologyOutlines: lichen sclerosus (Huvila, Gilks)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvalichensclerosus.html); [PathologyOutlines: lichen planus (Erem, Turashvili)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vulvalichenplanus.html)). Lichen simplex chronicus has "vertically oriented, thickened collagen bundles in the superficial dermis" ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).
- Fibroepithelial stromal polyp: "characteristic stellate and multinucleated stromal cells", without papillomatous architecture or koilocytes; desmin, ER and PR positive ([PathologyOutlines: fibroepithelial stromal polyp (Alhatem, Heller)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vaginafibroepithelial.html)).
- Hidradenoma papilliferum: a circumscribed dermal proliferation of branching papillae lined by apocrine columnar cells with decapitation secretion over a myoepithelial layer ([PathologyOutlines: hidradenoma papilliferum (Wondimu, Chang)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/skintumornonmelanocytichidradenomapap.html)).

## 9. Benign vaginal findings

- Vaginal adenosis: mucinous glands in 62% and tuboendometrial glands in 21%, usually in the lamina propria; squamous metaplasia is how adenosis heals ([PathologyOutlines: vaginal adenosis (Jones, Maldonado)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vaginaladenosis.html)).
- Gartner duct cyst: "single layer of cuboidal to low columnar nonmucinous epithelium"; Müllerian cyst: "mucin secreting or ciliated (tubal type) columnar cells" ([PathologyOutlines: vaginal cysts (Hrones, Buza)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/vaginacysts.html)).
- Atrophy is illustrated with an atrophic cervix, and the caption says so.

## 10. Printed lines

- VULVA, left 3 o'clock, punch biopsy - DIFFERENTIATED VULVAL INTRAEPITHELIAL NEOPLASIA (dVIN), HPV-independent
- VAGINA, vault, biopsy - NO EVIDENCE OF SQUAMOUS INTRAEPITHELIAL LESION OR MALIGNANCY
- VULVA, right labium majus, biopsy - EXTRAMAMMARY PAGET DISEASE, intraepithelial
- VULVA, biopsy - LICHEN SCLEROSUS; no evidence of VIN or malignancy
- A mixed case summarises each site in its own terms: "SUMMARY: Highest grade squamous lesion HSIL (CIN III) in part A and HSIL (VaIN 3) in part B."

## 11. Gaps

- No openly licensed images were found for flat vulval LSIL, VaIN 1, VaIN 2, p16-negative epithelium, a basal Ki-67 pattern, invasive or secondary Paget disease, or a Müllerian cyst. Each gallery with a text-only group says so in its note.
- The atrophy photograph is from the cervix.
- The carcinoma type field is shared with the cervix and opens on its cervical default; choose the type for each vulval or vaginal carcinoma.
