## 1. Biopsy level and adequacy

### 1.1 Where suction biopsies are taken, and why

**The physiological hypoganglionic zone**
- **Aldridge and Campbell (reported second-hand).** They examined cadaveric anorectal specimens from 20 patients, aged from premature infants to 15 years. Their conclusions were:
  - the terminal 0.5 cm above the dentate line (not the anal verge) should be considered "hypoganglionic" rather than aganglionic;
  - this zone is lined by transitional epithelium in some specimens;
  - myenteric ganglion cells extend further caudally than submucosal ones ([Ambartsumyan et al. 2020](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Aldridge and Campbell's recommended level.** They suggested biopsying at least 2 cm "above the anal valves [dentate line]", while others specified >2 cm above the mucocutaneous junction/anal verge ([Ambartsumyan et al. 2020](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **IWG 2009.** A normal zone of submucosal hypoganglionosis extends about 10–25 mm above the dentate line, increasing from 10 mm in neonates to 25 mm in children aged 3 years and above. The myenteric hypoganglionic zone extends only about 5 mm ([Gastro 2009 IWG guidelines](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Matsukuma et al. (AJCP 2023).** The distal-most 1 to 2 cm above the dentate line is called the "physiologic hypoganglionic zone" ([Matsukuma et al., AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- **Gonzalo and Plesec 2013.** Depending on age, an adequate suction biopsy should be taken 1 to 2.5 cm proximal to the anorectal squamocolumnar junction. They attribute this to the IWG ([Gonzalo & Plesec, Arch Pathol Lab Med 2013](https://meridian.allenpress.com/aplm/article/137/8/1099/65503/Hirschsprung-Disease-and-Use-of-Calretinin-in)).

**Recommended biopsy levels, by source**

| Source | Recommended level(s) | Link |
|---|---|---|
| IWG 2009 | Instrument passed to 3 cm above the anal valves. Some laboratories take 3 levels, e.g. 1, 2 and 3 cm above the pectinate line, or 2, 3 and 5 cm in the older child | [IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf) |
| ERNICA 2020 (unchanged in the 2025/2026 update) | Posterior and/or lateral wall, at least 2 cm proximal to the dentate line or 3 cm from the anal orifice. With suction biopsy, 2–3 biopsies are advisable | [ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf); [ERNICA update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jpn3.70442) |
| APSA HDIG 2019 | More than one level (e.g. estimated 1, 2 and 3 cm above the dentate line), submitted separately. Location should be stated relative to the dentate line and circumference | [Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/) |
| Ambartsumyan et al. 2020 | 2, 3 and 4 cm above the anal verge (estimated about 1, 2 and 3 cm above the dentate line), in separate containers. If AChE is planned, one extra unfixed biopsy (typically at 3 cm) | [Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf) |
| Matsukuma et al. 2023 | Sequential sampling, e.g. 1, 2 and 3 cm from the estimated dentate line | [AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555) |
| Monforte et al. 2025 | About 2–3 cm from the dentate line. Two sites at distinct levels is "optimal practice" | [Monforte et al., World J Pediatr Surg 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/) |

**Why the choice of landmark matters**
- The dentate line is estimated, not seen, during suction biopsy. Depending on age, it may lie 1 to 9 mm above the anal verge ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Biopsies at multiple levels reduce the chance that all biopsies are too low (transitional mucosa) or too high ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Multi-level biopsies also give mapping data that may help recognise ultrashort-segment disease ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- A biopsy taken above a very short aganglionic segment may give a false-negative result ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

### 1.2 Minimum size and amount of submucosa

**Size and proportion of submucosa, by source**
- **IWG 2009.** Ideally the biopsy is at least 3 mm in diameter, with at least a third of it submucosa. An adequate biopsy is >3 mm wide, and centrally the submucosa should be more than a third of the cross-sectional area ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **ERNICA 2020.** Cites the IWG as ≥3 mm diameter and at least one-third submucosa ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf)).
- **Ambartsumyan et al. 2020.** Confidently excluding ganglion cells generally needs a biopsy 2 to 3 mm in greatest dimension with 50% or more submucosa. Most sections should contain some submucosa, and in most sections submucosa should make up one-third or more of the cut surface ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Matsukuma et al. 2023.** A correctly performed suction biopsy has mucosa plus an equal thickness of submucosa. If no ganglion cells are found and submucosa is less than 1/3 of the biopsy thickness, sampling error is possible ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- **Monforte et al. 2025.** Submucosa should be at least as deep as the mucosa, usually 300–500 µm (ideally 1 mm). Any submucosa at all mandates review of the whole specimen, because ganglion cells may lie immediately beneath the muscularis mucosae. Their "insufficient" examples include submucosa limited to <30% after the block is exhausted ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).

**How submucosa is judged at the bench**
- On removal from the biopsy gun, the deep aspect is white (submucosal connective tissue) compared with the pink mucosa ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).

**Inadequacy rates and older children**
- Large series report inadequacy rates of 9–17%, mainly from insufficient submucosa. This is a particular problem over 1 year of age ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- APSA HDIG quotes inadequacy rates of 8–26% ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).
- As children grow, suction biopsies yield a smaller proportion of submucosa, so incisional biopsy is favoured over 1 year of age ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

### 1.3 Features marking a biopsy as too distal

**Anal (squamous) or transitional mucosa**
- Biopsies often contain anal (squamous) or transitional mucosa. HSCR should not be diagnosed from such biopsies, although ganglion cells in them do exclude HSCR ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- The surface should be lined entirely by colonic mucosa. Biopsies with transitional or squamous mucosa may be aganglionic and are "inadequate" unless a ganglion cell is found ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Squamocolumnar mucosa indicates sampling in the anal canal ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- Specimens covered by squamous or squamocolumnar junction mucosa, with no colorectal mucosa or submucosa, are "inadequate", and a repeat biopsy is indicated ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- APSA HDIG lists squamous or transitional mucosa, indicating a low biopsy, as a common reason for inadequacy ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).

**Low biopsies with rectal mucosa only**
- A low biopsy, even one lined by rectal mucosa, may contain few or no ganglion cells in a patient without HSCR ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- Biopsies with sparse ganglion cells probably come from near the dentate line ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- Ambartsumyan et al. say physiological hypoganglionosis is generally not a significant concern in a low biopsy with only colorectal mucosa, **unless** ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)):
  - submucosa is marginal;
  - exhaustive sections have not been examined;
  - the patient is older (e.g. >1 year); or
  - neither the aganglionic biopsy nor the biopsies just above it show nerve hypertrophy or abnormal cholinergic innervation.

**Other reasons for inadequacy**
- APSA HDIG adds problems with procurement or processing (e.g. crush artefact) ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).

### 1.4 Number of sections and levels, and whether any minimum is evidence-based

**Suction biopsy: sections examined, by source**

| Source | Sections examined | Link |
|---|---|---|
| IWG 2009 | Initial 50–75 H&E sections of each biopsy (typically 4–5 slides of about 15 sections), with a low threshold for more. Ganglion cells are usually seen within the first 10–20 sections, but 50–100 or even >200 sections may be needed | [IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf) |
| Ambartsumyan et al. 2020 | Routinely 50 to 75 H&E sections (4–5 µm); often prudent to cut more or exhaust the block when relying on H&E alone | [Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf) |
| Matsukuma et al. 2023 | 15 initial serial sections (5 slides × 3, at 5 µm). If no ganglion cell is seen: calretinin plus 60 more sections, 75 in total. Up to exhaustion if the only colonic-mucosa specimen shows squamocolumnar mucosa | [AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555) |
| Monforte et al. 2025 | Six H&E slides, each with about 18 serial 4 µm sections, with levels in between for calretinin, Glut-1 and choline transporter | [Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/) |
| Guinard-Samuel et al. 2009 | H&E alone "requires the analysis of 100 or more histological sections" to ensure good specificity | [Guinard-Samuel et al., Mod Pathol 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2009110) |
| Holland et al. 2010 | Eight levels of H&E plus peripherin and S-100. 93% of ganglionic cases were diagnosed on the original eight levels | [Holland et al., Mod Pathol 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2010104) |
| RCPath G085 | "Multiple serial H&E sections of a deep mucosal biopsy from the submucosal aspect towards the mucosal aspect" | [RCPath G085](https://www.rcpath.org/static/4593f557-d75c-4ca6-9307a9d688e02a2d/1f175bdf-4934-4ace-bbde65114401944b/g085-tissue-pathways-for-gastrointestinal-and-pancreatobiliary-pathology.pdf) |
| Venugopal et al. (cadaveric controls, cited by Ambartsumyan et al.) | With no more than 25 H&E sections, and excluding biopsies with anal transitional mucosa, submucosal ganglion cells were found in 68/68 specimens with submucosa | [Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf) |

**Incisional and full-thickness biopsies**
- Fewer H&E sections are generally enough (e.g. 2 slides with 2 ribbons per slide) ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- For full-thickness biopsies in neuromuscular disease generally, the IWG agreed on at least three levels of the paraffin block, with multiple (minimum 10) sections per level ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).

**Is any minimum evidence-based?**
- None of the fetched sources gives a validation study for a minimum section count. The figures above come from:
  - consensus: the IWG used "established consensus methodology" ([PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19360428/));
  - institutional practice (AJCP, Monforte et al.).
- ERNICA rates its biopsy recommendations as level of evidence III ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf)).
- A formal evidence grading for any section minimum: **n.a.**

## 2. Ganglion cells

### 2.1 Appearance and location

**Cytology**
- Classic features are ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)):
  - abundant cytoplasm;
  - an eccentric round nucleus;
  - a conspicuous nucleolus;
  - perinuclear pallor;
  - peripheral chromatin.
- Ganglion cells are usually, but not always, grouped in ganglia and/or in contact with neuropil ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Enteric glia have more condensed chromatin, oval nuclei and no easily seen cytoplasmic boundaries (Figure 4 legend in [Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

**Location in suction biopsies**
- The submucosa contains the deep Schabadasch (Henle) plexus, just inside the inner circular muscle, and the superficial Meissner plexus, just beneath the muscularis mucosae ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- The highest-yield areas are next to the muscularis mucosae and next to the muscularis propria ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- The amphophilic cytoplasm at low power is a useful clue ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Myenteric plexus**
- A network of large ganglia joined by nerve bundles. Each ganglion has ganglion cells, glia and neuropil ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- Adult colonic data on transverse sections give a mean of 4 nerve cells per myenteric ganglion. Robust normative data for H&E sections are lacking ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- Because of the size and density of myenteric ganglia, 10 or fewer serial sections of well-oriented bowel are usually enough to assess them ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Diagnostic threshold**
- A single unequivocal submucosal ganglion cell in a suction biopsy from the terminal 2 cm of rectum excludes HSCR, provided the submucosa is adequate ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- ERNICA states that "the presence of any number of ganglion cells on H&E staining excludes HSCR" ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf); [ERNICA update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jpn3.70442)).
- Ambartsumyan et al. qualify this: a ganglion cell usually excludes HSCR, *unless* transition-zone features are present, in which case very-short-segment HSCR must be considered ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Monforte et al. likewise say that ganglion cells together with nerve trunk hypertrophy or tortuosity "requires special consideration for Ultra Short or Very Short (Us/Vs) HSCR" ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- Quantifying ganglion cells in suction biopsies has limited value, apart from recording presence or absence (with the possible exception of IND) ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).

### 2.2 Immature ganglion cells (neonates, preterm infants)

**Appearance**
- Immature ganglion cells have less cytoplasm, stippled chromatin and an indistinct nucleolus. They may predominate in very young or premature infants, but "with practice, even immature ganglion cells can be identified with confidence" ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- In neonates and young children, ganglion cells are considerably smaller, with less cytoplasm, more stippled chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. They are still evidence of ganglionated bowel ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- In premature infants, immature ganglion cells are less distinctive but "similarly recognizable or confirmed". Prematurity is "important clinical information for pathologists" ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- Normal ganglion cell distribution is present by 24 weeks' gestation, but enteric ganglion cells keep differentiating during the first few years of life ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Ancillary stains to confirm ganglion cells**
- ERNICA 2020: calretinin and/or peripherin should be used to look for ganglion cells, particularly in premature infants ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf)).
- The ERNICA update changed this to "a combination of H&E and calretinin is recommended… especially in challenging samples of premature infants" ([ERNICA update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jpn3.70442)).
- For equivocal cells, Ambartsumyan et al. suggest immunostaining for neuron-specific markers (Hu C/D, Phox2b), possibly on a destained H&E section ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

**Immaturity as a diagnosis**
- Rigorous H&E criteria for diagnosing neuronal immaturity in a suction biopsy have not been developed or tested ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).

### 2.3 Pitfalls (mimics)

**Reported mimics**
- **Inflammatory and endothelial cells.** Immature ganglion cells, especially in premature infants, are harder to distinguish from inflammatory or endothelial cells ([Ambartsumyan et al., Table 2](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Cytomegalovirus.** CMV cytopathy in non-neuronal cells can closely mimic a mature ganglion cell. Viral inclusions, or CMV or neuronal immunostaining, resolve the question ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Other mimics** named by the AJCP review ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)):
  - plasma cells, particularly on frozen sections;
  - endothelial cells;
  - lymphocytes;
  - glia;
  - CMV.

  The same review notes that endothelial and perivascular cells may cluster with uniform round nuclei. Red cells in tiny lumina are a clue.
- **Mimics named by Monforte et al.** ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)):
  - tangential sections of crypt epithelium;
  - displaced glands;
  - swollen endothelial cells;
  - histiocytoid cells;
  - mesothelial cells on frozen seromuscular biopsies.
- **Histiocytes** are the only other cathepsin D-positive submucosal cells. They can be told apart from ganglion cells by their nuclei, by occurring singly, and by not being associated with nerves ([Abu-Alfa et al., Am J Surg Pathol 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9042287/)).

**Practical caution**
- If many plasma cells are near a candidate cell, or only one ganglion cell is found in many serial sections, extreme caution is advised, including sharing the case ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

## 3. Hypertrophic submucosal nerve fibres and trunks

### 3.1 Threshold and its source

**Original study**
- Monforte-Muñoz et al. 1998 studied patients aged 2 days to 3 years. Aganglionic segments contained many nerve trunks >40 µm in diameter, and ganglionic segments and biopsies showed none larger than this. They concluded that trunks ≥40 µm strongly correlate with aganglionosis ([Monforte-Muñoz et al., Arch Pathol Lab Med 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9701334/)).

**Criteria used by later sources**

| Source | Criterion | Link |
|---|---|---|
| IWG 2009 | Multiple submucosal hypertrophic nerves (>40 µm) are usually present | [IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf) |
| AJCP 2023 | ≥2 nerves ≥40 µm in one high-power field. No absolute definition exists | [AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555) |
| APSA HDIG 2019 | >2 submucosal nerves >40 µm thick in one high-power field (in the resection/transition-zone context) | [Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/) |
| Kapur 2014 | >4 nerves >30 µm per ×200 field, or >2 nerves >40 µm per ×200 field | [Kapur, Pediatr Dev Pathol 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2350/13-10-1387-OA.1) |
| Monforte et al. 2025 | Tortuous submucosal nerves ≥40 µm, measured perpendicular to the long axis, in an aganglionic biopsy are diagnostic. More than 2 are needed for confident validation. Normal submucosal nerves in infants measure 10–30 µm | [Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/) |

**Measuring without a micrometer**
- With a field number 22 ocular at ×600, the field is 367 µm across. If fewer than 9 nerve diameters fit across it, the nerve is >40 µm ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Glut1 as an aid**
- Glut1 highlights the perineurium of extrinsic nerves and helps measurement. Erythrocytes are also Glut1-positive ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/); [Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

### 3.2 Age dependence

**Sources disagree on the age limit**
- **Ambartsumyan et al.** In a neonate, a submucosal nerve >40 µm is unusual except in aganglionic HSCR bowel. Nerves >40 µm are common in toddlers and older children ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Kapur 2016.** In non-HSCR controls, nerves >40 µm are normal in the distal rectum after 1 year of age ([Kapur, J Pediatr Surg 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27364306/)).
- **AJCP 2023.** After 6 months of age, nerves ≥40 µm can be seen in normal submucosa, so the criterion is less reliable after 6 months ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- **Monforte et al. 2025.** Nerve trunk hypertrophy and tortuosity are "particularly useful in patients ≤3 years of age" ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).

**After pull-through**
- The >40 µm criterion is not a valid index of transition-zone pull-through ([Kapur 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27364306/)).

### 3.3 How often hypertrophic nerves are present in HSCR

**Qualitative statements**
- IWG: "usually present" in classical rectosigmoid disease ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- Ambartsumyan et al.: "almost always present" (Figure 6 legend); "a reliable diagnostic finding in most patients" ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- AJCP 2023: present in the majority of cases ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Quantitative data**
- Holland et al.: 81% of 27 biopsies without ganglion cells showed submucosal neural hypertrophy on S-100 ([Holland et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2010104)).
- Vincent et al. 2025 (256 patients) ([Vincent et al., Pediatr Surg Int 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40464991/)):
  - hypertrophic nerves were absent in 13 of 209 short-segment cases and 15 of 47 extended cases;
  - absence predicted aganglionosis beyond the sigmoid, with OR 6.13, sensitivity 32.6% and specificity 93.7%.

### 3.4 Settings where hypertrophic nerves may be absent

- **Total colonic aganglionosis (TCA):** hypertrophy is "paradoxically absent in some patients with TCA" ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)). It "may be absent" in total aganglionosis ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)). Suction biopsies may lack it in TCA or Down syndrome patients ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- **Long-segment disease and neonates:** the IWG says hypertrophy "may not always be the case for long-segment disease or in neonates (even in classical HSCR)" ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)). See also Vincent et al. above ([Vincent et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40464991/)).
- **Premature infants:** hypertrophy may be absent ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- **Very distal biopsies:** no fetched source gives data on whether hypertrophic nerves are absent in very distal (anal transitional) biopsies from HSCR patients (**n.a.**).
  - Related points: Ambartsumyan et al. treat the absence of hypertrophy in an aganglionic low biopsy, and in the ganglionic biopsies above it, as a reason to suspect the physiological hypoganglionic zone ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
  - Monforte et al.'s "insufficient" category includes "absent/unsampled" nerve trunk tortuosity or hypertrophy in distal specimens ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- **Transition zone:** hypertrophy usually extends into the distal transition zone, where large nerves coexist with ganglion cells ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

## 4. Calretinin immunohistochemistry

### 4.1 Staining patterns and what each means

**Normal (ganglionic) rectum**
- Small nerves in the lamina propria, muscularis mucosae and superficial submucosa contain granular aggregates of calretinin. This labelling is completely absent in aganglionic HSCR biopsies ([Kapur et al., Pediatr Dev Pathol 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18442301/)).
- Thin calretinin-positive nerves are normally present in the superficial submucosa and muscularis mucosae. In HSCR, reactivity is completely absent in these sites (Fig. 3f–g in the [IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- Calretinin labels a subset of submucosal and myenteric ganglion cells, including their cell bodies and processes ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- Mucosal nerve twigs (muscularis mucosae and lamina propria) correlate with ganglionated bowel ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- Calretinin can obscure nuclear features in some ganglion cells and stain only the cytoplasm of others ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).

**Aganglionic pattern**
- Complete absence of calretinin-positive mucosal nerves indicates aganglionic rectal tissue ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Guinard-Samuel et al. called absence of staining in the superficial submucosa and muscularis mucosae "pathognomonic" ([Guinard-Samuel et al. 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2009110)).

**Submucosal nerves are not a reliable indicator**
- Enlarged extrinsic submucosal nerves in aganglionic bowel may contain calretinin-positive fibres ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Only staining of mucosal twigs and ganglion cell bodies supports ganglionated bowel ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- Guinard-Samuel et al., however, scored as positive *any* specific staining in the submucosal nerve plexus, muscularis mucosae or lamina propria (excluding mast cells). Their one false negative was a 2 cm short-segment case with slight staining of some nerve fibres ([Guinard-Samuel et al. 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2009110)).

### 4.2 Mast cells as internal control

- Mast cells are calretinin-positive. They are round or oval with fine cytoplasmic staining, unlike the linear, dense granular profiles of mucosal nerves ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Mast cells give an internal control, but undue reliance should be avoided. Limited data exist on whether neuronal calretinin is more vulnerable to artefactual loss than mast-cell calretinin ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Nerve twigs stain granularly, whereas mast cells stain more uniformly but less intensely. Mast cells may occasionally look elongated ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- For a biopsy with absent calretinin, Ambartsumyan et al. advise ([Ambartsumyan et al., Table 2](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)):
  - check a positive control (ideally ganglionic gut on the same slide);
  - check mast cells and submucosal nerves as internal controls;
  - repeat the stain.

### 4.3 Pitfalls

**Near the dentate line or anal transitional zone**
- Mucosal calretinin nerves "may be sparse near the anorectal transitional zone" ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- In distal rectoanal transitional mucosa with at least some colorectal mucosa, calretinin in the lamina propria is informative, but only as a marginal specimen. Focal positive fibres in a distal specimen are "not conclusive and could lead to false-negative HSCR diagnosis" ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- In 17 inadequate biopsies (too little submucosa or taken within the anal transition zone), calretinin correctly predicted outcome in all ([Gonzalo & Plesec 2013](https://meridian.allenpress.com/aplm/article/137/8/1099/65503/Hirschsprung-Disease-and-Use-of-Calretinin-in)):
  - all 12 non-HSCR patients had positive nerve fibres;
  - all 5 HSCR patients had none.
- Title-only records (abstracts not retrieved, **n.a.**): an autopsy study of calretinin in the anorectal canal of young infants ([Rytting et al., Pediatr Dev Pathol 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10935266211030172)), and a multicentre study titled "Calretinin Staining in Anorectal Line Biopsies Accurately Distinguished Hirschsprung Disease" ([Logan et al., Pediatr Dev Pathol 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10935266221132602)).

**Very-short-segment disease and the transition zone**
- Calretinin-positive mucosal nerves extend into the proximal 1–2 cm of aganglionic bowel. They were present in preoperative aganglionic distal rectal biopsies from 4 of 9 patients with very-short-segment (≤2 cm) HSCR. Presence of calretinin-positive mucosal nerves therefore "does not exclude aganglionosis" ([Kapur 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2350/13-10-1387-OA.1)).
- ERNICA's update: calretinin is negative in HSCR "except within 1–2 cm of the transition zone" ([ERNICA update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jpn3.70442)).
- The AJCP review warns of the same 1–2 cm growth ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- False-negative calretinin can also come from a distal skip area ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Choline transporter immunohistochemistry is suggested where calretinin may mislead, e.g. very-short-segment HSCR ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

**Freezing**
- Freeze–thaw can cause loss of calretinin (a false positive for HSCR). Calretinin should therefore not be applied to biopsies after AChE use ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf); [AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Myenteric or intermuscular extrinsic nerves**
- These may express calretinin and do not predict ganglionated bowel ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

**Calretinin is not a stand-alone test**
- The IWG (2009) said a normal pattern argues against HSCR, "but insufficient data exists to rely on calretinin immunoreactivity alone" ([IWG, Box 1](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- Guinard-Samuel et al. advised that calretinin "should always be used in addition to limited hematoxylin and eosin sections" ([Guinard-Samuel et al. 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2009110)).

## 5. Acetylcholinesterase (AChE) histochemistry

### 5.1 Tissue requirement

- AChE needs a freshly frozen biopsy in addition to the paraffin biopsies ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- It is not compatible with formalin fixation and paraffin embedding ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- Enzyme activity is lost in routine processing ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- **Handling:**
  - The biopsy is received fresh on a moist pad and frozen in OCT, oriented for sections perpendicular to the mucosal surface ([IWG, Fig. 1](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
  - Sections of 10–15 µm are recommended, and results are most reliable at a constant 15 µm ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
  - Monforte et al. cut frozen sections at 15 µm "for standard comparable published threshold analyses" ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- Positive (known HSCR) and negative controls should be run with each biopsy ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).

### 5.2 Positive pattern

**Main descriptions**
- In aganglionic rectum, AChE-positive nerves in the muscularis mucosae, and typically also the lamina propria, are denser and thicker. The changes are quantitative and qualitative and "are not absolute" ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- IWG Figure 3 shows the two patterns ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)):
  - normal: relatively sparse, thin fibres (Fig. 3d);
  - HSCR: abundant coarse fibres in the muscularis mucosae and lamina propria (Fig. 3e).
- AChE highlights abnormally coarse, dense cholinergic mucosal innervation, probably mucosal projections from the hypertrophic submucosal nerves ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

**Other pattern descriptions**
- At least two histological patterns, types A and B, have been described ([Moore & Johnson, Pediatr Surg Int 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15759143/)).
- In Agrawal et al. ([Agrawal et al., Diagn Pathol 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4668621/)):
  - pattern A shows positive fibres in the muscularis mucosae running up into the lamina propria;
  - pattern B shows positive fibres in the muscularis mucosae and submucosa but none in the lamina propria.

### 5.3 Pitfalls

**Neonates**
- False negatives are mainly related to age. Absent AChE reaction does not exclude HSCR in neonates in the first 3 weeks after birth ([Moore & Johnson 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15759143/)).
- ERNICA: false negatives are primarily age-related, and a negative result does not reliably exclude HSCR in very young neonates ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf)).
- Increased AChE may be absent in premature infants with HD ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- Nakao et al. (459 cases, 91 neonates) reported specificity 100% and sensitivity 91%, with AChE-positive fibre proliferation increasing with age in the first year ([Nakao et al., J Pediatr Surg 2001 abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022346801360980)).

**Total colonic aganglionosis**
- Both TCA cases gave false-negative AChE; the authors describe this as a widely recognised pitfall ([Agrawal et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4668621/)).
- Guinard-Samuel et al. cite false negatives related to short or long aganglionic segments and to Down syndrome ([Guinard-Samuel et al. 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol2009110)).

**Other pitfalls reported by Agrawal et al.** ([Agrawal et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4668621/))
- False positives in haemorrhagic specimens (red-cell AChE), in colitis, and in IND.
- False negatives in ultrashort-segment HD, from poor orientation, and in inexperienced hands.

**Interpretation and proficiency**
- The method has no other laboratory use, so keeping up proficiency can be a problem. Equivocal patterns are always encountered, and most laboratories do not rely on AChE alone ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- AChE was harder for inexperienced pathologists: sensitivity 74.5% and specificity 65.4% for AChE+HE ([Beltman et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00428-022-03334-3)).
- Its use in the United States "appears to be declining" ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

### 5.4 Reporting alongside calretinin

- **IWG Box 1** lists both results per site, e.g. "Aganglionic acetylcholinesterase histochemical pattern" and "Aganglionic calretinin immunohistochemical pattern" ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
  - Box 1, Example 2 gives per-biopsy results: "Ganglion cells present and normal calretinin immunohistochemistry pattern (one biopsy)" and "…normal acetylcholinesterase histochemistry pattern (one biopsy)".
  - The IWG adds that the results should be reported "with appropriate controls".
- **Separate biopsies can disagree.** AChE is usually done on a separate biopsy, which can give discordant findings, especially in very-short-segment HSCR near the transition zone ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)). Their advice:
  - examine H&E frozen sections of the AChE tissue;
  - fix and process any residual frozen tissue.
- **ERNICA** summary statement: "In HSCR, acetylcholinesterase activity is increased, and calretinin immunohistochemistry is negative" ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf); [ERNICA update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jpn3.70442)).
- **Paraffin alternative.** Choline transporter immunohistochemistry is an FFPE alternative to AChE, but harder to interpret ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf); [AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).

## 7. Full-thickness (and incisional) rectal biopsy

### 7.1 Indications and procedure

- **Older children (>1 year).**
  - APSA HDIG: a single full-thickness biopsy is preferable, beginning 1–1.5 cm above the dentate line and extending 0.5 cm cephalad ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).
  - AJCP: a longitudinal strip, typically 1 × 0.3 cm, about 2 cm proximal to the dentate line ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
  - Monforte et al.: about 2–3 cm from the dentate line, usually 1–2 cm long ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)).
- **Can be taken lower than a suction biopsy.** A full-thickness biopsy can come from a lower level because the myenteric hypoganglionic zone extends only about 5 mm above the dentate line ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Dentate line visible.** Incisional biopsies are taken under direct view of the dentate line, so the location is precise ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Role in equivocal cases.** They help confirm or exclude very-short-segment HSCR when suction findings are equivocal ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

### 7.2 What is assessed beyond a suction biopsy

- **Both plexuses.** Full-thickness biopsies allow examination of both myenteric and submucosal plexuses ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf)).
- **What the report should state.** APSA HDIG: descriptions should state the extent to which the muscularis propria and myenteric plexus were sampled, and whether myenteric ganglia are present or absent. The gross description should give dimensions, how the specimen was divided and oriented, and whether any tissue was frozen ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).
- **Orientation and handling.** Orientation preserving mucosa–submucosa–muscularis propria/myenteric plexus relationships is important. A hand lens or dissecting microscope helps ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Often not truly full-thickness.** Biopsies thought to be full-thickness often contain only mucosa and submucosa, but are still adequate for HSCR diagnosis ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)). Specimens commonly lack full-thickness muscularis propria and the intermuscular space, and tangential sectioning can hide it ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)).
- **Whole wall.** In neuromuscular disease generally, the whole bowel wall should be examined, from surface epithelium to subserosal fat ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Transition-zone features in muscular biopsies.** Seromuscular or full-thickness biopsies may show transition-zone features ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/); [Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/)):
  - large nerves with extrinsic morphology;
  - tiny ganglia of 1–2 ganglion cells with little neuropil;
  - a "myenteric plexus aplasia"-like appearance with apposed muscle layers.
- **"No diagnostic alteration"** in a report implies that other neuropathic and myopathic findings have also been excluded ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).

### 7.3 Hypoganglionosis caveats

- **Normative data lacking.** With no robust normative data for H&E sections, quantitative diagnosis "should, however, be avoided at the current time" ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Density assessment needs rules.** Assessing ganglion cell density needs reproducible rules and age- and site-matched normative data ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Sparse submucosal ganglion cells** in a suction biopsy probably reflect a site near the dentate line. The idea that they reflect myenteric hypoganglionosis "has yet to be tested" ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Features suggesting hypoganglionosis** at a resection margin: hypertrophic nerves and large gaps between ganglia, especially flanked by tiny ganglia of 1–2 ganglion cells with almost no neural background ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).
- **Myenteric hypoganglionosis in transition-zone bowel.** AJCP defines it as rare ganglion cells, single or in doublets, with little or no neuropil or glia ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555)). APSA HDIG uses a threshold of ≥1/8 of the circumference in resections ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/)).
- **Distal rectum is normally hypoganglionic.** Cadaver studies confirm the distal-most rectum is hypoganglionic relative to more proximal colon ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).
- **Evidence gaps.** Most of those cadaver studies used full-thickness or deep forceps biopsies focused on myenteric ganglia, did not exclude transitional mucosa, or did not section exhaustively ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf)).

### 7.4 London Classification categories relevant to rectal biopsies for suspected HSCR

The categories below come from Table 1 (classification), Table 2 (criteria) and Table 3 (clinical relationships) of the [London Classification](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2010_GUT-London-Classification.pdf).

**Aganglionosis and hypoganglionosis**
- **1.1.1 Aganglionosis**
  - Assessment: qualitative/quantitative; minimum H&E or enzyme histochemistry.
  - Adjunctive tests: AChE, calretinin.
  - Findings: "complete absence of neurons"; hypertrophic submucosal extrinsic nerves.
  - Footnote: the category "can include rare cases of non-Hirschsprung disease severe hypoplastic hypoganglionosis with long interganglionic intervals (zonal aganglionosis)".
- **1.2.1 Hypoganglionosis**
  - Assessment: qualitative; minimum H&E.
  - Adjunctive tests: PGP9.5, NSE.
  - Finding: "severe reduction in ganglia and neurons".

**Associated phenotypes listed for HSCR (Table 3)**
- Aganglionosis of the rectosigmoid ± more proximal bowel.
- Phenotypes that "may be present in the transitional zone of Hirschsprung disease and correlate with persistent obstructive symptoms if incompletely resected":
  - hypoganglionosis in the transitional zone;
  - IND type B (1.3.2; >8 neurons in >20% of 25 submucosal ganglia by LDH histochemistry);
  - eosinophilic ganglionitis (1.5.2);
  - abnormal neurochemical coding (1.7).
- Also listed:
  - abnormal ICC networks (3.1);
  - muscular hyperplasia/hypertrophy (2.3).

**Neuronal immaturity**
- **1.8 "Relative immaturity of neurons"**: qualitative, H&E; adjuncts LDH and SDH.

**Age restriction on IND**
- The IWG says IND should not be diagnosed under 1 year of age ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf)).

## 8. Report elements and conclusion wording

### 8.1 Elements a report is expected to state

**IWG 2009 reporting recommendations for suction biopsies** ([IWG](http://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2009_Acta-Neuropathologica-GINMP-IWG.pdf))
1. Site of biopsy, age of patient and clinical diagnosis.
2. Each biopsy site described and diagnosed separately.
3. Microscopic description:
   - number of sections evaluated;
   - adequacy of submucosa;
   - inflammation;
   - presence or absence of ganglion cells and/or hypertrophic nerves;
   - abnormal ganglion cell cytology (immaturity, inclusions);
   - immunohistochemical and/or enzyme histochemical results with controls.
4. Diagnosis stating findings "consistent with" or "do not support" HSCR, with the relevant findings listed.
5. HSCR regarded by most practices as a "critical" result, with the date and time of communication documented.
6. Repeat biopsy recommended only when submucosa is insufficient.
7. Referral criteria (e.g. frozen tissue for AChE when HSCR cannot be confidently diagnosed or excluded).

**APSA HDIG 2019** ([Veras et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754813/))
- The report should state:
  - adequacy;
  - ganglion cells present or absent;
  - submucosal nerve hypertrophy present or absent;
  - active enterocolitis;
  - ancillary results (calretinin, other immunostains, AChE).
- "Inadequate" or "inconclusive" must be explained.
- When adequacy is in question, state the number of sections and whether the tissue was exhausted.
- Location and results for each biopsy should be documented separately.

**Ambartsumyan et al. 2020** ([Ambartsumyan et al.](https://www.singem.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/hirschsprung-diagnosi-2020.pdf))
- Minimum content:
  - location and type of each biopsy;
  - whether ganglion cells or submucosal nerve hypertrophy were found;
  - results of any ancillary staining.

**AJCP 2023** ([AJCP 2023](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/159/3/228/6960555))
- Keep diagnoses brief, with detail in a comment or synoptic table, following APSA HDIG synoptic reporting.
- Report HAEC-type histology when found.
- Their sample diagnoses and templates are in Supplemental Figure S6, which was not retrieved (**n.a.**).

**RCPath G085** ([RCPath G085](https://www.rcpath.org/static/4593f557-d75c-4ca6-9307a9d688e02a2d/1f175bdf-4934-4ace-bbde65114401944b/g085-tissue-pathways-for-gastrointestinal-and-pancreatobiliary-pathology.pdf))
- Ideally specialist pathologists report these specimens.
- In a general setting, multiple serial H&E sections will usually show ganglion cells if present, allowing HSCR to be excluded.
- "A positive diagnosis of Hirschsprung's disease requires specialist input."

**ERNICA** ([ERNICA 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13023-020-01362-3.pdf); [ERNICA update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jpn3.70442))
- Reporting by an experienced consultant histopathologist, with external consultation (an ERNICA centre / CPMS) if needed.

**Monforte et al. 2025** ([Monforte et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314976/))
- Recommend standardised structure.
- Grade and promptly notify HAEC if present.

## 10. Points where sources disagree

1. **Extent of the physiological hypoganglionic zone.**
   - Aldridge and Campbell, as reported by Ambartsumyan et al.: the terminal 0.5 cm above the dentate line.
   - IWG: 10–25 mm submucosal, age-dependent; myenteric about 5 mm.
   - AJCP: the distal 1–2 cm.
   - Gonzalo & Plesec (attributing it to the IWG): 1–2.5 cm above the squamocolumnar junction.
2. **Recommended suction biopsy level and landmark.**
   - ERNICA: ≥2 cm above the dentate line or 3 cm from the anal orifice.
   - APSA HDIG and AJCP: 1, 2 and 3 cm above the dentate line.
   - Ambartsumyan et al.: 2, 3 and 4 cm above the anal verge.
   - IWG: instrument at 3 cm above the anal valves, or 1/2/3 cm above the pectinate line.
   - Monforte et al.: 2–3 cm from the dentate line.
   - The dentate line and the anal verge are used as landmarks interchangeably.
3. **Minimum size and proportion of submucosa.**
   - IWG and ERNICA: ≥3 mm with ≥1/3 submucosa.
   - Ambartsumyan et al.: 2–3 mm with ≥50% submucosa.
   - AJCP: submucosa equal in thickness to the mucosa; <1/3 flagged.
   - Monforte et al.: submucosal depth at least the mucosal thickness (300–500 µm, ideally 1 mm), yet any submucosa mandates full review.
4. **Number of H&E sections.**
   - IWG and Ambartsumyan et al.: 50–75 initially.
   - AJCP: 15 initially, 75 in total if no ganglion cell is seen.
   - Monforte et al.: about 108 (6 slides × about 18).
   - Guinard-Samuel et al.: ≥100 for H&E alone.
   - Holland et al.: 8 levels.
   - Venugopal et al. (cited by Ambartsumyan et al.): ≤25 sufficed in controls.
   - None of the fetched sources gives a validated minimum.
5. **Does any ganglion cell exclude HSCR?**
   - ERNICA: "any number of ganglion cells … excludes HSCR".
   - IWG: a single unequivocal cell excludes HSCR if the submucosa is adequate.
   - Ambartsumyan et al. and Monforte et al.: ganglion cells together with nerve hypertrophy may be transition zone in very-short-segment HSCR.
6. **Can ancillary stains identify ganglion cells?**
   - ERNICA 2020: use calretinin and/or peripherin "to look for ganglion cells".
   - Monforte et al.: calretinin, Glut-1 and ChT "are not used to identify GCs".
   - Ambartsumyan et al.: Hu C/D and Phox2b for equivocal cells.
   - ERNICA's update dropped peripherin from the recommendation.
7. **Age limit for the >40 µm criterion.**
   - AJCP: less reliable after 6 months.
   - Kapur 2016: nerves >40 µm normal in the distal rectum after 1 year.
   - Monforte et al.: particularly useful at ≤3 years.
   - Ambartsumyan et al.: unusual in neonates, common in toddlers.
8. **Nerve hypertrophy criterion wording.**
   - IWG: multiple nerves >40 µm.
   - AJCP: ≥2 nerves ≥40 µm per HPF.
   - APSA HDIG: >2 nerves >40 µm per HPF.
   - Kapur 2014: >4 nerves >30 µm or >2 nerves >40 µm per ×200 field.
   - Monforte et al.: tortuous nerves ≥40 µm, >2 needed.
9. **How often hypertrophy is present.**
   - Ambartsumyan et al.: "almost always".
   - IWG: "usually".
   - Holland et al.: 81% on S-100.
   - Vincent et al.: absent in about 6% (13/209) of short-segment and 32% (15/47) of extended aganglionosis.
10. **Which calretinin-positive structures count as "positive".**
    - Guinard-Samuel et al.: any specific staining in submucosal nerve plexus, muscularis mucosae or lamina propria.
    - AJCP and Ambartsumyan et al.: only mucosal twigs and ganglion cell bodies, because extrinsic submucosal nerves in aganglionic bowel can be calretinin-positive.
11. **Calretinin as a stand-alone test.**
    - IWG (2009): insufficient data to rely on calretinin alone; no immunostain is standard of care.
    - Guinard-Samuel et al.: calretinin "could advantageously substitute for acetylcholinesterase", but should still be used with limited H&E.
    - ERNICA update: recommends H&E plus calretinin.
12. **Calretinin accuracy figures.**
    - Claxton meta-analysis: pooled sensitivity 98%, specificity 99%.
    - Beltman et al.: sensitivity 83.3%, specificity 98.1%.
    - Jeong et al. (cited by Beltman et al.): specificity 85.2%.
    - Holland 2011 (cited by Gonzalo & Plesec): sensitivity 100%, specificity 83%.
    - Kapur 2009 and Guinard-Samuel et al.: no false positives.
13. **Calretinin versus AChE.**
    - Kapur 2009, Guinard-Samuel et al. and Beltman et al.: calretinin equal or superior.
    - Claxton meta-analysis: AChE specificity 99%, similar to calretinin; sensitivity 94% vs 98%.
    - Jeong et al. (cited by Beltman et al.): AChE specificity 100% vs 85.2% for calretinin.
14. **Age at which AChE becomes reliable in neonates.**
    - Moore & Johnson: a negative result does not exclude HSCR in the first 3 weeks.
    - Nakao et al.: sensitivity 91% in a series including neonates.
    - ERNICA: "very young neonates" (unquantified).
15. **Calretinin in anal transitional / low biopsies.**
    - Ambartsumyan et al.: nerves may be sparse near the transitional zone.
    - Gonzalo & Plesec: calretinin predicted outcome in all 17 inadequate biopsies, including transitional-zone biopsies.
    - Monforte et al.: focal positivity in distal specimens is not conclusive.
16. **Full-thickness biopsy level.**
    - APSA HDIG: start 1–1.5 cm above the dentate line.
    - AJCP: about 2 cm.
    - Monforte et al.: 2–3 cm.
    - IWG: can be lower than suction, since the myenteric hypoganglionic zone is about 5 mm.
17. **Definition of very-short-segment disease.** Kapur 2014 defines very-short-segment HSCR as ≤2 cm of aganglionosis and describes calretinin-positive mucosal nerves extending 1–2 cm into aganglionic bowel; ERNICA's update gives the same 1–2 cm. A single agreed definition of ultrashort versus very-short-segment disease across the sources was not confirmed (**n.a.**).
