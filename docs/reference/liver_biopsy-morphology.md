# Medical (non-neoplastic) liver needle core biopsy: morphology reference for a diagnostic reporting template

Prepared 30 September 2026. Scope: percutaneous and transjugular needle core biopsies of non-neoplastic liver.

Conventions used in this document:
- Every statement, number and definition carries a link to the page it came from.
- Text in quotation marks is copied verbatim from the linked source, so that tier definitions can be coded as arithmetic checks.
- "n.a." means the value could not be confirmed from a source read for this report. No such value has been filled in from memory.
- Where sources disagree, both readings are shown and flagged **[DISAGREEMENT]**.
- PathologyOutlines was used only as a pointer. It is not the sole source for any statement.

## 1. Adequacy

### 1.1 Size thresholds

| Source | Length | Portal tracts | Needle / passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [RCPath G064 tissue pathway (2020)](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf) | "good: total core length >20 mm; compromised: total core length 10−20 mm; inadequate: total core length <10 mm" | For trials, "at least 20 mm in length and contain more than ten portal tracts per section" | 16 G, a diameter of about 0.8–1 mm |
| [BSG/RCR/RCPath guideline, Gut 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382) | "the specimen should be obtained with a 16 G needle and measure >20 mm" | "an adequate biopsy being 20 mm in length and including >10 portal tracts, inadequate <10 mm and <6 portal tracts, with intermediate samples described as 'compromised'" | Transjugular: "at least two biopsies are taken"; "four passes are required for optimal specimens" |
| [AASLD position paper 2009 (Rockey)](https://www.aasld.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Liver%20Biopsy%202009.pdf) | "a biopsy of at least 2-3 cm in length and 16-gauge in caliber is recommended"; "an ideal size is 3 cm long after formalin fixation obtained with a 16 gauge needle" | "the presence of fewer than 11 complete portal tracts be noted in the pathology report" | Transvenous: "3 cores be obtained with the typical 19-gauge needles" |
| [AASLD Pathology Pearls, liver biopsy interpretation](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/liver-biopsy-importance-and-interpretation) | "biopsies at least 1.5 cm to 2.5 cm in length allow for better interpretation" | "the results are conflicting and range from 6 - 11 portal tracts" | n.a. |
| [IJMS 2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/) (Figure 3 legend) | "approximately 2 cm in length" | "include 11 to 15 portal tracts" | n.a. |
| [Krishna, Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087941/) | 20 mm | more than 10 portal tracts | n.a. |

- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The thresholds differ between sources:
  - The UK sources set adequacy at >20 mm with >10 portal tracts ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf); [BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382)).
  - AASLD recommends 2–3 cm, with 3 cm as the ideal, and asks that fewer than 11 complete portal tracts be recorded ([AASLD 2009](https://www.aasld.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Liver%20Biopsy%202009.pdf)).
  - The IJMS review gives about 2 cm with 11–15 portal tracts ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
  - The AASLD Pearls page gives 6–11 portal tracts as the published range ([AASLD Pearls](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/liver-biopsy-importance-and-interpretation)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The BSG guideline records that "In 2014, the Royal College of Pathologists updated guidelines suggested minimum adequacy requirement for samples to be at least 10 mm long and containing six portal tracts", which is lower than the 2020 RCPath "good" tier ([BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The number of transjugular cores differs: at least two biopsies with four passes as optimal ([BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382)), versus 3 cores ([AASLD 2009](https://www.aasld.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Liver%20Biopsy%202009.pdf)).

### 1.2 Why size matters

- "Good evidence shows that a biopsy containing 10 or fewer portal tracts results in underestimation of both the severity of the fibrosis stage and of the inflammatory grade in chronic viral hepatitis" ([BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382)).
- "The number of portal tracts in a given length of biopsy is variable and cannot be predicted from the biopsy length" ([BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382)).
- Short specimens "may lead to a failure to recognize cirrhosis in up to 20% of cases" ([AASLD 2009](https://www.aasld.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Liver%20Biopsy%202009.pdf)).
- Fixation shrank 16-gauge cores from 19.6 ± 3.5 mm to 18.3 ± 2.9 mm ([AASLD 2009](https://www.aasld.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Liver%20Biopsy%202009.pdf)).
- "Adequate biopsy size is particularly important in assessing chronic biliary disease" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- The subcapsular 2 mm zone "may contain fibrotic portals with bridging bands, potentially mimicking cirrhosis in superficial samples" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

### 1.3 What the report should state

- Report "Biopsy size/adequacy—expressed as length of biopsy core (on receipt in the department or on the tissue sections), number of fragments and an approximate number of portal tracts per section" ([BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382); same wording in [RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- If the sample is small, "the pathologist should state in their report that the biopsy is too small to provide a full assessment of the liver disease" ([BSG 2020](https://gut.bmj.com/content/69/8/1382)).
- RCPath also asks for the following ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)):
  - relevant negative findings ("lack of iron overload or α1-AT globules");
  - "A concise, single-line summary to conclude the report";
  - a comparison with previous biopsies.
- "the unqualified diagnosis 'chronic hepatitis' as a morphological description for any biopsy with unexplained portal inflammation should be avoided" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- "the pathologist should not simply report a biopsy as 'consistent with' a proposed clinical diagnosis without considering alternative diagnoses" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Grade and stage can be given "either by descriptive text or using a semi-quantitative scoring system, as agreed locally". Trial systems ("Ishak for chronic viral hepatitis, Kleiner for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease") "are poorly reproducible in routine practice" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** Krishna argues that simpler systems such as Batts–Ludwig and METAVIR are sufficient for routine use ([Krishna 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087941/)). RCPath accepts descriptive text in place of any score ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

### 1.4 Routine stain panel

- RCPath lists H&E at two or more levels, reticulin, a collagen stain (van Gieson, Masson trichrome or picrosirius red), orcein (or Victoria blue), PAS ± diastase and Perls, with rhodanine as required ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- The IJMS review states: "In most laboratories, two H&E-stained levels, an iron stain, and a trichrome stain constitute the core panel for medical liver biopsies ... At minimum, every biopsy should receive H&E and a fibrosis stain" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

## 2. Scoring systems

### 2a. Ishak (modified HAI), 1995

The tables below are reproduced from [Brunt 2000 (Hepatology review, which reproduces the Ishak 1995 tables)](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf). Component ranges are confirmed by [Krishna 2021, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087941/) (interface 0–4, confluent 0–6, focal 0–4, portal 0–4, total 0–18, fibrosis 0–6).

**A. Periportal or periseptal interface hepatitis (piecemeal necrosis)** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent |
| 1 | Mild (focal, few portal areas) |
| 2 | Mild/moderate (focal, most portal areas) |
| 3 | Moderate (continuous around <50% of tracts or septa) |
| 4 | Severe (continuous around >50% of tracts or septa) |

**B. Confluent necrosis** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent |
| 1 | Focal confluent necrosis |
| 2 | Zone 3 necrosis in some areas |
| 3 | Zone 3 necrosis in most areas |
| 4 | Zone 3 necrosis + occasional portal-central (P-C) bridging |
| 5 | Zone 3 necrosis + multiple P-C bridging |
| 6 | Panacinar or multiacinar necrosis |

**C. Focal (spotty) lytic necrosis, apoptosis and focal inflammation** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

The source footnote says this item excludes diffuse sinusoidal infiltration ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf)).

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent |
| 1 | One focus or less per 10× objective |
| 2 | Two to four foci per 10× objective |
| 3 | Five to ten foci per 10× objective |
| 4 | More than ten foci per 10× objective |

**D. Portal inflammation** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | None |
| 1 | Mild, some or all portal areas |
| 2 | Moderate, some or all portal areas |
| 3 | Moderate/marked, all portal areas |
| 4 | Marked, all portal areas |

**Derivation rule:** modified HAI grade = A + B + C + D, with a maximum of 18 ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf); [Krishna 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087941/)). A secondary source notes that "Use of the X10 objective for the evaluation of necroinflammatory foci raises concerns of reproducibility" ([IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773), secondary).

**Ishak stage (fibrosis)** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | No fibrosis |
| 1 | Fibrous expansion of some portal areas, with or without short fibrous septa |
| 2 | Fibrous expansion of most portal areas, with or without short fibrous septa |
| 3 | Fibrous expansion of most portal areas with occasional portal to portal (P-P) bridging |
| 4 | Fibrous expansion of portal areas with marked bridging (P-P) as well as portal-central (P-C) |
| 5 | Marked bridging (P-P and/or P-C) with occasional nodules (incomplete cirrhosis) |
| 6 | Cirrhosis, probable or definite |

### 2b. METAVIR (Bedossa 1996)

- The algorithm grades activity from piecemeal necrosis and lobular necrosis only ([Bedossa 1996 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8690394/)).
- Portal inflammation was excluded because "this feature is a prerequisite for the definition of chronic hepatitis even without activity" ([IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773)).

**Piecemeal necrosis (PN)**

- Per [IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773): "0, absent; 1, focal alteration of the periportal plate in some portal tracts; 2, diffuse alteration of the periportal plate in some portal tracts or focal lesion around all portal tracts; 3, diffuse alteration of the periportal plate in all portal tracts".
- The labels in [Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf) are 0 none, 1 mild, 2 moderate, 3 severe.

**Lobular necrosis (LN)**

- Per [IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773): "0, less than one necroinflammatory foci per lobule; 1, at least one necroinflammatory foci per lobule; 2, several necroinflammatory foci per lobule or confluent or bridging necrosis".
- The labels in [Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf) are "0 (none or mild)", "1 (moderate)", "2 (severe)".
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The two sources word LN differently: one uses a count of foci per lobule, the other labels severity.

**Activity grade:** A0 no activity, A1 mild, A2 moderate, A3 severe ([IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773); [Krishna 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087941/)).

**PN × LN → A derivation table.** This table is read from the text layer of the algorithm table in [Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf), where the extracted columns are interleaved.

| PN | LN | A |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | A0 |
| 0 | 1 | A1 (see caveat) |
| 0 | 2 | A2 |
| 1 | 0 or 1 | A1 |
| 1 | 2 | A2 |
| 2 | 0 or 1 | A2 |
| 2 | 2 | A3 (see caveat) |
| 3 | 0, 1 or 2 | A3 |

- **Caveat:** an automated extraction of the same PDF returned A0 for PN0/LN1 and A2 for PN2/LN2 ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf)).
- The primary paper's full text ([Bedossa 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8690394/)) could not be read. Those two cells should therefore be treated as n.a. until checked against the original Table.

**Fibrosis (F)**

- Per [IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773): "F0, no fibrosis; F1, portal fibrosis without septa; F2, portal fibrosis with rare septa; F3 numerous septa without cirrhosis; F4, cirrhosis".
- Per [Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf): F1 "Stellate enlargement of portal tract but without septa formation"; F2 "Enlargement of portal tract with rare septa formation"; F3 "Numerous septa without cirrhosis"; F4 "Cirrhosis".
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** F1 and F2 are worded differently in the two sources.
- ≥F2 is "significant fibrosis" and ≥F3 is "advanced fibrosis" ([Chowdhury & Mehta 2022](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/169371963/Chowdhury_Mehta2022_Article_LiverBiopsyForAssessmentOfChro.pdf)).

**Crosswalk to Ishak**

- **[DISAGREEMENT]** Goodman 2007 places Ishak stage 5 in F4, whereas the METAVIR group's correspondence differs ([IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/18773)).
- Krishna's crosswalk maps Batts–Ludwig grades 1 and 2 both to A1 ([Krishna 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087941/)).

### 2c. Batts–Ludwig (1995) and Scheuer (1991)

**Batts–Ludwig reporting form**

The report should give aetiology, grade and stage, for example "autoimmune hepatitis, severe, stage 3" ([Batts & Ludwig 1995 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7503362/)).

**Batts–Ludwig grade** ([AASLD Pathology Pearls post 5](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-post-5-chronic-viral-hepatitis))

| Grade | Semiquantitative | Piecemeal necrosis | Lobular inflammation and necrosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Portal inflammation only, no activity | None | None |
| 1 | Minimal | Minimal, patchy | Minimal, occasional spotty necrosis |
| 2 | Mild | Mild, involving some or all portal tracts | Mild, little hepatocellular damage |
| 3 | Moderate | Moderate, involving all portal tracts | Moderate, with noticeable hepatocellular change |
| 4 | Severe | Severe, may have bridging fibrosis | Severe, with prominent diffuse hepatocellular damage |

**Batts–Ludwig stage** ([AASLD Pathology Pearls post 5](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-post-5-chronic-viral-hepatitis); also [PMC4721971](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721971/))

| Stage | Term | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No fibrosis | Normal connective tissue |
| 1 | Portal fibrosis | Fibrous portal expansion |
| 2 | Periportal fibrosis | Periportal or rare portal-portal septa |
| 3 | Septal fibrosis | Fibrous septa with architectural distortion; no obvious cirrhosis |
| 4 | Cirrhosis | Cirrhosis |

**Scheuer grade** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

| Grade | Portal/periportal activity | Lobular activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None or minimal | None |
| 1 | Portal inflammation (CPH) | Inflammation but no necrosis |
| 2 | Mild piecemeal necrosis | Focal necrosis or acidophil bodies |
| 3 | Moderate piecemeal necrosis | Severe focal cell damage |
| 4 | Severe piecemeal necrosis | Damage includes bridging necrosis |

- The rule for combining the two columns into a single grade is n.a. from the source read.

**Scheuer stage** ([Brunt 2000](https://www.natap.org/2003/feb/grading.pdf))

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | None |
| 1 | Enlarged, fibrotic portal tracts |
| 2 | Periportal or portal-portal septa but intact architecture |
| 3 | Fibrosis with architectural distortion but no obvious cirrhosis |
| 4 | Probable or definite cirrhosis |

**RCPath descriptive fibrosis tiers**

These may be used instead of a numerical score ([RCPath G064, Appendix A](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)):
- "fibrosis without bridging – mild/early stage fibrosis"
- "bridging fibrosis without nodules – moderate/intermediate fibrosis"
- "bridging fibrosis with nodules, appearance of early or established cirrhosis – severe or late-stage fibrosis"

### 2d. NASH CRN (Kleiner 2005)

Source for this section: [Kleiner 2005, Hepatology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701).

| Feature | Score: definition |
|---|---|
| Steatosis | 0: <5%; 1: 5%-33%; 2: >33%-66%; 3: >66% |
| Lobular inflammation | 0: no foci; 1: <2 foci per 200× field; 2: 2-4 foci per 200× field; 3: >4 foci per 200× field |
| Hepatocyte ballooning | 0: none; 1: few balloon cells; 2: many cells/prominent ballooning |
| Fibrosis stage | 0: none; 1: perisinusoidal or periportal; 1A: mild, zone 3, perisinusoidal; 1B: moderate, zone 3, perisinusoidal; 1C: portal/periportal; 2: perisinusoidal and portal/periportal; 3: bridging fibrosis; 4: cirrhosis |

**Derivation rule**
- NAS = steatosis (0–3) + lobular inflammation (0–3) + ballooning (0–2), giving a range of 0–8 ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).
- Fibrosis is not part of the NAS ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).
- In the validation set, NAS ≥5 correlated with a diagnosis of NASH and NAS <3 with "not NASH", while 3–4 was split ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).

**Intended use**
- "it is not intended that numeric values replace the pathologist's diagnostic determination of steatohepatitis" ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).
- The score was built on H&E and Masson trichrome only ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).
- The SAF authors state that the NAS "should not be used for diagnostic purposes" ([Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889)).

**[DISAGREEMENT]** The Kleiner 2005 abstract gives "lobular inflammation (0-2)" and "fibrosis (0-4)". The paper's table and NAS text give lobular inflammation as 0–3 ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).

### 2e. SAF / FLIP, and MASLD / MASH / MetALD terminology

**SAF (Bedossa 2012)** ([Bedossa 2012, Hepatology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889))

- **Steatosis (S):** S0 <5%; S1 5%-33% (mild); S2 34%-66% (moderate); S3 >67% (marked). Only large and medium droplets are counted, not foamy microvesicles.
- **Ballooning:**
  - 0: "normal hepatocytes with cuboidal shape and pink eosinophilic cytoplasm".
  - 1: "clusters of rounded pale, usually reticulated cells of similar size" to normal hepatocytes.
  - 2: as grade 1, with some enlarged hepatocytes "at least 2-fold" normal size.
- **Lobular inflammation:** a focus is ≥2 inflammatory cells, counted at 20×. 0: none; 1: ≤2 foci per 20× field; 2: >2 foci per 20× field.
- **Activity (A):** ballooning + lobular inflammation, range 0–4. A0 none, A1 mild, A2 moderate, A3 (A ≥3) severe.
- **Fibrosis (F):** as NASH CRN. F1 covers 1a/1b/1c; F2 is perisinusoidal plus periportal without bridging; F3 bridging; F4 cirrhosis.
- **Derivations:**
  - NASH = steatosis ≥1 AND ballooning ≥1 AND lobular inflammation ≥1.
  - NAFLD = S≥1, A any, F any.
  - Significant disease = A≥2 and/or F≥2.

**FLIP (Bedossa 2014)** ([Bedossa 2014, Hepatology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.27173))

- Ballooning 2: "at least one enlarged ballooned hepatocyte (at least 2-fold...)".
- Lobular inflammation foci are "organized either as microgranulomas or located within the sinusoids", counted at 20×: "1: < 2 foci per lobule; 2: >2 foci per lobule".
- Activity ranges A0–A4.
- The algorithm requires steatosis ≥1 to enter. "At least grade 1 of each of the three" features (steatosis, ballooning, lobular inflammation) = NASH; otherwise the diagnosis is steatosis.
- Severity: "mild disease (A<2 and F<2) and significant disease (A>2 and/or F>2)".
- F1a/b/c were pooled for kappa analysis.

**[DISAGREEMENT] SAF 2012 versus FLIP 2014 and NASH CRN**
- Steatosis cut-offs: SAF bands are 34–66% (S2) and >67% (S3); NASH CRN bands are >33–66% and >66% ([Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889); [Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).
- Lobular inflammation:
  - SAF 2012 uses "≤2 / >2 foci per 20×" ([Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889)).
  - FLIP 2014 uses "< 2 / >2 foci per lobule" ([Bedossa 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.27173)), which leaves exactly 2 undefined.
  - NASH CRN uses a 0–3 scale per 200× field ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701)).
- Significant disease: A≥2 and/or F≥2 in 2012 ([Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889)), versus the 2014 text "A>2 and/or F>2" ([Bedossa 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.27173)).
- Number of algorithm routes: 10 in 2012 versus 9 in 2014 ([Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889); [Bedossa 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.27173)).
- Activity categories: the A score runs 0–4, but the grade categories stop at A3 (A ≥3 = severe) ([Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889)).

**Morphology definitions** ([Leow et al., Clin Mol Hepatol 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf); [IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/); [RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf))

- **Ballooning** is "an enlarged hepatocyte with rarefied pale cytoplasm, usually with the presence of a large, hyperchromatic nucleus and a prominent nucleolus" ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)). The same review calls it "the sine qua non of steatohepatitis for practical purposes" ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).
- **Fibrosis** "is typically located in zone 3 with a perivenular and perisinusoidal pattern", described as a "chicken-wire pattern" ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)). Paediatric NASH more often shows periportal fibrosis ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).
- **Steatosis threshold:** "more than 5% in the parenchymal area" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)). Leow grades by "5–33%, 33–66%, or more than 66% of the hepatocytes" ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The denominator differs: parenchymal area in RCPath versus the percentage of hepatocytes in Leow ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf); [Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).
- "Cells with multiple or smaller droplets remain categorized as macrovesicular" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- Histology "is usually unable to distinguish" alcoholic from non-alcoholic causes ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**MASLD nomenclature (2023 multisociety Delphi)** ([Rinella et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10653297/))

- SLD (steatotic liver disease) is the umbrella term.
- MASLD = hepatic steatosis plus ≥1 of 5 cardiometabolic risk factors and no other cause.
- MASH replaces NASH. "The term steatohepatitis was felt to be an important pathophysiological concept that should be retained".
- MetALD = MASLD with alcohol intake of 140–350 g/week (female) or 210–420 g/week (male), which averages 20–50 g/day (F) and 30–60 g/day (M). **[DISAGREEMENT]** A figure footnote in the same paper reads "140–30 g" for females.
- Other categories include cryptogenic SLD and "possible MASLD" ([Rinella 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10653297/)).
- "the current consensus process does not deviate from prior case definitions for steatohepatitis and disease stages" ([Rinella 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10653297/)). The histological definitions of NASH CRN/SAF therefore carry over unchanged to MASH ([J Lipid Res 2024 commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10824973/)).
- MASLD can coexist with other liver diseases such as AIH or viral hepatitis ([Rinella 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10653297/)).

**How pathology reports should word MASLD**
- No pathology-body guidance (RCPath, CAP or ICCR) on MASLD report wording was found: n.a.
- The following practical implications follow from the cited definitions:
  - "MASLD" and "MetALD" require cardiometabolic and alcohol data that are not visible histologically ([Rinella 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10653297/)).
  - "steatohepatitis" remains the histological term ([Rinella 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10653297/)).
  - Histology cannot separate alcohol-related from metabolic steatohepatitis ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- A defensible template therefore reports a morphological diagnosis (for example "steatohepatitis" or "steatosis without steatohepatitis") with grade and stage. It uses "MASH/MASLD" only when clinical criteria are supplied, and otherwise states "clinicopathological correlation for metabolic risk factors and alcohol intake is required". This is a synthesis, not a quoted recommendation.

### 2f. Banff rejection activity index (RAI) and global assessment, including the 2016 update

**Global assessment, Banff 1997** ([Banff 1997 schema, UPMC TPIS](https://tpis.upmc.com/tpislibrary/schema/BanffRej.html); [Banff 1997 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9049215/))

| Global assessment | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Indeterminate | "Portal inflammatory infiltrate that fails to meet the criteria for the diagnosis of acute rejection" |
| Mild (grade I) | "Rejection infiltrate in a minority of the triads, that is generally mild, and confined within the portal spaces" |
| Moderate (grade II) | "Rejection infiltrate, expanding most or all of the triads" |
| Severe (grade III) | "As above for moderate, with spillover into periportal areas and moderate to severe perivenular inflammation that extends into the hepatic parenchyma and is associated with perivenular hepatocyte necrosis" |

**Global assessment, Banff 2016 update, Table 1** ([Demetris et al., AJT 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909))

| Global assessment | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Indeterminate | "Portal and/or perivenular inflammatory infiltrate that is related to an alloreaction, but shows insufficient tissue damage to meet criteria for a diagnosis of mild acute rejection" |
| Mild | "Rejection-type infiltrate in a minority of the triads or perivenular areas, that is generally mild, and mostly confined within the portal spaces for portal-based rejection and an absence of confluent necrosis/hepatocyte dropout for those presenting with isolated perivenular infiltrates" |
| Moderate | "Rejection-type infiltrate, expanding most or all of portal tracts and/or perivenular areas with confluent necrosis/hepatocyte dropout limited to a minority of perivenular areas" |
| Severe | "As above for moderate, with spillover into periportal areas and/or moderate-to-severe perivenular inflammation that extends into the hepatic parenchyma and is associated with perivenular hepatocyte necrosis involving a majority of perivenular areas" |

**RAI components, Banff 2016, Table 1** ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909))

| Category | Score | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Portal inflammation | 1 | "Mostly lymphocytic inflammation involving, but not noticeably expanding, a minority of the triads." |
| | 2 | "Expansion of most or all of the triads, by a mixed infiltrate containing lymphocytes with occasional blasts, neutrophils, and eosinophils. If eosinophils are conspicuous and accompanied by edema and microvascular endothelial cell hypertrophy is prominent, acute antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) should be considered." |
| | 3 | "Marked expansion of most or all of the triads by a mixed infiltrate containing blasts and eosinophils with inflammatory spillover into the periportal parenchyma" |
| Bile duct inflammation damage | 1 | "A minority of the ducts are cuffed and infiltrated by inflammatory cells and show only mild reactive changes such as increased nuclear:cytoplasmic ratio of the epithelial cells." |
| | 2 | "Most or all of the ducts infiltrated by inflammatory cells. More than an occasional duct shows degenerative changes such as nuclear pleomorphism, disordered polarity, and cytoplasmic vacuolization of the epithelium." |
| | 3 | "As above for 2, with most or all of the ducts showing degenerative changes or focal luminal disruption" |
| Venous endothelial inflammation | 1 | "Subendothelial lymphocytic infiltration involving some, but not a majority of the portal and/or hepatic venules" |
| | 2 | "Subendothelial infiltration involving most or all of the portal and/or hepatic venules with or without confluent hepatocyte necrosis/dropout involving a minority of perivenular regions." |
| | 3 | "As above for 2, with moderate or severe perivenular inflammation that extends into the perivenular parenchyma and is associated with perivenular hepatocyte necrosis involving a majority of perivenular regions." |

**Derivation rule**
- Total RAI = portal inflammation + bile duct damage + venous endothelial inflammation, with a maximum of 9 ([Can J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2023/3103335); [Rev Colomb Gastroenterol](http://www.scielo.org.co/pdf/rcg/v31n2/en_v31n2a11.pdf)).
- A score-0 definition for each component is n.a. in Table 1 as read.

**RAI to global grade mapping**
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** Published mappings differ:
  - The 2023 study uses "1-2 = indeterminate, 3-5 = mild, 6-7 = moderate, and 8-9 = severe (slightly modified from original groupings proposed in Banff 1997 by adding score 3...)" ([Can J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2023/3103335)).
  - A Colombian review gives no rejection 0–1, indeterminate 2–3, mild 3–4, moderate 5–7, severe 8–9. These ranges overlap at 3 ([Rev Colomb Gastroenterol](http://www.scielo.org.co/pdf/rcg/v31n2/en_v31n2a11.pdf)).
- The original 1997 mapping from the primary paper is n.a. The 2016 Table 1 does not give a numerical mapping ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
- For coding, the global assessment should be the primary field and RAI a separate numeric field. Do not auto-derive grade from RAI.

**Other 2016 changes** ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909))
- "T cell-mediated rejection (TCMR)" replaces "acute cellular rejection".
- Criteria for acute and chronic AMR, including C4d, are added.
- "Plasma cell-rich rejection" replaces "de novo autoimmune hepatitis". It is characterised by >30% plasma cells and usually RAI ≥5.
- Late chronic rejection includes duct loss in ≥50% of portal tracts.
- Fibrosis is scored in three compartments, with a total of 9.
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** Compared with 1997, the 2016 global assessment adds isolated perivenular (central perivenulitis) infiltrates and confluent necrosis to the tier criteria ([Banff 1997 schema](https://tpis.upmc.com/tpislibrary/schema/BanffRej.html); [Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).

| Item | Score | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Fibrosis | 0 | "No portal fibrosis, or fibrosis limited to portal tracts" |
| | 1 | "Portal fibrosis with periportal fibrosis or incomplete septal fibrosis" |
| | 2 | "Bridging fibrosis with variable lobular disarray" |
| | 3 | "Liver cirrhosis with regenerative nodules and extensive fibrosis" |
| Bile duct loss | 0 | No bile duct loss |
| | 1 | Bile duct loss in <1/3 of portal tracts |
| | 2 | Bile duct loss in 1/3–2/3 of portal tracts |

| | 3 | Deposition in "many hepatocytes in >2/3 of portal tracts" |

**Derivation rule** ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x); confirmed by [de Vries 2015](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/21700040/deVries_et_al_Applicability_prognostic_value_Journal_Hepatology_2015.pdf))

|---|---|---|

| Stage 2 (mild progression) | 1–3 | 1–2 |
| Stage 3 (moderate progression) | 4–6 | 3–4 |
| Stage 4 (advanced progression) | 7–9 | 5–6 |

- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The orcein definitions in the text are worded slightly differently from the table ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x)).

**Nakanuma necroinflammatory grading: original (Table 3)** ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x))

Cholangitis activity (CA):
- CA0: "No cholangitis, but mild duct epithelial damage may be present".
- CA1: chronic cholangitis in <1/3 of portal tracts.
- CA2: chronic cholangitis in 1/3–2/3 of portal tracts.
- CA3: chronic cholangitis in >2/3 of portal tracts.

Hepatitis activity (HA):
- HA0: "No interface hepatitis, and no or minimum lobular hepatitis".
- HA1: "Focal interface hepatitis in a few portal tract(s), and focal necrosis in the parenchyma".
- HA2: "Moderate interface hepatitis in several portal tracts, and variable lobular hepatitis".
- HA3: "Moderate–marked interface hepatitis in many portal tracts, or bridging or zonal necrosis, or both".

**Nakanuma necroinflammatory grading: revised (Table 4)** ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x))

Cholangitis activity (CA):
- CA1: "One evident chronic cholangitis in the specimen".
- CA2: "More than two bile ducts with evident chronic cholangitis".
- CA3: "At least one CNSDC in the specimen" (CNSDC = chronic non-suppurative destructive cholangitis, which includes granulomatous cholangitis).

Hepatitis activity (HA):
- HA1: "Interface hepatitis affecting 10 continuous hepatocytes in one portal tract or fibrous septa, and mild–moderate lobular hepatitis".
- HA2: "...10 continuous hepatocytes in more than two portal tracts or fibrous septa, and mild–moderate lobular hepatitis".
- HA3: "Interface hepatitis affecting 20 continuous hepatocytes in more than half of the portal tracts, and moderate lobular hepatitis, or bridging or zonal necrosis".

- **[DISAGREEMENT]** Tables 3 and 4 give two different CA/HA definitions. In addition, the running text uses ">20" and "many", where the Table 4 HA3 row says "20" and "more than half" ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x)).
- Revised CA2 says "More than two" ducts, which leaves exactly two ducts undefined between CA1 and CA2.

**Ludwig PBC stages** ([Korean PBC guideline 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10037524/))
- I: "Portal inflammation with bile duct damage with or without florid duct lesion".
- II: "Periportal inflammation, ductular reaction (periportal fibrosis often present)".
- III: "Bridging fibrosis (ductopenia usually present)".
- IV: "Biliary cirrhosis with regenerative nodules".

**Scheuer PBC stages** ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x))
- 1: florid duct lesions (CNSDC).
- 2: ductular proliferation.
- 3: fibrosis or scarring.
- 4: cirrhosis.

EASL uses both the Ludwig and the Scheuer four-stage systems and describes Nakanuma as a summed system ([EASL PBC guideline 2017](https://www.sigeitalia.it/multimedia/files/attivita-scientifica/linee-guida/epatologia/colangite-biliare-primitiva/easl-guidelines-pbc-2017.pdf)).

**Application to PSC**
- Sources do apply Nakanuma to PSC:
  - "[The Nakanuma system] was applied to PSC, where it showed a strong association with prognosis" ([Cazzagon et al., Tomography 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10820917/)).
  - de Vries found that "Applying the Nakanuma, Ishak, and Ludwig histopathological staging systems is feasible and clinically relevant given their association with transplant-free survival and time to LTx" ([de Vries et al., J Hepatol 2015](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/21700040/deVries_et_al_Applicability_prognostic_value_Journal_Hepatology_2015.pdf)).
- The same study reported that "Ishak and Nakanuma grading systems were not associated with CRS" (cirrhosis-related symptoms). de Vries regrouped scores for its analysis ([de Vries 2015](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/21700040/deVries_et_al_Applicability_prognostic_value_Journal_Hepatology_2015.pdf)).
- No source read states that Nakanuma is formally validated or recommended by a guideline body for routine PSC reporting: n.a.

### 2h. AIH: simplified IAIHG 2008 and IAIHG 2022 histology criteria

**Simplified criteria (Hennes 2008)** ([Hennes et al., Hepatology 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.22322))

Histology tiers:
- **Typical:** "Interface hepatitis, lymphocytic/lymphoplasmocytic infiltrates in portal tracts and extending into the lobule, emperipolesis (active penetration by one cell into and through a larger cell), and hepatic rosette formation were regarded as typical ... To be considered typical, each of the three features of typical AIH histology had to be present."
- **Compatible:** "a picture of chronic hepatitis with lymphocytic infiltration without all the features considered typical".
- **Atypical:** "showing signs of another diagnosis, such as steatohepatitis".

Points:

| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| Histology | Atypical 0; compatible 1; typical 2 |
| Autoantibodies (maximum 2) | ANA or SMA ≥1:40 = 1; ANA or SMA ≥1:80, or LKM ≥1:40, or SLA positive = 2 |
| IgG | >ULN = 1; >1.10 × ULN = 2 |
| Absence of viral hepatitis | 2 |

Derivation: total ≥6 = probable AIH; ≥7 = definite AIH ([Hennes 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.22322)). Histological evidence of hepatitis is a precondition ([Hennes 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.22322)).

- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The paper says "each of the three features" but lists four ([Hennes 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.22322)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The abstract states "greater than 6" for probable AIH, while the table gives ≥6 ([Hennes 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.22322)).

**IAIHG 2022 consensus, Table 5** ([Lohse et al., Liver Int 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf))

Portal hepatitis pattern:

| Likelihood | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Likely AIH | "Portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate PLUS one or both of: 1. more than mild interface hepatitis 2. more than mild lobular inflammation • in the absence of histological features suggestive of another liver disease" |
| Possible AIH | "Portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate • without either of the likely features 1 or 2 above • in the absence of [histological features suggestive of another liver disease]; OR • with one or both of likely features above • in the presence of histological features suggestive of another liver disease" |
| Unlikely AIH | "Portal hepatitis • without either of the likely features above • in the presence of histological features suggestive of another liver disease" |

Lobular hepatitis pattern:

| Likelihood | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Likely AIH | "More than mild lobular hepatitis (+/− centrilobular necroinflammation) PLUS at least one of: 1. lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates 2. interface hepatitis 3. portal-based fibrosis • in the absence [of histological features suggestive of another liver disease]" |
| Possible AIH | "Any lobular hepatitis (+/− centrilobular necroinflammation) • without any of the likely features 1–3 above • in the absence [...]; OR • with any of the likely features above • in the presence of [...]" |
| Unlikely AIH | "Any lobular hepatitis • without any of the likely features above • in the presence of [...]" |

Other 2022 statements ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)):
- "(sub-)massive hepatic necrosis" is classed as possible AIH.
- A plasma cell cluster is ≥5 plasma cells in one focus.
- There are no pathognomonic features.
- Emperipolesis ("intact lymphocyte within the cytoplasm of a hepatocyte") and rosettes ("small group of hepatocytes arranged around a small, occasionally visible, central lumen") "should be discarded as a diagnostic feature".
- The term "atypical" was rated inappropriate.
- Grading uses mHAI items A, B and C; item D "should not be included".
- "Mild" = A ≤1, B = 0 and C ≤2.
- Collagen plus elastic stains distinguish collapse from fibrosis.

They are discarded in 2022 ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)).

### 2i. Iron grading and distribution

**Scheuer-type grading as modified by Searle** (Table 1 in [Deugnier & Turlin, WJG 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/))

| Grade | Ease of observation and magnification required |
|---|---|
| 0 | "Granules absent or barely discernible at × 400" |
| 1 | "Granules barely discernible at × 250 and easily confirmed at × 250" (the second magnification appears to be a transcription error in the source; the correct value is n.a.) |
| 2 | "Discrete granules resolved at × 100" |
| 3 | "Discrete granules resolved at × 25" |
| 4 | "Masses visible at × 10, or naked eye" |

- The Scheuer system "is widely used because it is simple ... However, it was not satisfactorily validated" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** A 2021 review describes Scheuer as scoring hepatocellular iron "on a scale of 1 to 4, ranging from minimal iron accumulation (grade 1) to diffuse accumulation that involves the entire lobule and obliterates the typical gradient (grade 4)". That is an extent-based definition, not the magnification-based one in Table 1 ([Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087935/)).
- RCPath states that hepatocyte iron is "usually graded 0–4" and "Hepatocyte siderosis grade 2+ raises the possibility of haemochromatosis (especially in the absence of significant fibrosis)" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Deugnier–Turlin total iron score** (Table 2 in [Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/))

| Component | Per-area values | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Hepatocytic iron score (HIS) | 0, 3, 6, 9 or 12, according to granule size, in each Rappaport area | 0–36 |
| Sinusoidal iron score (SIS) | 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, according to granule size, in each Rappaport area | 0–12 |
| Portal iron score (PIS) | 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, according to the % of iron-overloaded macrophages, biliary cells and vascular walls | 0–12 |
| Total iron score (TIS) | HIS + SIS + PIS | 0–60 |

- The system was "well validated" but "remains mainly used for research purposes due to its relative complexity" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- The per-grade granule-size definitions within each area are n.a. from the source read.

**Distribution and pattern**

- **Parenchymal iron:** "iron deposits within hepatocytes as fine granules at the biliary pole of cells, and is distributed throughout the lobule according to a decreasing gradient from periportal to centrolobular areas" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- **Mesenchymal iron:** deposition "within Kupffer cells and/or portal macrophages ... without any lobular systematization" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- **Mixed iron:** "corresponds usually to complex conditions or to massive iron loading" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- The report should state "whether lobules (or, in case of cirrhosis, nodules) are equally iron loaded or not" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- Secondary overload in cirrhosis "typically has a mixed hepatocellular/sinusoidal pattern". "Pure sinusoidal cell siderosis ... is seen following haemolysis or blood transfusion, and in systemic chronic inflammatory diseases and acute hepatitis" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- "no formal grading system exists for Kupffer cell hemosiderosis". It "can be generally categorized as focal ... and diffuse" ([Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087935/)).
- Ferroportin disease (HH type 4) shows Kupffer cell haemosiderin ([Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087935/)).
- The hepatic iron concentration of normal liver is "lower than 20 μmol/g of dry weight". Iron excess is "mild up to 150, moderate between 150 and 300, and important above 300" ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- EASL 2022: "Liver biopsy is not recommended for the diagnosis of hepatic iron overload". Biopsy "can be performed to assess liver fibrosis if serum ferritin is higher than 1,000 µg/L or if liver enzymes are increased" ([EASL haemochromatosis guideline 2022](https://easl.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/PIIS01688278220021121.pdf)).

### 3.1 Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)

**Features**
- Portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, interface hepatitis and lobular hepatitis, with or without centrilobular necroinflammation ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)).
- Plasma cell clusters (≥5 plasma cells) ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)).
- Plasma cells are "typically IgG-dominant in AIH, whereas PBC contains relatively more immunoglobulin M-positive plasma cells" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- Chronic hepatitis is the "Dominant abnormality in autoimmune hepatitis (unless acute onset)" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Stains/IHC**
- Collagen plus elastic stains separate collapse from fibrosis ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)).
- Reticulin shows collapse in areas of confluent necrosis, which "may be mistaken for fibrous septa" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- If PBC or PSC is suspected and inflammation is "unusually dense or plasma cell rich and/or associated with moderate/severe interface hepatitis, consider the possibility of an 'overlap syndrome'" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Report should state**
- The IAIHG 2022 likelihood tier (likely, possible or unlikely), the pattern (portal or lobular), and grade by mHAI items A, B and C (without D) with stage ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)).
- Optionally, the 2008 histology points for the simplified score ([Hennes 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.22322)).
- Features suggesting another disease ([Lohse 2022](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/172809323/liv.15217.pdf)).

### 3.2 Chronic hepatitis B and C

**Features**
- A portal pattern with predominantly lymphocytic inflammation, "In some cases, lymphoid aggregates", and interface hepatitis. Hepatitis C is listed among the common causes ([AASLD Pearls, biopsy interpretation](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/liver-biopsy-importance-and-interpretation)).
- HBV ground-glass hepatocytes show "finely granular eosinophilic cytoplasm" ([AASLD Pathology Pearls post 5](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-post-5-chronic-viral-hepatitis)).
- Pseudo-ground-glass change occurs in DILI ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- Features may overlap, for example "chronic hepatitis and fatty liver in hepatitis C" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Other HCV-specific morphological features (for example bile duct lesions) are n.a. from the sources read.

**Stains/IHC**
- HBsAg: "Cytoplasmic positivity corresponds to ground glass hepatocytes in chronic hepatitis B. More sensitive for demonstrating cytoplasmic HBsAg than orcein staining. It also demonstrates membranous HBsAg, which is not shown with orcein staining. Typically negative in acute hepatitis B" ([RCPath G064, Appendix B](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- HBcAg: "Nuclei positive in patients with HBeAg-positive infection. Cytoplasmic or membrane positive during seroconversion, lobular activity" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- The Gut Liver 2017 review describes the following ([Gut Liver 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5417785/)):
  - HBcAg can be nuclear, mixed or cytoplasmic, and is regarded as positive if ≥10% of hepatocytes stain.
  - HBsAg can be intracytoplasmic (diffuse, globular or submembranous) or membranous; membranous staining correlates with HBV DNA.
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** RCPath says HBV immunostain use "has decreased massively" and that serology has "largely replaced the need" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)). The IJMS review says HBsAg/HBcAg IHC "is routinely performed in biopsies from seropositive patients" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

**Report should state**
- Aetiology, grade and stage, for example in Batts–Ludwig form ([Batts & Ludwig 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7503362/)).
- Where two diseases coexist (for example HBV with fatty liver disease), "an attempt should be made to identify the predominant cause" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

### 3.3 Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)

**Features**
- DILIN classified cases into "1 of 18 patterns" ([Kleiner et al., Hepatology 2014 (DILIN)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)):
  - acute hepatitis; chronic hepatitis;
  - acute cholestasis; chronic cholestasis; cholestatic hepatitis;
  - granulomatous;
  - macrovesicular or microvesicular steatosis; steatohepatitis;
  - zonal or nonzonal coagulative/confluent necrosis; massive/submassive necrosis;
  - vascular injury; hepatocellular alteration; nodular regenerative hyperplasia;
  - mixed/unclassifiable; minimal nonspecific; normal.
- **[DISAGREEMENT]** The count of patterns as listed in the source text differs from "18" unless subtypes are counted separately ([Kleiner 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)).
- Frequencies were acute hepatitis 21%, chronic hepatitis 14%, acute cholestasis 9%, chronic cholestasis 10% and cholestatic hepatitis 29%. The first five patterns account for 83% ([Kleiner 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)).
- Eosinophils and granulomas were associated with milder injury. Necrosis, fibrosis, microvesicular steatosis and ductular reaction were associated with more severe injury ([Kleiner 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)).
- The R ratio is >5 hepatocellular, <2 cholestatic and 2–5 mixed ([Kleiner 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)).
- "Drugs are the commonest cause of 'pure' or 'bland' cholestasis". The differential includes "early large duct obstruction, resolving cholestatic hepatitis, bile transporter protein defects, sepsis and occult malignancy (especially lymphoma)" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Features favouring DILI over AIH** ([Suzuki et al., Hepatology 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21674554/))
- Favour AIH: portal and intra-acinar plasma cells, rosettes, emperipolesis.
- Favour hepatocellular DILI: intra-acinar eosinophils and canalicular cholestasis.
- Favour cholestatic DILI: portal neutrophils and hepatocellular cholestasis.
- AUROC was 0.90 and 0.91 ([Suzuki 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21674554/)).
- See the 2022 disagreement on rosettes and emperipolesis in 2h.

**Stains/IHC**
- PAS-D shows ceroid-laden Kupffer cells ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Reticulin shows collapse in confluent necrosis ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- CK7/CK19 can be used for ductopenia after DILI ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

**Report should state**
- The DILIN pattern, severity features (necrosis, fibrosis, ductular reaction, microvesicular steatosis) and duct loss ([Kleiner 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)).
- A comment on the AIH differential ([Suzuki 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21674554/)).

### 3.4 Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)

**Features**
- Florid duct lesions and epithelioid granulomas ([EASL PBC 2017](https://www.sigeitalia.it/multimedia/files/attivita-scientifica/linee-guida/epatologia/colangite-biliare-primitiva/easl-guidelines-pbc-2017.pdf)).
- "lymphocytic/granulomatous cholangitis in PBC" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- "Diagnostic duct lesions are rarely seen in needle biopsy specimens" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- "Poorly formed epithelioid granulomas are the characteristic histologic findings in primary biliary cholangitis" ([AASLD Pearls, granulomatous liver diseases](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-granulomatous-liver-diseases-0)).
- Cholate stasis: "ballooning, feathery degeneration and Mallory-Denk bodies" in periportal hepatocytes ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Biopsy is not needed for diagnosis except when AMA is negative or AIH/NASH is suspected ([EASL PBC 2017](https://www.sigeitalia.it/multimedia/files/attivita-scientifica/linee-guida/epatologia/colangite-biliare-primitiva/easl-guidelines-pbc-2017.pdf)).

**Stains/IHC**
- "Copper-associated protein and K7 expression in periportal hepatocytes are sensitive early markers of biliary disease" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- D-PAS shows a "disrupted BM in primary biliary cholangitis" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Ductopenia is "best assessed by matching ducts to hepatic arteries" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)). CK7/CK19 help ([Nakanuma 2011 tutorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3168913/)).
- **[DISAGREEMENT] Ductopenia definition:**
  - "absence of interlobular bile ducts in at least 50% of portal tracts" ([Nakanuma 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3168913/)).
  - "less than 50% of portal tracts containing bile ducts" ([Korean PBC guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10037524/)).
  - Duct loss in "≥50%" of portal tracts for late chronic rejection ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
  - At exactly 50%, the Nakanuma and Korean definitions classify differently.

**Report should state**
- Stage by Nakanuma (with the component scores and whether orcein was used) or by Ludwig/Scheuer, plus CA/HA grade ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x)).
- The proportion of portal tracts with ducts ([Nakanuma 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1440-1827.2009.02500.x)).
- A comment on AIH overlap features ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

### 3.5 Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)

**Features**
- "obliterative concentric periductal loose fibrosis called 'onion skin fibrosis'". Fibro-obliterative lesions are seen only in a minority of biopsies ([Cazzagon 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10820917/)).
- "fibrosing cholangiopathy in PSC" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Other listed changes include "infiltration of inflammatory cells in portal tracts, loss of bile ducts, bile ductular reaction and focal accumulation of copper binding protein (CBP)" ([de Vries 2015](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/21700040/deVries_et_al_Applicability_prognostic_value_Journal_Hepatology_2015.pdf)).
- Biopsy is indicated for suspected small-duct PSC or AIH overlap ([de Vries 2015](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/21700040/deVries_et_al_Applicability_prognostic_value_Journal_Hepatology_2015.pdf); [Cazzagon 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10820917/)).
- Fibrous obliterative cholangitis is characteristic of PSC ([Nakanuma 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3168913/)).

**Stains/IHC**
- D-PAS: "thickened BM in primary sclerosing cholangitis" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Orcein or rhodanine for copper/CAP, and K7 as for PBC ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Report should state**
- Chronic biliary disease features and stage (Nakanuma, Ishak or Ludwig have been applied) ([de Vries 2015](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/21700040/deVries_et_al_Applicability_prognostic_value_Journal_Hepatology_2015.pdf)).
- The need for cholangiographic correlation, since "Biopsy evidence of biliary disease is indication for further investigation/imaging" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

### 3.6 Large-duct obstruction

**Features**
- "Acute obstruction shows bile ductular proliferation, mixed portal inflammation with lymphocytes, neutrophils, and eosinophils, and occasional portal edema. In longstanding obstruction, ductular proliferation and portal inflammation persist but are usually less pronounced" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- The ductular reaction triad is "(1) portal edema, (2) proliferation of ductular structures at the limiting plate, and (3) focal neutrophilic infiltrates" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- "Neutrophil-rich infiltration of bile ducts, including luminal aggregates of neutrophils, raises the possibility of ascending cholangitis" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Bile infarcts and other features: n.a. from the sources read.
- Patchy ductular proliferation in venous outflow disease "may mimic biliary obstruction" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

**Stains/IHC**
- CK7 highlights the ductular reaction ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/); [RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Rhodanine is positive in "prolonged bile duct obstruction" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

**Report should state**
- The biliary pattern, and recommend imaging correlation ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

### 3.7 MASLD / MASH

**Features**
- As in 2d/2e: steatosis, ballooning, lobular inflammation, MDBs, apoptotic bodies, lipogranulomas, and zone 3 perisinusoidal fibrosis ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).
- Fibrosis "is typically pericellular in the early stages. It may also be periportal, particularly in later stages" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Stains/IHC**
- Trichrome or Sirius red shows chicken-wire fibrosis ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).
- K8/18: ballooned hepatocytes "show loss of normal cytoplasmic/submembranous staining". Ubiquitin and p62 confirm MDBs ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- These markers "are unnecessary for routine diagnosis" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- MDBs are not specific to NAFLD ([Leow 2023](https://www.e-cmh.org/upload/pdf/cmh-2022-0329.pdf)).

**Report should state**
- Steatohepatitis present or absent, with grade (NAS or SAF components) and stage ([Kleiner 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.20701); [Bedossa 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hep.25889)).
- Nomenclature as discussed in 2e.

### 3.8 Acute T cell-mediated rejection (TCMR)

**Features**
- Mixed portal infiltrate, bile duct inflammation and damage, and venous subendothelial inflammation. Each is scored 1–3 as in 2f ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
- Isolated perivenular infiltrates can be part of TCMR ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
- Conspicuous eosinophils with oedema and endothelial hypertrophy should raise acute AMR ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).

**Stains/IHC**
- C4d for AMR ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
- CK7/CK19 to count ducts in chronic rejection ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

**Report should state**
- The global assessment (indeterminate, mild, moderate or severe) and the RAI components with their total ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
- Duct loss and fibrosis ([Demetris 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajt.13909)).
- "For complex cases and post-transplant biopsies, consider referral to specialist centre" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

### 3.9 Granulomatous hepatitis

**Features and subtypes** ([Hepatology Communications 2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10948139/))

| Subtype | Associated causes |
|---|---|
| Lipogranuloma | Steatotic liver disease, hepatitis C |
| Fibrin-ring granuloma | Q fever (Coxiella), leishmaniasis, toxoplasmosis, Hodgkin disease, ICI-related granulomas |
| Epithelioid necrotising granuloma | TB, nocardiosis, fungal infections |
| Epithelioid non-necrotising granuloma | Sarcoidosis, hepatitis C, PBC, DILI |

- Leading causes vary by country: idiopathic disease is most common in the USA, TB in Pakistan and PBC in Greece ([Hepatology Communications 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10948139/)).
- Sarcoid granulomas are "classically non-necrotizing epithelioid granulomas", "commonly found in portal or periportal locations", and "often contain abundant reticulin fibers" ([AASLD Pearls, granulomatous](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-granulomatous-liver-diseases-0)).
- DILI granulomas are "more commonly poorly defined histiocytic aggregates admixed with other inflammatory cells" ([AASLD Pearls, granulomatous](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-granulomatous-liver-diseases-0)).
- In the DILIN cohort, granulomas were associated with milder injury ([Kleiner 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946736/)).

**Stains**
- "acid-fast stain for mycobacteria or silver stains for *B. henselae*"; "GMS and PAS can highlight fungal organisms"; "negative stains do not exclude an infectious etiology" ([AASLD Pearls, granulomatous](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-granulomatous-liver-diseases-0)).
- Phosphotungstic acid haematoxylin shows fibrin in fibrin-ring granulomas ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).
- Serial sections are "invaluable when small focal lesions—granulomas ... are suspected" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

**Report should state**
- Granuloma type, location, necrosis, and the special stains performed with their results. Sarcoidosis is "a diagnosis of exclusion" ([AASLD Pearls, granulomatous](https://www.aasld.org/liver-fellow-network/core-series/pathology-pearls/pathology-pearls-granulomatous-liver-diseases-0)).

### 3.10 Iron overload

**Features**
- As in 2i. Parenchymal overload shows a periportal-to-centrilobular gradient; mesenchymal overload is in Kupffer cells and macrophages; mixed patterns also occur ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/)).
- In severe HH, iron also appears in bile duct epithelium and Kupffer cells ([Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087935/)).
- "Minimal hepatocellular hemosiderin ... should not be misinterpreted as primary iron overload" in secondary overload ([Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087935/)).

**Stains**
- Perls Prussian blue ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- "A 'blush' of ferritin staining is not considered to be significant" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).

**Report should state**
- Compartment, zonal gradient, grade, homogeneity and fibrosis stage ([Deugnier & Turlin 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611197/); [RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- A parenchymal pattern "should be followed up with serum iron studies and HFE gene testing" ([Clin Liver Dis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8087935/)).

### 3.11 Venous outflow obstruction (Budd–Chiari, SOS/VOD, congestive hepatopathy)

**Features**
- "The histological findings in VOD, BCS, and CH are almost identical: sinusoidal congestion and cell necrosis mostly in perivenular areas of hepatic acini which eventually leads to bridging fibrosis between adjacent central veins" ([Bayraktar, WJG 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4146966/)).
- Acute outflow obstruction shows "Sinusoidal dilatation" with "Perivenular hepatocytes replaced by extravasated red blood cells". "Changes are often patchy in distribution and easy to miss" ([RCPath G064](https://www.rcpath.org/static/2921c666-0f66-4272-820c16db2acaff99/32ca2ca1-cd3b-4692-a6b260aa49ddbbbe/G064-Tissue-pathways-for-liver-biopsies-for-publication.pdf)).
- Zone 3 sinusoidal dilatation is "not specific" ([IJMS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387026/)).

## 6. Consolidated list of source disagreements

1. **Adequacy thresholds.**
   - RCPath/BSG: >20 mm and >10 portal tracts.
   - AASLD 2009: 2–3 cm (3 cm ideal), and a note if <11 portal tracts.
   - AASLD Pearls: 1.5–2.5 cm and 6–11 portal tracts.
   - IJMS: about 2 cm and 11–15 portal tracts.
   - RCPath 2014, as cited by BSG: ≥10 mm and 6 portal tracts.
2. **Transjugular cores.** BSG: at least two biopsies, four passes optimal. AASLD: 3 cores.
3. **Routine use of scores.** RCPath: Ishak/Kleiner are poorly reproducible and descriptive text is acceptable. Krishna: the simpler Batts–Ludwig/METAVIR are adequate.
4. **METAVIR F1/F2 wording.** "Stellate enlargement of portal tract without septa" (Brunt) versus "portal fibrosis without septa" (IntechOpen).
5. **METAVIR LN definitions.** Severity labels (Brunt) versus foci per lobule (IntechOpen). Two cells of the PN×LN table (PN0/LN1 and PN2/LN2) read differently between a text-layer parse and an automated extraction, and need checking against Bedossa 1996.
6. **Ishak 5 crosswalk.** F4 (Goodman) versus the METAVIR group's placement.
7. **NASH CRN ranges.** The abstract gives lobular inflammation 0–2 and fibrosis 0–4; the table and text give lobular inflammation 0–3.
8. **Steatosis bands.** SAF 34–66% / >67% versus NASH CRN >33–66% / >66%. The denominator is parenchymal area (RCPath) versus % of hepatocytes (Leow).
9. **Lobular inflammation counting.** SAF 2012: ≤2 / >2 foci per 20× field. FLIP 2014: <2 / >2 foci per lobule, which leaves 2 undefined. NASH CRN: 0–3 per 200× field.
10. **SAF severity and routes.** A≥2/F≥2 (2012) versus "A>2 and/or F>2" (2014). 10 routes (2012) versus 9 (2014). A runs 0–4, but the grade categories stop at A3.
11. **MetALD alcohol threshold.** 140–350 g/week in the text versus "140–30 g" in the figure footnote of the same Delphi paper.
12. **Banff global assessment 1997 versus 2016.** 2016 adds perivenular-only infiltrates and confluent necrosis/dropout criteria.
13. **Banff RAI to grade mapping.** 1–2 / 3–5 / 6–7 / 8–9 (2023 study) versus 0–1 / 2–3 / 3–4 / 5–7 / 8–9, which overlaps at 3 (Colombian review). There is no numerical mapping in the 2016 Table 1.
14. **Simplified AIH 2008 internal inconsistencies.** "Each of the three features" while four are listed. Abstract ">6" versus table "≥6" for probable.
15. **Emperipolesis and rosettes.** Typical (2008) and favouring AIH over DILI (Suzuki 2011) versus "should be discarded as a diagnostic feature" (IAIHG 2022). The terminology changes from typical/compatible/atypical to likely/possible/unlikely.
16. **Nakanuma CA/HA.** Original (Table 3) versus revised (Table 4) definitions. The text says ">20" and "many" where the table says "20 continuous hepatocytes" and "more than half". Orcein wording differs between text and table. Revised CA leaves exactly two ducts undefined.
17. **Ductopenia threshold.** "Absence ... in at least 50%" (Nakanuma) versus "less than 50% ... containing bile ducts" (Korean guideline) versus ≥50% duct loss (Banff late chronic rejection).
18. **HBV immunostaining.** "Decreased massively", replaced by serology (RCPath) versus "routinely performed" in seropositive patients (IJMS 2025).
19. **Scheuer iron grade.** Magnification-based grades 0–4 (Searle modification, Deugnier Table 1) versus extent-based grades "1 to 4" (Clin Liver Dis 2021). The Table 1 grade 1 row repeats "×250", which is probably a transcription error.
20. **DILIN pattern count.** "18 patterns" versus a shorter list in the text unless subtypes are counted separately.
21. **Nakanuma in PSC.** Applied, with prognostic association for staging (Cazzagon 2024; de Vries 2015), but Nakanuma grading was not associated with cirrhosis-related symptoms (de Vries 2015). No guideline endorsement was found (n.a.).

## 7. Template decisions (added when the template was built, 30 September 2026)

Where the sources disagree, the template takes one reading and records it here. Printed report text carries no citations or thresholds; the grounding sits in `src/staging/liver.js` and in the schema help text.

1. **Adequacy.** RCPath G064 tiers on aggregate core length: good >20 mm, compromised 10–20 mm, inadequate <10 mm. A Check flags 10 or fewer portal tracts (BSG 2020). The microscopy states the number of cores, the aggregate length, fragmentation and the portal-tract count.
2. **METAVIR PN × LN cells.** Resolved as PN0/LN1 = A1 and PN2/LN2 = A3. The HepatitisCentral METAVIR chart (an HTML table marked "modified and reprinted with permission", https://www.hepatitiscentral.com/hepatitis-c/biopsy/charts/metavir/) gives these values, and a row-by-row re-parse of the Brunt 2000 text layer in (PN, LN, A) triples gives the same. Both are reproductions of the same table; Bedossa 1996 itself was still not read.
3. **METAVIR F1/F2 wording.** The template uses "portal fibrosis without septa" and "portal fibrosis with rare septa" (IntechOpen), which read better in a sentence than Brunt's "stellate enlargement".
4. **NASH CRN lobular inflammation.** 0–3, as in the paper's table and NAS text, not the abstract's 0–2.
5. **Steatosis grade.** One steatosis grade drives both NAS and SAF S. With whole-number percentages, the NASH CRN and SAF bands agree except at exactly 67%, which the template grades 3.
6. **SAF lobular inflammation.** A separate 0–2 field (SAF 2012: ≤2 or >2 foci per 20× field), because it is not the NAS 0–3 item. SAF A = ballooning + SAF lobular inflammation (0–4); FLIP reads steatohepatitis when steatosis, ballooning and lobular inflammation are each at least 1.
7. **Banff.** The global assessment is the primary field. RAI components are 0–3 (0 = absent), summed to /9. The grade is never derived from the RAI and no Check compares them, because no official mapping exists.
8. **Nakanuma.** Offered for PBC only. Stage from the three-item sum with orcein, or the two-item sum when orcein is not assessed. CA and HA help text follows the revised Table 4.
9. **AIH.** IAIHG 2022 likely/possible/unlikely is the default category; the simplified 2008 typical/compatible/atypical category is the alternative. For AIH the modified HAI is A + B + C (/14) without item D, following IAIHG 2022. Emperipolesis and rosettes stay as descriptive features only. Diagnosis qualifiers: likely or typical → "consistent with"; possible or compatible → "compatible with"; an unlikely or atypical category with AIH as the diagnosis raises a Check.
10. **Ductopenia.** The template prints the count of portal tracts without a bile duct rather than a ductopenia label, so the 50% boundary disagreement never reaches the report.
11. **MASLD wording.** No pathology-body guidance was found. The diagnosis gives the morphology (steatohepatitis, or steatosis without steatohepatitis) with grade and stage; MASH, MetALD or alcohol-related wording appears only when the clinical context is chosen, and otherwise a comment asks for correlation with metabolic risk factors and alcohol intake.
12. **Iron.** Hepatocellular grade 0–4 (Scheuer as modified by Searle) with the distribution (parenchymal, mesenchymal or mixed). The Deugnier–Turlin score is not offered.
13. **Scheuer grade.** Not offered, because the rule for combining its two columns is n.a.
14. **Gallery.** Figures from IJMS 2025 (CC BY 4.0) and Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0, CC BY 2.0) as listed in section 5. Phase 62 (9 October 2026) added a gallery under each heading (Inflammation, Hepatitis, Biliary, Steatosis, Transplant) and more fibrosis photos (section 5.1). PSC, TCMR, drug-induced granulomas, antibody-mediated rejection, chronic rejection and time-zero ischaemia-reperfusion injury are now illustrated. Still left for the user's own photographs: hepatitis C-specific features, CMV, plasma cell-rich rejection and C4d-positive AMR. Gallery points are short and carry their scheme name (Ishak, NASH CRN, SAF, Banff 2016, IAIHG 2022); they are reading aids and never print.
