# Ovary, fallopian tube and peritoneum — microscopic morphology reference

Sourced microscopic descriptions, immunoprofiles and WHO 5th edition (2020, *Female Genital Tumours*) quantitative rules for use in report-writing.

Every **Microscopy** field below is taken from a page actually fetched and read while compiling this document. Where a source page does not state a feature (e.g. no immunostains listed), that absence is stated explicitly rather than filled in. Primary workhorse source: PathologyOutlines ovary and fallopian tube chapters, supplemented by peer-reviewed WHO 5th edition summary papers.

---

## Serous borderline tumour

**Microscopy:** Numerous slender to bulbous, irregularly contoured papillae with fibrous, hyaline or myxoid cores showing hierarchical branching — larger papillae arborize into sequentially smaller papillae that terminate in epithelial clusters or single cells. The lining is pseudostratified and crowded with hobnailing, epithelial tufting and exfoliation of cells from the papillae. The cell population is heterogeneous (cuboidal, columnar, ciliated, secretory and eosinophilic cells), with atypia up to moderate consisting of mild cellular enlargement, chromatin coarsening and small nucleoli; mitotic activity is minimal and non-atypical. Psammomatous concentrically lamellated calcifications and dystrophic calcifications may be present. ([PathologyOutlines — ovarian serous borderline tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Diffusely positive for PAX8, WT1, ER and PR; negative for CK20, HNF-1β, napsin A, and (versus mesothelial hyperplasia) calretinin and D2-40. Wild-type p53 is patchy weak staining in scattered nuclei. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Epithelial proliferation must involve **>10%** of the tumour (≤10% = serous cystadenoma/cystadenofibroma with focal epithelial proliferation). Aberrant (mutation-type) p53 requires diffuse positivity in **>80%** of nuclei, complete negativity (null), or unequivocal cytoplasmic staining. "Serous borderline tumour" is the only recommended term under the 2014 and 2020 WHO classifications; atypical proliferative serous tumour, serous tumour of low malignant potential and non-invasive LGSC are obsolete. Under 2020 WHO, neither the micropapillary/cribriform variant nor microinvasion is by definition synonymous with LGSC. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html

## Serous borderline tumour, micropapillary / cribriform variant

**Microscopy:** Thread-like micropapillary extensions lacking true fibrovascular cores radiate directly from cyst walls or from central bulbous, smooth-contoured prominences with fibromatous/myxoid stroma; the micropapillae are at least **5 times longer than wide**, resembling the "mythical snakes of Medusa's head", and their complex interweaving creates labyrinthine spaces at low power. The cribriform pattern consists of confluent sieve-like microacini formed by fusion of micropapillae, occasionally appearing solid. In contrast to conventional SBT the epithelium is uniform rather than heterogeneous: low cuboidal monotonous cells with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio and small prominent nucleoli, with ciliation and cytoplasmic eosinophilia rare to absent. Atypia is up to moderate and mitoses are minimal and non-atypical; areas of conventional SBT are often admixed. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** As for conventional serous borderline tumour — PAX8, WT1, ER, PR positive; CK20, HNF-1β, napsin A negative; wild-type p53. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Requires a focus of **≥5 mm of pure micropapillary/cribriform growth measured as confluent linear extent**. Some experts also cite 10 mm², but this is **not recognised in the 2020 WHO** classification. Under 2020 WHO this variant is no longer definitionally synonymous with non-invasive LGSC. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html

## Serous borderline tumour with microinvasion

**Microscopy:** Individual cells to rounded clusters with the cytoarchitecture of serous borderline tumour invade papillary or intracystic stroma, often with retraction clefting. The cells are minimally atypical with hypereosinophilic cytoplasm (senescent-type cells), mitoses are scant and non-atypical at **<3–4/10 HPF**, and the Ki67 index is lower than in the surrounding tumour. Surrounding stroma is unremarkable or only minimally reactive (fibroblastic reaction, oedema). Multiple foci may occur within one tumour and confer no prognostic detriment. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** As for serous borderline tumour (PAX8, WT1, ER, PR positive; wild-type p53), with a Ki67 index lower than the adjacent borderline tumour. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Any single focus must measure **<5 mm** in greatest dimension; a focus **≥5 mm** in greatest linear extent is by definition invasive low-grade serous carcinoma arising in a background of serous borderline tumour. Adequate sampling is required to exclude frankly invasive LGSC. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html

## Microinvasive low-grade serous carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Micropapillae, inverted micropapillae or confluent cribriform structures haphazardly infiltrate papillary or intracystic stroma in a pattern identical to conventional invasive low-grade serous carcinoma. The cells are monotonous and hyperchromatic with scant cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli — cytologically distinct from the senescent, hypereosinophilic cells of SBT-type microinvasion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** As for low-grade serous carcinoma — WT1 and ER positive, PR often weak/focal, with wild-type p53 and patchy p16. ([PathologyOutlines — LGSC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomalg.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** The focus must measure **<5 mm** in greatest dimension, measured as confluent linear growth in any single focus; **>5 mm** is frankly invasive low-grade serous carcinoma. Terminology accepted: "microinvasive low-grade serous carcinoma" or "serous borderline tumour with microinvasive low-grade serous carcinoma". Proper sampling is required. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html

## Low-grade serous carcinoma

**Microscopy:** A uniform, homogeneous population of small cells with scant cytoplasm, mild to moderate nuclear atypia at most (grade 1–2) and no nuclear pleomorphism (**<3× variation in nuclear size**), often with a conspicuous nucleolus and vesicular chromatin. Invasive patterns are multiple and frequently coexist within one tumour: micropapillary or complex papillae, compact cell nests, inverted macropapillae with broad fibrovascular cores, cribriform/glandular/cystic architecture, and solid sheets with slit-like spaces and single cells; the non-invasive pattern shows non-hierarchical micropapillary or cribriform architecture with significant expansile growth. Mitoses are few (**<12/10 HPF**), necrosis is minimal to absent, and psammoma bodies are frequent. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomalg.html))

**Immunoprofile:** WT1 positive; ER strongly positive (e.g. 2–3+, 90%) with weaker PR (1+, 5%); p53 wild-type (patchy, slightly elevated) and p16 patchy — in contrast to aberrant p53 and diffuse p16 in high-grade serous carcinoma. *KRAS* and *BRAF* mutations occur in **50–60%**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomalg.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Invasion is defined as **>5 mm**; mitotic index **<12/10 HPF** and **<3× nuclear size variation** separate it from HGSC (≥12/10 HPF, >3× variation, aberrant p53, diffuse p16, comedo/geographic necrosis). Serous borderline tumour has no or minimal (<5 mm) invasion and lacks prominent nucleoli. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomalg.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomalg.html

## High-grade serous carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Solid masses of columnar to cuboidal cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm punctuated by slit-like spaces representing fusion of papillae, together with hierarchical papillary branching, glandular and cribriform patterns. There is significant nuclear atypia and pleomorphism (**>3× variation in nuclear size**) with large, bizarre and multinucleated forms, and a prominent, often large eosinophilic nucleolus. The mitotic index is high (**≥12/10 HPF**, often atypical) and necrosis (comedo or geographic) is frequent; psammoma bodies are variable and CD3+/CD8+ tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes may be conspicuous, particularly in *BRCA1*-associated tumours. The SET (solid, pseudoendometrioid, transitional cell carcinoma-like) pattern is more common in *BRCA1* mutation carriers and tends to show a higher mitotic index, increased TILs and necrosis. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomahg.html))

**Immunoprofile:** PAX8, WT1, CK7 positive; p16 positive in ~60%; ER positive in 80% and PR in 30%; D2-40 and calretinin variable. Aberrant p53 in three patterns: overexpression (strong diffuse nuclear staining in **>80%** of cells, missense mutation), null phenotype (complete absence), or cytoplasmic staining. Ki67 proliferation index **>75%**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomahg.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **≥12 mitoses/10 HPF** and **>3× nuclear size variation** define high grade (versus <12/10 HPF and <3× in LGSC). Recognition of the SET pattern indicates referral for genetic testing. Associated STIC occurs in the fimbria in 90% of cases and the ampulla/isthmus in 10%, is bilateral in 10–20%, and may be multifocal. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomahg.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserouscarcinomahg.html

## Mucinous borderline tumour

**Microscopy:** Complex architecture with epithelial tufting and villus formation lined by gastrointestinal-type mucinous epithelium containing goblet cells, neuroendocrine cells and occasional Paneth cells, resembling low-grade dysplasia of the intestine. The neoplastic cells show hyperchromasia, crowding, stratification and mitotic activity, and the glands contain luminal mucin. Areas of mucin extravasation with an inflammatory response and histiocytes reflect gland rupture and are **not** diagnostic of invasion. Mural nodules may occur (reactive sarcoma-like, anaplastic carcinoma, or sarcoma). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**Immunoprofile:** CK7 positive with variable CK20 and CDX2 (**extent of CK7 > CK20/CDX2**); PAX8 focal; SATB2 positive in a small subset, including teratoma-associated mucinous tumours. No negative stains are listed on the page. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Epithelial proliferation must involve **>10% of the tumour**. Microinvasion = stromal invasion **<5 mm**; expansile (confluent) mucinous carcinoma = confluent glandular crowding without intervening stroma measuring **≥5 mm**. More than 90% are unilateral; *KRAS* mutations in 30–75%. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html)) The 2020 WHO definition of microinvasion as **<5 mm greatest linear extent**, with expansile/confluent and infiltrative/destructive carcinoma each requiring **≥5 mm** linear extent (superseding the older 10 mm² area or >3 mm in two dimensions criteria), is confirmed in the review literature. ([Expansile versus infiltrative mucinous carcinoma review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605042/))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605042/

## Mucinous borderline tumour with intraepithelial carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Within an otherwise typical gastrointestinal-type mucinous borderline tumour there are foci resembling **high-grade dysplasia of the intestine** — marked nuclear stratification and high-grade cytologic atypia — with **no stromal invasion**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**Immunoprofile:** As for mucinous borderline tumour — CK7 positive with variable CK20/CDX2 (CK7 > CK20/CDX2), focal PAX8. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Defined by high-grade cytologic atypia **without** stromal invasion; no size threshold applies. Overall survival is **95–100%**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html

## Mucinous borderline tumour with microinvasion

**Microscopy:** Foci of stromal invasion by single cells or small nests, morphologically resembling invasive mucinous carcinoma but measuring **<5 mm** in greatest dimension. The degree of cytologic atypia is mild and similar to that of the background borderline tumour; where the invasive component shows complex architecture and severe (high-grade) cytologic atypia the lesion is designated **microinvasive carcinoma**. Mucin extravasation with inflammation and histiocytes from gland rupture must not be mistaken for microinvasion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**Immunoprofile:** As for mucinous borderline tumour — CK7 > CK20/CDX2, focal PAX8. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Invasive focus **<5 mm** greatest dimension; severe cytologic atypia in the focus warrants "microinvasive carcinoma". Microinvasion is not associated with adverse behaviour; recurrence rate **5%**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html)) 2020 WHO uses **<5 mm greatest linear extent** for microinvasion, with **≥5 mm** required for expansile or infiltrative carcinoma. ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605042/))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorborderlinemucinous.html ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605042/

## Mucinous adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** Stromal invasion in one of two patterns: **infiltrative (destructive)** — disorderly penetration of stroma by neoplastic glands, single cells and cell clusters, often with a desmoplastic response; or **expansile (confluent)** — complex arrangements of glands, cysts or papillae lined by malignant epithelium with minimal or no intervening stroma and a broad, sharply defined border. The glands are almost always intestinal type and show greater complexity than in borderline tumours, with more solid growth, atypia, stratification, papillae, loss of glandular architecture and necrotic luminal debris that may resemble colon carcinoma. The carcinoma commonly merges with adjacent benign or borderline mucinous tumour; signet-ring cells occur rarely and the tumour differs from a Krukenberg tumour. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormucinouscarcinoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** CEA, CK7 and CK20 positive; CA125 weak. No negative stains are stated on the page. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormucinouscarcinoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Grading (not standardised, not independently prognostic): **grade 1** no solid areas; **grade 2** up to 50% solid foci; **grade 3** more than 50% solid foci — severe nuclear atypia may raise grade 1 or 2 by one grade. PathologyOutlines states stromal invasion **>10 mm** distinguishes carcinoma from borderline tumour; the current 2020 WHO threshold for expansile/confluent or infiltrative/destructive carcinoma is **≥5 mm greatest linear extent** (microinvasion <5 mm). Note: a "**>10% infiltrative component**" rule is **not** stated in the sources read. Primary ovarian origin is favoured by unilaterality, expansile invasion, complex papillae, size >10 cm, smooth external surface and an accompanying teratoma, adenofibroma, endometriosis or Brenner tumour; 77% of ovarian mucinous carcinomas are metastases. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormucinouscarcinoma.html); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605042/))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormucinouscarcinoma.html ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605042/

## Endometrioid borderline tumour

**Microscopy:** Aggregates, glands or cysts of endometrioid-type epithelium that is atypical or cytologically malignant, frequently with architectural atypia in the form of non-branching villous papillae and cribriform glands, set in an adenofibromatous background in 47% and showing squamous differentiation in 47%. The lesion lacks destructive stromal invasion, glandular confluence and stromal disappearance. Foci of intraepithelial carcinoma (7%) show large pleomorphic grade 3 nuclei with coarse chromatin or hypochromasia, large irregular nucleoli and numerous mitotic figures, and are associated with villoglandular architecture or microinvasion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendoborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** n.a. — the page states no positive or negative immunostains for this entity. (By analogy the endometrioid carcinoma profile is CK7/EMA/ER/PR/PAX8 positive; see that section.) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendoborderline.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Microinvasion is defined as areas of invasion measuring **10 mm² or less** (present in 7%); intraepithelial carcinoma in 7%. No destructive stromal invasion, glandular confluence or stromal disappearance is permitted. 97% present at stage I and follow-up is typically free of recurrence even with intraepithelial carcinoma or microinvasion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendoborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendoborderline.html

## Endometrioid carcinoma

**Microscopy:** The commonest pattern is confluent, back-to-back endometrioid glands with stromal invasion usually by expansion; destructive stromal invasion is rare. Squamous metaplasia with morules and keratin pearls is common, and cytoplasmic mucin, intracytoplasmic vacuoles, oncocytic change, clear cell change, cilia and sex cord-like (sertoliform) elements may all be present — none of which affects grade. Background endometriosis or adenofibroma is often present and vascular invasion is rare. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendometrioidcarcinoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Keratin cocktails, EMA, CK7, ER and PR positive; PAX8 positive but **negative in 15%**; p53 wild-type in most tumours. MMR deficiency in up to 23%. Common mutations: *CTNNB1* 53%, *PIK3CA* 40%, *KRAS* 33%, *ARID1A* 30%, *PTEN* 17%. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendometrioidcarcinoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** FIGO grading, as for endometrial endometrioid carcinoma: **grade 1 <5% solid**, **grade 2 6–50% solid**, **grade 3 >50% solid**. High-grade tumours may show a mutational p53 pattern (nuclear staining in >80% of cells, complete absence, or diffuse cytoplasmic staining). Seromucinous carcinoma is included as a **subtype of endometrioid carcinoma** in the 2020 WHO classification. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendometrioidcarcinoma.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendometrioidcarcinoma.html

## Endometrioid carcinoma, seromucinous type

**Microscopy:** Predominantly papillary architecture with large fibro-oedematous papillae, lined by mucinous epithelium resembling that of the endocervical canal, together with small nipple-like papillary projections; squamous metaplasia is seen in a proportion of cases. These fibro-oedematous papillae, squamous metaplasia, endocervical-type mucinous epithelium and small nipples were the features distinguishing seromucinous carcinoma from ovarian endometrioid carcinoma and mucinous-differentiated low-grade serous carcinoma, with which it morphologically overlaps. ([Ovarian seromucinous carcinoma clinicopathological study, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9862510/))

**Immunoprofile:** Müllerian phenotype — CK-pan, ER, PR, PAX8 and CA125 positive (PAX8 strongly expressed), **WT1 negative**; gastrointestinal markers CK20 and CDX2 are not the expected profile. ([PMC9862510](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9862510/))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** The **2020 WHO removed seromucinous carcinoma as a distinct entity** and reclassified it as a subtype of ovarian endometrioid carcinoma ("endometrioid carcinoma with mucinous differentiation"); the reclassification was based on pathological features and no separate quantitative criteria are given. Grade using FIGO endometrioid criteria (grade 1 <5% solid, grade 2 6–50%, grade 3 >50%). ([PMC9862510](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9862510/); [PathologyOutlines — endometrioid carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendometrioidcarcinoma.html))

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9862510/ ; https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorendometrioidcarcinoma.html

## Seromucinous borderline tumour

**Microscopy:** A papillary tumour with hierarchical branching, the papillae lined by cytologically bland cells of **mixed Müllerian types** — endometrioid, squamous, endocervical-type mucinous, ciliated, hobnail, clear and indifferent (nondescript) eosinophilic cells. The papillary stromal cores are oedematous or fibrotic with conspicuous inflammatory cells, characteristically **neutrophils and eosinophils**. Background endometriosis or an endometriotic cyst is frequently present, and there is no evidence of stromal invasion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryseromucinousborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** CK7 positive, PAX8 positive, CK20 negative, CDX2 negative, WT1 usually negative (may be patchy positive but not diffuse) — i.e. **CK7+/PAX8+/CK20−/WT1−**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryseromucinousborderline.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Essential criteria are hierarchically branching papillae lined by a mix of Müllerian cell types with **no stromal invasion**. No numeric threshold is stated for microinvasion, mitoses, necrosis, grading or the proportion of Müllerian cell types. Up to 33% bilateral; mean size 9 cm; mean age 34–39 years. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryseromucinousborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryseromucinousborderline.html

## Clear cell borderline tumour

**Microscopy:** An adenofibromatous tumour with abundant fibromatous stroma containing small glands or cysts lined by **1–2 layers** of cuboidal, flat or hobnail cells with clear cytoplasm and low-grade nuclear atypia (nuclear enlargement, hyperchromasia, small nucleoli). Intraluminal eosinophilic secretions and simple cystic outpouchings may be present. There is **no stromal invasion** and the tumour lacks architectural complexity — no confluent, solid, papillary or cribriform growth. The page does not state mitotic activity or necrosis. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryclearcellborderlinetumor.html))

**Immunoprofile:** PAX8, CK7 and napsin A positive; ER, PR, TTF1 and AFP negative. ARID1A loss is seen in clear cell carcinoma and its adjacent adenofibromatous component. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryclearcellborderlinetumor.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No numeric threshold is stated for glandular crowding, atypia, mitoses, necrosis or size; the defining rules are qualitative — lining of **1–2 cell layers**, low-grade atypia, no stromal invasion and no solid/papillary/cribriform architecture. **Extensive additional sampling is mandatory** when a pure clear cell borderline tumour or clear cell adenofibroma is found, to exclude clear cell carcinoma (~21% of clear cell carcinomas contain a clear cell adenofibromatous component; pure clear cell borderline tumours are <1% of ovarian borderline tumours). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryclearcellborderlinetumor.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovaryclearcellborderlinetumor.html

## Clear cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Three classic growth patterns — papillary (70%), tubulocystic (65%) and solid (62%) — are frequently admixed. The papillae are small, round, simple and non-branching with fibrous, hyalinised, oedematous, myxoid or empty stromal cores (producing "open tumour rings"), lined by 1–2 layers of cuboidal, flattened or hobnail cells; the tubulocystic pattern comprises variably sized tubules and cysts with dense intraluminal eosinophilic secretions lined by a single layer of hobnail, cuboidal or flattened cells; the solid pattern comprises diffuse sheets or nests of polyhedral cells separated by delicate septa. Cytoplasm is clear (PAS-positive, diastase-sensitive glycogen) to eosinophilic or granular eosinophilic in the oxyphilic variant, and diastase-resistant intracytoplasmic material may impart a signet-ring/targetoid appearance. Nuclear atypia is uniform with prominent nucleoli, hobnail nuclei hyperchromatic and protruding into lumina; mitoses are usually low (**<6/10 HPF**); eosinophilic hyaline globules, nuclear pseudoinclusions and a peritumoural or diffuse lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate may be present, and psammoma bodies are rare. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorclearcell.html))

**Immunoprofile:** CK7, EMA and PAX8 positive; HNF1β sensitive but not specific; napsin A specific with intermediate sensitivity; AMACR specific but not sensitive. Negative for WT1, ER, PR, SALL4 and CD10; ARID1A loss in 15–75%; MMR loss in 6–43.75%; aberrant p53 in only 7–24%; block-like p16 in 20%; AFP, glypican 3 and OCT4 rarely positive. Recommended distinction panel from HGSC: WT1, napsin A, HNF1β, ER. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorclearcell.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Clear cell carcinoma **is not graded — it is high grade by definition**. Mitoses are usually **<6/10 HPF** and brisk mitoses are not characteristic (favouring HGSC instead). No solid-percentage or size threshold applies. Extensive sampling of a clear cell adenofibroma/borderline tumour is required, as up to 33% of clear cell adenofibromas are associated with clear cell carcinoma. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorclearcell.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorclearcell.html

## Borderline Brenner tumour

**Microscopy:** Papillary architecture in which the papillae are covered by **multilayered transitional (urothelial-like) epithelium**, set against a fibromatous stroma. Cytological atypia is variable and usually low grade, though moderate or marked atypia is occasionally present. A benign Brenner tumour component — smooth-contoured nests of bland transitional epithelium with uniform oval nuclei showing longitudinal nuclear grooves within fibromatous stroma, sometimes with central mucinous epithelium and microcysts, and frequent calcification — is often present. There is no stromal invasion. ([PathologyOutlines — Brenner tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Brenner tumours are **GATA3 and p63 positive**. No negative stains are listed under the page's negative stains section. (Differential: adult granulosa cell tumour is inhibin positive/epithelial marker negative; HGSC with transitional pattern is WT1 and ER positive; endometrioid tumours are ER positive.) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Definitional criteria are qualitative — papillary growth with multilayered transitional epithelium and variable, usually low-grade, atypia, **without stromal invasion**. No mitotic-count threshold, necrosis threshold, grading percentage or size rule is stated; no quantitative criteria separate moderate from marked atypia. Borderline and malignant Brenner tumours are usually >10 cm (benign usually <2 cm); coexistent mucinous cystadenoma in 10%. All reported benign and borderline Brenner tumours have behaved benignly, with rare local recurrence. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html

## Malignant Brenner tumour

**Microscopy:** Stromal invasion by carcinoma with transitional cell features, in the form of **irregular nests of cells and single cells in an infiltrative pattern**; squamous or mucinous differentiation may be present. A benign or borderline Brenner tumour component is present by definition. The page does not describe cytoplasm, nucleoli, mitotic rate or necrosis for this entity. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html))

**Immunoprofile:** GATA3 and p63 positive (as for Brenner tumours generally). No dedicated negative stain list is provided. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Requires **demonstrable stromal invasion** plus an associated benign or borderline Brenner tumour component; **no specific percentage or measurement** is given for the amount of benign/borderline component required, and no mitotic, necrosis or grading threshold is stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorb9brenner.html

## Mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** The morphologic hallmark is a **combination of architectural patterns within the same tumour, without squamous or mucinous differentiation**. The commonest pattern is small tubules with ductal/glandular growth containing **dense intraluminal eosinophilic secretions**; other patterns include papillary, solid, trabecular, retiform, sex cord-like, sieve-like, glomeruloid and spindle cell. Tumour cells are flattened, cuboidal or columnar with mild to moderate atypia, and the nuclei show **vesicular chromatin and nuclear grooves**; clear cell features occur but are less common and high-grade atypia is usually not predominant. There are no associated mesonephric remnants; sarcomatoid transformation is rare. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymesonephriclikeadenocarcinoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** PAX8 usually diffusely positive, **GATA3 and TTF1 focal or diffuse with a characteristic inverse pattern** (GATA3-positive cells are TTF1-negative and vice versa), CD10 focal apical/luminal, p53 wild-type, MMR proficient. **ER and PR negative** (PR is the more reliable negative marker); HNF-1β and napsin A usually negative but can be positive. A hormone receptor-negative, PAX8/GATA3/TTF1-positive tumour is highly supportive of MLA. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymesonephriclikeadenocarcinoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No mitotic count or grading rule is specified for ovarian MLA; the diagnosis rests on the mixed architectural pattern combination, characteristic immunoprofile and absence of mesonephric remnants. For uterine mesonephric adenocarcinoma, **>10 mitoses/10 HPF** and coagulative tumour cell necrosis are associated with metastasis. Ovarian MLA is usually unilateral, 4–32 cm, and may coexist with endometriosis or other Müllerian neoplasms including low-grade serous carcinoma. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymesonephriclikeadenocarcinoma.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymesonephriclikeadenocarcinoma.html

## Small cell carcinoma, hypercalcaemic type

**Microscopy:** Diffuse growth of tightly packed small round cells with scant cytoplasm, small nucleoli and brisk mitotic activity, sometimes in nested or corded architecture; the cells are monomorphic and discohesive. **Follicle-like spaces containing eosinophilic or basophilic fluid are present in 80%** of cases. Necrosis is seen in most tumours and focal myxoid stroma is common. Most tumours contain a large cell component of varying extent, often with rhabdoid features; intermixed mucinous glands are reported in some cases. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsmallcellhyper.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **Loss of nuclear SMARCA4/BRG1 in >93%** (with SMARCA2/BRM co-loss); WT1 positive 86–93%, CD10 93%, EMA 87%, calretinin 73%, AE1/AE3 60%, CAM5.2 20%; p53 wild-type. Negative for inhibin, CD99, TTF1, OCT3/4 and FOXL2. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsmallcellhyper.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Defined by inactivating *SMARCA4* mutation with loss of nuclear SMARCA4/BRG1 expression in **>93%**. **Large cell variant** is designated when the large cell component comprises **>50% of the tumour**. Follicle-like spaces in 80%; mean size 15 cm (range 6–26 cm); size **<10 cm** is favourable. No grading system applies. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsmallcellhyper.html)) *SMARCA4* mutation is present in almost all SCCOHT, distinguishing it from SWI/SNF-deficient undifferentiated/dedifferentiated carcinoma. ([Epithelial ovarian tumours review, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsmallcellhyper.html ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215

## Dedifferentiated carcinoma

**Microscopy:** A biphasic tumour with an **abrupt, sharply demarcated transition** between a differentiated component — usually low-grade (FIGO grade 1–2) endometrioid adenocarcinoma, occasionally high-grade endometrioid, high-grade serous or clear cell carcinoma — and an undifferentiated component showing patternless, diffuse sheet-like growth of monomorphic medium-sized discohesive cells with variable, sometimes rhabdoid cytoplasm, brisk mitoses and frequent necrosis; abrupt keratinisation, focal spindling and myxoid change may occur, and glands from the differentiated component may be entrapped. The undifferentiated component is characteristically deep/invasive to the superficial differentiated component. ([PathologyOutlines — undifferentiated and dedifferentiated carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusundifferentiated.html); [Silva/Tafe series, *Modern Pathology*](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201041))

**Immunoprofile:** Differentiated component CK7, PAX8, ER/PR and E-cadherin positive with wild-type p53 and patchy p16. Undifferentiated component: high Ki67; EMA, keratins and CK18 or PAX8 may be positive but CK7, PAX8, ER, WT1 and claudin-4 are often negative or only weak/focal; neuroendocrine markers (chromogranin, synaptophysin, INSM1, CD56) are frequently focally expressed in 1–2 markers; **loss of SWI/SNF proteins (BRG1/SMARCA4, INI1/SMARCB1, co-loss of ARID1A and ARID1B)**; MMR deficiency in ~50%; p53 usually wild-type. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusundifferentiated.html)) SWI/SNF deficiency occurs in up to **70%** of undifferentiated/dedifferentiated carcinomas; E-cadherin is often negative and hormone receptors almost never expressed. ([ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Dedifferentiated carcinoma = undifferentiated carcinoma **combined with** a differentiated (usually low-grade endometrioid) carcinoma; the differentiated component usually constitutes **10–20% of tumour volume**, and the transition is abrupt with a sharp border. Tumours with diffuse staining (**strong staining of >20% of tumour cells**) for synaptophysin and/or chromogranin should be classified as neuroendocrine carcinoma instead. Solid FIGO grade 3 endometrioid and solid serous carcinoma are excluded. ([*Modern Pathology*](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201041))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusundifferentiated.html ; https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201041 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215

## Undifferentiated carcinoma, NOS

**Microscopy:** Diffuse, patternless, **sheet-like growth with total absence of nests, papillae, glands or trabeculae** and no squamous or mucinous metaplasia, composed of monomorphic medium-sized cells that are at least focally discohesive, with variable cytoplasm sometimes showing rhabdoid features. Mitoses are brisk and necrosis is frequently present; abrupt keratinisation, focal spindling and myxoid change may be seen. There is no associated differentiated component (distinguishing it from dedifferentiated carcinoma). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusundifferentiated.html); [*Modern Pathology*](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201041))

**Immunoprofile:** High Ki67; EMA, keratins, CK18 or PAX8 may be positive, often only focally; CK7, PAX8, ER, WT1 and claudin-4 negative or weak/focal; focal expression of 1–2 neuroendocrine markers is common; **SWI/SNF loss (BRG1, INI1, co-loss of ARID1A/ARID1B)**; p53 usually wild-type, p16 negative to patchy; MMR deficiency ~50%. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusundifferentiated.html)) In ovarian UC/DC: PAX8 highly variable, hormone receptors almost never expressed, WT-1 usually negative, p53 wild-type, E-cadherin often negative, no aberrant CK5/6; SWI/SNF deficiency in up to 70%. ([ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Essential features are at least focal sheet-like growth, **lack of glandular or papillary differentiation**, and high mitotic and proliferation indices. Diffuse neuroendocrine marker staining (**>20% of cells** for synaptophysin/chromogranin) reclassifies the tumour as neuroendocrine carcinoma; solid grade 3 endometrioid and solid serous carcinoma must be excluded (both retain intact SWI/SNF, and serous shows mutant p53). Ovarian UC/DC accounts for **<0.5%** of ovarian malignancies and most present at stage III/IV. ([*Modern Pathology*](https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201041); [ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusundifferentiated.html ; https://www.nature.com/articles/modpathol201041 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772736X24000215

## Carcinosarcoma (malignant mixed Müllerian tumour)

**Microscopy:** Intimately admixed **high-grade carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements** with high-grade cytologic atypia and brisk mitotic activity. The carcinomatous element is most often high-grade serous, then endometrioid, clear cell, squamous, mixed or undifferentiated carcinoma. The sarcomatous element is homologous in 50% (non-specific malignant mesenchymal cells resembling fibrosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma or other Müllerian-native sarcoma) and heterologous in 50% (chondrosarcomatous, osteosarcomatous, rhabdomyoblastic, liposarcomatous or neuroectodermal elements — usually only one heterologous type). **Intracytoplasmic hyaline globules in the sarcomatous component are frequently seen**, and lymphovascular invasion is common. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormmt.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Both components show **abnormal p53** and p16 expression; tumour cell droplets are PAS-positive/diastase-resistant. Carcinomatous component CK7 positive, may express ER/PR and neuroendocrine markers, with higher Ki67; HER2/neu variable and typically epithelial. Sarcomatous component variably expresses keratins (CAM5.2, AE1/AE3), CEA, EMA, vimentin, SALL4 and glypican 3, with myogenin/MyoD1/desmin/HHF35 in rhabdomyoblastic areas, S100 in chondro-/osteosarcomatous areas, SATB2 in osteosarcoma, and CD99/FLI1/GFAP in neuroectodermal areas. MMR protein loss may occur. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormmt.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Diagnosis requires the presence of **both** high-grade carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements; the page states **no minimum percentage** for either component and no mitotic, necrosis or grading threshold. Homologous versus heterologous sarcomatous differentiation is 50:50. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormmt.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormmt.html

## Mixed carcinoma

**Microscopy:** At least **two ovarian carcinoma histological types must be clearly recognisable on morphology**, each showing distinct morphological differences and each with the appearance of the corresponding pure tumour type (HGSC, endometrioid, clear cell, mucinous, LGSC). The commonest combination is mixed endometrioid and clear cell carcinoma, frequently arising from a common precursor such as endometriosis. The page does not provide separate architectural/cytological descriptions, referring instead to the individual tumour type topics. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymixedcarcinoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Each component mirrors the immunophenotype of the corresponding pure carcinoma — endometrioid component PAX8/ER/PR positive with wild-type p53 and weaker HNF-1β; clear cell component PAX8/napsin A/HNF-1β positive and ER/PR/WT1 negative. **Napsin A (clear cell) versus PR (endometrioid)** is the practical discriminating pair. Mixed endometrioid/clear cell tumours are often MMR deficient (e.g. MLH1 and PMS2 loss with retained MSH2/MSH6), a subset Lynch-associated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymixedcarcinoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Under the **2020 WHO classification any percentage of a second histologic type is sufficient** to label the tumour mixed — **no minimum percentage threshold applies**; the types present **and their percentages must be reported** (e.g. clear cell carcinoma 60%, endometrioid carcinoma 40%). No size, mitotic, necrosis or grading rule is stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymixedcarcinoma.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarymixedcarcinoma.html

## Endometrioid stromal sarcoma, low-grade

**Microscopy:** An infiltrative, diffuse proliferation of uniform round to oval cells with abundant small vessels; in the ovary the growth is permeative with irregular cellular islands, and tumour cells characteristically **whorl around delicate arteriolar-type vessels**, resembling proliferative-phase endometrial stroma. The cells are monotonous oval to spindle cells with **scant cytoplasm, minimal cytologic atypia** and small round-to-spindle nuclei with fine/vesicular chromatin; admixed collagen bands or plaques and foamy histiocytes may be present, and smooth muscle differentiation (starburst morphology with radiating collagen bands) or sex cord-like differentiation is common. Mitoses are usually low (**<5/10 HPF**), necrosis is usually absent and vascular invasion is frequent. ([PathologyOutlines — ovarian endometrioid stromal sarcoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoress.html); [PathologyOutlines — low-grade ESS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESSlowgrade.html))

**Immunoprofile:** The ovarian page lists no stains. The uterine low-grade ESS page gives **CD10 (sensitivity 91%, specificity 45%)**, IFITM1 (sensitivity 83%, specificity 70%), **ER 40–100% and PR 69–100%** positive, with smooth muscle markers positive only in areas of smooth muscle differentiation and sex cord markers in sex cord-like areas. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESSlowgrade.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Defined by cells resembling proliferative-phase endometrial stroma with **infiltrative growth and/or lymphovascular invasion**; mitoses usually **<5/10 HPF**. A stromal nodule (rather than sarcoma) shows absent to minimal invasion with **≤3 protrusions each <3 mm** and no lymphovascular invasion, so extensive sampling of the tumour interface is required. *JAZF1* and *PHF1* rearrangements are common (*JAZF1-SUZ12* 50%, *JAZF1-PHF1* 6%, *EPC1-PHF1* 4%) but their absence does not preclude the diagnosis. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESSlowgrade.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoress.html ; https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESSlowgrade.html

## Endometrioid stromal sarcoma, high-grade

**Microscopy:** Permeative, tongue-like invasion by a vaguely nested proliferation of round cells with scant cytoplasm (small round blue cell appearance) or moderate eosinophilic cytoplasm, showing **uniform** nuclear atypia (overt pleomorphism is rare), set against a delicate network of arborising or curvilinear vessels. Mitotic activity is brisk (**>10/10 HPF**), tumour cell necrosis is common and lymphovascular invasion is frequent; approximately **50%** contain an admixed low-grade spindle cell component of monomorphic bland cells in fibrous to fibromyxoid matrix with **≤3 mitoses/10 HPF** and no necrosis. Pseudoglandular, pseudopapillary, sex cord-like and rosette-like patterns occur. *BCOR*-rearranged tumours show haphazard fascicles of uniformly atypical spindle cells with extensive myxoid stroma, collagen plaques in ~50%, small arterioles **without** perivascular whorling, ≥10 mitoses/10 HPF and infarct-type necrosis, and lack a low-grade component. ([PathologyOutlines — high-grade ESS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESShighgrade.html); [PathologyOutlines — ovarian ESS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoress.html))

**Immunoprofile:** *YWHAE::NUTM2A/B* high-grade component: **cyclin D1 positive in >70% of cells**, plus BCOR, CD117/KIT, pan-TRK, CD56 and CD99. *BCOR*-rearranged: CD10, cyclin D1 (>70% of cells), pan-TRK, BCOR (~50%). *BCOR* ITD: BCOR and cyclin D1 in both components, pan-TRK. No specific negative stain panel is provided. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESShighgrade.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** High grade is defined by brisk mitotic activity — **>10/10 HPF** (*YWHAE::NUTM2A/B*) or **≥10/10 HPF** (*BCOR*-rearranged and *BCOR* ITD) — with tumour cell necrosis, in contrast to the low-grade component (**≤3/10 HPF**, no necrosis). In high-grade transformation of low-grade ESS the high-grade component comprises **10–90%** of the tumour and shows a median of **16 mitoses/10 HPF**. The ovarian page grades only as "high or low grade" without numeric thresholds. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESShighgrade.html); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoress.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/uterusESShighgrade.html ; https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoress.html

## Adenosarcoma

**Microscopy:** A biphasic tumour with a **benign or atypical epithelial component and a low-grade malignant stromal component**, in which conspicuous non-invasive Müllerian-type glands are widely spaced throughout a predominant malignant stroma (90% of cases). **Periglandular cuffs of cellular stroma are present in 80%** and **intraglandular protrusions of cellular stroma in 60%**; either or both may be present. The stroma is usually markedly cellular, resembling endometrial stromal sarcoma or cellular ovarian fibroma, though hypocellular areas occur; at least mild stromal atypia is present in 33%, and stromal cellularity and mitotic count are variable. The stroma may be homologous or heterologous, with sarcomatous overgrowth in 30%, sex cord-like elements in 15% and heterologous elements in 12%. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormullerianadeno.html))

**Immunoprofile:** n.a. — **the page lists no positive or negative immunohistochemical stains** for ovarian adenosarcoma. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormullerianadeno.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No mitotic count, size or grading threshold is stated; the diagnosis rests on the architectural criteria above, with the reported frequencies serving as diagnostic support (widely spaced glands 90%, periglandular cuffs 80%, intraglandular protrusions 60%, stromal atypia 33%, sarcomatous overgrowth 30%). The tumour tends to recur as pure sarcoma or adenosarcoma. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormullerianadeno.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormullerianadeno.html

## Leiomyosarcoma

**Microscopy:** A smooth muscle neoplasm ranging from **well differentiated to highly pleomorphic sarcoma**, diagnosed by the presence of usually **two of three** features: moderate to severe cytologic atypia, **≥10 mitoses/10 HPF**, and tumour cell (coagulative) necrosis. The page does not describe fascicular architecture, cytoplasmic detail, nucleoli or stromal features. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorleiomyosarcoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** n.a. — **no positive or negative immunostains are stated on the page** for ovarian leiomyosarcoma; in the differential, undifferentiated carcinoma is pan-CK positive in areas and H-caldesmon negative. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorleiomyosarcoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **Two of three** criteria: moderate/severe cytologic atypia, **10+ mitoses/10 HPF**, tumour cell necrosis. Cellular fibroma in the differential has low mitotic activity (**<10/10 HPF**). Tumours may be of teratomatous or non-teratomatous ("true" leiomyosarcoma) origin. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorleiomyosarcoma.html))

## Fibrosarcoma

**Microscopy:** Spindle cells arranged in sheets and intersecting fascicles producing a **diffuse herringbone appearance**, with mild to moderate nuclear atypia and **mitotic activity >4/10 HPF**, which is the single most important diagnostic criterion. A reticulin stain separates individual neoplastic cells. Nucleoli, cytoplasmic detail and necrosis are not described on the page. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorfibrosarcoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** n.a. — **no positive or negative immunostains are stated on the page**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorfibrosarcoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **>4 mitoses/10 HPF** is the most important criterion for fibrosarcoma; cellular fibroma shows **<3/10 HPF** and lacks trisomy 8, whereas trisomy 8 supports fibrosarcoma. Tumours are solid, 5–23 cm. No grading rules or percentage thresholds are stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorfibrosarcoma.html))

## Granulosa cell tumour, adult type

**Microscopy:** Small, bland, cuboidal to polygonal cells with scant cytoplasm and pale, uniform, angulated nuclei that are usually **grooved ("coffee bean" nuclei)**, growing in a mixture of patterns — diffuse (commonest), trabecular, corded, insular, microfollicular with **Call-Exner bodies** (small follicle-like structures filled with eosinophilic material) and macrofollicular (least common). The stroma is usually hypervascular with variable fibroblasts and theca cells, and a prominent fibrothecomatous stroma may be present (theca cell proliferation is a stromal response, not a second tumour population). Mitotic activity is usually not brisk (**<3/10 HPF**). Rare findings include focal hepatic cell differentiation (large cells with abundant granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, HepPar1 positive/inhibin negative) and focal degenerative-type bizarre multinucleated cells (2%) that carry no adverse significance. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctadult.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **FOXL2** (positive in 93% of AGCT; 80% sensitive/99% specific for sex cord-stromal tumours), **SF1** (most sensitive), **inhibin A** (more specific), calretinin, vimentin, WT1, CD56 and SMA positive; CD99 70%, S100 50%, low-molecular-weight cytokeratin (CAM5.2/AE1-AE3) 30–60% with a dot-like perinuclear pattern; EGFR 65% of primary and 85% of recurrent tumours. Reticulin shows lack of pericellular staining, highlighting nests of granulosa cells (versus fibrothecoma). No general negative panel is given; hepatic-differentiated areas are inhibin negative. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctadult.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** At least **10% granulosa cells** are required to classify a tumour as adult granulosa cell tumour; **<10%** granulosa cells = thecoma or fibroma with minor sex cord elements. Mitoses are usually **<3/10 HPF**. The **luteinised** variant (1%) is designated when luteinisation is extensive, defined as **>50%** — plump cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, conspicuous nucleoli, **no nuclear grooves** and myxoid/oedematous stroma. When adult and juvenile patterns coexist, classify by the predominant histology. *FOXL2* C134W mutation is present in ~95%. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctadult.html); [PathologyOutlines — JGCT](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctjuv.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctadult.html

## Granulosa cell tumour, juvenile type

**Microscopy:** Diffuse or nodular appearance at low power with **macrofollicle and microfollicle formation containing eosinophilic secretions**, composed of primitive-appearing granulosa cells that are often **luteinised**. The nuclei are round to oval and hyperchromatic with **irregular nuclear contours, small nucleoli and no or only rare nuclear grooves** — in contrast to adult-type granulosa cell tumour. The mitotic rate is high (**mean 11/10 HPF**), bizarre/atypical nuclei are rarely present, and a pseudopapillary architecture may occur. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctjuv.html))

**Immunoprofile:** n.a. — **the page lists no positive or negative immunostains** for juvenile granulosa cell tumour. (In the differential, SCCOHT shows loss of BRG1/SMARCA4 expression.) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctjuv.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Essential criteria are a sex cord-stromal tumour with **primitive granulosa cell differentiation and solid plus follicular growth**; the *FOXL2* **402C>G** mutation is consistently **absent** (versus 95% of adult-type). Mean mitotic rate **11/10 HPF** (no threshold is applied for diagnosis). Almost always occurs under 30 years (80% before 20, 97% before 30; mean 13 years), mean size 12.5 cm, and the majority are stage IA. *DICER1* mutation in **5–10%** (may be germline). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctjuv.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgctjuv.html

## Steroid cell tumour, NOS

**Microscopy:** A diffuse proliferation (occasionally nested, corded, pseudoglandular or follicle-like) of polygonal to rounded cells with distinct cell borders, central nuclei and moderate to abundant cytoplasm that ranges from eosinophilic and granular (lipid-poor) to vacuolated and spongy (lipid-rich); mixtures of both cell types occur and this subtype is the most lipid-rich of the steroid cell tumours. **Lipochrome pigment is present in 40%**; signet-ring appearance, adipocytic metaplasia and hyaline globule formation are unusual. Nuclear atypia is usually absent or slight with small central round nuclei and conspicuous nucleoli, and mitoses are usually **<2/10 HPF**. The stroma is sparse in 85% and may be fibrotic, hyalinised, rarely oedematous or myxoid, exceptionally with calcification and psammoma body formation; **Reinke crystals are absent** (their presence indicates Leydig cell tumour). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html); [Steroid cell tumour review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10861727/))

**Immunoprofile:** **Inhibin and calretinin positive**, MelanA positive; **PAX8 negative** and CD10 negative. In the differential, metastatic melanoma is MelanA/S100 positive but inhibin, calretinin and SF1 negative; metastatic renal cell carcinoma is CD10 positive; oxyphilic struma ovarii is thyroglobulin positive. The page's dedicated positive/negative stain sections are empty; these results are stated in the sample reports and board-review commentary. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** WHO 5th edition defines steroid cell tumour simply as "an ovarian parenchymal tumour comprised of steroid cells", with **no WHO quantitative percentage, size, grading or mitotic criteria**. Usual mitotic activity is **<2/10 HPF** (up to 15/10 HPF in tumours with grade 1–3 nuclear atypia). Stromal luteoma has not been recognised as a distinct entity since the 4th edition (2014); tumours formerly called stromal luteoma and SCT NOS are subsumed under steroid cell tumour. ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10861727/); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10861727/

## Steroid cell tumour, malignant

**Microscopy:** As for steroid cell tumour NOS — sheets/nests of polygonal to rounded cells with eosinophilic granular to vacuolated cytoplasm — but with the constellation of adverse features: **grade 2 or 3 nuclear atypia**, **increased mitotic activity (2 or more per 10 HPF, up to 15/10 HPF)**, **necrosis** and **intratumoral haemorrhage**, in a tumour that is typically **≥7 cm**. Necrosis and haemorrhage are particularly seen in tumours with significant cytologic atypia. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Inhibin, calretinin and MelanA positive; PAX8 and CD10 negative (as for steroid cell tumour NOS). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Malignancy-associated features with their reported rates of malignant behaviour: **≥2 mitoses/10 HPF — 92%**; **necrosis — 86%**; **size ≥7 cm — 78%**; **intratumoral haemorrhage — 77%**; **grade 2 or 3 nuclear atypia — 64%**. **Aggressive behaviour is associated with 4 or more of these features.** No other grading system is stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html

## Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour

**Microscopy:** **Well differentiated** tumours show open or compressed Sertoli cell tubules admixed with clusters of Leydig cells in the intervening stroma; Sertoli cells are low columnar to cuboidal with oval to round, often grooved nuclei and small nucleoli, while Leydig cells have round nuclei, abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, **intracytoplasmic Reinke crystals** and lipofuscin pigment, with no significant atypia or mitotic activity. **Moderately differentiated** tumours have a diffuse or lobulated pattern with alternating hypo- and hypercellularity, Sertoli cells forming compressed tubules, cords or sheets with hyperchromatic oval or spindled nuclei, mild to moderate atypia and an average of **5 mitoses/10 HPF**, with only rare small Leydig cell clusters. **Poorly differentiated** tumours consist of diffuse sheets of immature sarcomatoid Sertoli cells with moderate to marked atypia, only rare vague cord formation, mitoses **up to 20/10 HPF**, necrosis and Leydig cells confined to nodule peripheries. A **retiform** pattern (anastomosing slit-like spaces, multicystic, sieve-like or papillary) occurs in ~15%. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsertolileydig.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Sex cord markers positive — **inhibin, calretinin, SF1, FOXL2, CD56, WT1, CD99** — plus vimentin and pancytokeratin, with MelanA/MART1 in Leydig cells; **CK7 and EMA negative**. Heterologous intestinal-type mucinous elements are CK20/CDX2 positive; hepatoid elements are AFP, arginase and HepPar1 positive. Reticulin is completely absent or nested (versus pericellular in fibroma/thecoma). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsertolileydig.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Graded by differentiation, with mitotic activity paralleling grade — well differentiated: no significant mitoses; moderately differentiated: **average 5 mitoses/10 HPF**; poorly differentiated: **up to 20 mitoses/10 HPF**. **Heterologous elements** (most often intestinal- or gastric-type mucinous epithelium, less often cartilage or skeletal muscle) occur in approximately **20%** of moderately/poorly differentiated and retiform tumours; the **retiform** pattern occurs in ~**15%**. Follicular differentiation in moderately/poorly differentiated tumours may mimic juvenile granulosa cell tumour. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsertolileydig.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsertolileydig.html

## Other sex cord-stromal tumour

The PathologyOutlines "ovarian sex cord-stromal tumour, NOS" topic is an unwritten (pending) stub, so this section is sourced from the specific rarer sex cord-stromal entities that this category covers.

**Microscopy:** **Sex cord tumour with annular tubules (SCTAT):** variably sized round nests and sharply delineated simple and complex tubules containing (and sometimes surrounded by) **basement membrane-like material**; the tubules are lined by tall cells with ample pale cytoplasm and basally located round nuclei showing a characteristic **antipodal nuclear distribution**, with small concentric secondary ring-shaped acini arranged around the circumference of larger tubules. Intratubular calcifications occur (especially in Peutz-Jeghers-associated cases); sporadic tumours may show focal transition to granulosa or Sertoli cell morphology, hyalinisation, and rare atypia or mitoses. ([PathologyOutlines — SCTAT](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsexcordannular.html)) **Microcystic stromal tumour:** a distinctive triad of **microcysts, solid cellular regions and hyalinised hypocellular fibrous stroma**, often multinodular with fibrous, hyalinised or myxoid septa; microcysts are small round/oval spaces that may coalesce into irregular channels with eosinophilic or basophilic contents. The cells have moderate finely granular eosinophilic cytoplasm with frequent intracytoplasmic vacuoles that may impart **signet-ring morphology**, bland round/oval nuclei with fine chromatin and indistinct nucleoli, occasional multinucleation or bizarre hyperchromatic nuclei, and a low mitotic rate. ([PathologyOutlines — microcystic stromal tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormicrocysticstromal.html)) **Gynandroblastoma:** a sex cord-stromal tumour containing both granulosa-theca and Sertoli-Leydig cell components — the page provides no microscopic description beyond this. ([PathologyOutlines — gynandroblastoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgynandro.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Microcystic stromal tumour: **aberrant nuclear/cytoplasmic beta-catenin** (diffuse nuclear beta-catenin is a desirable diagnostic feature) and **diffuse nuclear cyclin D1**, with CD10, WT1, SF1, androgen receptor and FOXL2 positive. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormicrocysticstromal.html)) SCTAT: n.a. — no positive or negative stains are listed on the page. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsexcordannular.html)) Gynandroblastoma: n.a. — no stains stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgynandro.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **Gynandroblastoma** requires **equal numbers of granulosa-theca cells and Sertoli-Leydig cells**; no other numeric threshold, size limit or grading rule is stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgynandro.html)) **SCTAT**: no quantitative mitotic, atypia, lymphovascular invasion or surface involvement threshold is given; bilateral multifocal disease should prompt germline *STK11* testing for Peutz-Jeghers syndrome. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsexcordannular.html)) **Microcystic stromal tumour**: low mitotic rate with no numeric threshold stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumormicrocysticstromal.html))

## Immature teratoma

**Microscopy:** Variable amounts of mature elements derived from all three germ layers admixed with immature elements, which are predominantly **neuroectodermal**. The immature neuroepithelium may be spindled (sarcomatoid) or form **rosettes, pseudorosettes and primitive tubules** (easier to recognise than the spindled pattern); the cells appear primitive with **scant cytoplasm, hyperchromatic nuclei and frequent mitoses**, which distinguishes them from mature brain tissue. Benign vascular proliferation with Wagner-Meissner-like corpuscles in neural tissue and fetal cartilage are **not** immature elements. Peritoneal and nodal gliomatosis nodules consist of mature glial tissue. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html))

**Immunoprofile:** The page provides no dedicated immunostain panel for immature teratoma; diagnosis and grading are morphological. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **Only immature neuroepithelium is graded**, by the number of low-power fields (**4× objective**, i.e. 40× total magnification) containing aggregated immature neuroepithelium **on any one slide**. Original 3-tier system: **grade 1 ≤1 LPF**; **grade 2 >1 but ≤3 LPF**; **grade 3 >3 LPF** (grades 2–3 are high grade). Updated, more reproducible **2-tier system: low grade ≤1 LPF; high grade >1 LPF** on any slide. Peritoneal/nodal gliomatosis of mature glia is **grade 0**. Small foci (**<3 mm**) of yolk sac tumour or embryonal carcinoma do not change prognosis, but the presence of **any** yolk sac tumour or embryonal carcinoma component reclassifies the tumour as a **mixed germ cell tumour**. Thorough sampling is required. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html

## Teratoma with malignant transformation

**Microscopy:** A **confluent growth of a somatic tumour type within a teratomatous background** — a typical mature cystic teratoma or, rarely, an immature teratoma, with elements from all three germ layers — which is definitionally required for the diagnosis; the somatic neoplasm may overgrow the teratoma, leaving only focal teratomatous elements. **Squamous cell carcinoma is the most common somatic malignancy**, defined by infiltrative nests of keratinising or non-keratinising atypical squamous cells, sometimes with cystic degeneration and adjacent dysplasia or carcinoma in situ. Other transformations include cutaneous adnexal tumours (sebaceoma, sebaceous carcinoma), melanoma, mucinous tumours resembling gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma or appendiceal LAMN (with mucin dissecting the ovarian stroma to form **pseudomyxoma ovarii**), thyroid carcinomas, pulmonary/bronchial and neuroendocrine carcinomas, and sarcomas. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarysomaticneoplasmsteratomas.html))

**Immunoprofile:** No single panel applies; the immunoprofile is that of the specific somatic tumour type (e.g. mucinous tumours arising in teratoma may be immunohistochemically identical to gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarysomaticneoplasmsteratomas.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Diagnosis **requires demonstration of background teratomatous elements**; essentially any histologic tumour type may arise. No percentage threshold for the somatic component is stated. Squamous cell carcinoma is the most common type. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarysomaticneoplasmsteratomas.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarysomaticneoplasmsteratomas.html

## Malignant struma ovarii

**Microscopy:** A background of struma ovarii — variably sized macro- and microfollicles often containing colloid, lined by flattened to cuboidal/columnar cells with small round to oval nuclei, even chromatin and pale to eosinophilic cytoplasm, with solid, trabecular, corded or pseudotubular areas, occasional clear cell change, peripheral stromal luteinisation and birefringent calcium oxalate crystals in colloid — containing a thyroid-type carcinoma. **Papillary thyroid carcinoma is the most common**, showing confluent papillary or follicular growth with atypical cells having crowded, overlapping, elongated, optically clear nuclei with irregular contours, chromatin clearing and longitudinal nuclear grooves. **Follicular carcinoma** is the second most common and is difficult to diagnose because ovarian struma typically lacks a capsule and is architecturally disorganised. Undifferentiated (anaplastic) and medullary carcinomas have also been described. ([PathologyOutlines — struma ovarii](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorstruma.html); [PathologyOutlines — somatic neoplasms in teratomas](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarysomaticneoplasmsteratomas.html))

**Immunoprofile:** n.a. — **the struma ovarii page lists no immunostain panel**; thyroglobulin positivity is cited only in the differential diagnosis of oxyphilic struma versus steroid cell tumour. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorstruma.html); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorsteroidcelltumorNOS.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Struma ovarii is a monodermal teratoma composed **primarily (>50%) or exclusively of thyroid tissue**; any mature teratoma containing malignant thyroid tissue is also included. For **follicular carcinoma** arising in struma, evidence of malignancy requires **one or more of: invasion into surrounding ovarian tissue, vascular invasion, or metastases** — architectural disorganisation and papillary formations lacking papillary carcinoma nuclear features are insufficient. Highly differentiated follicular carcinoma of ovarian origin is defined by **extra-ovarian spread of thyroid elements that histologically resemble non-neoplastic thyroid tissue**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorstruma.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorstruma.html ; https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarysomaticneoplasmsteratomas.html

## Dysgerminoma

**Microscopy:** Histologically identical to testicular seminoma: **nests of large, uniform polygonal cells with clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm, distinct cell membranes and an alveolar pattern**, separated by **fibrous septa containing cytotoxic T cells and epithelioid histiocytes** that often spill over into the tumour. Nuclei are centrally located, round to oval with **angulated or squared-off borders**, vesicular to coarse chromatin and prominent single or multiple nucleoli; mitotic figures are numerous. Other patterns include sheets, cords, macronodules, insular growth, microcysts, tubules, pseudoglandular spaces and trabeculae. Plasma cells, eosinophils, Langhans-type giant cells, non-caseating granulomas and lymphoid follicles with germinal centres may be present; extensive haemorrhage, necrosis and dystrophic calcification may occur. Rare cases contain scattered syncytiotrophoblastic cells without cytotrophoblast. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumordysgerminoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **SALL4 (nuclear), OCT3/4 (nuclear), D2-40 (membranous/cytoplasmic), CD117/KIT (membranous), PLAP and NANOG positive**. In the differential, yolk sac tumour is OCT3/4-, CD117- and D2-40-negative. No comprehensive negative panel is provided on the page. Serum LDH is elevated in **95%**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumordysgerminoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No mitotic, size or grading threshold applies. Large areas of dystrophic calcification **without** necrosis should prompt thorough sampling for an underlying **gonadoblastoma**, and syncytiotrophoblastic cells require thorough sampling to exclude choriocarcinoma. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumordysgerminoma.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumordysgerminoma.html

## Yolk sac tumour

**Microscopy:** Multiple patterns coexist with one or two predominating. The **reticular/microcystic** pattern (commonest) is a loose meshwork of anastomosing channels and variably sized macro- and microcysts lined by primitive tumour cells with clear to eosinophilic cytoplasm, sometimes flattened and deceptively bland or with signet ring-like lipid vacuoles, in a loose hypocellular or myxoid stroma. The **endodermal sinus** pattern shows an anastomosing network of labyrinthine spaces with **Schiller-Duval bodies** — rounded to elongated papillary structures with a central fibrovascular core containing a single vessel, surrounded by tumour cells projecting into a cystic/sinusoidal space, resembling immature glomeruli. Solid, papillary (brisk mitoses, hyalinised fibrovascular cores), festoon and glandular (endometrioid, intestinal, hepatoid, foregut) patterns also occur. Cytoplasm ranges from pale eosinophilic to clear, nucleoli are prominent, and **intracellular eosinophilic hyaline globules** and basement membrane-like material are characteristic; extramedullary haematopoiesis and mono- or multinucleated tumour giant cells may be seen. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoryolksac.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **SALL4** positive (primitive germ cell differentiation); **AFP highly specific but only 60% sensitive**, often patchy/focal and weak; **glypican 3** less specific but stronger; PLAP, HNF1β and pancytokeratin positive; **GATA3** positive in reticular/microcystic, papillary and polyvesicular vitelline patterns but not glandular; HepPar1 in glandular and hepatoid patterns; CEA and albumin in hepatoid; TTF1 in foregut/respiratory; CDX2 in intestinal glandular; villin in reticular and intestinal glandular. Yolk sac tumour is **OCT3/4, SOX2, CD117 and D2-40 negative** (distinguishing it from dysgerminoma and embryonal carcinoma). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoryolksac.html); [PathologyOutlines — embryonal carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **Schiller-Duval bodies are the hallmark but are present in only 20–75% of cases and their absence does not exclude the diagnosis.** No mitotic or size threshold applies. Any yolk sac tumour component within an immature teratoma reclassifies the tumour as a mixed germ cell tumour (foci <3 mm do not alter prognosis). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoryolksac.html); [PathologyOutlines — immature teratoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoryolksac.html

## Embryonal carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Sheets and nests of **large, primitive, pleomorphic cells with abundant cytoplasm**, round nuclei with coarse nuclear membranes, **at least one prominent nucleolus** and brisk mitotic activity, with tumour cell necrosis; occasional papillae and glandular formations occur and **hyaline globules** similar to those of yolk sac tumour may be present. **Syncytiotrophoblast-like cells** are seen around sheets of tumour cells or within stroma and are hCG positive. It is often admixed with other malignant germ cell tumour components. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **CD30 (membranous) is the most specific marker**; OCT3/4 (nuclear, more sensitive than CD30 but also positive in dysgerminoma), SOX2 (nuclear), SALL4 (nuclear) and AE1/AE3 positive; βhCG positive in syncytiotrophoblastic cells; AFP may stain tumour cells and hyaline globules. **Glypican and CD117 negative.** CD30 expression may decrease after chemotherapy. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No mitotic, size or percentage threshold is stated. Presence of an embryonal carcinoma component within a teratoma or other germ cell tumour reclassifies the tumour as a **mixed germ cell tumour**; isolated syncytiotrophoblastic cells alone do not constitute a choriocarcinoma component, but extensive sampling is needed to exclude it. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html); [PathologyOutlines — immature teratoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html

## Gonadoblastoma

**Microscopy:** A non-invasive neoplasm (germ cell neoplasia in situ) composed of multiple **nests or cords of admixed germ cells and small sex cord-stromal cells surrounding deposits of basement membrane-like material**. The germ cells vary — some with round nuclei, prominent nucleoli and clear/pale cytoplasm resembling dysgerminoma cells, others with less cytoplasm and inconspicuous nucleoli resembling spermatogonia. The sex cord cells are bland with angulated, occasionally grooved nuclei and inconspicuous cytoplasm, arranged in peripheral palisades, around eosinophilic material in a **Call-Exner-like pattern**, or around germ cells in a follicle-like pattern. The eosinophilic basement membrane-like material calcifies, early calcifications being **psammomatous** and later ones forming larger **mulberry-like** aggregates; in involuted ("burnt out") lesions only the calcifications remain. The gonadal stroma is non-specific spindle cell stroma with occasional Leydig-like cells that **lack Reinke crystals**. Dissecting/infiltrative patterns (cord-like, anastomosing, solid expansile) are present in **76%**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgonadoblastoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Germ cells **OCT3/4, PLAP, CD117 and podoplanin positive** (immature germ cells express OCT3/4, mature germ cells TSPY1, with a small subpopulation expressing both); sex cord cells **inhibin, cytokeratin, calretinin, FOXL2 (strong) and SF1 positive**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgonadoblastoma.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Gonadoblastoma is a **non-invasive lesion (germ cell neoplasia in situ)** and is staged as **pTis**; mixed dissecting patterns occur in 76% and the solid expansile pattern in particular must be distinguished from dysgerminoma. No mitotic or size threshold is stated. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgonadoblastoma.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorgonadoblastoma.html

## Choriocarcinoma, non-gestational type

**Microscopy:** A trimorphic proliferation of **cytotrophoblast, intermediate trophoblast and multinucleated syncytiotrophoblast arranged around the periphery of blood channels**. Cytotrophoblasts are mononucleated with clearly defined cytoplasmic borders, vesicular nuclei and a distinct nucleolus; syncytiotrophoblasts have basophilic to amphophilic cytoplasm and multiple nuclei. Stroma, mitoses and necrosis are not described on the page. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorchorio.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Cytokeratin, CD10, human placental lactogen and EMA positive, with **hCG positive in syncytiotrophoblast**; **CD31 and CD34 negative**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorchorio.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No numeric or percentage threshold, size limit or grading rule is stated. The distinction is by **origin**: non-gestational choriocarcinoma arises from a germ cell tumour (pure or as part of a mixed germ cell tumour), gestational choriocarcinoma from an ovarian pregnancy (which contains paternal chromosomes and is often aneuploid); after puberty an ovarian ectopic pregnancy cannot be excluded on morphology alone. Represents <1% of primitive ovarian germ cell tumours. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorchorio.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorchorio.html

## Mixed malignant germ cell tumour

**Microscopy:** Two or more distinct malignant germ cell tumour components are recognisable, each with its own characteristic morphology — for example immature neuroepithelium forming rosettes and primitive tubules (immature teratoma), reticular/microcystic channels with Schiller-Duval bodies and hyaline globules (yolk sac tumour), sheets of large pleomorphic cells with prominent nucleoli and brisk mitoses (embryonal carcinoma), nests of uniform polygonal cells with clear cytoplasm in lymphocyte-rich fibrous septa (dysgerminoma), or trophoblast around blood channels (choriocarcinoma). The dedicated PathologyOutlines mixed germ cell tumour topic contains no microscopic description; the morphology above is taken from the individual component topics read. ([PathologyOutlines — immature teratoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html); [yolk sac tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoryolksac.html); [embryonal carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html); [dysgerminoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumordysgerminoma.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Composite, reflecting each component: SALL4 positive across components; OCT3/4 + CD117 + D2-40 + PLAP (dysgerminoma); AFP + glypican 3 + HNF1β + GATA3, OCT3/4-negative (yolk sac tumour); CD30 + SOX2 + OCT3/4, glypican- and CD117-negative (embryonal carcinoma); hCG in syncytiotrophoblast (choriocarcinoma). ([PathologyOutlines — dysgerminoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumordysgerminoma.html); [yolk sac tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumoryolksac.html); [embryonal carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html); [choriocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorchorio.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** **Any** amount of a second malignant germ cell component qualifies — the presence of **any** yolk sac tumour or embryonal carcinoma component in an immature teratoma reclassifies the tumour as a mixed germ cell tumour, although foci **<3 mm** do not change prognosis. Report the components present with their percentages. ([PathologyOutlines — immature teratoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorteratomaimmature.html); [PathologyOutlines — embryonal carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorembryonal.html))

## Gastrointestinal stromal tumour

**Microscopy:** Most tumours are **spindle cell type**, with epithelioid morphology in about 25% and combined spindle/epithelioid in a minority. The cells have round to elongated nuclei with uncommon nuclear pleomorphism and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm; a **distinctive perinuclear vacuolisation** is characteristic. Architectural features include **nuclear palisading**, perivascular hyalinisation and, in 40–50% of small intestinal GISTs, **skeinoid fibres** — extracellular eosinophilic PAS-positive collagen globules. Myxoid stromal change is rare. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissueGIST.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **KIT/CD117** and **DOG1** positive (DOG1 is useful in KIT-negative cases), CD34 positive, h-caldesmon positive; SDHB expression is lost in SDH-deficient GIST. Negative for desmin, S100, keratin AE1/AE3, ALK, HMB45/MelanA, nuclear beta-catenin and STAT6 (the last distinguishing GIST from solitary fibrous tumour). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissueGIST.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Risk assessment is by **mitotic count, tumour size and anatomic site** rather than a grading system; sarcomatous (high-grade) subtypes show mitotic counts **>4/10 HPF**, and epithelioid sarcomatous GISTs often exceed **20 mitoses/50 HPF**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissueGIST.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissueGIST.html

## Solitary fibrous tumour, malignant

**Microscopy:** Ovoid to fusiform spindle cells with indistinct cell borders arranged **haphazardly ("patternless pattern") or in short ill-defined fascicles**, set in hyalinised to collagenous stroma with cells streaming between collagen bundles, and centred on **dilated, branching, thin-walled hyalinised "staghorn" (haemangiopericytoma-like) vessels**; prominent myxoid change may be present. **Malignant** tumours show a high mitotic count, hypercellularity, cytologic atypia (including round cell or anaplastic morphology and nuclear pleomorphism) and necrosis; the **dedifferentiated (anaplastic)** variant shows abrupt transition to a high-grade sarcoma. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuesft.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **STAT6 nuclear positivity in close to 100%** (surrogate for the *NAB2::STAT6* fusion) and **CD34 diffuse to focal in 85–95%**; CD99 ~70%, BCL2 ~30%, EMA ~30%, actin ~20%. STAT6 and CD34 expression may be **lost in dedifferentiated tumours**. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuesft.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Solitary fibrous tumour is **risk-stratified rather than graded** — the FNCLCC grading system is not applicable. The Demicco (2017) risk model uses **mitotic count, patient age, tumour size and necrosis** to stratify into low, intermediate and high risk of metastasis. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuesft.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuesft.html

## Desmoplastic small round cell tumour

**Microscopy:** Sharply demarcated **nests of small round cells separated by abundant desmoplastic stroma**; in more desmoplastic areas the nests become thin trabeculae or single files of cells. The cells are uniform with small hyperchromatic nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli, scant cytoplasm and indistinct cell borders; **peripheral palisading** of nests is common, and rosette-like structures and pseudopapillary formations may be seen. Rhabdoid or clear cells occur, mitoses are frequent and focal tumour cell necrosis is common. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuedsrct.html))

**Immunoprofile:** Characteristic **polyphenotypic** profile — keratin AE1/AE3 positive (occasionally dot-like), **desmin positive in a dot-like/perinuclear pattern**, **WT1 (C-terminus) strong nuclear positivity**, plus EMA, vimentin, GFAP, neurofilament, synaptophysin and NSE. **Negative for myogenin, MYOD1, S100 and WT1 N-terminus** (the N-/C-terminus discrepancy reflects the *EWSR1::WT1* fusion). ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuedsrct.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** No grading system, mitotic threshold or size criterion is stated; diagnosis rests on the characteristic morphology, polyphenotypic immunoprofile and demonstration of the **t(11;22) *EWSR1::WT1*** fusion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuedsrct.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissuedsrct.html

## Serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC)

**Microscopy:** A non-invasive lesion **confined to the tubal epithelium**, usually in the fimbriated end, showing **epithelial stratification, loss of ciliated cells, high nuclear:cytoplasmic ratio, nuclear pleomorphism and hyperchromasia, prominent nucleoli and loss of nuclear polarity** (which may be focal). **Epithelial fractures with exfoliation of small clusters of epithelial cells** are characteristic. There is no stromal invasion. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/fallopiantubesstic.html))

**Immunoprofile:** **Mutant-pattern p53** — diffuse strong nuclear overexpression, complete absence (null pattern) or cytoplasmic staining — together with an **elevated Ki67/MIB1 proliferation index (suggested cutoff >10%)**. Immunohistochemistry is supportive but not diagnostic on its own; morphology remains the basis of the diagnosis. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/fallopiantubesstic.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Identification of STIC **assigns the fallopian tube as the primary site** for high-grade tubo-ovarian serous carcinoma, **irrespective of the size of ovarian or peritoneal disease**, and STIC itself is staged as **pT1**. Ki67 cutoff of **>10%** is suggested as supportive. The entire fallopian tube, particularly the fimbriae, should be examined (SEE-FIM protocol) in risk-reducing specimens. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/fallopiantubesstic.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/fallopiantubesstic.html

## Peritoneal implants (non-invasive epithelial, non-invasive desmoplastic, invasive)

**Microscopy:** **Non-invasive epithelial implants** consist of hierarchical branching papillary proliferations or cellular epithelial tufts **without underlying stroma**, planted on the mesothelial surface or lying within cystic mesothelial invaginations, with a **smooth, non-destructive epithelial-stromal interface**; the cytology matches that of the conventional serous borderline tumour, with cuboidal to columnar cells, ciliation, mild atypia and psammoma bodies. **Non-invasive desmoplastic implants** show true papillae together with clusters or single **hypereosinophilic cells** that blend into, or appear compressed by, an inflamed **fibroblastic, granulation tissue-like stroma**; despite the reactive-looking stroma they remain **serosa-based with smooth, sharply delineated contours and no stromal destruction**, and in the omentum produce infiltrative-looking inflammatory fibrous septation that does **not** engulf or extend into adipose tissue. **Invasive implants** — now classified as **extra-ovarian low-grade serous carcinoma** — show destructive stromal growth apparent at low magnification, haphazard infiltration by solid epithelial nests and micropapillae, **peripheral retraction clefting/halos** mimicking lymphatic spaces, and in the omentum expansile to infiltrative borders that interrupt the normal lobulated fibroadipose architecture. ([PathologyOutlines — serous borderline tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Immunoprofile:** As for serous borderline tumour / low-grade serous carcinoma — **WT1, PAX8, ER and PR positive with wild-type p53** and low Ki67; mutant p53 argues for high-grade serous carcinoma instead. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**WHO quantitative/diagnostic rule:** Implants are classified by the **presence or absence of destructive stromal invasion**, not by size: non-invasive (epithelial or desmoplastic) implants retain a smooth serosa-based interface, while **invasive implants are diagnosed as low-grade serous carcinoma** in the 2020 WHO classification and carry the adverse prognosis. An **autoimplant** is a deposit on the ovarian surface, usually of desmoplastic non-invasive type, and is not counted as extra-ovarian disease. **Lymph node involvement is staged as N1 rather than as an implant** and does not by itself worsen prognosis. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html))

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ovarytumorserousborderline.html
