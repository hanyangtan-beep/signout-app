# Inflammatory dermatoses by reaction pattern and cutaneous GVHD: morphology for a diagnostic reporting template

*Specimens: skin punch, shave and incisional biopsies. Compiled 11 October 2026. British spelling; quotations keep the original spelling. "n.a." = not confirmed from a fetched source.*

## 1. Sources, scope and conventions

- **Primary standard.** The current Royal College of Pathologists (RCPath) document is *Tissue pathways for dermatopathology* (G075), **version 3, February 2024**. It became active in November 2023 (to be implemented within 3 months), is due for full review in November 2028, and replaces the 2019 2nd edition ([RCPath G075 v3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf); [RCPath G075 landing page](https://www.rcpath.org/resourceLibrary/g075-tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathology.html)). It covers inflammatory dermatoses (§3), immunofluorescence (§4) and scalp/hair specimens (§5). It does **not** cover GVHD, and it does not give entity-level morphological criteria; a text search of the PDF found no mention of "GVHD/graft", "eosinophil", "plasma cell", "Congo" or "C1q" ([RCPath G075 v3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **WHO / CAP / ICCR.** I found no WHO Classification of Tumours volume, CAP protocol or ICCR dataset covering inflammatory dermatoses or cutaneous GVHD morphology. Where the template might have pointed to one of these, the field is **n.a.**
- **GVHD.** The governing document is the NIH 2014 Consensus Pathology Working Group report (Shulman et al., *Biol Blood Marrow Transplant* 2015) ([PMC4359636](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/); [PubMed 25639770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25639770/)).
- **Entity morphology.** These statements come from peer-reviewed reviews, consensus papers and guidelines (S2k/EADV, Delphi). Each statement is linked to the page it was read from. Where sources disagree, both versions are given (see also §21). **"n.a."** means the value could not be confirmed from a fetched source.
- **Margins / lymphovascular invasion.** Neither appears in the RCPath list of required report content for inflammatory biopsies (§3.6). In G075, "margin" appears only in the nail and benign-lesion sections ([RCPath G075 v3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). For inflammatory and GVHD biopsies these fields are therefore **not applicable**.

## 3. Pre-analytic adequacy: biopsy choice, depth, lesion age, transport

**Choosing the lesion and the biopsy type**
- Choose "a recent active lesion without secondary changes, such as excoriation and ulceration" ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Panniculitis and large-vessel vasculitis.** RCPath requires "deeper biopsies, including a sufficient volume of subcutaneous adipose tissue" ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). JDDG recommends a "Deep biopsy (scalpel)" for both panniculitis and vasculitis of larger vessels ([JDDG 2017, Table 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)). J Clin Pathol states that a 6 mm punch or an incisional biopsy "might be necessary in panniculitis or cutaneous lymphoproliferative disorders" ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Punch size and shave biopsies.** "A 2 mm punch biopsy is too small… A 4 mm punch biopsy is preferred… A superficial or shave biopsy should be avoided". The biopsy should represent all four compartments and include hair follicles ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)). *Disagreement:* RCPath accepts a broad shave for dermatitis herpetiformis DIF and a scooped shave ("saucerisation") of an intact bulla ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Alopecia.** JDDG recommends a punch "from the periphery of the alopecic area, including skin that still contains hair follicles; biopsy at an oblique angle in the direction of hair growth" ([JDDG 2017, Table 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)). RCPath describes the Tyler technique on a 4 mm biopsy, and allows a biopsy to be pre-bisected vertically by the clinician, with half sent for each modality ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.2–5.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Fixation.** Biopsies are "almost always received in 10% neutral buffered formalin", with each biopsy in a separate, labelled container ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). JDDG recommends "at least 6–24 hours of tissue fixation" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Immunofluorescence (DIF) biopsies**
- **Site for immunobullous disease.** For light microscopy, biopsy an intact fresh vesicle or bulla, or punch the edge of a large bulla to include intact skin and the blister cavity. For DIF, take **perilesional** skin, "variably defined as being within 5–10 mm of a lesion". Lesional samples (consumption of immunoreactants) or prodromal samples can give false negatives ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Site for connective tissue disease.** Use "established lesional skin". In SLE, "sun-protected non-lesional skin" may be used ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Lesion age in vasculitis.** RCPath: "samples taken for light microscopy should be taken from established (>72 hours) lesions, while samples for immunofluorescence should be from newly established (<24 hours) lesions" ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). *Disagreement:* JDDG says deposits "can be detected in fresh lesions within the first 24–48 hours" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Transport medium.** Use Michel's medium or saline. Formalin contamination must be avoided, "particularly relevant in the assessment of pemphigus, as even brief exposure to formalin can result in false negative results". Michel's specimens "can remain at room temperature for up to 6 months". Salt-split specimens may travel in either medium ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). JDDG adds immediate immersion in liquid nitrogen as an option ([JDDG 2017, Table 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)). *Disagreement within RCPath:* some studies report that 24 h storage in saline at room temperature reduces background IgG; "other reports recommend rapid processing (<24 hours)" ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

## 4. Laboratory handling and levels

- **Macroscopic description.** Record specimen type (punch or incisional) and three-dimensional measurements *including depth*. Comment on the colour of the epidermis. Describe any lesion by size, type (Box 1 terms), borders, colour and shape ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Box 1 definitions (abridged).** Papule: raised, <0.5 cm. Nodule: ≥0.5 cm. Plaque: usually flat-topped, >2 cm. Vesicle: fluid-filled, <0.5 cm. Bulla: ≥0.5 cm. Pustule: usually <0.5 cm, purulent ([RCPath G075 v3 Box 1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Dissection.** "Punch biopsies with a diameter of 4 mm or greater should be bisected longitudinally, while smaller punches can be embedded intact." Intact vesicles should not be cut through. Embed with the skin surface perpendicular to the plane of section. Bisect incisional ellipses along the long axis ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). *Internal inconsistency:* the Figure 1 legend reads "≤4 mm punch biopsy… embedded intact" and "≥4 mm punch biopsy, bisected", so a 4 mm punch falls under both ([RCPath G075 v3 Fig. 1](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- **Levels.** "3 levels should routinely be requested in the first instance. Further deeper levels or serial sectioning of the block are recommended in cases where the histological findings are expected to be focal or subtle" ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). J Clin Pathol considers "three deeper haematoxylin and eosin stained sections and one section stained with periodic acid Schiff (PAS)" sufficient ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Frozen sections.** RCPath: used rarely, for suspected TEN or SSSS (level of cleavage, degree of necrosis), and may be considered in early necrotising fasciitis ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.7](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). *Disagreement:* JDDG calls TEN "the only rational indication for frozen-section studies" in inflammatory skin disease ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Scalp.** Vertical sections show the full skin thickness and the dermoepidermal junction but contain only "4 to 6" terminal follicles per section. Horizontal sections show all follicles and are "of particular use in the assessment of non-scarring alopecias". The HoVert and Tyler protocols combine both planes from one biopsy ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

## 5. What the report must state (RCPath)

**Routine histology (§3.6)**
1. Type of specimen ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
2. Components present (epidermis, dermis, subcutaneous adipose tissue, other tissues such as muscle or cartilage) ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
3. Description of the histological changes ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
4. Any additional investigations (levels and/or special stains) "and what additional information these provided" ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
5. A diagnosis proffered after combining clinical information, photographs and histology ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
6. If no single diagnosis is possible, "a list of potential differential diagnoses" ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

**Immunofluorescence (§4.6)**
1. Direct or indirect test, and the immunoreactants stained ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
2. Positive or negative for each reactant ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
3. If positive: the primary location (BMZ or intercellular in blistering disease; dermoepidermal "lupus band" in connective tissue disease; vessel walls in vasculitis) ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
4. The pattern (linear or granular, confluent or focal, e.g. "granular IgA in dermal papillae… in dermatitis herpetiformis"), and the intensity if more than one reactant is detected ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
5. The reactant type ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
6. The location of any secondary deposits ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
7. Correlation with clinical and light-microscopy findings, a preferred diagnosis or a differential, and consideration of CPC ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
8. Re-biopsy may be recommended when secondary changes prevented a diagnosis ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- A photomicrograph of a positive result "should be considered" ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.5](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- *Typo in the source:* the reactant list in §4.6 reads "(i.e. IgG, IgM, IgG, C3, fibrinogen)" ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.6](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

**Scalp (§5.5)**
- Use a pattern-based approach. For cicatricial alopecia, "most pathologists follow the schema produced by the North American Hair Research Society". Favour a specific diagnosis that addresses the clinical differentials ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.5](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

**Clinicopathological correlation and turnaround**
- Frequent attendance at CPC meetings by pathologists who report inflammatory skin "is mandatory" ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- Turnaround standard: "80% of cases must be reported within 7 calendar days and 90% within 10 calendar days" ([RCPath G075 v3 §8](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- *Audit criteria.* A 2026 audit used these RCPath-derived criteria: lesion morphology and duration on the request; favoured or differential diagnosis; adequate microscopic description; non-specific diagnoses addressing the clinical differentials; documentation of ancillary investigations; documentation of a suboptimal biopsy; documentation of collegial discussion; CPC amendment by addendum; turnaround time ([Cureus 2026 audit](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428490/)).

## 7. Spongiotic pattern

**Definition and tiers.** Spongiotic dermatitis is "defined by the presence of epithelial intercellular oedema". It is subclassified into acute, subacute and chronic "depending on the histological features and the time the biopsy was performed" ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)). Mod Pathol describes the same continuum, "artificially separated into three categories" ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)). No numerical grading exists.

| Tier | Criteria | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | Variable spongiosis and vesicles (proteinaceous fluid with lymphocytes and histiocytes); superficial dermal oedema; perivascular lymphocytes; exocytosis; "no acanthosis or parakeratosis"; eosinophilic spongiosis may occur in contact and atopic dermatitis. Mod Pathol adds a normal basket-weave stratum corneum and visible stretched desmosomes; microvesicles in severe cases; superficial perivascular lymphocytes with histiocytes, eosinophils and "not uncommonly" plasma cells | [J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/); [Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z) |
| Subacute (most frequent) | Mild to moderate spongiosis and exocytosis; irregular acanthosis and parakeratosis; superficial perivascular lymphohistiocytic infiltrate; papillary dermal oedema. Mod Pathol: parakeratosis, acanthosis, diminished granular layer, microvesicles uncommon | [J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/); [Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z) |
| Chronic | Spongiosis mild to absent; pronounced irregular acanthosis, hyperkeratosis and parakeratosis; ± papillary dermal fibrosis; "The PAS stain is essential to exclude fungal infection". Mod Pathol: compact hyperkeratosis, thickened granular layer, superficial dermis "somewhat fibrotic" | [J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/); [Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z) |

**Separating entities within the pattern**

- Routine IHC panel: **n.a.** (none described).

## 8. Psoriasiform pattern

**Classification.** Psoriasiform hyperplasia is "regular" (prototype: psoriasis) or "irregular" (prototype: chronic eczema) ([JDDG 2017, Table 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)). J Clin Pathol tabulates the epidermal and dermal discriminators ([J Clin Pathol 2005, Tables 4–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)). No grading scheme: **n.a.**

| Entity | Discriminating features | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Psoriasis | Hyperkeratosis with "horizontally confluent but vertically intermittent parakeratosis" alternating with basket-weave orthokeratosis; thin granular layer; frequent mitoses; uniform elongated, fused rete; oedematous papillae with dilated capillaries; Munro microabscesses in "about 75%"; few Kogoj spongiform pustules. J Clin Pathol: diffuse parakeratosis, regular acanthosis, thin suprapapillary plate, decreased or absent granular layer, minimal spongiosis; dermis: PVLI, mild oedema, dilated vessels | [Arch Clin Cases 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8565680/); [J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/) |
| Psoriasis vs seborrhoeic dermatitis (scalp) | Favour psoriasis: mounds of parakeratosis with neutrophils, Kogoj micropustules, clubbed and evenly elongated rete, mitoses (≥6/HPF cut-off proposed). Favour SD: follicular plugging, shoulder parakeratosis, prominent lymphocytic exocytosis. IHC (Ki-67, K10, caspase, GLUT-1) not discriminatory | [Ann Dermatol 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4969470/) |
| Psoriasis vs eczema | "The presence of eosinophils in the dermal infiltrate argues against psoriasis in most cases"; neutrophils in stratum corneum "without serum" prompt consideration of psoriasis | [Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z) |
| Lichen simplex chronicus | Compact hyperkeratosis ± focal parakeratosis, thickened granular layer, marked acanthosis; "vertically oriented, thickened collagen bundles in the superficial dermis"; little dermal inflammation. Arch Clin Cases: stellate or multinucleated fibroblasts, papillary fibrosis in vertical streaks, fewer neutrophils in the stratum corneum | [Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z); [Arch Clin Cases 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8565680/) |
| Pityriasis rubra pilaris | Alternating ortho- and parakeratosis in vertical and horizontal directions; shoulder parakeratosis (parakeratotic mounds at follicular ostia); follicular plugging; irregular rete; thickened suprapapillary plates; hypergranulosis; infrequent acantholysis; superficial perivascular lymphocytes. Versus psoriasis: short rete, preserved granular layer, no neutrophils | [Arch Clin Cases 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8565680/); [J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/) |
| Mimics to exclude | Fungal infection (compact hyperkeratosis, neutrophilic exocytosis); secondary syphilis (band-like lymphocytes, numerous plasma cells, endothelial proliferation); scabies (eosinophilic spongiosis, numerous deep eosinophils); MF (atypical lymphocytes along the DEJ, ± Pautrier microabscesses) | [J Clin Pathol 2005, Tables 4–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/) |

**Ancillary tests.**
- Psoriasis, pustular psoriasis and LSC: "PAS stain with diastase predigestion Gram and Gomori stainings" ([Arch Clin Cases 2021, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8565680/)).
- Multiple deeper levels "if the histological features in the initial cuts do not correlate well with the clinical history" ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- IHC panel: **n.a.**

## 9. Superficial versus superficial-and-deep perivascular pattern

**Definitions.**
- *Superficial perivascular:* the infiltrate is in the papillary or upper reticular dermis ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Superficial and deep:* the infiltrate "is also located in deeper areas of the dermis" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Differentials by depth and epidermal change** (Ackerman-based, [JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176))

| Depth | Epidermis | Differentials |
|---|---|---|
| Superficial | Unaffected | Urticaria; dermatophytosis; drug or viral rash ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial | Spongiotic | ACD; nummular; dyshidrotic; photodermatitis; pityriasis rosea ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial | Psoriasiform | Psoriasis; seborrhoeic or chronic dermatitis; dermatophytosis ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial | Interface | EM; drug rash; pityriasis lichenoides; LP; MF; drug eruption ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial and deep | Unaffected | Lupus tumidus; polymorphic light eruption ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial and deep | Spongiotic | Arthropod reaction; photodermatitis ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial and deep | Psoriasiform | Dermatophytosis; syphilis; MF ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Superficial and deep | Interface | Lupus erythematosus; fixed drug eruption ([JDDG 2017, Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |

**Composition of the perivascular infiltrate** ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/))
- **Lymphocytic:** drug eruption, urticarial reactions, viral exanthem, insect bites, pigmented purpuric dermatoses, gyrate erythemas ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Lymphoeosinophilic:** drug, urticaria, pre-vesicular BP, insect bites, PUPPP ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Lymphoplasmacytic:** adjacent to trauma, ulcer or scar; rosacea; secondary syphilis; erythema chronicum migrans; early connective tissue disease. Plasma cells are non-specific in mucosal biopsies ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Mast cells:** urticaria pigmentosa (toluidine blue) ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Lymphohistiocytic:** drug, viral, HIV, leprosy. ZN, GMS, PAS and Fite are advised ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Neutrophilic:** neutrophilic urticaria, DH, early linear IgA, early Sweet, early lupus, early herpes, AGEP. "Leucocytoclastic vasculitis should be ruled out in all cases of neutrophilic dermatosis." ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/))

JDDG adds that a diffuse dermatitis rich in plasma cells "should always raise suspicion for a pathogen-induced skin disease (for example, borreliosis, syphilis, or leishmaniasis)" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Depth as a discriminator** ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3881081/))
- Drug eruptions with a wholly lymphocytic infiltrate "usually affect only the superficial dermis". The infiltrates of borrelia infection, polymorphous light eruption, pernio and tumid lupus "usually affect the entire dermis" ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3881081/)).
- Lupus is "a superficial and deep perivascular vacuolar interface dermatitis composed of lymphocytes only" ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3881081/)).

**Tumid lupus.** "Intense perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate in the superficial and deep dermis, in addition to abundant mucin deposition, without the presence of epidermal alterations and interface dermatitis" ([An Bras Dermatol 2023 CLE review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10173173/)).

**Limit of the method.** A superficial perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate without epidermal change cannot be resolved into drug versus viral rash "merely on morphological grounds" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)). A very sparse superficial perivascular infiltrate "is physiologic", and no meaningful differential can be given ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3881081/)).


## 10. Interface pattern — definitions and the vacuolar/lichenoid split

**Definitions**
- *Interface dermatitis:* "Degeneration and necrosis of basal keratinocytes with vacuolization of the basement membrane zone; lymphocytes along the dermoepidermal junction" ([JDDG 2017, Table 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Vacuolar subtype:* prototype erythema multiforme, with "only sparse lymphocytes in the papillary layer" ([JDDG 2017, Table 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Lichenoid subtype:* prototype lichen planus, with a "dense, band-like lymphocytic infiltrate" ([JDDG 2017, Table 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Vacuolar change.** "A mild inflammatory cell infiltrate along the dermoepidermal junction, with vacuolar change within the basal keratinocytes". Individual necrotic, mainly basal keratinocytes (colloid or Civatte bodies) are frequent ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).

**Lichenoid change.** "A confluent, band-like, dense accumulation of inflammatory cells in the papillary dermis, mainly consisting of small lymphocytes and a few histiocytes along or hugging the dermo–epidermal junction". It is often accompanied by vacuolar degeneration and apoptotic bodies ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).

**Civatte bodies.** "Homogeneous, periodic-acid–Schiff-positive and diastase resistant, eosinophilic rounded bodies of 10–25 μm in diameter, resulting from degeneration and apoptosis of keratinocytes, particularly in the lower layers of the epidermis" ([Indian J Dermatol 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8208273/)).

**Entity lists (J Clin Pathol Table 2).**
- *Vacuolar:* viral exanthems, phototoxic dermatitis, acute radiation dermatitis, EM, EM-like drug eruption, FDE, LS, erythema dyschromicum perstans, LE, DM, acute GVHD ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- *Lichenoid:* LP, LPLK, lichenoid actinic keratosis, lichenoid drug eruption, LE, chronic GVHD, lichen nitidus, pigmented purpuric dermatosis, lichen amyloidosus, PLC, HIV, syphilis, MF, urticaria pigmentosa ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).

**Clues from the stratum corneum and the infiltrate.**
- Parakeratosis "is usually absent in lichen planus"; "confluent parakeratosis is a clue to pityriasis lichenoides" ([Indian J Dermatopathol Diagn Dermatol 2019](https://journals.lww.com/ijdd/fulltext/2019/06010/_interface__dermatoses__revisited.2.aspx)).
- Lymphocytes plus neutrophils ± eosinophils are "usually seen in drug reactions" ([Indian J Dermatopathol Diagn Dermatol 2019](https://journals.lww.com/ijdd/fulltext/2019/06010/_interface__dermatoses__revisited.2.aspx)).
- Predominant plasma cells suggest acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans or syphilis ([Indian J Dermatopathol Diagn Dermatol 2019](https://journals.lww.com/ijdd/fulltext/2019/06010/_interface__dermatoses__revisited.2.aspx)).

**Severity tiers used in drug-eruption work.** "Severe" versus "mild" vacuolar interface dermatitis. Mild = "only subtle vacuolar changes at the dermo-epidermal junction and few, if any, necrotic keratocytes" ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3881081/)). No formal grading exists outside GVHD (§20): **n.a.**

## 13. Vasculitis and vasculopathy

**Definitions and lesion age**

- **Vasculitis vs vasculopathy.** A vasculopathy is an occlusive vascular disorder with "only secondary vascular inflammation", whereas vasculitis "constitutes primary vessel inflammation" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Vessel size and level.** Small-vessel vasculitis is found "throughout the dermis"; larger-vessel vasculitis is in the subcutis ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Acute lesions.** Inflammatory cells sit inside the lumen and within the vessel wall, sometimes with destruction of the wall and intramural or intraluminal fibrin ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Longstanding lesions.** Features include vessel-wall fibrosis and luminal occlusion ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Leukocytoclastic vasculitis (LCV).** Neutrophilic infiltration of vessel walls, nuclear dust, fibrinoid degeneration, endothelial oedema and red-cell extravasation around postcapillary venules. In older lesions neutrophils decrease and lymphocytes predominate ([Front Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9532537/)).
- **Infiltrate type as a clue.** Granulomatous vasculitis was reported more often with systemic vasculitis. Lymphocytic vasculitis is commoner in connective tissue disease, viral infection and drug eruptions ([Front Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9532537/)).
- **When histology develops.** Typical changes develop "24–48 h after the appearance of lesions". A "deep punch biopsy or excisional biopsy reaching the subcutis is recommended" ([Front Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9532537/)).
- **Excluding vasculitis in neutrophilic dermatoses.** LCV "should be ruled out in all cases of neutrophilic dermatosis", and multiple deeper H&E sections help ([J Clin Pathol 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1770784/)).
- **Nomenclature.** The current standard is the Dermatologic Addendum to the 2012 Chapel Hill Consensus (D-CHCC). It names ([Arthritis Rheumatol 2018, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29136340/)):
  - cutaneous components of systemic vasculitides (e.g. cutaneous IgA vasculitis) ([Arthritis Rheumatol 2018, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29136340/));
  - skin-limited variants (e.g. skin-limited IgA vasculitis, drug-induced skin-limited ANCA-associated vasculitis) ([Arthritis Rheumatol 2018, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29136340/));
  - cutaneous single-organ vasculitides with no systemic counterpart (e.g. nodular vasculitis) ([Arthritis Rheumatol 2018, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29136340/)).

## 14. Intraepidermal and subepidermal vesiculobullous dermatoses, DIF and salt-split skin

### 14.1 Pattern algorithm

**Intraepidermal blisters, by mechanism** ([JDDG 2017, Table 7](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)):
- *Acantholysis:* pemphigus foliaceus, pemphigus vulgaris, Grover, Darier, Hailey-Hailey, SSSS ([JDDG 2017, Table 7](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Spongiosis:* arthropod bite, dermatophytosis, ACD ([JDDG 2017, Table 7](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Ballooning:* herpes virus infection, pellagra ([JDDG 2017, Table 7](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Subepidermal blisters, by the infiltrate** ([JDDG 2017, Table 8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)):
- *Hardly any infiltrate:* EBA, hereditary EB, cell-poor BP, porphyria cutanea tarda ([JDDG 2017, Table 8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Lymphocytes:* bullous LP, EM/TEN, bullous drug eruption ([JDDG 2017, Table 8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Neutrophils:* DH, LAD, EBA, anti-p200 pemphigoid, bullous LE ([JDDG 2017, Table 8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Eosinophils:* BP, bullous arthropod reaction, pemphigoid gestationis ([JDDG 2017, Table 8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Limits of histology.**
- "A clear differentiation based on histopathology alone is not feasible in pemphigoid diseases" ([Front Immunol 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1363032/full)).
- "Even in BP alone, lymphocyte-rich eosinophil-dominated and neutrophil-rich patterns can be observed" ([Front Immunol 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1363032/full)).

**Pitfalls.**
- Sun-exposed skin may show granular IgM at the BMZ ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5297263/)).
- Lower-leg vessels may stain for fibrinogen ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5297263/)).
- Serration pattern analysis was only feasible "in approximately 75% of all samples" and "cannot be performed in mucosal biopsies" ([Front Immunol 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1363032/full)).

**Salt-split skin.**
- *Method:* 1 M NaCl for 24 h splits through the lamina lucida ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5297263/)).
- *Results:* BP binds the roof (70%) or roof and floor (30%). The floor is bound in EBA, anti-laminin-332 MMP, anti-p200 pemphigoid and bullous SLE ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5297263/)).
- *JDDG summary:* BP roof, EBA base ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- *Transport:* salt-split specimens may travel in Michel's medium or saline ([RCPath G075 v3 §4.3.2](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

## 15. Granulomatous dermatitis

### 15.1 Definition and granuloma types

**Definition.** "According to A. B. Ackerman, an inflammation has to be considered granulomatous, if at least 50 % of the infiltrate is made up of histiocytes/macrophages" ([JDDG 2015, Part 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.12610)).

**Morphological granuloma types:** xanthogranuloma, sarcoidal, foreign body, tuberculoid, palisaded, suppurative, plasma-cell granuloma ([JDDG 2015, Table 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.12610)).

**Types with typical diseases** ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)):

| Granuloma type | Description | Typical diseases |
|---|---|---|
| Tuberculoid | "Typically rimmed by lymphocytes" | Tuberculosis, leishmaniasis ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Sarcoidal | "No or only scarce lymphocytic infiltrates" | Sarcoidosis, orofacial granulomatosis ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Palisading | "Mucin and fibrin deposits at the center… surrounded by radially arranged histiocytes" | GA, NL, gout ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |
| Suppurative | "Central neutrophil accumulations" | Atypical mycobacteriosis, deep mycosis, foreign-body granuloma ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)) |

**Further type definitions** ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354400/)):
- *Tuberculoid:* epithelioid histiocytes with Langhans and foreign-body giant cells, ± central caseation, fusing and surrounded by lymphoid cuffing ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354400/)).
- *Foreign-body:* foreign-body giant cells with haphazard nuclei, ± endogenous or exogenous material. That material is "not demonstrable in some cases" ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354400/)).

**Excluding infection.** Special stains are ZN, modified ZN and Fite (AFB), PAS and Grocott (fungi), and Giemsa (leishmaniasis) ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354400/)). Culture is "the gold standard" but useful in limited cases; PCR is an adjunct ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354400/)). A worked educational case ran AFB, PAS and Gram stains, cultures and polarisation before concluding a non-infectious, non-foreign-body cause ([Acad Pathol 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6920582/)).

## 16. Panniculitis

### 16.1 Approach and adequacy

**Three-step approach** ([Adv Rheumatol 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42358-019-0077-5)):
1. Mostly septal or lobular ([Adv Rheumatol 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42358-019-0077-5)).
2. Vasculitis present or absent ([Adv Rheumatol 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42358-019-0077-5)).
3. Type of infiltrate and the presence and pattern of necrosis ([Adv Rheumatol 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42358-019-0077-5)).

**Lesion age.** Long-standing lesions overlap septal and lobular, so "fresh lesions are therefore best suited" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Biopsy.**
- EN should be confirmed "by a deep incisional or excisional biopsy with a generous portion of subcutaneous fat" ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7938036/)).
- RCPath requires sufficient subcutaneous fat (see §3) ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

## 17. Sclerosing dermatoses

### 17.1 Definitions

- **Fibrosis:** "a proliferation of fibroblasts and collagen fibers in association with a loss of elastic fibers" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Sclerosis:** "a decreased number of fibroblasts accompanied by an increase in the number or density of collagen fibers" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- **Fibrosclerosis:** the combination of the two ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

The ISSVD defines sclerosis as fine cross-linked collagen forming a pink "ground glass" upper dermis, and fibrosis as "the replacement of thin collagen fibers with thick ones" ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).

### 17.2 Morphoea

**Adequacy.** "For complete histological representation, sampling should include the subcutis" ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)). The MAC cohort took 4 mm punches at the erythematous border of inflammatory lesions, or from central sclerosis when there was no clinical inflammation ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)).

**Morphology by stage and subtype** ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)):
- *Active inflammatory phase:* thickened collagen bundles parallel to the surface. Lymphocytes, eosinophils, plasma cells and histiocytes lie between collagen bundles, around vessels and around adnexa ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).
- *Late phase:* avascular, thick-walled vessels and densely packed collagen ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).
- *Deep morphoea:* sclerosis and hyalinisation extending into fascia, fat or muscle ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).
- *Pansclerotic morphoea:* lymphocytes, plasma cells and pansclerosis throughout dermis and panniculus ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).
- *Eosinophilic fasciitis:* inflammation and fibrosis of the fascia and lower subcutis. Early lesions contain monocytes, plasma cells and eosinophils; later there are few or no eosinophils ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).
- *Limitation:* biopsies "do not permit to distinguish between morphea and skin manifestation in systemic sclerosis" ([JDDG 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).

**Grading scheme** (MAC V, [J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)). The authors state that "patterns of sclerosis and severity of inflammation should be included in pathology reports".

| Parameter | Tiers and exact criteria |
|---|---|
| Pattern of sclerosis | **Top-heavy:** hyalinised collagen exclusively in the papillary to superficial reticular dermis. **Bottom-heavy:** hyalinised collagen in the deep dermis and subcutis, sparing papillary to mid dermis. **Full-thickness:** thickened collagen throughout the dermis ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)) |
| Global sclerosis score | Severe / moderate / mild (previously published GSS; cut-offs not restated) ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)) |
| Inflammation | **Mild:** mild perivascular infiltrate. **Moderate:** dense perivascular infiltrate. **Severe:** dense infiltrate (perivascular, periadnexal, interstitial or at the dermal–subcutaneous junction) taking on the appearance of a round "nodule" ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)) |
| Cell types | Plasma cells, lymphocytes, eosinophils "present" if >5 at low power ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)) |
| Location | Perivascular (>10 cells around capillaries), periadnexal (>10 cells), interstitial, dermal–subcuticular junction, septal, lobular ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)) |
| Clinical correlate | Bottom-heavy pattern associated with pain and tightness (P = 0.0039 and 0.001) ([J Am Acad Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5438266/)) |

**Clinical classification.** Limited (plaque, guttate, atrophoderma of Pasini–Pierini, deep), generalised (generalised, disabling pansclerotic), linear (extremities, head) and mixed. Eosinophilic fasciitis is listed within the classification ([JDDG 2025, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12548314/)).

### 17.3 Lichen sclerosus

**Classic and late lesions.**
- Classic LS is "a lichenoid tissue reaction combined with stromal sclerosis" ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).
- Late-stage LS shows fibrosclerosis, with homogenisation/hyalinisation of collagen and fewer fibroblasts and elastic fibres ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Early LS.** Interface dermatitis without papillary homogenisation. Features: lichenoid lymphocytes, exocytosis, dyskeratotic cells at multiple levels, BMZ thickening, superficial dermal fibrosis, perivascular hyalinisation, irregular to psoriasiform hyperplasia ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).

**Early LS vs LP** ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)):
- LP lacks BMZ thickening and acanthosis ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).
- LP dyskeratotic cells concentrate at the DEJ, with limited exocytosis ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).
- LP rete are sawtoothed ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).
- *Note:* the "no acanthosis" statement refers to vulvar biopsies; for cutaneous LP other sources describe sawtooth acanthosis (§12) ([J Dermatol Case Rep 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5439688/)).

**Early LS vs MF.** Favour LS when there is focal pronounced sclerosis, intraepidermal lymphocytes confined to the lower half and basal vacuolisation. A monoclonal T-cell population is "rarely encountered" in early LS ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).

**Non-sclerotic LS (ISSVD consensus)** — four subtypes ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)):
1. Lichenoid dermatitis ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).
2. Hypertrophic lichenoid dermatitis: acanthosis, basal degeneration, ± papillary fibrosis, parakeratosis, suprabasal apoptosis, spongiosis. Cannot readily be separated from hypertrophic LP ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).
3. Dermal fibrosis without acanthosis: horizontally oriented fibrosis; main differential is scar ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).
4. Dermal fibrosis with acanthosis: confluent horizontal fibrosis, sometimes "single-file" lymphocytes. LSC shows perpendicular papillary fibrosis instead ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).

Thickened BMZ supports LS in 11–44% but is hard to separate from a thin band of sclerosis on H&E and PAS ([J Low Genit Tract Dis 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545066/)).

**Pre-analytic (S3 guideline)** ([JDDG 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13059072/)):
- Biopsy an untreated, typical whitish lesion, or the end of a fissure or the edge of an erosion (not its middle) ([JDDG 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13059072/)).
- If treatment has started, pause for three weeks; otherwise "inform the pathologist about the type of pretreatment" ([JDDG 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13059072/)).
- Early lesions may lack upper-dermal hyalinisation ([JDDG 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13059072/)).
- Any new hyperkeratotic lesion, erosion or ulcer should be biopsied to exclude HPV-independent (differentiated) VIN ([JDDG 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13059072/)).

**IHC for sclerosing disease.** CD34 (altered distribution of CD34-positive dermal cells) is listed by RCPath ([RCPath G075 v3 §3.5](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)). The p53 panel for dVIN is outside this scope: **n.a.**

## 18. Folliculitis and scalp

**Definitions.**
- "Folliculitis means the presence of inflammatory cells in the wall and lumen of a hair follicle"; superficial folliculitis involves the upper portion. There is "no official classification yet". The Herman (modified Ackerman) scheme separates superficial folliculitis (infectious, non-infectious) from perifolliculitis. Classification by infiltrate (neutrophilic, lymphocytic, eosinophilic, mixed) is also possible ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412046/)).
- Folliculitis is "a superficial or deep suppurative inflammatory response". Differentials include dermatophytosis, furuncle and secondary syphilis ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Deep trichophytia.**
- Biopsy a fresh lesion with hair shafts, because dermatophytes are found "in hair shafts but not in the affected tissue" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).
- Examine all hair shafts with PAS, "sometimes in numerous serial sections" ([JDDG 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddg.13176)).

**Infiltrate by cause** (99 scalp cases; [Dermatol Pract Concept 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412046/)):
- *Mixed:* dermatophytes, Candida, Malassezia, acneiform eruption, rosacea ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412046/)).
- *Neutrophilic:* bacterial, herpes zoster and pox virus ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412046/)).
- *Eosinophilic:* Ofuji (eosinophilic pustular folliculitis) ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412046/)).
- *Commensal overgrowth* (Malassezia, Demodex): a perifollicular reaction of lymphocytes, neutrophils and occasional multinucleated histiocytes ([Dermatol Pract Concept 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412046/)).

**Scarring alopecia.**
- Classification is neutrophilic, lymphocytic or mixed ([J Clin Med 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11856343/)).
- RCPath: "most pathologists follow the schema produced by the North American Hair Research Society" ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.5](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).
- LPP and DLE both show dense perifollicular lymphocytes with "subtle histopathologic differences" ([J Clin Med 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11856343/)).
- Lichen planus follicularis tumidus vs LE: LE has increased mucin ([Dermatopathology 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8161827/)).

**Biopsy and sectioning.**
- Biopsy the periphery of the patch: the centre shows only scarring ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12419723/)).
- *St John's protocol:* two 4 mm biopsies. Non-scarring: both horizontal. Scarring: one horizontal, one vertical, with half of the second sent in Michel's medium for DIF ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12419723/)).
- In LPP and DLE the infiltrate at the DEJ "may be missed in horizontal sections" ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12419723/)).
- *Disagreement on follicle numbers:* a vertical 4 mm punch holds "2 to 3 follicles" ([Indian Dermatol Online J 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12419723/)), whereas RCPath says vertical sections show "4 to 6" terminal follicles ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.3](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

**DIF and IHC (scalp)** ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)):
- Perifollicular IgM cytoid bodies suggest LPP ([RCPath G075 v3 §5.4](https://www.rcpath.org/static/88bfeeaf-7b1c-458e-be2019c1324cd7ac/G075-Tissue-pathways-for-dermatopathologyfor-publication.pdf)).

**Notes on the wording** (all [Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)):
- "Likely GVHD" is "synonymous with probable, favor or suggestive", and the pathologist should add qualifiers as needed ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- A biopsy read as "likely GVHD" is sufficient to establish chronic GVHD if there is at least one distinctive clinical feature ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- **Wording differs within the paper.** ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/))

  - The abstract says "no GVHD, possible, and likely GVHD" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
  - The abstract describes merging "consistent with and definite GVHD"; the body and Table 2 say "consistent with" and "unequivocal" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Table footnotes** ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)):
- Lesser degrees of change result from "immunosuppressive treatment, biopsy very soon after onset of signs, suboptimal or small tissue sample, insufficient serial sectioning, confounding infection, drug reaction or inflammatory conditions" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- After diagnosis or treatment, active chronic GVHD "may meet only minimal diagnostic criteria for activity" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- Different chronic manifestations "may all be present together in one biopsy" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Further criteria in the text** ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)):
- *Minimal criterion:* "apoptosis within the basilar or lower spinosum layers of the epidermis". In lichenoid disease "some element of inflammation should be present" for a confident diagnosis ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- *Archetypal pattern:* "superficial interface dermatitis with vacuolar change predominantly in the basilar layer or a lichenoid pattern of lymphocytic inflammation with or without lymphocyte satellitosis". "No single histological feature is pathognomonic" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- *Lichen planus-like chronic GVHD:* acanthosis, orthohyperkeratosis and parakeratosis, hypergranulosis, a band-like DEJ infiltrate, extensive basal apoptosis and vacuolisation, saw-toothed rete and periadnexal inflammation. "Especially when accompanied by lymphoplasmacytic inflammation around the eccrine coils", this is "highly specific for chronic GVHD, but has low sensitivity". The dermatopathology subcommittee regarded "compact orthohyperkeratosis, hypergranulosis and acanthosis with shortened or saw-toothed rete ridges" as favouring lichenoid chronic GVHD ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- *Sclerotic chronic GVHD:* suggested minimal criterion is "homogenization (sclerosis) of most of the papillary dermis or reticular dermis or subcutaneous septa" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
  - Morphoea-like and diffuse forms: reticular dermis or septa, with little epidermal involvement ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
  - LS-like form: papillary dermis, with mild vacuolar change, melanophages and sparse lymphocytes ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
  - Fasciitis: "only fibrous thickening of the fascia with adjacent inflammation without epidermal or dermal involvement" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- *Activity after treatment:* "residual apoptotic changes in the epidermis or appendages" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- *Busulfan:* keratinocyte dysmaturation "can persist for many months" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- *Screening biopsies:* biopsies of normal skin at days 80–100 "often contain rare isolated keratinocyte apoptotic bodies"; their interpretation "depends on an institution's minimal diagnostic criteria" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Use and limits of grading:**
- Grade II "is most commonly encountered". In 120 patients, grade "did not correlate with survival". "Chronic GVHD is not graded with the Lerner system" ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).
- With the Horn-adapted Lerner scheme (I mild, II moderate, III/IV severe), histological grade III/IV carried a higher non-relapse mortality than grade I (HR 3.85). Clinical grade was not associated with histological grade ([JAMA Dermatol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11840680/)).
- One study used a separate 0–3 interface-alteration scale ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4966921/)).
- NIH 2014, in the GI section, notes that the Lerner and Sale schemes "combine diagnostic criteria with the extent and chronologic stage of disease". The committee "was divided" on routine grading. If grading is used, "the site with the greatest damage or highest histological grade should be noted" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

### 20.7 Differential diagnosis

**Drug eruption and viral exanthem.** All three show "vacuolar interface change, dyskeratosis, and dermal infiltrate that may include eosinophils", so biopsy has limited value early ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12085335/)). Lymphocyte satellitosis supports GVHD but "can occur in drug reactions" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Timing, engraftment syndrome and eruption of lymphocyte recovery.**
- Engraftment syndrome "histologically resembles acute GVHD", occurs 10–14 days after transplant, and "it is rare to see acute GVHD prior to 14 days" ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).
- Engraftment syndrome occurs several days before engraftment (ANC >500/µL for 3 consecutive days), typically 1–3 weeks post-transplant, with frequent pulmonary involvement ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12085335/)).
- Eruption of lymphocyte recovery falls in the same 1–3 week window, without organ involvement ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12085335/)).
- A morbilliform eruption at days 20–40 was the strongest risk association for aGVHD ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12085335/)).
- A rare hyperacute GVHD occurs within 2 weeks ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5797560/)).

**Spongiotic dermatitis.** Marked spongiosis with only a rare apoptotic keratinocyte "likely excludes the diagnosis of GVHD" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Lichen planus vs chronic lichenoid GVHD.** The GVHD infiltrate is "less robust", with more eosinophils and plasma cells ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)). A perieccrine lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate "is a specific feature of GVHD", and follicular (bulge) involvement occurs in both acute and chronic forms ([Dermatopathology 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8161827/)).

**Sclerotic GVHD vs morphoea/scleroderma.** Distinguished "largely … by the clinical setting rather than histology" ([Mod Pathol 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41379-019-0400-z)).

**Ancillary tests.**
- FISH for donor cells works only in sex-mismatched transplants ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12085335/)).
- Dermal CD163+ macrophages correlated with steroid refractoriness (research finding) ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- No routine skin IHC panel is recommended: **n.a.**

### 20.8 Biopsy, request information and report content

**Biopsy** ([Hillen JEADV abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jdv.12737)):
- Punch biopsy is adequate in non-sclerotic GVHD; a scalpel biopsy is preferred in sclerotic GVHD ([Hillen JEADV abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jdv.12737)).
- Most centres favour biopsy before topical or systemic immunosuppression ([Hillen JEADV abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jdv.12737)).
- 88% considered biopsy indicated in suspected chronic GVHD lacking diagnostic features; 62% in acute GVHD ([Hillen JEADV abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jdv.12737)).

**Normal-looking skin.** After allogeneic HSCT it may resemble GVHD histologically in about 30% ([Am J Clin Dermatol 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5797560/)).

**Report items supported by NIH 2014** ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)):
- Final category (Not / Possible / Likely GVHD), with alternative diagnoses and reasons when "Possible" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- Chronic pattern(s) present (LP-like, morpheic, LS-like, fasciitis) ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- Activity (residual apoptosis), with a note of treatment status ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Further report fields from the Hillen consensus:** **n.a.** (full text not accessed).

### 20.9 GI GVHD grading (brief, for a later GI form)

**NIH 2014** ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)):
- Minimal criterion: "Variable apoptotic criteria (≥1/piece) in crypts" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- A threshold of ≥6/10 consecutive crypts was judged "too insensitive". The previously recommended >1–2 apoptotic bodies per piece raises sensitivity ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- Examine "at least 8, and up to 20, serial sections" ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- Caspase-3 IHC is research-only ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- Increased eosinophils favour MMF injury over GVHD ([Shulman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).

**Grading** ([AJCP 2016](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/145/5/591/2195645)):
- "No collectively accepted grading system for gut GVHD" exists; most centres use a modified Lerner scheme ([AJCP 2016](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/145/5/591/2195645)).
- Grade on the worst area, and describe Paneth cell loss (small bowel) and crypt dropout (colon) ([AJCP 2016](https://academic.oup.com/ajcp/article/145/5/591/2195645)).
