# Graft-versus-host disease in gastrointestinal and oral biopsies: morphology for a reporting template

*Specimens: oesophageal, gastric, duodenal, ileal, colonic and rectal mucosal biopsies, oral mucosal biopsies and lip biopsies with minor salivary glands, after allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplantation. Compiled 14 October 2026. British spelling; quotations keep the original spelling. "n.a." = not confirmed from a fetched source. Cutaneous GVHD is reported on the Dermatoses form (`docs/reference/skin_inflammatory-morphology.md` §20).*

## 1. Sources and scope

- **Governing document.** NIH 2014 Consensus Pathology Working Group report, Shulman et al., *Biol Blood Marrow Transplant* 2015 ([PMC4359636](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)). Table 1 sets the organ criteria and Table 2 the three diagnostic categories.
- **Gut grade.** The four-tier histological grade as tabulated in the *Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol* review of acute gut GVHD ([PMC6240460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6240460/)), and the Lerner grades on [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colongvhd.html).
- **Oral chronic GVHD.** NIH 2014 Table 1, and the oral mucosa study in *Einstein (São Paulo)* 2014, which restates the NIH sampling rule ([PMC4891164](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4891164/)).
- **Oesophagus.** Textbook chapter excerpt (Elsevier eLibrary): "Histologic features of esophageal graft-versus-host disease are similar to those in the skin and include basal epithelial cell apoptosis, mucosal atrophy, and submucosal fibrosis without significant acute inflammatory infiltrates" ([Elsevier eLibrary](https://elsevier-elibrary.com/contents/fullcontent/58947/epubcontent_v2/OEBPS/B9781437707922500225.htm)).

## 2. Minimal criteria by site (NIH 2014, Table 1)

| Site | Minimal criteria for acute / active GVHD | Specific criteria for chronic GVHD |
|---|---|---|
| Gastrointestinal | "Variable apoptotic criteria (≥1/piece) in crypts" | "Destruction of glands, ulceration or submucosal fibrosis may reflect severe or long-standing disease but are not specific for chronic GVHD" |
| Oral / oropharyngeal mucosa | "Lichenoid interface lymphocytes with infiltration of mucosa (exocytosis) and variable apoptosis" | none listed |
| Minor salivary gland | none listed | "Periductal lymphocytic infiltrate with infiltration and damaged intralobular ducts, fibroplasia in periductal stroma, mixed lymphocytic and plasmacytic inflammation with destruction of acinar tissue" |

Source: [NIH 2014, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/). The oral footnote: "Inflammation of the oral mucosa and within the minor salivary glands may persist from prior chemo-irradiation or prior inflammation. The distinction between acute and chronic GVHD requires the addition of distinctive oral manifestations." The salivary footnote: activity "relies on assessing lobules that are not completely fibrotic"; "Acinar and periductal inflammation with features of damage to ducts, such as vacuolar change, lymphocytic exocytosis nuclear dropout, dyspolarity or apoptosis, and resultant fibroplasia indicate chronic GVHD activity."

In the gut, "histological changes are not helpful in distinguishing chronic GVHD from acute GVHD" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)). The form therefore prints no acute or chronic label on a GI part unless the clinical phase is chosen. A salivary gland part with the Table 1 changes reads as chronic, the specific criterion.

## 3. Diagnostic categories (NIH 2014, Table 2)

| Category | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Not GVHD | "No evidence for GVHD" | |
| Possible GVHD | "Evidence of GVHD but other possible explanations" | "Obvious CMV enteritis with inclusions near the apoptotic changes"; "Focal colonic ulcers with marked apoptotic cryptitis and destruction of crypts associated with use of MMF"; "Clinical features which suggest or favor a drug reaction". Comment: "Indicate possible alternate diagnoses and reasons for suspicion" |
| Likely GVHD | "Clear evidence of GVHD without a competing cause of injury OR Clear evidence of GVHD with mitigating factors OR GVHD most likely diagnosis but relevant clinical information is limited …" | "Abundant epithelial apoptosis without clinical or histological evidence of drug injury or infection"; "Evidence of CMV yet abundant apoptotic epithelial changes that are not associated with CMV infected cells by immunostaining"; "Limited sample or minimal or focal findings"; "Proximity to recent chemotherapy or radiotherapy" |

"Likely GVHD" combines the former "consistent with" and "unequivocal" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)). Recent conditioning is a mitigating factor under Likely, not a reason for Possible. The form's differential sentence ("The differential diagnosis includes …") carries the Possible comment.

## 4. Gut grade

| Grade | Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol, Table 2 | PathologyOutlines (Lerner) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Isolated apoptotic epithelial cells without crypt loss" | "crypt apoptosis without crypt dropout" |
| 2 | "Crypt necrosis, withering and individual crypt loss" | "single crypt dropout" |
| 3 | "Contiguous areas of multiple crypt loss" | "contiguous crypt dropout" |
| 4 | "Extensive crypt dropout with denudation of the epithelium" | "diffuse crypt dropout with ulceration" |

Sources: [PMC6240460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6240460/); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colongvhd.html). PathologyOutlines adds that the "Utility of histologic grading is questionable because of poor correlation with patient outcome" and that "When grading is performed, the Lerner system is most commonly utilized". The form prints the grade as "(grade 1)" to "(grade 4)", the same as the skin form's "(Lerner grade II)" but in arabic numerals as in the gut table. Grade is optional ("Not graded").

## 5. Apoptosis threshold: where the sources disagree

- NIH 2014: "≥1/piece" in crypts ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- "Others propose > 6 apoptotic bodies per 10 contiguous crypts for definitive diagnosis", and "Cases with ≤ 6 apoptotic bodies per 10 contiguous crypts are considered indeterminate" ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colongvhd.html), citing AJSP 2013 and Histopathology 2016).
- Lip biopsy: "Nakhleh et al. used a threshold of >3 mucosal apoptotic bodies" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- The form records apoptosis in words (isolated, at least one per piece, numerous, exploding crypts) and prints no threshold.

## 6. Pitfalls the Checks name

- **Superficial apoptosis.** "Apoptotic debris limited to the superficial epithelium and lamina propria is a non-specific finding and should not be used to diagnose GVHD" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- **Eosinophils.** "the presence of increased number of eosinophils within the lamina propria and epithelium should favor a diagnosis of MMF-induced injury over GVHD" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- **Serial sections.** "At least 8, and up to 20, serial sections should be analyzed in order to avoid missing infrequent apoptotic changes" ([NIH 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359636/)).
- **Conditioning.** Chemotherapy or radiation changes "Usually resolve within 20 days" ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colongvhd.html)).
- **CMV.** Inclusions "via immunohistochemistry or on H&E; always helpful to rule out in cases of suspected graft versus host disease" ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colongvhd.html)).
- **Salivary sampling.** Oral biopsies should "include epithelium and five lobules, at minimum, of minor salivary glands" ([PMC4891164](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4891164/)).

## 7. House wording (biopsy export, aggregate only)

- Fewer than 10 released GI biopsies in the 2021-2026 biopsy file name graft-versus-host disease, and none in the LIS export diagnoses; there is no oral example.
- The GI example prints the site line in capitals ending ":" ("ASCENDING COLON, biopsy:") and the clause as findings, then "in keeping with GRAFT VERSUS HOST DISEASE". The microscopy opens "Serial sections show a piece of colonic mucosa …", names "Apoptosis … within the basal cryptal epithelium", states "No viral cytopathic change or granuloma is seen", and closes with the overall features and "Please correlate with clinical findings."
- The form keeps that shape and takes the NIH qualifier and the hyphenated name from the Dermatoses form ("likely GRAFT-VERSUS-HOST DISEASE"), so skin and gut parts of one patient read alike.

## 8. What prints and what stays in the Checks

- **Prints.** The site, the number of pieces, the mucosa, the apoptosis and crypt findings in words, Paneth cell and eosinophil findings, viral change and the CMV stain, the oral lichenoid and salivary findings, the NIH category as the qualifier, the grade when given, and the differential.
- **Checks only.** The thresholds (≥1 per piece, >6 per 10 crypts, >3 apoptotic bodies), the serial-section count, the 20-day conditioning window, the five-lobule rule, and the MMF and eosinophil note.
