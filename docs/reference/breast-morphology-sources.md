# Breast tumour entities — defining microscopic features (source-linked)

Scope: microscopic (histological) defining features of breast tumour entities as classified in the WHO Classification of Tumours: Breast Tumours, 5th edition, as reflected in expert-authored online sources. Every descriptor below was taken from a page fetched in this session, and the fetched URL sits next to it. Where a fetched page did not state something, the field reads **n.a.**

British spelling is used in the connecting prose; direct wording taken from sources retains its original form.

---

## 1. Invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (IBC-NST)

**Microscopy:** Architecture varies from sheets, nests, clusters and cords to individual cells, with margins ranging from highly infiltrative and permeating the surrounding tissue to continuous pushing margins ([PathologyOutlines – invasive carcinoma NST](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)). Tumour cells may have abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm or clear, foamy or granular cytoplasm, and nuclei range from regular and uniform to highly pleomorphic with prominent or multiple nucleoli, with mitoses from virtually absent to extensive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)). Large solid nests or syncytial infiltrative growth show little associated stroma and compress the surrounding stroma, whereas small cancer nests are often accompanied by marked fibrosis producing a desmoplastic/scirrhous, irregularly shaped spiculated mass ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)). Tubule formation is prominent in well differentiated and absent in poorly differentiated tumours; calcification occurs in 60% of cases, necrosis is variable, and elastosis involves stroma, vessel walls and ducts ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)). DCIS is present in up to 80% of cases, usually of the same nuclear grade as the invasive component, there is no myoepithelial cell lining, and angiolymphatic invasion occurs in 35% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Positive for luminal low molecular weight cytokeratins, pancytokeratins (AE1/AE3, CAM 5.2, MNF-116), EMA, E-cadherin, p120, GATA3, mammaglobin, milk fat globule, lactalbumin, BCA225 and TRPS1; the invasive component is negative for the myoepithelial markers p63, CD10 and calponin ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)). ER, PR and HER2 are variable and not definitional ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** NST comprises 75–80% of invasive breast carcinomas; in mixed tumours <10% special type is classified as IBC-NST (with an option to describe the focal pattern), >90% special type is classified as that special type, and 10–90% special type is classified as mixed IBC-NST and special subtype ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html

## 2. IBC-NST with specific morphological patterns

The 5th edition places these patterns within IBC-NST: "medullary pattern, invasive carcinoma with neuroendocrine differentiation, carcinoma with osteoclast-like stromal giant cells, pleomorphic pattern, choriocarcinomatous pattern, melanocytic pattern, oncocytic pattern, lipid-rich pattern, glycogen-rich clear cell pattern, sebaceous pattern" ([WHO classification history review, PMC8138497](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/)). The same source records that pleomorphic carcinoma, carcinoma with osteoclast-like giant cells, and choriocarcinomatous and melanocytic features have been subsets of the NST category since the 3rd edition ([PMC8138497](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/)). The corresponding list of special patterns is also given on the NST topic page ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)).

### 2a. Pleomorphic carcinoma pattern

**Microscopy:** A high-grade carcinoma characterised by "distinctive pleomorphic giant tumor cells exhibiting bizarre nuclei and atypical mitosis", with proliferation of pleomorphic and irregular tumour giant cells, many of which are large and multinucleated, set in a background of adenocarcinoma or adenocarcinoma with spindle or squamous differentiation ([Pleomorphic carcinoma of the breast, three cases, PMC11197633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197633/)). The tumour cells have unusually large and irregular nuclei and atypical mitoses ([PMC11197633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197633/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. — the fetched review states that immunohistochemical profiles varied between malignant components without specifying a defining marker; the entity has been associated with increased HER-2 expression and triple-negative phenotype, and adjuvant therapy depends on tumour markers rather than pleomorphic histology ([PMC11197633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197633/)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Pleomorphic/irregular giant cells constitute more than 50% of the tumour cells ([PMC11197633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197633/)).

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197633/

### 2b. Carcinoma with osteoclast-like stromal giant cells

**Microscopy:** Osteoclastic giant cells lie in the stroma, associated with vascular stroma containing extravasated red blood cells and haemosiderin, together with chronic inflammatory cells and fibroblasts; the giant cells are of variable size and contain variable numbers of nuclei, and the same features are seen in nodal metastases and recurrences ([PathologyOutlines – osteoclast-like giant cells](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastosteoclast.html)). The carcinoma component may be of any type ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastosteoclast.html)). A fetched case report adds that the infiltrative tumour was composed of ducts, small nests and a cribriform pattern with embedded multinucleated osteoclast-like giant cells, with background red blood cell extravasation and haemosiderin deposition, and that inflammatory giant cells are associated with an inflamed, fibroblastic, hypervascular stroma containing extravasated erythrocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes and mononucleated/binucleated histiocytes that sometimes contain haemosiderin ([case report, PMC10011994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011994/)). The unique stromal feature can be found within invasive, ductal, lobular, squamous or papillary breast carcinomas ([PMC10011994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011994/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** The giant cells are CD68, acid phosphatase, lysosome, non-specific esterase, MMP9, TRAP and cathepsin K positive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastosteoclast.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. — no threshold is stated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastosteoclast.html)); the pattern constitutes only 0.5–1.2% of breast carcinomas ([PMC10011994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011994/)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastosteoclast.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011994/

### 2c. Choriocarcinomatous pattern

**Microscopy:** Highly pleomorphic cells with a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio and prominent nucleoli, with syncytiotrophoblast-like multinucleated giant cells (cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts) admixed with high-grade invasive ductal carcinoma; the tumour mass contains necrotic and haemorrhagic tissue ([breast carcinoma with choriocarcinomatous features, PMC3800734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/)). The pattern must be distinguished from metastatic choriocarcinoma, in which most patients are pregnant, previously pregnant, or have a history of hydatidiform mole or choriocarcinoma ([PMC3800734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/)). Under the WHO classification this represents a distinct variant of invasive carcinoma of no special type and is not classified as a common metaplastic carcinoma ([PMC3800734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** The choriocarcinoma-like tumour cells react with human placental lactogen and hCG; only the choriocarcinoma-resembling cells were hCG antibody positive, and they were negative for HER2/neu, ER and PR ([PMC3800734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/)). Caveat from the same source: isolated hCG-immunoreactive cells occur in about 5–21% of ordinary invasive ductal carcinomas without choriocarcinomatous differentiation ([PMC3800734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. — no defining percentage is stated; in the reported case the trophoblast-like cells formed only about 25% of the lesion ([PMC3800734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/)). The dedicated PathologyOutlines topic page for this pattern was fetched but its content is marked "[Pending]".

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800734/

### 2d. Melanocytic pattern

**Microscopy:** No fetched page provided a WHO-style descriptive histology for a carcinoma with intrinsic melanocytic differentiation as an NST pattern; the classification review explicitly records the term without a histological description ([PMC8138497](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/)). What was confirmed: in pigmented breast carcinoma, elongated and dendritic melanocytes carrying abundant melanin colonise the neoplastic epithelial cells that have infiltrated the superficial dermis and penetrated the dermo-epidermal junction, with focal cytoplasmic pigmentation of the neoplastic epithelial cells and melanophages resulting from ingestion of released pigment ([melanocyte colonisation and pigmentation of breast carcinoma, PMC8071371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/)). Rare metaplastic carcinomas with genuine melanocytic differentiation show a melanocytic tumoral area lacking keratin staining but positive for S100 and HMB-45 in the midst of CK-positive metaplastic carcinomatous cells, sometimes with focal squamous, glandular or osseous metaplasia ([PMC8071371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** In colonisation, the carcinoma cells are cytokeratin (AE1/AE3), GATA3, ER and PR positive and Melan-A, S100 and HMB45 negative, with the melanocytic markers positive only in the colonising melanocytes ([PMC8071371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/)). In true melanocytic differentiation, non-pigmented ductal carcinoma cells are CK+ S100− HMB-45− while pigmented cells are CK− S100+ HMB-45+ ([PMC8071371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/)). Melanin is black with Fontana-Masson and unstained with PAS and Prussian blue ([PMC8071371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. ([PMC8138497](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/), [PMC8071371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/)).

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8071371/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/

## 3. Invasive lobular carcinoma, classic type

**Microscopy:** Tumour cells are arranged in single files, cords and single cells, and may be arranged concentrically around normal ducts giving a targetoid appearance ([PathologyOutlines – classic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)). The cells are discohesive, small and monomorphic and lack marked atypia, with round or notched ovoid nuclei, usually nuclear score 1 or 2 ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)). Cytoplasm is scant, occasionally with an intracytoplasmic lumen, and mitoses are infrequent ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)). Desmoplastic reaction and necrosis are uncommon ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)). On grading pages, ILC is described as infiltrative small cells similar in size to adjacent benign glandular epithelial cells, with minimal pleomorphism, arranged in linear files with no tubule formation ([PathologyOutlines – histologic grading](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Absence or reduction of membranous E-cadherin staining — helpful but not required for diagnosis — with abnormal catenin expression including cytoplasmic p120 and lost or aberrant beta-catenin ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Classic ILC comprises about 10% of invasive breast carcinomas; tumour cells are usually nuclear grade 1 or 2 and mitoses are infrequent; E-cadherin was negative in 16% in the cited immunohistochemical data ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html

## 4. Invasive lobular carcinoma — variant (non-classic) patterns

The classic ILC page lists the non-classic patterns as solid sheets or large nests; alveolar clusters and aggregates; tubulolobular carcinoma with small round tubules mixed with classical lobular carcinoma; pleomorphic carcinoma with markedly pleomorphic nuclei; histiocytoid carcinoma with foamy cytoplasm resembling histiocytes; apocrine carcinoma with abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm; and signet ring carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines – classic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).

### 4a. Solid pattern

**Microscopy:** Discohesive lobular-type cells grow as solid sheets or large nests rather than single files ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)). Solid, alveolar and mixed non-classical growth patterns may coexist within a single tumour ([PathologyOutlines – pleomorphic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)).
**Immunoprofile:** As for classic ILC (E-cadherin loss/reduction, cytoplasmic p120) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).
**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).
**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html

### 4b. Alveolar pattern

**Microscopy:** Lobular-type cells form rounded clusters and aggregates rather than single files ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).
**Immunoprofile:** As for classic ILC ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).
**Quantitative / defining rule:** Clusters and aggregates of ≥20 cells ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).
**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html

### 4c. Pleomorphic pattern

**Microscopy:** Tumour cells are rounded, dyscohesive and lack polarity, dispersed individually and in single files, invading along collagen bundles and fibrovascular septa or encircling ducts and lobules in a targetoid pattern ([PathologyOutlines – pleomorphic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)). Compared with classic ILC the cells are larger with greater cytonuclear atypia and a higher mitotic count, and cytoplasm is more abundant and may be eosinophilic, granular, foamy or vacuolated, with apocrine, histiocytoid, signet ring, plasmacytoid or polygonal rhabdomyoblast-like features ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)). Nuclei are enlarged and variable in size and shape with contour irregularities, hyperchromasia, vesicular or coarse chromatin, prominent single or multiple nucleoli, abnormal bilobed, multilobed or multinucleated forms, and atypical mitoses ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)). The background stroma shows dense fibrosis with no or minimal host response; apoptosis, necrosis and lymphovascular invasion may be present, and classical ILC and classic or pleomorphic LCIS are frequently associated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)).
**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** E-cadherin usually negative (helpful, not required), beta-catenin membranous staining absent, p120 cytoplasmic in most cases; myoepithelial markers p63, smooth muscle myosin heavy chain and calponin are absent, supporting invasion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)).
**Quantitative / defining rule:** Pleomorphic nuclei are defined as ≥4 times the size of a lymphocyte, or nuclear pleomorphism equivalent to high-grade DCIS; usually Nottingham grade 2 or 3 depending on mitotic activity; about 15% are E-cadherin positive ([PathologyOutlines – pleomorphic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html), [PathologyOutlines – classic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularclassic.html)).
**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html

### 4d. Tubulolobular pattern

**Microscopy:** An invasive tumour with a mixed pattern of small tubules and a lobular pattern of infiltration within dense collagenous stroma, often with prominent elastosis ([PathologyOutlines – tubulolobular carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html)). The tubular component consists of small round to angulated tubules with low-grade nuclei and variable apical snouts, and the lobular component of single cells and single-file cords, often with targetoid growth around benign ducts ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html)). Cells are uniform with low-grade nuclear atypia, inconspicuous nucleoli and minimal amphophilic cytoplasm; cells with lobular growth may show occasional intracytoplasmic lumina and rare signet ring morphology ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html)). The proportion of tubular and lobular components is variable, associated DCIS or LCIS may be present, and a surrounding myoepithelial layer is lacking ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html)). Described elsewhere as typical areas of ILC with single-file cords merging with small round to angulated tubules with minute or undetectable lumina ([PathologyOutlines – mixed carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmixedNST.html)).
**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** E-cadherin positive in 75–100% (supporting ductal differentiation), 34 beta E12 positive in 93%, alpha/beta/gamma catenin membranous in 100%, p120 positive in 100%; almost all ER and PR positive, HER2 negative; SMMHC, p63, calponin and S100 negative; GATA3, mammaglobin and GCDFP-15 positive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html)).
**Quantitative / defining rule:** 1–2% of invasive breast carcinomas; nuclear grade 1–2 and usually Nottingham grade I; tumours usually <2 cm (range 0.5–2.5 cm, median 1.4 cm); multifocal in about 20% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html)).
**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubulolobular.html

### 4e. Mixed pattern

**Microscopy:** Mixed non-classical growth patterns — solid, alveolar and mixed — may occur within a single tumour ([PathologyOutlines – pleomorphic ILC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlobularpleomorphic.html)). For mixed ductal–lobular carcinoma, the definition fetched is a mixture of ductal carcinoma NOS and lobular carcinoma, with definite features of invasive ductal carcinoma and invasive lobular carcinoma in the same tumour; E-cadherin may show no staining (lobular-like), full staining (ductal-like), or staining of ductal areas only ([PathologyOutlines – mixed carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmixedNST.html)).
**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** E-cadherin patterns as above ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmixedNST.html)).
**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. — no percentage thresholds for the ductal/NST and lobular components are stated on the fetched mixed-carcinoma page, which also notes that "mixed NST" is not part of the WHO breast classification ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmixedNST.html)). The general mixed rule (10–90% special type = mixed) is on the NST page ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)).
**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmixedNST.html

## 5. Tubular carcinoma

**Microscopy:** An infiltrative growth pattern, often invading adipose tissue, with an associated fibrous or desmoplastic stromal response ([PathologyOutlines – tubular carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)). More than 90% of the tumour is composed of small, ovoid or angulated tubules with open lumina, lined by a single layer of cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells with uniform, small to intermediate-sized, low-grade nuclei and sparse mitoses ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)). Tumour cells often show apical cytoplasmic tufting or snouts, and luminal secretions or calcifications may be present ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)). There is no myoepithelium around the tubules and no significant cytological atypia, multilayering or high mitotic activity ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)). Three-dimensional modelling showed a beaded-necklace structure of blebs bridged by solid cords of cells, without continuous or branching tubules; frequent associated non-obligate precursors include columnar cell lesions (particularly flat epithelial atypia), atypical ductal hyperplasia, ER-positive low-grade DCIS, and the Rosen triad of tubular carcinoma, LCIS and columnar cell lesions ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** ER positive, PR positive, HER2 negative, E-cadherin positive, with myoepithelial markers p63, CD10, smooth muscle actin and CK5/6 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** >90% of the tumour must be composed of the characteristic tubules; the tumour is grade 1 with low-grade nuclei and sparse mitoses; significant cytological atypia, multilayering or high mitotic activity excludes the diagnosis; 10–90% tubular morphology should be reported as a mixed tumour, and a tumour with ≥90% tubular carcinoma whose remaining component is nuclear grade 3 is classified as mixed; mean size 1.2 cm; columnar cell lesions in 95% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttubular.html

## 6. Invasive cribriform carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Invasive islands or nests of malignant epithelial cells with round or angulated contours contain well-defined cribriform spaces formed by arches of epithelial cells, giving a sieve-like appearance ([PathologyOutlines – invasive cribriform carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html)). Tumour cells are small with mild to moderate pleomorphism and no nuclear atypia, and show no or sparse mitotic activity ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html)). The stroma is desmoplastic, and mucin-positive secretion and microcalcifications may be present in the lumina ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html)). Osteoclastic giant cells may be seen, myoepithelial cells are absent, and cribriform DCIS may be associated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** ER positive, HER2 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Pure invasive cribriform carcinoma requires cribriform architecture in >90% of the tumour; 10–90% of another morphological type defines a mixed tumour; the tumour is low grade (Nottingham grade 1/nuclear grade I) with no or sparse mitoses; size usually <2 cm, some 2–5 cm ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantcribriform.html

## 7. Mucinous carcinoma (including Capella type A and type B)

**Microscopy:** Clusters and nests of tumour cells with low or intermediate nuclear grade float in pools of extracellular mucin, and the mucin pools are separated by fibrous septa containing capillaries ([PathologyOutlines – mucinous carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html)). Capella type A shows abundant extracellular mucin production with scattered small epithelial clusters, strips or cribriform structures floating in the mucin pools ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html)). Capella type B shows large sheets of tumour cells with mucin production and neuroendocrine features, and may display discohesive clusters with plasmacytoid cells and low-grade nuclei ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html)). A micropapillary pattern may be present, consisting of clusters of tumour cells with intermediate to high-grade nuclei, occasional hobnailing and reverse polarity ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** ER positive (94%), PR positive (80%), HER2 negative (up to 7% positive), MUC2 positive, WT1 positive, GATA3 positive, MUC1 negative, AR often negative; type B is chromogranin, synaptophysin and neuron-specific enolase positive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Pure mucinous carcinoma requires a mucinous component >90% of the tumour; mixed mucinous carcinoma 50–90%; "mucinous features" <50% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinous.html

## 8. Invasive micropapillary carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Tufts of cells are arranged in pseudopapillae, hollow tubules and morulae surrounded by empty clear spaces formed by fibrocollagenous stroma ([PathologyOutlines – invasive micropapillary carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)). The cells show characteristic reverse polarity, also called an inside-out pattern, with the apical surface abutting the epithelial–stromal interface ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)). The tumours are densely cellular with minimal stroma and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, and peripheral nuclei bulge with a knobby or serrated appearance ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)). Extensive true angiolymphatic invasion is present, and foci of necrosis and intracytoplasmic mucin may occur ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)). Uncommon variant features include psammoma bodies, microcystic dilatation of lumina with cell clusters, apocrine differentiation and multinucleated giant cells ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** EMA and CD15s show membranous staining of tumour cells abutting the stroma, demonstrating the inside-out pattern; E-cadherin and p120 catenin show cup-shaped basolateral membranous staining sparing the apical surface ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** More than 90% of the invasive component must be micropapillary carcinoma; extensive true angiolymphatic invasion in 78%; grade is variable, usually 2 or 3; mean size 2 cm (range 0.1–10 cm) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicropapillary.html

## 9. Invasive carcinoma with apocrine differentiation

**Microscopy:** Infiltrating tumour cells have abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm, well-defined cell borders, enlarged round nuclei and prominent nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines – apocrine carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html)). Apocrine morphology is present in >90% of tumour cells and the tumour is typically high grade/poorly differentiated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html)). It is often associated with DCIS showing apocrine features, which retains a myoepithelial cell layer ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html)). The related oncocytic pattern shows tumour cells with abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm, well-defined borders, round centrally located nuclei and prominent nucleoli; apocrine adenosis/atypical apocrine adenosis is a lobulocentric proliferation with apocrine cytology distorted by stromal fibrosis/sclerosis ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** AR positive, ER negative, PR negative, with AMACR positive in the majority of carcinomas with apocrine differentiation ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Apocrine morphology in >90% of tumour cells is required; focal apocrine differentiation may be present in up to 60% of invasive ductal carcinoma NST ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantapocrine.html

## 10. Metaplastic carcinoma — spindle cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Spindle cell metaplastic carcinoma may be a pure (monophasic) sarcomatoid carcinoma or mixed with a squamous or conventional invasive carcinoma component; the spindle-shaped cells may be large and pleomorphic and present as isolated or clustered malignant cells ([PathologyOutlines – metaplastic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html)). The spindle cell component shows a fascicular, storiform or haphazard growth pattern, usually with an infiltrative border; cytological atypia is moderate or marked and mitotic figures are usually easily identified, and foci of necrosis may be present ([spindle cell lesions of the breast review, PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)). Architecture is of variable cellularity — fascicular and storiform, but rarely herringbone — and an associated epithelioid component is frequently poorly differentiated adenocarcinomatous but may be squamous, with associated DCIS not uncommon; in mixed tumours a gradual transition between the spindle cell and epithelioid components is an important finding ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)). Another fetched review describes the spindle cell subtype as poorly cohesive sheets of predominant spindle cell morphology that resemble a low-grade sarcoma or a reactive process such as granulation tissue ([metaplastic breast carcinoma review, PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratins (including high molecular weight cytokeratins) and p63 are used to confirm epithelial differentiation when DCIS or a conventional carcinoma component is lacking; a sample spindle cell carcinoma was pancytokeratin, p63, CK5 and AE1/AE3 positive and CD34 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html)). Cytokeratin expression can be focal or diffuse so a large panel should be used; p63 is typically positive in lower-grade spindle cell and squamous areas but may be positive in high-grade spindle cell areas; there is variable positivity for myoepithelial markers, and tumours are typically negative for CD34, hormone receptors and HER2 ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)). Epithelial differentiation may also be shown by E-cadherin and MUC1 ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Pure and mixed spindle cell metaplastic carcinomas are graded with the Nottingham system according to the highest-grade component ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)). Metaplastic carcinoma overall comprises 0.2–1% of breast carcinomas, with mean gross size 3.9 cm (range 2 cm to >10 cm) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html)). No mitotic-count threshold is stated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/

## 11. Metaplastic carcinoma with heterologous mesenchymal differentiation / matrix-producing carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Heterologous mesenchymal components include chondroid, osseous, rhabdomyosarcomatous, angiosarcomatous, liposarcomatous and neuroglial differentiation, or combinations; the mesenchymal component shows a wide spectrum of atypia from minimal to frankly malignant, and malignant cartilage, bone or osteoclast-like giant cells are rarely identified ([PathologyOutlines – metaplastic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html)). The tumour is a poorly differentiated heterogeneous neoplasm containing ductal carcinoma cells admixed with areas of spindle, squamous, chondroid or osseous elements; the matrix-producing subtype contains overt carcinoma with a transition to cartilaginous and/or osseous stromal matrix without a spindle component ([PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)). Carcinoma with osseous metaplasia is infiltrating carcinoma with osseous differentiation of the mesenchymal component, and carcinosarcoma is infiltrating carcinoma with a malignant mesenchymal component containing both malignant epithelium and malignant stroma ([PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)). A fetched teaching case emphasises direct transition from the glandular component to chondroid/osseous heterologous matrix with no intervening spindle cell or osteoclastic component between the two components ([UPMC case 116](https://path.upmc.edu/cases/case116/dx.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratins and p63 confirm epithelial differentiation when a conventional or in situ component is lacking ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html)). Carcinoma with chondroid metaplasia is immunoreactive for S-100 and patchy for cytokeratin while negative for smooth muscle antigen ([PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. — no percentage threshold for the heterologous component is stated on the fetched pages ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html), [PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)). A fetched classification review notes that the >90% purity rule is not mentioned in the metaplastic chapter, that a mixed metaplastic carcinoma category exists requiring the percentage of each component to be reported, and that a <10% metaplastic component is best noted while the tumour is classified as IBC-NST ([PMC8138497](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplastic.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/ · https://path.upmc.edu/cases/case116/dx.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138497/

## 12. Metaplastic squamous cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** It usually presents as a cystic lesion with a cavity lined by squamous cells of variable nuclear atypia ([PathologyOutlines – squamous cell carcinoma of breast](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html)). Infiltration of surrounding parenchyma occurs as sheets, cords and nests of polygonal cells with pleomorphic hyperchromatic nuclei and moderate to abundant dense eosinophilic cytoplasm, with variable keratinisation and possible spindle cell transformation ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html)). The tumour often elicits a significant stromal reaction with prominent inflammatory infiltrates, and squamous cells may be isolated or aggregated at various stages of maturation ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html)). The acantholytic subtype has a pseudovascular or pseudoglandular appearance due to tumour cell discohesiveness, with squamous-lined spaces containing necrotic debris and keratin ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html)). The pattern is described elsewhere as infiltrating squamous carcinoma with polygonal cells, eosinophilic cytoplasm and possible keratin pearl formation ([PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** EGFR positive in 85%, CK5/6 in 75%, p63 in 70%, and p40 positive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** All, or at least 90%, of the tumour must be composed of malignant squamous cells; pure squamous cell carcinoma is defined when the squamous component comprises >90% of the tumour; tumours often present >4 cm, and 20–40% of reported cases exceed 5 cm ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantscc.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/

## 13. Low-grade adenosquamous carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Infiltrative glandular elements show a variable degree of squamous differentiation ([PathologyOutlines – low grade adenosquamous carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html)). The glands are lined by cytologically bland epithelial cells and may be angulated, compressed or ovoid, with luminal keratin debris sometimes present ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html)). The associated stroma may be fibrotic or cellular, and where cellular, bland spindled cells frequently show a circumferential distribution around the neoplastic glands; epithelial and stromal mitoses are infrequent ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html)). Peripheral lymphoid aggregates and intratumoral lymphocytic infiltrates are frequent, and the lesion may be associated with intraductal papilloma, adenomyoepithelioma, radial or complex sclerosing lesions, collagenous spherulosis and fibroepithelial tumours ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html)). For comparison, adenosquamous carcinoma is described as glands displaying varying degrees of squamous differentiation with pavement-like architecture, prominent intercellular bridges and, to a lesser extent, keratin formation ([PMC3832232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Typically measures <5 cm; no mitotic-count threshold or formal grading rule is stated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenosquamous.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3832232/

## 14. Fibromatosis-like metaplastic carcinoma

**Microscopy:** A bland spindle cell tumour resembling desmoid-type fibromatosis, composed of spindle-shaped fibroblast-like and myofibroblast-like cells arranged in interlacing fascicles with irregular infiltrative margins into surrounding breast parenchyma ([PathologyOutlines – fibromatosis-like metaplastic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplasticfibro.html)). Nuclei vary from thin and spindled with tapered ends to plump, round to oval with discrete nucleoli, with minimal nuclear pleomorphism, and mitotic activity is absent or rare ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplasticfibro.html)). The background stroma may be fibrotic, sclerotic and collagenised, with stromal lymphocytic infiltrates often at the periphery, and in situ and conventional invasive carcinoma components are usually lacking ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplasticfibro.html)). The fetched review adds that the tumour combines spindle cell metaplasia of malignant epithelial cells with bland cytology, pale eosinophilic cytoplasm and slender nuclei with tapered edges and finely distributed chromatin, admixed with plump fusiform and polygonal cells having more rounded nuclei arranged in "epithelioid" clumps mainly centrally, with entrapped normal breast structures and regressive changes including collagenisation and lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with occasional lymphoid follicles at the tumour edge ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)). Nuclear spacing seen in the long fascicles of fibromatosis is typically lacking, and any mild atypia — particularly dark hyperchromatic pleomorphic nuclei — should raise suspicion of this entity ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratin positive (broad-spectrum and high molecular weight antibodies advised as expression may be focal to diffuse) and p63 positive, with ER, PR and HER2 negative and nuclear beta-catenin expression absent ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplasticfibro.html)). Cytokeratin-positive cells usually appear as cords or sheets of polygonal cells, SMA is often positive particularly in cytokeratin-negative cells, and focal weak nuclear beta-catenin may be seen in some (23%) spindle cell metaplastic carcinomas ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Squamous or glandular elements may be present but must be a minor component comprising less than 5% of tumour area; tumour size usually 2–3 cm (range 1–7 cm); mitotic activity absent or rare with no numerical cut-off stated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplasticfibro.html), [PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmetaplasticfibro.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/

## 15. Invasive solid papillary carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Solid epithelial proliferation punctuated by delicate fibrovascular cores with cellular palisading around the cores, showing destructive growth within extralobular stroma; tumour nests have angulated or ragged contours or form irregularly anastomosing islands in a jigsaw-like pattern ([PathologyOutlines – solid papillary carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). Additional clues to infiltrative growth are desmoplastic stroma, engulfment of normal glands or fat, vascular invasion, geographic confluence, expansile mass-forming growth, random distribution and reactive stroma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). Cells are monomorphic and round, oval, plasmacytoid or spindle shaped, with low to intermediate-grade nuclear atypia, mildly enlarged round to oval nuclei, fine chromatin and indistinct nucleoli; nuclear grooves are occasionally conspicuous and mitotic activity is variable ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). Cytoplasm ranges from pale and amphophilic to eosinophilic and granular, intracellular and extracellular mucin are common, and signet ring cells are occasionally seen ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). Fibrovascular cores may be hyalinised and myoepithelial cells are often attenuated or absent along the internal cores ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** ER strong and diffuse, PR variable but often strong and diffuse, HER2 negative; chromogranin and synaptophysin positive in approximately half of cases and INSM1 positive in half (sometimes when chromogranin and synaptophysin are negative); high molecular weight cytokeratins CK5/6, CK14 and 34 beta E12 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. — no percentage threshold or size cut-off is stated; axillary nodal metastasis occurs in 6% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html

## 16. Adenoid cystic carcinoma (classic)

**Microscopy:** A dual population of luminal/ductal epithelial cells — which may show variable glandular, squamous or sebaceous differentiation — and myoepithelial/basaloid cells, arranged in cribriform, solid, tubular and trabecular patterns ([PathologyOutlines – adenoid cystic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)). Two types of lumen are present: true glandular lumina lined by luminal/ductal epithelial cells, and pseudolumens containing eosinophilic cylinders of basement membrane material and lined by myoepithelial/basal-type cells ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)). The classic variant is the most frequent, is of low to intermediate nuclear grade, and is composed of a mixture of three architectural patterns — tubular, cribriform and solid ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)). The solid–basaloid variant shows solid nests, increased mitotic rate, necrosis, Ki67 >30% and prominent basaloid features, and high-grade transformation is associated with a higher-grade component such as IBC-NST ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)). The tumour is histomorphologically identical to its salivary gland counterpart and may be associated with microglandular adenosis ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Luminal/ductal cells are low molecular weight cytokeratin and CK7 positive; myoepithelial/basal cells are p63/p40, smooth muscle myosin, calponin, high molecular weight cytokeratin and S100 positive; pseudoluminal basement membrane material is laminin and collagen IV positive; the tumours generally express CD117/KIT and MYB and are triple negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)). Associated with MYB::NFIB fusion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. for the classic variant; the solid–basaloid variant shows Ki67 >30% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantadenoidcystic.html

## 17. Secretory carcinoma

**Microscopy:** The tumour is well circumscribed with pushing borders but may be infiltrative at the periphery, and central sclerosis may be seen ([PathologyOutlines – secretory carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html)). Architecture is usually microcystic, solid or tubular, or an admixture of all three, and peripheral papillary architecture can occur ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html)). It is composed of tumour cells with abundant vacuolated, foamy or granular cytoplasm and abundant intracellular and extracellular pale blue to dense pink secretions that are PAS positive and diastase resistant ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html)). Nuclei are bland and uniform with low-grade cytological atypia and a low mitotic rate, and an in situ component is often present ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Generally triple negative or focal/weak for ER, PR and HER2; positive for CK5/6, EGFR, MUC4, SOX10, GATA3, S100, CK14 (33%) and focal CD117, with PAS positive diastase-resistant secretions and p63 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** ETV6-NTRK3 fusion gene present in 95% of cases; median tumour size 1.9–2.4 cm; no morphological percentage threshold is stated ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantjuvenile.html

## 18. Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** Cystic spaces are lined by bland, tall columnar cells with abundant intracytoplasmic mucin ([PathologyOutlines – mucinous cystadenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinouscystadenocarcinoma.html)). Tumour cells show stratification, tufting and papillary formation with variable cytological atypia ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinouscystadenocarcinoma.html)). There is gradual loss of intracytoplasmic mucin and transformation to eosinophilic squamoid cells that are focally invasive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinouscystadenocarcinoma.html)). The columnar cell variant has round and convoluted glands in loose aggregates lined by tall columnar mucinous epithelium with basal bland nuclei, and the tumour demonstrates both intracellular and extracellular mucin ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinouscystadenocarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmucinouscystadenocarcinoma.html)).

## 19. DCIS — cytology by nuclear grade

**Microscopy:** DCIS is a neoplastic proliferation of mammary ductal epithelial cells confined within ducts and lobules, with an intact — though sometimes attenuated — surrounding myoepithelial cell layer ([PathologyOutlines – DCIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

- **Low nuclear grade:** monotonous round nuclei with smooth contours, diffuse fine chromatin, absent or indistinct nucleoli, and no or rare mitotic figures ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **Intermediate nuclear grade:** moderate pleomorphism with mild to moderate nuclear size variability, variably coarse chromatin, occasional nucleoli and infrequent mitoses ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **High nuclear grade:** prominent pleomorphism, large nuclei, vesicular chromatin with irregular distribution, prominent nucleoli and frequent mitoses ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

Associated stromal chronic inflammatory infiltrate, fibrosis or sclerosis may be prominent, especially around high-grade DCIS, and cancerisation of lobules represents extension into acini of the terminal duct lobular unit; cytological variants include apocrine, cystic hypersecretory, squamous, spindle cell, clear cell, signet ring cell, mucinous and small cell types ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Surrounding myoepithelial cells are highlighted by p40, p63, SMMS, calponin, CK5/6 and CK5; ductal/acinar cells show strong membranous E-cadherin and p120, whereas reduced or absent membranous E-cadherin with cytoplasmic p120 supports LCIS instead ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Low nuclear grade — nuclei the size of a normal ductal epithelial cell or 1–1.5 diameters of a normal red blood cell; high nuclear grade — nuclei >2.5 times the size of a normal ductal epithelial cell; the page states there is no universally accepted grading system for DCIS ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html

## 20. DCIS — architectural patterns

**Microscopy:**

- **Comedo:** central expansile necrosis containing cellular debris, frequently with coarse microcalcifications ([PathologyOutlines – DCIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **Cribriform:** multiple round, rigid extracellular lumens with a punched-out appearance, evenly distributed polarised cells, trabecular bars and Roman bridges ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **Micropapillary:** papillary fronds and tufts without fibrovascular cores, often composed of uniform club-shaped cells ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **Papillary:** fronds with prominent fibrovascular septa ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **Solid:** ducts or lobules filled with sheets of cohesive, evenly spaced cells ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).
- **Flat / clinging (additional pattern stated):** one to two layers of generally high-grade malignant cells lining a gland with a large empty lumen ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** As for entity 19 — intact myoepithelial layer demonstrable with p40, p63, SMMS, calponin, CK5/6, CK5 ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Extensive intraductal component / extensive DCIS is defined as DCIS constituting >25% of the entire area of the invasive carcinoma with extension beyond the invasive carcinoma into surrounding breast tissue; flat/clinging DCIS is 1–2 cell layers; breast-conserving surgery aims for negative margins ≥2 mm; sentinel node sampling is considered when DCIS is >5 cm or there is a high risk of microinvasion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html

## 21. Encapsulated (intracystic) papillary carcinoma

**Microscopy:** A papillary tumour with pushing borders, often within a cystically dilated duct surrounded by a thick fibrous capsule ([PathologyOutlines – encapsulated papillary carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)). Delicate papillary fronds with fibrovascular cores are lined by cuboidal to columnar epithelial cells with low to intermediate-grade atypia, and cribriform and solid architecture may be present ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)). Most tumours show complete lack of myoepithelial cells along the papillae and around the periphery, although basement membrane markers may be expressed at the periphery ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)). High-grade lesions show nuclear pleomorphism, frequent mitoses and occasional necrosis and are more often ER-negative or triple-negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)). Invasive carcinoma is defined by neoplastic cells infiltrating beyond the fibrous capsule, and epithelial displacement or entrapment within the capsule must be distinguished from true invasion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** ER and PR strong and diffuse; myoepithelial markers p63, calponin, actin, smooth muscle myosin heavy chain and CD10 negative within the papillae and around the tumour, or only focally present at the periphery ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Typical size 1–2 cm; low mitotic activity averaging 3 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, versus a mean of 22 per 10 high-power fields in high-grade lesions (about 3% of cases); if invasion beyond the capsule is present, staging is based on the size of the conventional invasive component rather than the encapsulated papillary carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantencapsulatedpapillary.html

## 22. Solid papillary carcinoma in situ

**Microscopy:** Expansile nodules with smooth rounded contours and a distribution pattern consistent with involvement of the underlying glandular tree, composed of solid epithelial proliferation punctuated by delicate fibrovascular cores with cellular palisading; streaming growth is common in cases with spindle cells and microcystic spaces are occasionally present ([PathologyOutlines – solid papillary carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). Cells are monomorphic, round, oval, plasmacytoid or spindled, with low to intermediate-grade nuclear atypia, fine chromatin and indistinct nucleoli, and cytoplasm from pale amphophilic to eosinophilic granular, with common intracellular and extracellular mucin ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). Myoepithelial cells may be retained, attenuated or absent around the perimeter, and in situ carcinoma is diagnosed even when no myoepithelial layer is seen provided the morphology is consistent with an intraductal process ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)). The DCIS page adds that solid papillary carcinoma shows solid growth punctuated by delicate fibrovascular cores, perivascular pseudorosettes, neuroendocrine features and extracellular mucin ([PathologyOutlines – DCIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantdcis.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** ER strong and diffuse, PR often strong and diffuse, HER2 negative; chromogranin/synaptophysin positive in about half and INSM1 in half; CK5/6, CK14 and 34 beta E12 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** n.a. — no percentage or size rule stated; the sample report records in situ disease spanning at least 1.0 cm ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantsolidpapillarycarcinoma.html

## 23. Paget disease of the nipple

**Microscopy:** Single cells or clusters of cells spread throughout the epidermis ([PathologyOutlines – Paget disease](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)). Paget cells have abundant pale cytoplasm and large irregular nuclei with prominent nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)). The underlying dermis may show chronic inflammation, and the epidermis may show hyperkeratosis and possibly ulceration ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)). Florid cases can show gland formation, and Paget cells may phagocytose melanin, mimicking melanocytes ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)). The underlying carcinoma is most commonly high-grade IBC-NST or DCIS ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** CK7 positive in >90% and HER2 positive in 80–100%; CAM5.2 70–100% positive, with HMB45 and Melan-A each <1% positive and CK5/6 0–2% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Underlying high-grade DCIS or invasive carcinoma is present in >95% of patients (high-grade IBC-NST in 53–64%, DCIS in 24–43%) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpaget.html

## 24. Lobular carcinoma in situ, classic

**Microscopy:** LCIS involves the terminal duct lobular unit, filling and distending the acini, and may grow along the basement membrane of ducts as pagetoid spread producing a convoluted "cloverleaf" pattern ([PathologyOutlines – LCIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)). Classic LCIS consists of monomorphic, evenly spaced, loosely cohesive cells without polarisation or gland formation, with pale to lightly eosinophilic cytoplasm, indistinct cell borders, and small uniform nuclei with evenly distributed chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)). Type A cells have uniform round nuclei whereas type B cells have larger nuclei, more abundant cytoplasm and more prominent nucleoli, and the two may coexist ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)). Intracytoplasmic vacuoles or lumina are present in at least some cells in almost all cases, sometimes containing an eosinophilic globule, and large vacuoles may produce signet ring forms ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)). The myoepithelial layer is retained though possibly attenuated, LCIS may secondarily involve sclerosing adenosis, radial scar, fibroadenoma, collagenous spherulosis or papilloma, and classic LCIS lacks significant nuclear pleomorphism or mitoses and typically lacks comedo-type necrosis, although single-cell apoptosis, minute necrosis and rarely comedo necrosis may occur ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)). Florid LCIS shows marked duct distension in a solid architectural pattern with or without central necrosis ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Most cases show complete loss of E-cadherin staining (attenuated, aberrant, dot-like discontinuous, weak membranous or patchy cytoplasmic expression may occur), with cytoplasmic p120 catenin and loss of membranous beta-catenin in most cases; ER and PR are strong and diffuse, Ki67 is low, HER2 is negative, and myoepithelial markers are retained ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** More than 50% of acini in a terminal duct lobular unit must be filled and expanded; atypical lobular neoplasia applies when fewer than 50% of acini are involved or all acini are involved without significant distension; distension requires at least 8 cells across the cross-sectional diameter of an acinus; type A nuclei are 1–1.5 times and type B nuclei approximately 2 times the size of a lymphocyte ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantlcis.html

## 25. Pleomorphic LCIS

**Microscopy:** Architectural features resemble classic LCIS, with solid proliferation of dyscohesive neoplastic cells within terminal duct lobular units, lack of cell polarity and possible pagetoid spread to adjacent ducts; acini are often substantially expanded but may be only mildly distended or undistended ([PathologyOutlines – pleomorphic LCIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html)). Individual cells are medium to large with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios, moderate to abundant dense eosinophilic or granular cytoplasm, and intracytoplasmic vacuoles with or without mucin that may produce signet ring forms ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html)). Nuclei are large and often eccentric, with moderate to marked pleomorphism, coarse chromatin, distinct small to prominent nucleoli and frequent binucleated or multinucleated cells, and mitoses can be seen ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html)). Central comedo-type necrosis and microcalcifications are common but necrosis is not required, and the apocrine variant shows abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, cytoplasmic granules and prominent nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Loss of membranous E-cadherin, with loss of membranous beta-catenin supporting the lobular phenotype and aberrant cytoplasmic accumulation of p120 ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Nuclei are ≥4 times the size of a mature lymphocyte, with 2–3 times variation in nuclear size and shape; some have suggested calling lobular lesions pleomorphic when at least 10% of tumour cells show high-grade nuclei, but there is no established cut-off; classic type A nuclei are approximately 1.5 times and type B approximately 2 times lymphocyte size, and florid LCIS nuclei 1.5–2 times; PLCIS coexists with classic LCIS in >40% of cases ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantpleolcis.html

## 26. Microinvasive carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Tumour cells are present in the stroma measuring ≤1 mm, usually directly adjacent to DCIS ([PathologyOutlines – microinvasive carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)). Microinvasive carcinoma is usually of no special type, while rare cases are invasive lobular carcinoma associated with LCIS, particularly pleomorphic LCIS ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)). The associated DCIS usually has high-grade nuclei and rarely low-grade nuclei, and the sample report describes high-grade DCIS with cribriform and papillary patterns, comedonecrosis and calcifications ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)). The invasive cells must not appear attached to an adjacent focus of DCIS and no myoepithelial cells are present; multiple foci may be present, often with periductal fibrosis and a dense lymphocytic infiltrate ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)). Invasion into the dermis can occur from DCIS involving nipple skin in Paget disease ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Keratin stains highlight the microinvasive focus; myoepithelial immunostains including p63, SMMS and calponin are negative in the area of microinvasion; E-cadherin identifies ductal carcinoma while its loss identifies lobular carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Invasive carcinoma measuring ≤1 mm, classified AJCC T1mi; multiple foci remain microinvasive provided each individual focus is ≤1 mm and foci must not be summed; a focus >1 mm is T1a, and a 1.1 mm focus must not be rounded down; DCIS <5 mm carries a 3% risk and DCIS >50 mm a 23% risk of microinvasion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantmicroinvasion.html

## 27. Phyllodes tumour, benign

**Microscopy:** A biphasic fibroepithelial lesion with a leaf-like (phyllodal) epithelial pattern, subepithelial stromal condensation and increased stromal cellularity adjacent to the epithelium; the epithelial elements are usually bland and the myoepithelial layer is preserved though it can be attenuated ([PathologyOutlines – phyllodes tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). In the benign category the stroma shows mild atypia, mildly (and possibly focally) increased cellularity, and the tumour border is well defined, with stromal overgrowth absent ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). Cystic degeneration, haemorrhage, stromal hyalinisation, myxoid change and multinucleated stromal giant cells can occur in phyllodes tumours of all histological grades, as can squamous metaplasia and incidental involvement by in situ and invasive carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Stromal atypia mild; stromal cellularity mildly increased, possibly focal; stromal overgrowth absent; mitotic count <5/10 HPF or <2.5/mm²; tumour border well defined; malignant heterologous elements absent; benign tumours constitute 60–75% of phyllodes tumours ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html

## 28. Phyllodes tumour, borderline

**Microscopy:** The same biphasic leaf-like architecture with subepithelial stromal condensation, but the stroma shows moderate atypia and moderately (possibly focally) increased cellularity, with stromal overgrowth absent or very focal and a border that is well defined or focally permeative ([PathologyOutlines – phyllodes tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). Periductal stromal expansion may be predominant, and cystic degeneration, haemorrhage, hyalinisation, myxoid change and multinucleated stromal giant cells may occur at any grade ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Stromal atypia moderate; cellularity moderately increased, possibly focal; stromal overgrowth absent or very focal; mitotic count 5–9/10 HPF or 2.5 to <5/mm²; border well defined or focally permeative; malignant heterologous elements absent; borderline tumours constitute 15–26% of phyllodes tumours ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html

## 29. Phyllodes tumour, malignant (including malignant heterologous elements)

**Microscopy:** A malignant spindle cell proliferation with diffusely increased cellularity, stromal overgrowth, marked stromal atypia and frequent stromal mitotic figures, with a diffusely permeative tumour border; epithelial elements may be scarce where there is extensive stromal overgrowth ([PathologyOutlines – phyllodes tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). Malignant heterologous elements include chondrosarcoma, liposarcoma (except well-differentiated liposarcoma), osteosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma and leiomyosarcoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). Heterologous chondrosarcomatous and osteosarcomatous components may also be seen in metaplastic breast carcinoma, which is a differential consideration for malignant phyllodes tumour ([PMC8983634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Stromal atypia marked; stromal cellularity markedly and diffusely increased; stromal overgrowth present; mitotic count ≥10/10 HPF or ≥5/mm²; border diffusely permeative; the presence of malignant heterologous differentiation directly upgrades a phyllodes tumour to the malignant category regardless of other features; malignant tumours constitute 8–20% of phyllodes tumours; a margin clearance of 10 mm is generally considered acceptable although there is no consensus ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983634/

## 30. Periductal stromal condensation and leaf-like architecture as the defining fibroepithelial feature

**Microscopy:** The phyllodal (leaf-like) epithelial pattern is formed by an exaggerated intracanalicular pattern, with subepithelial condensation and increased stromal cellularity adjacent to the epithelium — the defining architecture of phyllodes tumour ([PathologyOutlines – phyllodes tumour](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). Periductal stromal expansion, termed periductal stromal tumour by some authors, may be the predominant pattern ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)). The epithelial component is usually bland with a preserved (possibly attenuated) myoepithelial layer, and the stromal component determines grade ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Quantitative / defining rule:** Stromal overgrowth is defined as the absence of epithelial elements, with stroma only, in one low-power field ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastphyllodesgeneral.html

## Additional: Nottingham (Elston–Ellis) grading

**Microscopy relevant to scoring:** Tubules and glands must have clear central lumina to be counted ([PathologyOutlines – histologic grading](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)). Nuclear pleomorphism ranges from minimal variation with even chromatin and inapparent to inconspicuous nucleoli, through moderate variation with typically visible but small nucleoli, to marked variation with often prominent nucleoli ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)). Mitotic figures are assessed in the most mitotically active area, particularly at the peripheral leading edge of the tumour, excluding hyperchromatic, karyorrhectic and apoptotic nuclei; central fibrosis or necrosis may be present and central areas may be less well preserved in resection specimens ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)).

**Components and cut-offs:** Tubule/gland formation — 1 point >75% of tumour, 2 points 10–75%, 3 points <10% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)). Nuclear pleomorphism — 1 point minimal variation, nuclei <1.5 times benign epithelial cell nuclei; 2 points moderate variation, 1.5–2 times; 3 points marked variation, >2 times ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)). Mitotic figures per 10 high-power fields are scored according to field diameter: at 0.44 mm, 0–5 = 1 point, 6–11 = 2 points, 11+ = 3 points; at 0.59 mm, 0–9, 10–19, 20+; at 0.63 mm, 0–11, 12–22, 23+ ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)).

**Score-to-grade bands:** Total 3–5 points = well differentiated, grade I; 6–7 points = moderately differentiated, grade II; 8–9 points = poorly differentiated, grade III ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html)). The same components and bands are restated on the NST page ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanthistologic.html · https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantductalnos.html

## Additional: post-neoadjuvant tumour bed and "no residual invasive carcinoma"

**Microscopy:** Histological evidence of tumour response includes fibrosis, hyalinisation, lymphohistiocytic infiltration, haemosiderin-laden macrophages, giant cell formation and vascular proliferation, which together constitute the tumour bed ([PathologyOutlines – treatment effect](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)). Patterns of partial response include concentric tumour shrinkage, reduction in tumour cellularity and scattered multifocal tumour deposits ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)). Residual tumour cells show degenerative changes including nuclear and cytoplasmic vacuolation, karyorrhexis, karyolysis and pyknosis, while normal breast tissue shows ductal and lobular atrophy, atypia and hyalinisation of vessel walls ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)). In lymph nodes, treatment response is shown by fibrosis, mucin pools, histiocytic infiltrates and lymphoid depletion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)); a completely responded node may show fibrous scarring, lymphocytic depletion and histiocytic aggregation (including aggregates of foamy histiocytes) without identifiable tumour cell clusters, and cytokeratin immunohistochemistry helps identify residual metastatic clusters in a regressed node ([Journal of Pathology and Translational Medicine – post-NAT evaluation](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)). Neoadjuvant therapy can alter histological architecture, nuclear features and mitoses, so some cases require comparison with the pretreatment biopsy ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)). Post-treatment samples are best assessed fresh, allowing precise identification and measurement of the tumour bed, i.e. the tissue encompassing the original tumour site ([post-neoadjuvant pathology review, PMC9097800](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9097800/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratin immunohistochemistry to detect residual metastatic tumour cell clusters in regressed lymph nodes ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)).

**Quantitative / defining rules:** Pathological complete response requires no residual invasive carcinoma in breast and axillary nodes; residual DCIS is permitted (ypT0 ypN0 or ypTis ypN0) but residual tumour within lymphovascular spaces is not allowed ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)). AJCC post-treatment categories: ypT0 no residual cancer including in situ carcinoma, ypTis residual in situ carcinoma only, ypT1–4 residual disease ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)). Clinical partial response is a ≥50% reduction in greatest tumour dimension ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)). DCIS and lymphovascular invasion are regarded as treatment-resistant components, and cases whose only residuum is tumour emboli in lymphovascular spaces should not be regarded as pCR ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)). Sampling: when no invasive carcinoma is identified, a minimum of 1 block per cm of pretreatment tumour size or at least 10 blocks in total, whichever is greater ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)); the JPTM review recommends one or two blocks from every 1 cm of pretreatment tumour and at least 10 blocks from the entire specimen, with specimens sliced into ≤5 mm sections to locate the tumour bed ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)).

**Miller–Payne grading:** Grade 1, no change or some minor alteration in individual malignant cells but no reduction in overall cellularity; grade 2, a minor loss of tumour cells but overall high cellularity, up to 30% reduction of cellularity; grade 3, between an estimated 30% and 90% reduction in tumour cellularity; grade 4, marked disappearance of more than 90% of tumour cells such that only small clusters or widely dispersed individual cells remain; grade 5, no invasive malignant cells identifiable in sections from the site of the tumour ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)). The system assesses presence of invasive carcinoma and tumour cellularity, and its pCR definition is no invasive carcinoma in the breast ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)); residual in situ carcinoma is allowed for grade 5 complete response ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html)).

**Residual Cancer Burden:** RCB uses size of the tumour bed in two dimensions, tumour cellularity, and lymph node status (number of involved nodes and size of the largest metastatic deposit), with pCR defined as no invasive carcinoma in breast and lymph nodes; categories are RCB 0 no residual disease, RCB 1 minimal, RCB 2 moderate and RCB 3 extensive residual disease ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)). Residual invasive cancer need not be contiguous, the largest cross-section of invasive tumour is measured in two dimensions, pretreatment cellularity is not considered, and average tumour cellularity should be mentioned ([JPTM](https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02)).

**Source:** https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignanttreatmenteffect.html · https://www.jpatholtm.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4132/jptm.2016.02.02 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9097800/
