# Lung tumour morphology — sourced descriptors (WHO Thoracic Tumours, 5th ed. 2021)

Scope: defining microscopic (histological) features for each lung tumour type, plus STAS and the IASLC 2020 grading system. Every descriptor below comes from a page fetched in this session; the exact URL is given per entry. Quantitative WHO rules are kept in a separate field (intended for code comments, not for report prose). Where a feature could not be confirmed from a fetched page, it is written `n.a.`

Primary sources used:
- PathologyOutlines.com lung tumour chapter pages (individual topic URLs cited per section)
- [Nicholson et al., "The 2021 WHO Classification of Lung Tumors", Journal of Thoracic Oncology (full text)](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext) — also mirrored at [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1556086421033165)
- Selected PMC / peer-reviewed reviews, cited inline

## 1. Adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), non-mucinous

**Microscopy:** Pure lepidic growth only — back-to-back neoplastic cells growing along pre-existing alveolar structures, with no stromal, vascular or pleural invasion and no necrosis. Cytology shows mild to moderate atypia in various combinations: nuclear membrane irregularity, intranuclear pseudoinclusions, nuclear grooves, hyperchromasia, anisocytosis, small nucleoli, increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio and hobnailing; not all features are present in every case. Diagnosable only on a completely sampled resection — not on small biopsy, cytology or frozen section ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma in situ](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO 2022](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Typical lung adenocarcinoma profile — CK7+, TTF-1+, napsin A+, CK20−, p40− ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Size ≤ 30 mm (≤ 3 cm); zero invasion permitted ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma in situ](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 2. Adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), mucinous

**Microscopy:** Extremely rare; purely lepidic, non-invasive proliferation of mucinous cells showing minimal atypia, abundant intracytoplasmic mucin and basally oriented nuclei. Most mucinous tumours with this morphology in fact have invasion and are therefore classified as invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma in situ](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. (as a distinct profile for mucinous AIS); mucinous tumours generally are CK7+, TTF-1−/weak, HNF4α+ ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Tumour ≤ 3 cm with mucinous morphology and no invasion ("In small tumors less than or equal to 3 cm with similar morphology, mucinous subtypes of AIS or MIA (if the size of the invasive component is <5 mm) can be diagnosed") ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma in situ](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 3. Minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), non-mucinous

**Microscopy:** Predominantly lepidic tumour containing one or more small invasive foci; invasion is recognised as angulated glands set in desmoplastic stroma with increased cytological atypia, or as any non-lepidic pattern (acinar, papillary, micropapillary, solid, colloid, fetal). Necrosis, lymphovascular invasion, pleural invasion and spread through air spaces must all be absent ([PathologyOutlines: minimally invasive adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenominimallyinvasive.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** CK7+, TTF-1+, napsin A+, p40− ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenominimallyinvasive.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Total tumour size ≤ 3 cm AND invasive/non-lepidic component ≤ 0.5 cm (5 mm); measure the largest single invasive focus, or with multiple foci sum the percentage of invasive component per section multiplied by greatest tumour dimension (aggregate must remain ≤ 0.5 cm) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenominimallyinvasive.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: minimally invasive adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenominimallyinvasive.html)

## 4. Minimally invasive adenocarcinoma, mucinous

**Microscopy:** Same architectural rules as non-mucinous MIA (lepidic-predominant with a single small invasive focus, no LVI/pleural invasion/necrosis) but composed of mucinous (goblet/columnar) cells with abundant intracytoplasmic mucin and basally located, minimally atypical nuclei; a rare occurrence, as most mucinous tumours are frankly invasive ([PathologyOutlines: minimally invasive adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenominimallyinvasive.html); [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma in situ](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoinsitu.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Mucinous phenotype — CK7+, TTF-1− (or focal/weak), CDX2 focal, CK20 focal, HNF4α+ ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Tumour ≤ 3 cm with invasive component < 5 mm ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PathologyOutlines: minimally invasive adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenominimallyinvasive.html)

## 5. Invasive adenocarcinoma, non-mucinous (overview)

**Microscopy:** Malignant epithelial tumour with morphological or immunohistochemical glandular differentiation and invasion greater than 5 mm; classified by predominant pattern among five patterns (lepidic, acinar, papillary, micropapillary, solid), with each pattern recorded in 5–10% increments. Invasive foci show angulated glands in desmoplastic stroma with increased atypia; necrosis, lymphovascular invasion, pleural invasion and STAS may be present ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [PathologyOutlines: lepidic adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenolepidic.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** TTF-1+, napsin A+, CK7+, pankeratin+; p40/p63/CK5/6 negative. In purely solid tumours, evidence of adenocarcinomatous differentiation is required (TTF-1 or napsin A, or histochemical mucin) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Invasion > 5 mm (otherwise MIA/AIS); patterns recorded in 5–10% increments; for purely solid tumours a D-PAS stain showing mucin in ≥ 5 tumour cells in each of 2 high-power fields (~0.4 mm²) satisfies glandular differentiation ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

### 5a. Lepidic pattern

**Microscopy:** Neoplastic type II pneumocytes and club (Clara) cells proliferating along and lining pre-existing alveolar walls; bland cytology, no architectural complexity, no lymphovascular or perineural invasion. In an invasive tumour the lepidic areas coexist with separate invasive foci ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [PathologyOutlines: lepidic adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenolepidic.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** CK7+, TTF-1+, napsin A+ ([PathologyOutlines: lepidic adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenolepidic.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Lepidic-predominant adenocarcinoma requires invasive component > 0.5 cm (or presence of necrosis, LVI, pleural invasion or STAS); lepidic-predominant tumours > 3.0 cm with ≤ 0.5 cm invasion are still called lepidic adenocarcinoma and staged pT1a ([PathologyOutlines: lepidic adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenolepidic.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: lepidic adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenolepidic.html); [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)

### 5b. Acinar pattern

**Microscopy:** Gland-forming tumour with round to oval glands invading a usually fibrous stroma; the category includes high-grade complex glandular (including cribriform) subtypes ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. (as above for adenocarcinoma generally).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Acinar-predominant tumours are grade 2 provided high-grade patterns are < 20%; complex glandular/cribriform counts as a high-grade pattern ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)

### 5c. Papillary pattern

**Microscopy:** Malignant cuboidal to columnar cells replacing the alveolar lining and forming papillae that contain true fibrovascular cores ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a.

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Papillary-predominant = grade 2 when high-grade patterns are < 20% ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)

### 5d. Micropapillary pattern

**Microscopy:** Ill-defined papillary projections and tufts of tumour cells that lack fibrovascular cores ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a.

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** High-grade pattern — ≥ 20% of tumour makes the tumour grade 3 ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

### 5e. Solid pattern

**Microscopy:** Sheets of neoplastic cells without gland formation, papillae or lepidic growth ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** In purely solid tumours, TTF-1 or napsin A positivity (or histochemical mucin) is required to call it adenocarcinoma ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** High-grade pattern — ≥ 20% makes grade 3; mucin criterion for solid tumours is ≥ 5 mucin-containing tumour cells in each of 2 HPF (~0.4 mm²) on D-PAS ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 6. Invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** Goblet cells or GI-tract-type tall columnar cells with abundant intracytoplasmic mucin and basally oriented, minimally atypical nuclei; usually well differentiated and homogeneous rather than admixed with non-mucinous glands. Growth is often predominantly lepidic with slight distortion/destruction of alveolar architecture, with minor papillary, acinar or micropapillary components; adjacent alveolar lumens are frequently filled with mucin, and spread through air spaces is common ([PathologyOutlines: invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorinvmucadenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** CK7+, HNF4α+ (>90%), MUC2+, PAS+ mucin; TTF-1 and napsin A negative or only weakly/focally positive; CDX2 and CK20 variable/focal ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorinvmucadenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Invasion > 5 mm (below that, mucinous MIA/AIS if ≤ 3 cm); no percentage-of-mucinous-cells threshold is specified ([PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [PathologyOutlines: invasive mucinous](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorinvmucadenocarcinoma.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorinvmucadenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 7. Mixed invasive mucinous and non-mucinous adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** A tumour containing both a mucinous component (goblet/columnar cells with abundant intracytoplasmic mucin, basal nuclei) and a conventional non-mucinous adenocarcinoma component (lepidic/acinar/papillary/micropapillary/solid pattern of pneumocyte-type cells), each forming a substantial part of the tumour ([Mixed invasive mucinous and non-mucinous adenocarcinoma, PMC11081798](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11081798/); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a. (no definitional profile; components show their respective mucinous vs pneumocyte-marker phenotypes) ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** "When both mucinous and non-mucinous adenocarcinoma components comprise over 10 % of the tumor, it is diagnosed as a mixed invasive mucinous and non-mucinous adenocarcinoma" ([PMC11081798](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11081798/)).

**Source:** [PMC11081798 — mixed invasive mucinous and non-mucinous adenocarcinoma](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11081798/); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 8. Colloid adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** Abundant pools of extracellular mucin that distend and destroy alveolar spaces, with relatively inconspicuous, well-differentiated mucin-laden cuboidal to columnar tumour cells floating within the mucin or lining fibrous walls of the mucin-filled spaces; signet-ring morphology may occur. Mitotic count is low and necrosis absent; an inflammatory infiltrate may be present. Peripheral lung parenchyma is the usual site ([PathologyOutlines: colloid adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcolloidadeno.html); [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** CK7, CK20 and CDX2 diffusely and strongly positive; TTF-1 only focally positive in a proportion of cases; napsin A and SATB2 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcolloidadeno.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** When mixed with conventional invasive non-mucinous adenocarcinoma, > 50% of the tumour must show pools of extracellular mucin ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: colloid adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcolloidadeno.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 9. Fetal adenocarcinoma (low grade and high grade)

**Microscopy:** Irregular branching tubular structures lined by columnar epithelial cells with clear (glycogen-rich) cytoplasm and oval, optically clear nuclei, resembling fetal lung in the pseudoglandular stage. Low-grade tumours show lesser nuclear atypia, subnuclear vacuoles, small uniform round nuclei and squamoid morules, with neuroendocrine differentiation in the glandular epithelium. High-grade tumours show disorganised glands, large vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli, pronounced anisonucleosis, broad areas of necrosis, desmoplastic stroma, transition to conventional adenocarcinoma and no morules ([PathologyOutlines: fetal adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenofetal.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Low grade — nuclear/cytoplasmic beta-catenin staining (WNT pathway activation) distinguishes it from high grade; high grade — p53+ and AFP+ ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenofetal.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** High-grade fetal adenocarcinoma requires > 50% high-grade fetal morphology when admixed with other adenocarcinoma patterns ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: fetal adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenofetal.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 10. Enteric-type adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy:** Morphology of colorectal adenocarcinoma — glandular, papillary or cribriform structures with dirty luminal necrosis, tall columnar cells with pseudostratified atypical nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm; mitoses are frequent, the stroma is often desmoplastic with prominent inflammatory infiltrate, and the enteric pattern may be admixed with other adenocarcinoma patterns ([PathologyOutlines: enteric adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoenteric.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** At least one intestinal marker is required for the diagnosis — CDX2, CK20 and/or villin (also MUC2, HNF4α); CK7 is usually positive (CK7 in > 80% of tumour cells helps exclude colorectal primary), while TTF-1 and napsin A are negative or only weakly/focally positive; CDH17 and SATB2 are only rarely expressed ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoenteric.html); [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** When mixed with conventional invasive non-mucinous adenocarcinoma, > 50% of the tumour must show enteric morphology ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: enteric adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenoenteric.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 11. Squamous cell carcinoma in situ

**Microscopy:** Arises in large central airways as full-thickness replacement of the bronchial epithelium by atypical squamous cells: complete lack of maturation, significant cytological abnormality, coarse chromatin, inconsistent nuclear orientation and mitoses present through the full epithelial thickness, with an intact basement membrane and no invasive growth (extension into submucosal gland ducts is allowed) ([PathologyOutlines: preinvasive lesions / squamous dysplasia and CIS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumordysplasiagen.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Not definitional; some dysplastic lesions show patchy p53 and elevated Ki-67, with Ki-67 index significantly higher in severe dysplasia than mild/moderate dysplasia ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumordysplasiagen.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Graded on a 4-tier (mild/moderate/severe dysplasia/CIS) or 2-tier (low/high grade) scheme; CIS is defined by full-thickness atypia with mitoses at all levels (mild = lower third, moderate = lower two-thirds, severe = up to upper third with mitoses confined to lower two-thirds) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumordysplasiagen.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: preinvasive lesions](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumordysplasiagen.html)

## 12. Invasive squamous cell carcinoma, keratinising

**Microscopy:** Invasive squamous carcinoma showing recognisable keratinisation, keratin pearls and/or intercellular bridges; abundant tumour-associated inflammation and necrosis are frequent but not required. Poorly differentiated examples show severe cellular and nuclear atypia with abundant mitoses ([Lung and Mediastinum chapter, PMC7189424](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [PathologyOutlines: squamous cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Not required when morphology is diagnostic; p40, p63 and CK5/6 are positive and TTF-1 is usually negative ([PMC7189424](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** None; there is no established grading system for pulmonary squamous cell carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html)).

**Source:** [PMC7189424 — Lung and Mediastinum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [PathologyOutlines: squamous cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html)

## 13. Invasive squamous cell carcinoma, non-keratinising

**Microscopy:** Invasive carcinoma growing in solid nests and sheets that is "without recognizable keratinization, keratin pearls, or intercellular bridges", so that squamous differentiation cannot be established on morphology alone; tumour-associated inflammation and necrosis are frequent as in keratinising tumours ([PMC7189424](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [PathologyOutlines: squamous cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Yes — "Immunohistochemistry is required for the diagnosis of nonkeratinizing squamous cell carcinomas": p40, p63 and CK5/6 positive; TTF-1 negative or at most weak and focal. ([PMC7189424](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** n.a.

**Source:** [PMC7189424 — Lung and Mediastinum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [PathologyOutlines: squamous cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorSCC.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 14. Invasive squamous cell carcinoma, basaloid

**Microscopy:** Solid lobules, nests and clusters of relatively small to intermediate-sized basaloid cells with only a thin rim of cytoplasm and a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, showing prominent peripheral palisading, nuclear moulding and focal rosetting, a high mitotic rate and comedo-type necrosis; nuclear pleomorphism and nucleoli are less prominent than in poorly differentiated non-basaloid squamous carcinoma, and keratinisation is absent or minimal ([ERJ: lung carcinomas with a basaloid pattern](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/31/4/854); [PMC9088121: basaloid variant of lung squamous cell carcinoma](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9088121/); [PMC7189424](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Squamous markers p40/CK5 positive; neuroendocrine markers must be absent (or expressed in < 10% of cells) to exclude LCNEC/SCLC, and 34βE12 (CK1/5/10/14) positivity supports a basaloid carcinoma rather than a neuroendocrine tumour ([ERJ 2008](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/31/4/854); [PMC9088121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9088121/)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Basaloid component must exceed 50% of the tumour ("tumors with a basaloid component in greater than 50% of the tumor, regardless of the presence of any keratinization"); conversely the basaloid variant is retained when obvious squamous differentiation represents < 50% of the tumour ([PMC7189424](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [ERJ 2008](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/31/4/854)).

**Source:** [PMC7189424 — Lung and Mediastinum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7189424/); [ERJ: lung carcinomas with a basaloid pattern](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/31/4/854); [PMC9088121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9088121/)

## 15. Lymphoepithelial carcinoma (lung)

**Microscopy:** Anastomosing islands, nests, cords or diffuse sheets with a distinctly syncytial growth pattern of monomorphous polygonal epithelial cells having large vesicular nuclei and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli, set in a marked CD8-positive lymphocytic infiltrate with admixed plasma cells, histiocytes and occasional neutrophils/eosinophils; predominantly pushing borders with a permeative interface with adjacent lung, and rare intratumoral amyloid deposition. It is a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma in the 5th edition scheme ([PathologyOutlines: lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorLEL.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Diffuse CK5/6, p40 and p63 positivity (variable on PathologyOutlines), CK7+, EMA+, high Ki-67; TTF-1, napsin A and neuroendocrine markers negative; CD45 negative in tumour cells. EBER1 in situ hybridisation is a desirable diagnostic criterion — positive in > 90% of Asian patients, while EBV-negative cases occur especially in patients of European descent ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorLEL.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** No morphometric threshold; EBER1 positivity in > 90% of Asian cases is descriptive, not a cut-off ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorLEL.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 16. Large cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Sheets or solid nests of large round to polygonal cells with moderately abundant cytoplasm and well-defined cell borders, vesicular nuclei and prominent nucleoli; foci of central necrosis and haemorrhage may be present. No glandular, squamous or neuroendocrine differentiation by morphology or immunohistochemistry ([PathologyOutlines: large cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecell.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Definitional by exclusion — null or unclear phenotype: TTF-1, napsin A, p40, p63, synaptophysin, chromogranin, CD56 and mucicarmine all negative, with only broad-spectrum/low-molecular-weight keratins (e.g. CAM5.2) positive ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecell.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** No percentage threshold; the diagnosis is restricted to thoroughly sampled resection specimens and must not be made on small biopsy or cytology ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: large cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecell.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 17. Adenosquamous carcinoma

**Microscopy:** A single tumour containing both squamous differentiation (intercellular bridges, keratin pearls) and glandular differentiation (lumens with mucin; papillary, lepidic, acinar or tubular patterns), the two components either spatially separate or intermingled ([PathologyOutlines: adenosquamous carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenosquamous.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** TTF-1 in the adenocarcinoma component, p40 and CK5/6 in the squamous component; CK7 positive; neuroendocrine markers negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenosquamous.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Each component must comprise at least 10% of the tumour ("Adenosquamous carcinoma (if both components ≥10%)"); requires a resection specimen ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenosquamous.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenosquamous carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenosquamous.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 18. Pleomorphic carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Non-small cell carcinoma — most often adenocarcinoma, less often squamous cell or large cell carcinoma — containing a substantial population of malignant spindle cells and/or bizarre neoplastic giant cells. Stroma (collagenous or myxoid) can be abundant; necrosis, haemorrhage and vascular invasion are common, and spread through air spaces may occur ([PathologyOutlines: pleomorphic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratins positive (especially in the carcinomatous component; spindle/giant cells may be only weakly/focally positive or negative), TTF-1 in adenocarcinoma areas, variable p40/p63; neuroendocrine, lymphoid, mesothelial and melanocytic markers negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** ≥ 10% spindle cells and/or giant cells within an NSCLC (or a tumour composed entirely of such cells); requires a resection specimen ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: pleomorphic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 19. Spindle cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Carcinoma composed entirely (or almost entirely) of tightly nested, elongated spindle cells growing in fascicular or storiform patterns; cytological atypia ranges from pronounced to almost bland, and chronic inflammation may be pronounced. Collagenous or myxoid stroma, necrosis and haemorrhage are common ([PathologyOutlines: pleomorphic carcinoma / spindle cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratin expression (may be weak/focal), variable p63/p40; neuroendocrine, lymphoid, mesothelial and melanocytic markers negative — needed to exclude sarcoma, melanoma and mesothelioma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Tumour composed purely of spindle cells (no other NSCLC component); if ≥ 10% spindle cells accompany a conventional NSCLC, the tumour is pleomorphic carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: pleomorphic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)

---

## 20. Giant cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Tumour composed of highly pleomorphic malignant giant cells with widely variable size and shape, abundant eosinophilic or granular cytoplasm and frequently multiple, irregular, sometimes multilobated nuclei; leukocyte emperipolesis is a characteristic finding, and necrosis/haemorrhage are common ([PathologyOutlines: pleomorphic carcinoma / giant cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Cytokeratins may be only weakly/focally positive or negative in the giant cells; neuroendocrine, lymphoid, mesothelial and melanocytic markers negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Tumour composed purely of neoplastic giant cells; ≥ 10% giant cells with a conventional NSCLC component = pleomorphic carcinoma ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: pleomorphic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpleomorphic.html)

---

## 21. Carcinosarcoma

**Microscopy:** Biphasic, poorly differentiated tumour with both a carcinomatous component (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma or small cell carcinoma) and a genuinely sarcomatous component with spindle cell morphology; heterologous elements (rhabdomyosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, osteosarcoma) may be identified ([PathologyOutlines: carcinosarcoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinosarcoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** AE1/AE3 and CAM5.2 positive in the carcinomatous component (cytokeratin occasionally positive in spindle areas); p40 if squamous, TTF-1/napsin A if adenocarcinoma; desmin/myogenin mark a rhabdomyosarcomatous component and S100 a chondrosarcomatous component; WT1 and calretinin negative (excludes mesothelioma) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinosarcoma.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** n.a.

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: carcinosarcoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinosarcoma.html)

---

## 22. Pulmonary blastoma

**Microscopy:** Biphasic tumour with a well-differentiated glandular component of branching tubules resembling fetal lung — pseudostratified columnar cells with subnuclear and supranuclear glycogen vacuoles and little nuclear atypia — set in a loose, primitive (blastematous) undifferentiated mesenchymal stroma with variable atypia; heterologous differentiation (skeletal muscle, cartilage, bone) may occur, and squamoid morules with neuroendocrine cells are present in about two-thirds of cases. Grossly a peripheral, well-circumscribed unencapsulated mass ([PathologyOutlines: pulmonary blastoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpulmonaryblastoma.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Glandular component — nuclear beta-catenin, CK7, AE1/AE3, TTF-1, CEA, synaptophysin (focal chromogranin), vimentin negative; blastemal stroma — nuclear beta-catenin, vimentin, SMA, with CAM5.2 and TTF-1 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpulmonaryblastoma.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** n.a.

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: pulmonary blastoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorpulmonaryblastoma.html)

---

## 23. NUT carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Diffuse, sheet-like architecture of primitive-looking, monotonous medium-sized cells with frequent cytoplasmic clearing, large nuclei and prominent nucleoli, showing occasional cell spacing/discohesion; scattered foci of abrupt keratinisation are the hallmark, on a background of necrosis with prominent neutrophils. Occasionally pseudoglandular formations, mucoid stroma and crush artefact ([PathologyOutlines: NUT carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumornutcarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Yes — speckled nuclear NUT immunoreactivity is the diagnostic hallmark (sensitivity 87%, specificity 100%); keratins (especially high molecular weight), p63 and p40 are frequently positive, MYC is strong in the primitive component; NUTM1 rearrangement can be confirmed by FISH ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumornutcarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Speckled nuclear NUT expression in ≥ 50% of tumour cells ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumornutcarcinoma.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: NUT carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumornutcarcinoma.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 24. Thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumour

**Microscopy:** Diffuse sheets of variably discohesive large round to epithelioid cells with vesicular chromatin and prominent nucleoli; nuclei are relatively monotonous with only occasional mild pleomorphism, and rhabdoid cells may be present but are not required. The overall appearance recalls paediatric malignant rhabdoid tumour. Unequivocal epithelial architecture (glandular or squamous differentiation) should be absent, except in rare combined cases with juxtaposed conventional NSCLC ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Yes — SMARCA4 (BRG1) expression lost or severely reduced by immunohistochemistry (sequencing unnecessary if IHC confirms deficiency); SMARCA2 (BRM) lost in most cases; many express CD34, SOX2 and/or SALL4; cytokeratin is often only focal/weak or entirely negative and claudin-4 is negative or only focally positive ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** n.a. (no percentage or count threshold; defined by morphology plus SMARCA4 loss) ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext) (also [ScienceDirect version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1556086421033165))

## 25. Adenoid cystic carcinoma (lung)

**Microscopy:** Tumour of tracheobronchial submucosal seromucinous glands with three architectural patterns — cribriform (classic "Swiss cheese"), tubular and solid — which may coexist but with one usually predominating; dilated pseudocystic spaces containing basement-membrane-like material, and a dual (biphasic) population of luminal epithelial cells and abluminal myoepithelial cells. Perineural invasion is a hallmark ([PathologyOutlines: adenoid cystic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorACC.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Biphasic profile is definitional — luminal cells CD117/KIT+ (p63−), abluminal myoepithelial cells p63+, SMA+, S100+, CK5/6+; pankeratin/CK7 diffusely positive; collagen IV/laminin highlight pseudocystic spaces and hyaline basement membrane; TTF-1, napsin A, chromogranin and CD56 negative ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorACC.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** No diagnostic threshold; a solid component > 30% is associated with worse prognosis ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorACC.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: adenoid cystic carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorACC.html)

## 26. Epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma (lung)

**Microscopy:** Well-circumscribed mass with a pushing margin, divided by thin fibrous septa with variable hyalinisation/sclerosis; biphasic proliferation of inner epithelial and outer myoepithelial cells forming bilayered duct-like structures in variable proportions. Epithelial cells are flattened, cuboidal or columnar with clear to eosinophilic cytoplasm; myoepithelial cells may be indistinct, flattened, spindled or clear; lumens contain pale eosinophilic amorphous material or debris. Mitoses, significant nuclear atypia, necrosis and haemorrhage are typically absent ([PathologyOutlines: epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorepithelialmyo.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Yes — epithelial cells CK7+, pankeratin+, EMA+ (S100 negative); myoepithelial cells S100+, muscle-specific actin+, p63+, CK5/6+ ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorepithelialmyo.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** n.a.

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorepithelialmyo.html)

## 27. Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (lung)

**Microscopy:** Central, endobronchial or peribronchial tumour composed of a mixture of squamoid, mucin-producing and intermediate-type cells, characteristically lacking keratinisation and lacking in situ carcinoma in the overlying epithelium. Squamoid cells are oval with moderate eosinophilic cytoplasm, round-oval nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli and intercellular bridges but no keratin pearls; mucous cells are medium-sized with peripherally displaced nuclei and pale blue-grey intracytoplasmic mucin; intermediate cells are polygonal and bland, often with clear-cell features. Low-grade tumours lack significant atypia and pleomorphism; high-grade tumours show atypia, pleomorphism, mitotic activity, necrosis and haemorrhage ([PathologyOutlines: mucoepidermoid carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumormucoepidermoid.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** All cell types broad-spectrum keratin+ and CK7+; squamoid cells p40/p63/CK5/6+ (at least focal p63); mucicarmine, Alcian blue and PAS-D highlight mucous cells; TTF-1 and napsin A negative (along with CK20, SMA, S100, SOX10, HMB45, PAX8). CRTC1::MAML2 (MAML2) fusion supports the diagnosis ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumormucoepidermoid.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** n.a. (graded low vs high grade on cellular atypia, mitotic activity, necrosis and haemorrhage rather than a numeric cut-off) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumormucoepidermoid.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: mucoepidermoid carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumormucoepidermoid.html)

## 28. Typical carcinoid (neuroendocrine tumour, grade 1)

**Microscopy:** Neuroendocrine growth patterns — organoid, trabecular, nested, rosette-forming, or pseudoglandular/follicular/papillary — of uniform polygonal cells with round to oval nuclei showing salt-and-pepper chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli and moderate to abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Spindle-cell and clear-cell change may occur. Stroma is fine and highly vascularised, sometimes with hyalinisation, cartilage or bone formation. Necrosis is absent and mitoses are rare ([PathologyOutlines: typical carcinoid](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinoid.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Chromogranin, synaptophysin, CD56 and INSM1 diffusely and strongly positive; pankeratin positive (distinguishes from paraganglioma); TTF-1 positive in fewer than 50% (usually focal/weak); CDX2 negative. Ki-67 is not a classification criterion but is desirable to report ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinoid.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** Size ≥ 5 mm (0.5 cm) AND < 2 mitoses per 2 mm² AND no necrosis; mitoses counted in the area of highest activity, reported per 2 mm² (not per 10 HPF), and for borderline values three 2-mm² areas are counted and the mean reported ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinoid.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: typical carcinoid](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorcarcinoid.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 29. Atypical carcinoid (neuroendocrine tumour, grade 2)

**Microscopy:** Same neuroendocrine architecture and cytology as typical carcinoid (organoid, trabecular, rosettes, papillary, pseudoglandular, follicular; polygonal cells, salt-and-pepper chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli, moderate to abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm) but with increased mitotic activity and/or necrosis, which is usually punctate but can form larger zones; greater pleomorphism than typical carcinoid is common, and spindle/clear-cell change may be seen in a fine, highly vascular stroma ([PathologyOutlines: atypical carcinoid](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoratypicalcarcinoid.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Chromogranin, synaptophysin, CD56 diffusely positive (rarely negative); pankeratin positive in ~80%; TTF-1 positive in about 50%; CDX2 negative (excludes GI/pancreatic primary); Ki-67 should not be used as a diagnostic criterion ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoratypicalcarcinoid.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** 2–10 mitoses per 2 mm² OR presence of necrosis; mitoses counted in the hot spot, with three 2-mm² areas averaged when near the cut-off. Carcinoid morphology with > 10 mitoses per 2 mm² is classified as LCNEC under the WHO scheme ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoratypicalcarcinoid.html)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: atypical carcinoid](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoratypicalcarcinoid.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 30. Small cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Small to medium-sized round/oval "blue" cells with scant cytoplasm growing in sheets, clusters, ribbons, rosettes or with peripheral palisading; nuclei show finely dispersed chromatin, absent or indistinct nucleoli, nuclear moulding, smudging and a high mitotic rate. Stroma is thin, delicate and fibrovascular; necrosis and single-cell apoptosis are common, and the Azzopardi phenomenon (basophilic nuclear material encrusting vessel walls) may be seen ([PathologyOutlines: small cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorsmallcell.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** At least one neuroendocrine marker positive (synaptophysin, chromogranin A, CD56, INSM1); TTF-1 positive (negative in 15–20%), CK7 punctate; p40, p63 and napsin A negative. High Ki-67 separates it from carcinoid ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorsmallcell.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** No mitotic cut-off is applied (high-grade by definition); mitoses are reported per 2 mm² for NENs generally ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: small cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorsmallcell.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## 31. Combined small cell carcinoma

**Microscopy:** Small cell carcinoma (as above) with an additional, morphologically distinct non-small cell component — most often adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma, also large cell carcinoma and, less often, giant cell or spindle cell carcinoma; SCLC combined with LCNEC is reported as "combined SCLC and LCNEC". The diagnosis is worded as, for example, "combined SCLC and adenocarcinoma". Such combinations arise de novo or as a manifestation of resistance in treated adenocarcinoma ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [Pathology and Classification of SCLC, PMC7919820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7919820/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** As for SCLC in the small cell component, plus component-appropriate markers (TTF-1/napsin A for adenocarcinoma, p40/CK5-6 for squamous) ([PathologyOutlines: small cell carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorsmallcell.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** "To establish a diagnosis of combined SCLC and large cell carcinoma, large cells have to constitute at least 10% of the tumor; however, no assessment of the percentage of the secondary histology is necessary to render a combined SCLC diagnosis for other NSCLC histologies" ([PMC7919820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7919820/)).

**Source:** [PMC7919820 — Pathology and Classification of SCLC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7919820/); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 32. Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC)

**Microscopy:** Neuroendocrine organoid architecture — nesting with peripheral palisading, anastomosing trabeculae and rosette-like structures — composed of large cells with abundant amphophilic cytoplasm, nuclear pleomorphism, coarse granular or vesicular chromatin and prominent nucleoli; brisk mitotic activity and extensive/geographic necrosis. Occasional rhabdoid features. Cells are larger, of higher nuclear grade and more mitotically active than atypical carcinoid ([PathologyOutlines: large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Yes — neuroendocrine confirmation required: usually at least two of chromogranin, synaptophysin, CD56 focal to diffuse (caution if CD56 alone), INSM1 nuclear in ~91%; keratins AE1/AE3 and CAM5.2 positive; TTF-1 in ~50%; p40/p63 and napsin A negative; high Ki-67 (40–80%) helps separate from carcinoid ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** > 10 mitoses per 2 mm² (with necrosis), counted in the area of highest activity ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

---

## 33. Combined large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma

**Microscopy:** LCNEC (organoid nesting/palisading/rosettes, large cells with prominent nucleoli, > 10 mitoses per 2 mm², extensive necrosis) admixed with a non-neuroendocrine carcinoma component — most commonly adenocarcinoma, also squamous cell carcinoma, giant cell carcinoma or spindle cell carcinoma; accounts for 20–25% of surgically resected LCNEC ([PathologyOutlines: large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** Neuroendocrine markers in the LCNEC component (as above) plus component-appropriate markers (TTF-1/napsin A for adenocarcinoma, p40 for squamous) ([PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html)).

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** No percentage threshold stated for the non-neuroendocrine component in the WHO 5th edition summary; combined LCNEC and combined SCLC together may comprise up to 25% of resected cases ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)).

**Source:** [PathologyOutlines: large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumorlargecellNE.html); [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext)

## A. Spread through air spaces (STAS)

**Microscopy:** Tumour cells within air spaces of the lung parenchyma beyond the edge of the main tumour. Three morphological patterns: (1) micropapillary or ring-like clusters, (2) solid nests or tumour islands, and (3) discohesive single cells. Solid nests are recognisable at low magnification; micropapillary clusters and single cells need high magnification. An imaginary line drawn along the most peripheral edge of the main tumour helps — cell nests outside that line count as STAS — and true STAS follows the configuration of the circumferential tumour edge rather than being haphazard. Features favouring artefact rather than STAS are: randomly situated, ragged-edged clusters at the edge of the section or out of the plane of section; lack of continuous spread from the tumour edge to the most distant airspace cells; benign cytology or cilia; and linear strips of cells lifted off alveolar walls. In adenocarcinoma and pleomorphic carcinoma all three patterns occur, whereas squamous cell carcinoma shows exclusively solid nests ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [Spread Through Air Spaces (STAS) in Lung Cancer, PMC7186879](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7186879/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a.

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** STAS is a pattern of tumour spread and is therefore not included in the total percentage of patterns nor in tumour size for staging. No WHO count threshold; research studies have used no STAS / low STAS (1–4 single cells or clusters) / high STAS (≥ 5 single cells or clusters), and measured distances from the tumour edge of 0.2–8.5 mm (median 1.0–1.5 mm) or 1–58 alveolar spaces (median 6–7) in adenocarcinoma ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PMC7186879](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7186879/)).

**Source:** [Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PMC7186879 — STAS in lung cancer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7186879/)

---

## B. IASLC 2020 grading system for invasive non-mucinous adenocarcinoma

**Microscopy / definitions:** A three-tier grade based on the combination of the predominant histological pattern plus the worst (high-grade) pattern, where the high-grade patterns are micropapillary, solid, cribriform and complex glandular. Grade 1 (well differentiated) = lepidic-predominant tumour with no or < 20% high-grade pattern. Grade 2 (moderately differentiated) = acinar- or papillary-predominant tumour with no or < 20% high-grade pattern. Grade 3 (poorly differentiated) = any tumour with ≥ 20% high-grade pattern. Applies to invasive non-mucinous adenocarcinoma; the scheme outperformed models based on nuclear/cytological grade, STAS or necrosis in validation ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PathologyOutlines: adenocarcinoma overview](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html); [The IASLC grading system for invasive pulmonary adenocarcinoma, PMC9837269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9837269/)).

**Immunoprofile (if definitional):** n.a.

**WHO quantitative rule (if any):** 20% threshold for high-grade patterns (micropapillary, solid, cribriform/complex glandular); patterns recorded in 5–10% increments; invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma is excluded from the system ([Nicholson et al., JTO](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)03316-5/fulltext); [PMC9837269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9837269/); [PathologyOutlines](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/lungtumoradenocarcinoma.html)).
