## Part A — Biopsy entities

### 1. Oral epithelial dysplasia

**Architectural criteria (WHO 5th ed. update):** irregular epithelial stratification, loss of polarity of basal cells, drop-shaped rete ridges, keratin pearls within rete ridges, loss of epithelial cell cohesion, and — new in the 2022 update — altered keratin pattern appropriate for the oral subsite, verrucous/papillary architectural change, extension of the abnormal epithelium along minor salivary gland ducts, a sharply defined lesion margin, more than one distinct pattern of dysplasia within the same lesion, multifocal/skip-area involvement, an expanded proliferative (basal/parabasal) compartment, and basal-cell clustering or nesting ([Müller & Tilakaratne, Head Neck Pathol 2022;16:54–62](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).

**Cytological criteria:** abnormal variation in nuclear size and shape, abnormal variation in cell size and shape, increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, atypical mitotic figures, increased number and/or size of nucleoli, hyperchromasia, an increased number of mitotic figures, mitoses situated high in the epithelium, single-cell keratinization, and — new — apoptotic mitotic figures and increased nuclear size. Increased/superficial mitoses and single-cell keratinization were moved from the architectural to the cytological list in the 2022 revision ([Müller & Tilakaratne](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).

**Three-tier grading (mild/moderate/severe):** the WHO retains three-tiered grading based traditionally on the fraction of epithelial thickness involved by architectural/cytological change (lower, middle, upper third), but the update explicitly states that "defining dysplasia by epithelial thirds oversimplifies the complexity" of the process — change confined to the basal third can still be graded severe if enough of the listed features, especially cytological atypia, are present ("cytological atypia upgrade") ([Müller & Tilakaratne](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).

**Binary (two-tier) grading — published thresholds:** the original Kujan et al. binary system (acknowledged by WHO) sets low-risk/low-grade OED at up to 4 architectural and 5 cytological features, and high-risk/high-grade at more than 4 architectural and more than 5 cytological features. A 2025 study proposing a WHO-2022-updated cutoff, using Youden-statistic optimization against malignant transformation, moved the cytological threshold from 5 to 6: low-grade = fewer than 4 architectural AND fewer than 6 cytological alterations; high-grade = 4 or more architectural AND 6 or more cytological alterations. Architectural criteria were found more determinative than cytological criteria, and the revised cutoff discriminated malignant transformation better on Kaplan–Meier analysis (log-rank p = 0.002 vs p = 0.006 for the original cutoff) ([Rodrigues et al., Head Neck Pathol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11806176/)).

**Which system WHO 5th recommends:** the WHO 5th edition update maintains the three-tiered system as its primary recommendation, while noting the binary system exists but "continues to lack validation against malignant transformation" as a fully WHO-endorsed replacement ([Müller & Tilakaratne](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).

**Carcinoma in situ:** the WHO 5th edition states the term "carcinoma in situ" is "not recommended for the oral cavity and is considered synonymous with severe dysplasia" ([Müller & Tilakaratne](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).

**Keratinising vs non-keratinising (HPV-associated) dysplasia:** conventional oral epithelial dysplasia is keratinising and graded using the above criteria. By contrast, HPV-associated oropharyngeal squamous change arises from tonsillar crypt reticulated epithelium with a discontinuous/porous basement membrane; because of this porosity, the concept of dysplasia/carcinoma in situ in HPV-positive oropharyngeal epithelium "remains highly controversial," and any non-keratinising HPV-positive epithelial change is regarded a priori as potentially infiltrating rather than staged through a dysplasia hierarchy ([Badoual, Head Neck Pathol 2022;16:19–30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019010/)).

### 2. Proliferative verrucous leukoplakia (PVL)

PVL is described as a histological *spectrum* rather than a single lesion, requiring clinicopathological correlation because none of its individual patterns is by itself diagnostic:
- **Early PVL — corrugated ortho(para)hyperkeratotic lesion, not reactive:** premature keratinization, a sharply demarcated lateral margin from unaffected epithelium, skip areas of keratosis, and increased keratin; shows wave-like/undulating "basket-weave" surface corrugation with prominent crests (historically "Christmas-tree keratosis" or chevrons); cytologic atypia is absent or minimal, and verrucous morphology without atypia should be graded as mild dysplasia. A lichenoid, band-like lymphohistiocytic infiltrate can mimic lichen planus, but PVL retains intact basal cells at the epithelial–stromal interface and/or shows dysplasia, unlike lichen planus ([Thompson et al. expert consensus, Head Neck Pathol 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134585/); [Müller & Tilakaratne](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).
- **Bulky hyperkeratotic epithelial proliferation, not reactive:** amplified exophytic and/or endophytic proliferation; epithelial bulk accounts for lesion thickness; bulbous rete pegs that may coalesce; architectural abnormality overshadows cytological findings ([Thompson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134585/)).
- **Suspicious for SCC/unequivocal SCC in the PVL setting:** voluminous acanthotic, endophytic-appearing columns with complex folding and invaginations, epithelial thickness often 3–4× that of adjacent uninvolved epithelium, bulbous/blunt downwardly directed rete pegs, intraepithelial microabscesses sometimes associated with keratin pearls, and "cumbrous" or "stuck-on" epithelium without conventional invasive growth in some cases (proposed "barnaculate carcinoma" pattern) ([Thompson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134585/)).

Because early PVL and benign frictional/reactive keratosis can be histologically indistinguishable, the consensus guideline and the WHO update both stress that a diagnosis of PVL requires clinicopathological correlation with the multifocal, progressive clinical course rather than histology alone ([Thompson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134585/); [Müller & Tilakaratne](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9018914/)).

### 3. SCC in a small oral biopsy

**Features establishing invasion:** breach of the basement membrane by tumour cells/nests into the underlying lamina propria/submucosa, with the invading epithelium showing the cytological features of malignancy (nuclear pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, atypical mitoses) and typically eliciting a desmoplastic/inflammatory stromal response — the same conceptual threshold used across CAP's Oral Cavity protocol for diagnosing invasive squamous cell carcinoma before any of the resection-specific measurements (DOI, WPOI) can be applied ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, HN.Oral_4.3.0.0](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

**Differentiation grade:** graded G1 (well differentiated), G2 (moderately differentiated), G3 (poorly differentiated), or GX (cannot be assessed) — the same three/four-tier scheme used on resection. The CAP protocol notes that for conventional SCC, "histologic grading as a whole does not perform well as a prognosticator," though it is still recorded ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

**What cannot be assessed on a small biopsy:** depth of invasion (DOI) cannot be reliably measured, because DOI requires reconstruction of the original mucosal (basement-membrane) surface as a reference plane and a "plumb line" dropped to the deepest point of invasion — a measurement that depends on seeing the full lesion contour, which a partial biopsy does not provide. The worst pattern of invasion (WPOI) similarly cannot be assessed on a small biopsy because it depends on evaluating the invasive front architecture across the full tumour, including satellite dispersion ≥1 mm (WPOI-5) ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

### 4. Oral SCC types (QMH template list)

- **Conventional keratinising SCC:** invasive nests/cords of squamous epithelium with variable keratinisation, intercellular bridges, and keratin pearl formation; the default WHO 5th-edition histologic type against which other variants are compared ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, Note C, citing WHO 5th ed.](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf); [tumourclassification.iarc.who.int](https://tumourclassification.iarc.who.int/chapters/52)).
- **Non-keratinising SCC:** morphologically similar to HPV-associated non-keratinising SCC of the oropharynx — high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, oval-to-spindled nuclei, syncytial cytoplasm with indistinct borders, no intercellular bridges, and usually no significant keratinisation; when at an oral cavity primary site this pattern is distinct from HPV-associated OPSCC and is not the trigger for routine HPV/p16 testing ([Badoual, Head Neck Pathol 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019010/); [CAP HPV testing guideline](https://www.cap.org/cap-guidelines/human-papillomavirus-testing-in-head-and-neck-carcinomas/)).
- **Adenosquamous carcinoma:** biphasic tumour combining a squamous cell carcinoma component with a true glandular (adenocarcinomatous) component; listed among the QMH-recognised WHO 5th-edition histologic types for oral cavity SCC ([QMH oral cavity template](https://awiloqmh.github.io/CancerReportingTemplate/HN-Lip%20and%20oral%20cavity.html); WHO 5th ed. classification reference cited in [CAP Oral Cavity protocol](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).
- **Basaloid SCC:** biphasic pattern with small, crowded basaloid cells with hyperchromatic round nuclei and scant cytoplasm, arranged in sheets/lobules producing a "jigsaw puzzle" growth pattern; cystic spaces containing PAS-positive myxoid material; possible stromal hyalinisation; a squamous component of surface SCC or severe dysplasia is intimately associated, with focal abrupt squamous differentiation within basaloid areas ([El-Mofty, Med Oral Patol Oral Cir Bucal 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4119314/)).
- **Acantholytic SCC:** listed on the QMH template as a WHO 5th-edition oral cavity SCC variant ([QMH oral cavity template](https://awiloqmh.github.io/CancerReportingTemplate/HN-Lip%20and%20oral%20cavity.html)); n.a. for further microscopic description — no fetched source in this session gave a dedicated morphological account of oral acantholytic SCC.
- **Papillary SCC:** exophytic papillary architecture; a non-keratinising type has papillae covered by immature basaloid cells, while a keratinising type shows dysplastic surface epithelium with a maturation trend and minimal surface parakeratin; HPV-related cases show p16 immunoreactivity together with HPV in situ hybridisation and E6/E7 mRNA in situ hybridisation as ancillary confirmatory tests ([El-Mofty](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4119314/)). Distinguished from benign squamous papilloma by immature/dysplastic basaloid cells and possible invasion (squamous papilloma shows bland maturing epithelium with scattered but non-atypical basal mitoses) ([Betz, Head Neck Pathol 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405797/)).
- **Spindle cell (sarcomatoid) SCC + keratin-negative pitfall:** classic biphasic pattern combines a differentiated SCC/dysplasia component with a malignant spindle/pleomorphic epithelioid submucosal component, but a monophasic (spindle-only) pattern also occurs, especially in ulcerated lesions or limited biopsies, and absence of a differentiated component does not exclude the diagnosis. Broad-spectrum cytokeratins are positive in the majority but are negative in up to ~40% of cases; p63 is more consistently positive than p40 but can also be negative; vimentin is consistently positive (near 100%, diffuse/strong) — mesenchymal marker expression does not exclude a carcinoma diagnosis. **Pitfall:** when cytokeratin/p63/p40 are all negative and the spindle cells express vimentin/desmin/actins, the tumour can be misdiagnosed as a sarcoma (e.g. leiomyosarcoma); WHO guidance is that such keratin-negative spindle-cell lesions arising in the aerodigestive mucosa should still be considered spindle cell carcinoma given the clinical context ([Head Neck Pathol 2020, spindle cell SCC pitfall discussion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12105-020-01273-6)).
- **Verrucous carcinoma:** exophytic papillary growth with hyperkeratosis and parakeratosis; endophytic papillae/rete ridges situated at *different* levels, extending both up and down rather than terminating at one plane; thin or absent fibrovascular cores; no dysplasia, no cytological atypia, no koilocytotic atypia; broad, pushing, *non-infiltrative* margins. Very good prognosis with low metastatic potential. Differs from verrucous hyperplasia, whose rete ridges remain at the same level as adjacent normal epithelium ([Chauhan et al., Head Neck Pathol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8291099/)).
- **Carcinoma cuniculatum:** predominantly endophytic "burrowing" growth; hyperkeratotic squamous epithelium forms vertically aligned, deeply infiltrative, interconnected keratin-filled crypts resembling a rabbit burrow ("cuniculus"); broad bulbous rete pegs; minimal cytological atypia; crypts may contain neutrophilic microabscesses; can show bone invasion (tumour islands infiltrating between bone trabeculae) and perineural invasion despite bland cytology. IHC shows low Ki-67 (<10%) and low p53 (<10%), limited to basal layers — contrasting with verrucous carcinoma and conventional SCC, which show strong p53/Ki-67 staining. Superficial/incisional biopsies are frequently misdiagnosed as pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia; definitive diagnosis often needs excisional/deep sampling ([Raut et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13435886/)).
- **Lymphoepithelial carcinoma (non-nasopharyngeal), with EBER:** solid sheets, trabeculae, nests, and single neoplastic epithelial cells intimately intermingled with lymphocytes and plasma cells; large epithelial cells with indistinct borders forming a syncytium; round-to-oval vesicular nuclei with large central nucleoli — morphologically identical to nasopharyngeal-type undifferentiated carcinoma but occurring outside the nasopharynx ([El-Mofty](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4119314/)). n.a. — no fetched source in this session gave the site-specific EBER positivity rate for non-nasopharyngeal (oral cavity) lymphoepithelial carcinoma; EBER in situ hybridisation is the standard ancillary test implied by the entity's nasopharyngeal-type morphology but its exact reported positivity at oral sites was not confirmed in a fetched source.

### 5. HPV-associated oropharyngeal SCC

**Morphology:** arises from tonsillar crypt epithelium (lingual and palatine tonsils), whose reticular, discontinuous/porous basement membrane is heavily infiltrated by lymphocytes, making assessment of invasion difficult; because of this porous basement membrane, non-keratinising HPV-positive OPSCC within the epithelium should be considered a priori as infiltrating. Cytologically: high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, oval-to-spindled nuclei, syncytial cytoplasm with indistinct cell borders, no intercellular bridges, and usually no significant keratinisation ([Badoual, Head Neck Pathol 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019010/)).

**p16 IHC positivity criterion:** the WHO classification and the CAP HPV-associated Oropharynx synoptic both set the positivity threshold at ≥70% of tumour cells showing moderate-to-strong nuclear *and* cytoplasmic staining; <50% is negative; 50–<70% is equivocal ([Badoual](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019010/); [CAP HPV-Assoc Oropharynx protocol, HN.HPV-Assoc_Oropharynx.Res_1.1.0.0](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/HN.HPV-Assoc_Oropharynx.Res_1.1.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

**Latest CAP guideline — p16 alone vs HPV-specific testing:** the CAP guideline (2018, updated 2026) recommends high-risk HPV testing on all newly diagnosed OPSCC, with p16 IHC as the first-line surrogate marker sufficient alone for diagnosis and staging in most cases. Additional HPV-specific testing (RNA in situ hybridisation, PCR) is recommended when the morphology does not match the p16 result, or in geographic areas where the HPV-attributable fraction is below 50%; it can also be chosen at pathologist/clinician discretion or for clinical trials. The 2026 update adds enhanced guidance for scenarios where p16 IHC alone is insufficient, identifies the highest-performing HPV-specific tests with comprehensive HPV genotype coverage, clarifies the approach for FNA samples, and extends routine testing to sinonasal SCC, cervical metastatic SCC of unknown primary, and cervical FNA specimens ([CAP HPV testing guideline, 2018](https://www.cap.org/cap-guidelines/human-papillomavirus-testing-in-head-and-neck-carcinomas/); [CAP 2026 guideline update](https://www.cap.org/article/cap-updates-guideline-to-improve-hpv-testing-in-head-and-neck-cancers/); [Badoual, citing Lewis JS et al. Arch Pathol Lab Med 2018;142:559-597](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019010/)).

**Not recommended at non-oropharyngeal sites:** the CAP guideline explicitly states pathologists should NOT routinely perform high-risk HPV testing on non-OPSCC of the larynx, oral cavity, nasopharynx, or hypopharynx, citing insufficient evidence of prognostic or therapeutic impact at those sites; testing there is permitted case-by-case but not as routine practice ([CAP HPV testing guideline](https://www.cap.org/cap-guidelines/human-papillomavirus-testing-in-head-and-neck-carcinomas/)).

**Not graded:** the CAP HPV-Associated Oropharynx protocol states there is "no standard grading system for HPV-associated squamous cell carcinoma," and the Badoual WHO update paper states grading should be avoided in HPV-positive OPSCC ([CAP HPV-Assoc Oropharynx protocol](https://www.cap.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/HN.HPV-Assoc_Oropharynx.Res_1.1.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf); [Badoual](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019010/)).

### 6. Benign lesions

- **Fibroepithelial polyp (irritation fibroma):** nodular mass of dense collagen bundles with wavy, haphazardly arranged spindle fibroblasts; the covering squamous epithelium shows rete ridge atrophy secondary to the underlying connective tissue proliferation; regarded as "focal fibrous hyperplasia." No special stain/IHC is required or reported ([PMC review, oral fibrous lesions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4688387/)).
- **Pyogenic granuloma (lobular capillary haemangioma):** passes through three histologic phases — cellular/early (compact stroma, diffuse endothelial proliferation, minimal lumen formation), capillary/vascular (numerous engorged capillaries with proliferating endothelium), and involutionary/healing (fibrosis, thick collagen bundles). The lobular capillary haemangioma subtype shows lobular vascular aggregates with peripheral connective-tissue septae and perivascular cells positive for α-smooth muscle actin; the non-lobular subtype lacks this organisation, resembles granulation tissue, and is α-SMA negative. Distinguished from Kaposi sarcoma (absence of cytological atypia/bizarre vessels) and bacillary angiomatosis (absence of granular bacterial material) ([Marla et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4921146/)).
- **Oral lichen planus/lichenoid lesion (AAOMP 2016/WHO criteria):** a band-like or patchy, predominantly lymphocytic infiltrate confined to the epithelium–lamina propria interface; basal-cell liquefactive/hydropic degeneration; lymphocytic exocytosis; absence of epithelial dysplasia; absence of verrucous architectural change (the five AAOMP 2016 criteria). Direct immunofluorescence shows shaggy fibrinogen deposition at the basement membrane zone, IgM within colloid/Civatte bodies, and granular/linear C3, distinguishing erosive/bullous OLP from pemphigus/pemphigoid. Lichenoid drug reaction is distinguished by a more diffuse infiltrate with plasma cells/eosinophils and deeper, perivascular extension ([Rotaru et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8212651/); [Fitzpatrick et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405793/)).
- **Frictional keratosis:** marked hyperparakeratosis with a shaggy/shredded keratin surface producing a macerated appearance with fissures/clefting; bacteria often colonise the tongue keratin surface; epithelial hyperplasia with intracellular oedema/ballooned spinous cells; the superficial connective tissue lacks inflammation unless secondarily ulcerated; no dysplasia features. **Pitfall:** early PVL of the alveolar ridge/gingiva can be histologically indistinguishable from benign frictional keratosis (both show marked orthokeratosis, corrugated surface, prominent granular layer); clinical correlation is essential, and lesions on the lateral tongue border warrant particular caution as the most common OSCC site ([Müller, Head Neck Pathol 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405791/)).
- **Candidosis (PAS/GMS):** biopsy is generally unnecessary except for Candida leukoplakia (chronic hyperplastic candidosis), which appears homogeneous or nodular/speckled; confirmed by demonstrating fungal hyphae plus lesion resolution after antifungal therapy. Malignant transformation is reported in 9–40% of Candida-infected speckled leukoplakia versus 2–6% for homogeneous leukoplakia generally. Stains: **PAS and GMS** on smears/sections; culture on Sabouraud's agar ([Lu, J Fungi 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8306613/)).
- **Granular cell tumour (S100, SOX10, CD68, pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia pitfall):** polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm and small hyperchromatic central nuclei. Approximately 50% of intraoral cases show pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia (PEH) of the overlying epithelium that can mimic invasive well-differentiated SCC — a major pitfall, especially on a superficial biopsy with pronounced PEH plus clinical risk factors. IHC: strong (+++) nuclear and cytoplasmic S-100; strong (+++) vimentin; strong (+++) cytoplasmic CD68 in the granular cells; cytokeratin AE1/AE3 is *negative* in the granular cells (but positive in the overlying PEH epithelial cells), which helps exclude SCC; low Ki-67 supports a benign diagnosis, and p53 is low-to-moderate ([Ferreira et al., BMC Res Notes 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5217610/)). n.a. — SOX10 positivity was not independently confirmed in a fetched source for this task; only S-100, vimentin and CD68 are confirmed.
- **Mucocele (extravasation vs retention):** extravasation type (most common) is a pseudocyst without a defined wall, arising from duct rupture/trauma with mucin extravasating into the stroma, no epithelial lining, and a secondary inflammatory reaction. Retention type (less common) results from duct obstruction (e.g. sialoliths or strictures) with mucus retained within a lined space. Presence of salivary gland tissue with sialomucin is diagnostic ([More et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4211243/)).
- **Squamous papilloma:** exophytic/papillary architecture, often with a stalk; fronds of vascular connective tissue surfaced by bland squamous epithelium with variable keratinisation; scattered basilar/parabasilar mitoses *without* atypia; koilocytes are often present in the upper spinous layers. Distinguished from verruca vulgaris (less prominent keratinisation/granular layer, no inward cupping) and from papillary SCC (which shows immature/dysplastic basaloid cells and possible invasion) ([Betz, Head Neck Pathol 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405797/)).
- **Traumatic/chronic ulcer:** "most traumatic lesions have nonspecific histologic findings." Traumatic ulcerative granuloma with stromal eosinophilia (TUGSE) shows deep-seated ulceration extending into underlying tissue with a mixed infiltrate (lymphocytes, macrophages, numerous eosinophils, proliferating vessels); large CD30-positive cells may be present and do not indicate malignancy. Necrotising sialometaplasia can mimic malignancy clinically, presenting as deep hard-palate ulceration ([Fitzpatrick et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405793/)).

## Part B — Resection elements (oral cavity SCC)

### 7. Depth of invasion (DOI)

AJCC 8th/CAP definition: DOI is measured from the basement membrane of the adjacent normal mucosa to the deepest point of tumour invasion, using an "arcuate plumb line" method to handle uneven tumour contours — the original mucosal surface contour is reconstructed and a vertical (plumb) line is dropped from that reference line to the deepest invasive tumour cell. This differs from tumour thickness, which is measured from the mucosal surface itself (which may be elevated in an exophytic tumour or destroyed in an ulcerated one) straight down, perpendicular, to the deepest point of invasion. DOI and thickness are discordant in only about 5–6% of cases, but the discordance matters most in exophytic tumours (where the true DOI is less than the visible bulk) and ulcerated tumours (where thickness can be deceptively thin relative to true DOI, because the surface has been lost). For markedly exophytic tumours where DOI genuinely cannot be determined, selecting "less than 1 millimetre" is the appropriate default ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, Note B](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)). The RCPath oral cavity dataset confirms the same plumb-line method (reconstructing the basement membrane reference line and dropping a vertical line to the deepest tumour point), reports DOI in millimetres to one decimal place, and confirms DOI ≠ tumour thickness, with the mucosal surface similarly reconstructed for nodular or ulcerated tumours ([RCPath Oral Cavity dataset](https://www.rcpath.org/static/c4a9faf7-393a-4ba8-9532f719d8cdff3b/7b0a5709-ce18-4694-8e4c84a4ffd466f2/Dataset-for-histopathology-reporting-of-carcinomas-of-the-oral-cavity.pdf)). Both sources confirm the AJCC 8th/UICC T-category DOI thresholds: T1 ≤2 cm and DOI ≤5 mm; T2 ≤2 cm with DOI >5–10 mm, or 2–4 cm with DOI ≤10 mm; T3 >4 cm or DOI >10 mm ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf); [RCPath dataset](https://www.rcpath.org/static/c4a9faf7-393a-4ba8-9532f719d8cdff3b/7b0a5709-ce18-4694-8e4c84a4ffd466f2/Dataset-for-histopathology-reporting-of-carcinomas-of-the-oral-cavity.pdf)).

Note (checked against the CAP 4.3.0.0 pT list): the T3 summary above is the UICC shorthand. CAP prints pT3 as > 2 to ≤ 4 cm with DOI > 10 mm, or > 4 cm with DOI ≤ 10 mm, and pT4a as > 4 cm with DOI > 10 mm; `src/staging/oral.js` follows CAP.

### 8. Worst pattern of invasion (WPOI)

Five patterns are recognised, numbered 1 through 5, describing progressively more infiltrative/dispersed growth at the invasive tumour front. WPOI-5 is specifically defined as "tumour dispersion ≥1 mm between tumour satellites," and extratumoral perineural invasion and angiolymphatic invasion also count toward WPOI-5. WPOI has been validated as an independent prognosticator on multivariate analysis, but CAP notes it "can be viewed as redundant and only optional for reporting purposes," since extratumoral PNI and angiolymphatic invasion — both separately reported data elements — already capture the same adverse biology that WPOI-5 represents ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, Note F](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

### 9. Perineural invasion (PNI)

The CAP protocol distinguishes intratumoral from extratumoral PNI as separate reportable categories, with extratumoral PNI described as "an emerging element" that is "suggested for reporting" given its association with adverse outcome and its role in defining WPOI-5. On nerve calibre, the protocol states that reporting should include nerves of *all* sizes, "including small peripheral nerves (i.e., less than 1 mm in diameter)," because the prognostic significance of PNI has been shown to be independent of nerve diameter — there is no minimum-calibre threshold below which PNI can be safely ignored ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, Note E](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)). The QMH oral cavity synoptic's PNI field further distinguishes "present intraneural with nerve size" as its own response option, implying nerve calibre should be recorded when known, though this session's sources did not surface an explicit instruction to name the specific nerve (e.g., inferior alveolar) by anatomical designation — **n.a.** for a "name the nerve" requirement beyond size/intratumoral-extratumoral distinction.

### 10. Bone invasion

The RCPath oral cavity dataset lists bone invasion as a core data item with response categories "Not identified" / "Present"; when present, the pattern is recorded as "Erosive" or "Infiltrative," and the level of involvement as "Cortical" or "Medullary." Infiltrative bone involvement correlates with worse prognosis than erosive involvement. Critically, both the RCPath dataset and the CAP Oral Cavity protocol state the AJCC T4a rule: "superficial erosion of bone/tooth socket by gingival primary" alone is *not* sufficient to classify a tumour as T4a — for UICC TNM8, involvement of the medullary space is required. Recording cortex-limited invasion may still have descriptive/documentary value even though it does not by itself change TNM8 stage. Where bone is resected, bone resection margins should also be reported for carcinoma involvement ([RCPath Oral Cavity dataset](https://www.rcpath.org/static/c4a9faf7-393a-4ba8-9532f719d8cdff3b/7b0a5709-ce18-4694-8e4c84a4ffd466f2/Dataset-for-histopathology-reporting-of-carcinomas-of-the-oral-cavity.pdf); [CAP Oral Cavity protocol](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

### 11. Margins

**CAP distance reporting:** the CAP protocol requires the closest distance from invasive carcinoma to the surgical margin to be reported, together with a separate assessment of whether high-grade dysplasia/carcinoma in situ reaches a margin (cut-through). A positive margin is defined as invasive carcinoma, or CIS/high-grade dysplasia, present at the cut surface. Both the specimen margin and the separately submitted tumour-bed margin should be reported, since specimen margins carry more reliable prognostic value than tumour-bed margins. A "close" margin cut-off of 5 mm is commonly used (with a literature range of 3–7 mm); a multi-institutional review of 494 patients found that each additional millimetre of clearance reduced local recurrence risk by roughly 33%, up to about 5 mm, where the risk-reduction curve flattens, supporting the 5 mm breakpoint ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, Note G](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

**RCPath/ICCR categories with mm thresholds:** the RCPath dataset defines Involved margin as <1 mm, Close margin as 1–5 mm, and Clear/not involved margin as >5 mm, with a further "Cannot be assessed" category; margins are measured to one decimal place. Presence of high-grade dysplasia/CIS at the margin is recorded separately from invasive carcinoma at the margin, because HGD/CIS at the margin independently increases local recurrence risk. Laser or electrocautery artefact (cellular/nuclear polymorphism, hyperchromatism, cell separation, collagen denaturation) is flagged as a pitfall that can compromise margin assessment. The UICC residual tumour classification (RX/R0/R1/R2) is also recorded ([RCPath Oral Cavity dataset](https://www.rcpath.org/static/c4a9faf7-393a-4ba8-9532f719d8cdff3b/7b0a5709-ce18-4694-8e4c84a4ffd466f2/Dataset-for-histopathology-reporting-of-carcinomas-of-the-oral-cavity.pdf)).

### 12. Extranodal extension (ENE)

**Definition:** ENE is "extension of viable tumour outside the lymph node capsule into the perinodal soft tissues, with or without a desmoplastic reaction." It should be diagnosed only when viable tumour transgresses the entire thickness of the original (normal/primary) lymph node capsule and extends into perinodal soft tissue where it can directly interact with the extranodal host microenvironment; a desmoplastic stromal response may be present but is not required for the diagnosis ([HN-CLEAR Initiative Update 1, Head Neck Pathol 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11805726/)).

**ENEmi (minor) vs ENEma (major):** minor ENE (ENEmi) is defined as extension ≤2 mm; major ENE (ENEma) is defined as extension >2 mm. This matches the ICCR structured-report categories cited in the same source. Lymph node matting constitutes major/extensive ENE by definition, regardless of measured distance ([HN-CLEAR Initiative Update 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11805726/); consistent terminology given in [NAACCR SSDI data item specification](https://apps.naaccr.org/ssdi/input/gum/extranodal_ext_hn_path/?breadcrumbs)).

**Measurement method:** ENE extent is measured perpendicular to the lymph node capsule (never horizontally), in millimetres, from the reconstructed contour of the original lymph node capsule to the furthest extent of tumour within the extranodal soft tissue; the measurement usually includes the thickness of any reduplicated capsule. The original capsule contour is traced for reference; if it is not unequivocally identifiable in the section, a normal/benign node from the same neck dissection specimen may be used as a reference for expected capsule thickness (approximately 0.1–0.5 mm). Measurement precision near the 1–2 mm threshold is acknowledged to be affected by block selection, the level cut by the microtomist, and the pathologist's tracing of the original capsule contour ([HN-CLEAR Initiative Update 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11805726/)).

**Related diagnostic criteria (CAP oral cavity protocol, citing HNCIG/HN-CLEAR consensus):** "Matted" nodes, in which tumour crosses between adjacent lymph nodes, are ENE-positive. "Fused, adherent, confluent, conglomerate" nodes — where adjacency is due to inflammatory/stromal adherence without tumour transgression — are ENE-negative. Soft tissue tumour deposits are ENE-positive. Extranodal lymphovascular or perineural invasion is counted as LVI/PNI even where the primary tumour itself does not show it, but such extranodal LVI/PNI alone is not, by itself, classified as ENE ([CAP Oral Cavity protocol, Note H, citing Abou-Foul et al., Lancet Oncol 2024;25(7):e286-96, and Gupta et al., Head Neck Pathol 2025;19(1):20](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/HN.Oral_4.3.0.0.REL_CAPCP.pdf)).

**AJCC 8th edition pN category:** the AJCC and UICC recommend upstaging head-and-neck cutaneous and HPV-independent mucosal HNSCC (which includes oral cavity SCC) when ENE is present — that is, any definitive ENE (minor or major) counts as "ENE-positive" and increases the nodal category, consistent with the QMH oral cavity template's pN0–pN3b scheme, which explicitly distinguishes ENE(+) from ENE(-) at each applicable nodal tier. ENE may potentially influence staging for HPV-associated oropharyngeal SCC in the future, but under the current (9th edition) HPV-oropharynx staging scheme used on the QMH HPV-oropharynx template, ENE already directly determines the pN category (pN1a/1b have no ENE; pN2 requires ENE in 1–4 nodes, or more than 4 nodes without ENE; pN3 requires ENE in more than 4 nodes) rather than acting as a separate modifier as in the 8th-edition oral cavity scheme ([HN-CLEAR Initiative Update 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11805726/); QMH oral cavity and HPV-oropharynx template pTNM tables, rendered from [awiloqmh.github.io/CancerReportingTemplate/HN-Lip%20and%20oral%20cavity.html](https://awiloqmh.github.io/CancerReportingTemplate/HN-Lip%20and%20oral%20cavity.html) and [awiloqmh.github.io/CancerReportingTemplate/HN-HPV-Oropharynx.html](https://awiloqmh.github.io/CancerReportingTemplate/HN-HPV-Oropharynx.html)). The RCPath dataset's pN category text is consistent with the AJCC 8th-edition scheme for oral cavity (pN1 no ENE; pN2a single node <3 cm with ENE, or 3–6 cm without ENE; pN2b multiple ipsilateral without ENE; pN2c bilateral/contralateral without ENE; pN3a >6 cm without ENE; pN3b >3 cm with ENE, or multiple/any contralateral node with ENE) but did not itself define ENEmi/ENEma terminology, referring instead to a separate nodal-excision dataset not fetched in this session ([RCPath Oral Cavity dataset](https://www.rcpath.org/static/c4a9faf7-393a-4ba8-9532f719d8cdff3b/7b0a5709-ce18-4694-8e4c84a4ffd466f2/Dataset-for-histopathology-reporting-of-carcinomas-of-the-oral-cavity.pdf)).
